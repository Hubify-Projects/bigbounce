# Cover Letter

**To:** The Editor, Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics

**Re:** Submission of "Geometric Dark Energy: Phenomenological Viability, Systematic Closures, and Requirements for Completion"

---

Dear Editor,

I am submitting the enclosed manuscript for consideration as a Regular Article in JCAP.

The paper presents a phenomenological dark-energy framework based on Einstein-Cartan-Holst gravity, performs MCMC fits to Planck, DESI BAO, Pantheon+, and SH0ES data, and then systematically tests five first-principles routes to deriving the late-time equation of state w = -1. All five routes fail with clean, documentable negative results.

The central new contribution is the identification of a **mass-coupling lock** in Poincare gauge theory (PGT): in the ghost-free 0^- axial torsion mode, canonical normalization forces the mass and matter coupling to decrease together, so that making the mode cosmologically light simultaneously suppresses its coupling ~10^29 times below gravitational strength. This structural constraint — which applies generically when a field's kinetic normalization, mass, and coupling share a common parameter — has not been previously stated as a constraint on geometric dark-energy programs. From this and four additional closures, the paper formulates five concrete decision rules (DR1-DR5) that any viable geometric dark-energy theory must satisfy.

The paper is relevant to JCAP's scope in several ways:
- It combines cosmological data analysis (MCMC constraints, Bayesian model comparison) with theoretical model assessment (closure program, structural requirements)
- The mass-coupling lock constrains a broad class of torsion-based dark-energy models currently under active investigation (e.g., Liu et al. arXiv:2503.02599; Legner et al. arXiv:2507.09228)
- The DR framework provides a practical checklist for evaluating future geometric dark-energy proposals

The manuscript is 31 pages with 6 tables and no figures. A companion technical note documenting the detailed closure calculations is referenced and will be made available on arXiv.

This work has not been submitted elsewhere and has no conflicts of interest.
