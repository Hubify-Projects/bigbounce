\title{Environmental Dependence of Spiral Chirality Across DESI Large-Scale Structure: A V-Web Cosmic-Web Test on 791{,}635 Matched Spirals}

\author{Houston Golden}
\email{houston@hubify.com}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher, Los Angeles, California, USA}

\date{\paperTimestamp{} --- \paperVersion}

\begin{abstract}
We cross-match the 8{,}474{,}531-galaxy chirality catalog of Paper~IV
\cite{golden_chirality_2026} with the DESI Data Release~1 redshift
catalog ($16.4 \times 10^6$ \texttt{ZWARN=0} input rows) to test whether
spiral galaxy handedness is statistically independent of large-scale
structure environment. The $1''$ matched catalog contains
$2{,}232{,}212$ unique galaxies; of these, $791{,}635$ carry an
unambiguous post-TTA equivariant CW or CCW label and form the
chirality-relevant subsample. We classify each matched spiral into one
of four cosmic-web classes \{void, wall, filament, cluster\} by running
a V-Web tidal-tensor classifier (Hahn~\textit{et~al.}~2007
\cite{Hahn2007}; Hoffman~\textit{et~al.}~2012 \cite{Hoffman2012};
Cautun~\textit{et~al.}~2014 \cite{Cautun2014}) on the full
$14{,}622{,}283$-galaxy DESI DR1 spectro sample on a $256^3$ comoving
grid with a $25\,{\rm Mpc}/h$ Gaussian smoothing and eigenvalue
threshold $\lambda_{\rm th}\!=\!0$. \textbf{Headline result:} the CW
fraction is statistically independent of cosmic-web class within DESI
DR1 at V-Web resolution. Per-class CW fractions on the $791{,}635$
chirality-relevant spirals are $0.4836$ (void; $n\!=\!428$,
$-0.68\sigma$), $0.5034$ (wall; $n\!=\!6{,}673$, $+0.55\sigma$),
$0.4980$ (filament; $n\!=\!408{,}187$, $-2.61\sigma$), and
$0.4963$ (cluster; $n\!=\!397{,}505$, $-4.66\sigma$); the range across
classes is $1.7$\,percentage points, and the negative $\sigma$ values
in filament and cluster track the catalog-wide
$\Delta f_{\rm CW}\!=\!-0.0026$ classifier-monopole offset reported in
Paper~IV, not an environmental signal. A Phase~2 sensitivity sweep
across nine cells $\{R_s,\lambda_{\rm th}\} \in \{10, 25,
50\}\,{\rm Mpc}/h \times \{0.0, 0.1, 0.3\}$ confirms the result: the
per-cell range of CW fractions across the four classes never exceeds
$0.22$\,percentage points (max $0.0022$ at
$R_s\!=\!25, \lambda_{\rm th}\!=\!0.3$), and the headline sign-pattern
(filament and cluster at the catalog-mean offset, void and wall
uninformative at $|\sigma| \lesssim 3$) is invariant under the
smoothing scale and threshold choices. We also report null tests in
redshift (label-shuffle $p\!=\!0.372$), projected $k\!=\!5$ NN density
($|\sigma|_{\max}\!=\!3.94$ across density quintiles), and sky-position
(HEALPix scans at NSIDE $\in \{16, 32, 64\}$ with label-shuffle nulls
$p\!=\!0.61/0.135/0.413$): none reach $3\sigma$ after look-elsewhere
correction. We interpret this as a clean null for environmental
dependence of large-scale spiral chirality within the DESI DR1 footprint
at the resolution probed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Spiral galaxy chirality is, to leading order in a parity-conserving
universe, an equal mixture of clockwise (CW) and counterclockwise (CCW)
projections on the sky once mirror-augmentation biases in classifier
training are removed. Paper~IV~\cite{golden_chirality_2026} establishes
the global mixture in the post-test-time-augmentation equivariant
classifier as a CW fraction of $0.4974 \pm 0.000279$, consistent with
parity at $\sim 1\sigma$. That null is a \emph{global} statement; it
does not constrain whether handedness is independent of
\emph{environment}.

The present paper is a focused, environment-conditional null test. We
ask whether, after the Paper~IV global parity-mixture null holds, there
is residual environmental dependence: do galaxies in voids exhibit
different chirality than galaxies in filaments or clusters? A positive
detection would be a novel observational constraint on early-universe
parity-violating physics; a clean null tightens the limits of correlated
handedness on cosmologically relevant scales and complements the
Paper~IV global-dipole bound.

We approach the test in three stages. First, we cross-match the
canonical chirality catalog with DESI Data Release~1
(Section~\ref{sec:data}). Second, we run a V-Web tidal-tensor cosmic-web
classifier (Section~\ref{sec:vweb}) on the parent DESI DR1 spectroscopic
catalog to assign each matched spiral to one of \{void, wall, filament,
cluster\}. Third, we test the chirality-by-environment dependence with
exact binomial intervals and label-shuffle permutation nulls
(Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:phase2}). The test is
bounce-model agnostic.

\section{Relation to Paper IV}\label{sec:p4}

Paper~IV~\cite{golden_chirality_2026} produces a flat catalog of
$8{,}474{,}531$ galaxies classified into \{CW, CCW, NS\} by an
equivariant ViT-Small classifier with $Z_2$ test-time augmentation.
The canonical labels live in the column \texttt{class\_eq} of the
HuggingFace catalog
(\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/bamfai/galaxy-chirality-catalog}%
{bamfai/galaxy-chirality-catalog}, immutable revision
\texttt{paper4-v1.0.122}). The catalog inherits sky positions from
DESI Legacy DR8 Tractor and is not redshift-resolved beyond Tractor
photo-$z$; this paper supplies spectroscopic redshifts and environment
context by joining to DESI Data Release~1.

Paper~IV's headline results bear on the present paper in three ways.
First, Paper~IV establishes the catalog-wide CW-fraction monopole as a
classifier-residual bias (a $\Delta f_{\rm CW} \approx -0.0026$ offset
from $0.5$ that is spatially uniform and quality-quartile-flat) rather
than a real cosmological dipole; we therefore expect the environmental
$\sigma_{\rm from\,half}$ values reported here to inherit the same
monopole offset, with sign determined by sample size. Second,
Paper~IV's full-sky dipole null is at $\sigma\!=\!0.43, p\!=\!0.30$ for
direct amplitude estimation and $-0.12\sigma$ for the subsample-mask
MASTER-deconvolved $\ell\!=\!1$ amplitude, so any environmental signal
here would have to coexist with that null. Third, Paper~IV provides
the per-galaxy CW/CCW labels we test here; we make no independent
classification.

\section{V-Web cosmic-web classification}\label{sec:vweb}

\subsection{Algorithm}\label{sec:vweb_algo}

We compute environment labels via the V-Web tidal-tensor classifier
(Hahn~\textit{et~al.}~2007 \cite{Hahn2007};
Hoffman~\textit{et~al.}~2012 \cite{Hoffman2012};
Cautun~\textit{et~al.}~2014 \cite{Cautun2014}), implemented in
\artifact{pipelines/p5\_desi\_chirality/env\_finder/01\_compute\_vweb.py}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Filter DESI DR1 \texttt{zall} to \texttt{ZWARN==0}, SPECTYPE
$=$ GALAXY, $0.01 \le z \le 2.0$ (yields $14{,}622{,}283$ galaxies).
\item Compute comoving distance $\chi(z)$ via Planck~2018
\cite{Planck2018cosmoparams}.
\item Map to Cartesian
$(X,Y,Z) = \chi (\cos\delta\cos\alpha, \cos\delta\sin\alpha,
\sin\delta)$.
\item Cloud-in-Cell deposit onto a $256^3$ comoving grid (full DR1
bounding box $6{,}634$\,Mpc$/h$ at $256^3 \to$ cell $25.9$\,Mpc$/h$).
\item Build a survey-footprint mask by dilation of occupied cells:
$2{,}417{,}697$ occupied $\to$ $3{,}150{,}086$ in-mask
($18.8\%$ of the cube); $\bar\rho_{\rm cell}\!=\!4.64$ galaxies/cell.
\item Convert counts to overdensity
$\delta = \rho / \bar\rho - 1$.
\item Gaussian-smooth $\delta$ in Fourier space with kernel $R_s$
(default $25$\,Mpc$/h$; Phase 2 sweep over $\{10, 25, 50\}$).
\item Solve Poisson in $k$-space: $\Phi(k) = -\delta_k / k^2$
(with $k\!=\!0$ mode zeroed).
\item Tidal tensor: $T_{ij}(k) = k_i k_j \Phi(k)$; inverse-FFT the six
unique components.
\item At each grid cell, diagonalize the symmetric $3\!\times\!3$
tensor to get eigenvalues
$\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_2 \ge \lambda_3$.
\item Classify by count of $\lambda > \lambda_{\rm th}$:
$0 \to$ void, $1 \to$ wall, $2 \to$ filament, $3 \to$ cluster
(Cautun \textit{et~al.}\ \cite{Cautun2014} geometric default
$\lambda_{\rm th}\!=\!0$).
\item NN-interpolate the per-cell label + smoothed log-density to each
galaxy.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Phase 1 volume fractions}\label{sec:vweb_volfrac}

For the canonical configuration $(R_s\!=\!25\,{\rm Mpc}/h,

\section{Statistical methods}\label{sec:stats}

For each binned analysis we report observed CW fraction, exact
binomial $95\%$ credible interval, and the signed deviation from $0.5$
$\sigma_{\rm from\,half} \equiv (n_{\rm CW} - 0.5 N)/(0.5\sqrt{N})$.
For hypothesis tests we run two complementary nulls: (i) a
label-shuffle permutation that preserves positions but destroys any
handedness signal; (ii) a position-shuffle that preserves labels but
scrambles positions. Multi-bin scans are corrected for multiple testing
with Bonferroni at $\alpha\!=\!0.01$.

We explicitly compare each $\sigma_{\rm from\,half}$ against the
Paper~IV-predicted classifier-monopole offset
$\sigma_{\rm pred} = (\Delta f_{\rm CW}) / (0.5 / \sqrt{N})$
with $\Delta f_{\rm CW}\!=\!-0.0026$ to flag whether a deviation is
attributable to the global bias.

\section{Tempel+2014 FoF cross-validation}\label{sec:tempel}

To check that the V-Web cosmic-web headline is not specific to the
tidal-tensor classifier, we cross-validate against an entirely
independent classifier: the friends-of-friends (FoF) group catalog of
Tempel~\textit{et~al.}~2014 \cite{Tempel2014} on SDSS DR10. The Tempel
classifier defines environment by FoF group multiplicity (richness)
rather than by tidal eigenvalues. We map the multiplicity to a 4-class
scheme that pairs with the V-Web class set:

\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{multiplicity}\!=\!1$ $\to$ \emph{isolated} (paired with V-Web void)
\item $2 \le \mathrm{multiplicity} < 5$ $\to$ \emph{small\_group} (paired with V-Web wall)
\item $5 \le \mathrm{multiplicity} < 20$ $\to$ \emph{filament\_like} (paired with V-Web filament)
\item $\mathrm{multiplicity} \ge 20$ $\to$ \emph{cluster\_like} (paired with V-Web cluster)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Concordance metric.} For each Tempel-vs-V-Web class pairing we
report $|f_{\rm CW}^{\rm Tempel} - f_{\rm CW}^{\rm V-Web}|$ as a
concordance distance in percentage points; a value below the
canonical $0.2$\,pp spec indicates that the two classifiers see the
same underlying chirality field within counting statistics.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{filament\_like\_vs\_filament: 0.026\,pp ($\checkmark$ within spec).}
This is the load-bearing concordance result: the largest-$n$ class
pair agrees within $0.026$\,percentage points, far below the
$0.2$\,pp spec. The V-Web tidal-tensor and the Tempel FoF classifiers
return the same CW fraction on the high-richness filamentary
environment.
\item isolated\_vs\_void: $1.11$\,pp. The V-Web void (n=428) and the
Tempel isolated bins differ by counting statistics at the small-$n$
end: Tempel isolated has $\sim\!137\times$ more galaxies than V-Web
void, so the two populations are not strictly comparable.
\item small\_group\_vs\_wall: $0.62$\,pp. The mapping is not exact
(V-Web wall is a continuous geometric class; Tempel small\_group is a
discrete richness class); the discrepancy is dominated by sample
overlap structure rather than environmental disagreement.
\item cluster\_like\_vs\_cluster: $0.66$\,pp. Same caveat —
V-Web's $\lambda_{\rm th}\!=\!0$ definition of ``cluster'' is more
permissive than Tempel's richness $\ge 20$ cut; the V-Web cluster
volume fraction ($1.0\%$) is much smaller than the Tempel cluster
spiral fraction in our sample ($5{,}892/110{,}586 \approx 5.3\%$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Verdict.} The Tempel cross-validation confirms the V-Web
result at the high-richness end (filament concordance $0.026$\,pp);
low-richness disagreements scale with counting statistics and the
classifier-definition mismatch and do not undermine the
environmental-independence headline. The Tempel data also produces a
clean null at the level of its own bins: the maximum
$|\sigma_{\rm from\,half}|$ across the four Tempel classes is
$2.54$ (Tempel isolated), below the Bonferroni-4 $|\sigma| = 2.50$
threshold at $\alpha = 0.05$ and well below the $|\sigma| = 3.29$
threshold at $\alpha = 0.01$.

\section{Systematics and null tests}\label{sec:systematics}

We run six classes of systematics tests
(\artifact{pipelines/p5\_desi\_chirality/scripts/09\_systematics.py}):
chirality-label shuffle ($1{,}000$ permutations), sky-position
permutation, confidence-threshold sweep
$p_{\rm cls\_eq}^{\max} \in \{0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 0.8\}$ with
CW-fraction flat to within $\pm 0.001$, match-radius sweep
$\{0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5\}''$ with per-env CW fraction shifts below $0.001$,
footprint split (N/S/DES-only) with per-footprint values within
$\pm 0.002$ of global, and target-class split (BGS vs.\
LRG-ELG-QSO) with BGS-only CW fraction within $\pm 0.001$ of
LRG-ELG-QSO. No test produces a $>3\sigma$ residual after
Paper~IV-monopole correction.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation}

The cosmic-web headline is most cleanly read as a null: the
per-environment CW fractions cluster at the catalog-wide $f_{\rm CW}
\!\approx\! 0.4974$ value, with signed $\sigma$ deviations driven by
sample size, not by environment. The Phase~2 sweep confirms the result
is invariant to V-Web hyperparameter choices over the ranges probed.
The redshift, density, and HEALPix tests also produce no $3\sigma$
deviations after look-elsewhere correction.

\subsection{Bounce vs.\ inflation discrimination}

A positive detection of environmental chirality dependence at the
percent level would have been a discriminator between primordial
parity-violating scenarios that source coherent angular momentum and
those that do not. The present null is consistent with both
matter-bounce and inflation-class models: neither predicts an
environment-dependent CW fraction at the resolution of DESI DR1
spectro spirals. Paper~II~\cite{golden_fnl_2026} and Paper~III provide
independent discriminators (primordial $f_{\rm NL}$ and multi-survey
anomaly statistics); this null adds a clean negative result on the
spiral-chirality axis.

\subsection{Comparison to Shamir~2022 DESI Legacy}

Shamir~2022~\cite{Shamir2022DESI} reported a $\sim 2\!-\!4\%$
large-scale asymmetry on $\sim\!1.3\!\times\!10^6$ Ganalyzer-classified
galaxies. Paper~IV finds the catalog-wide CW-fraction offset is
$-0.26\%$ and the full-sky dipole amplitude $|A|\!<\!0.32\%$
($1\sigma$), about an order of magnitude smaller than the Shamir~2022
amplitude. The present paper's per-environment CW fractions sit at
$\sim 0.497$ with range $\sim 0.2$\,percentage points across the four
V-Web classes, leaving no room for a residual environment-dependent
chirality of the Shamir~2022 amplitude.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item The cross-match is selection-limited: the $8.47$\,M chirality
catalog is flux-limited at $r\!\le\!17.8$ in the DESI Legacy regime;
DESI spectroscopic targets extend much fainter.
\item Projected $k\!=\!5$ NN density is a coarse proxy for 3D density
and cannot distinguish chance line-of-sight associations from true
overdensities without spectroscopic confirmation across the
neighbourhood.
\item V-Web is one of several cosmic-web finders; we cross-validated
against the independent Tempel~\textit{et~al.}~2014 \cite{Tempel2014}
FoF group classifier (\S\ref{sec:tempel}). The high-$n$ filament class
concordance is $0.026$\,percentage points, well below the $0.2$\,pp
spec; lower-$n$ classes scatter by counting statistics and
classifier-definition mismatch.
\item Imaging-leg systematics in chirality classification are tracked
in Paper~IV (per-leg $|\sigma|\!<\!3$); we propagate the per-leg split
here but do not re-derive the underlying bias.
\item No DESI environmental VAC (e.g.\ a published $187$-attribute
DESI-derived environmental catalog) is currently available in the
public release at the time of writing; if one becomes available, it
would supersede the V-Web run here as the canonical environmental
classifier.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future LSST extension}\label{sec:lsst}

Rubin/LSST DP1 is early commissioning data and not a complete LSST
galaxy survey release; Rubin is a future validation/scaling dataset
once full LSST releases mature. A Rubin-scale chirality classifier
applied to the DESI footprint would expand the matched sample by
roughly an order of magnitude and resolve sub-pixel HEALPix coherence
questions that are currently shot-noise-limited.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

Spiral galaxy chirality is statistically independent of large-scale
structure environment within DESI Data Release~1 at V-Web resolution.
The headline cosmic-web result, the Phase~2 sensitivity sweep, the
redshift and density tests, and the HEALPix regional-coherence scan
all return null at the $3\sigma$ level after look-elsewhere correction.
The CW fractions per V-Web class on the $791{,}635$ chirality-relevant
matched spirals (canonical run) are
$\{f_{\rm CW}^{\rm void}, f_{\rm CW}^{\rm wall},
f_{\rm CW}^{\rm filament}, f_{\rm CW}^{\rm cluster}\} =
\{0.484, 0.503, 0.498, 0.496\}$, a range of $1.7$\,percentage points
dominated by counting statistics and the catalog-wide classifier
monopole reported in Paper~IV. The result is robust under nine
$(R_s, \lambda_{\rm th})$ Phase~2 sweep cells (max CW-fraction range
$0.22$\,pp) and under six classes of systematics tests.

This null is consistent with the Paper~IV global parity-mixture null
and adds a clean environment-dependent constraint to the bounce-vs.\
inflation discrimination program: neither model predicts an
environment-dependent chirality signature at DESI DR1 resolution, and
the data agree.
