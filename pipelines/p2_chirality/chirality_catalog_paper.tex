\begin{abstract}
We present the largest galaxy chirality catalog to date: 8,474,531
galaxies classified from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR8
(Smith42/galaxies)---of which $3{,}201{,}160$ are spirals (1{,}592{,}107
\CW{} + 1{,}609{,}053 \CCW{} after equivariant test-time symmetrization;
the remaining 5{,}273{,}371 are \NS{} or edge-on)---each classified as
clockwise~(\CW), counter-clockwise~(\CCW), or not
spiral~(\NS) by a Vision Transformer (\vit) fine-tuned on 26,626 images
from Galaxy Zoo~1, high-confidence CE-ResNet classifications, and
synthetic hard negatives. The classifier achieves 93.7\% three-class
validation accuracy (with 67.6\% of training labels derived from
CE-ResNet predictions rather than independent ground truth;
see Sec.~\ref{sec:labels}) and passes all eight tests in a purpose-built
bias-hardening suite on the validation split. We disclose the
8-test split (Sec.~\ref{sec:bias}): four model-perturbation
\emph{stress tests} (flip-swap, rotation, perturbation,
metadata-leakage) probe response at the signal-amplitude level
and constitute the load-bearing constraints on residual
orientation-dependent bias, while four result-level
\emph{sanity checks} (artifact rejection, hemispheric null,
calibration, CW-balance) rule out gross failure modes at coarser
qualitative thresholds. The residual rotational/perturbation
sensitivity is absorbed at the equivariant\footnote{\label{fn:equivariant_scope}\emph{Scope of ``equivariant'' in this paper.}---Throughout this work, ``equivariant'' refers specifically to the test-time horizontal-flip ($Z_2$) symmetrization defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:tta} (``flip-TTA symmetrized''), \emph{not} to architectural rotation equivariance (e.g., the $C_4$/$D_4$ steerable convolutions of CE-ResNet~\cite{Jia:2023} or fully SO(2)/SE(2)-equivariant networks). The procedure ensures $\pcw \leftrightarrow \pccw$ exchange under horizontal reflection of the input image, which is the symmetry relevant to chirality assignment; rotational orientation invariance is \emph{not} enforced and remains a residual source of bias (Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac}).}
post-processing stage with a measured suppression factor of $3.86\times$
on the full catalog. A flip-equivariance
consistency loss during training, combined with two-fold test-time
averaging (TTA, not architectural equivariance), eliminate the
horizontal-flip component of orientation-dependent bias by
construction (Catalog~C). Rotational orientation dependence
($90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $270^\circ$) is not eliminated and remains
a potential source of the residual $9.5\sigma$ asymmetry; see
Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac}. The equivariant catalog yields a global
clockwise fraction $\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.4974 \pm 0.0003$.
We find no significant large-scale chirality dipole: a simple fit gives
$0.43\sigmaunit$, while a full angular power spectrum yields a marginal
$2.75\sigmaunit$ at $\ell = 1$. A $3.05\sigmaunit$ hemisphere asymmetry
is detected at only 0.17\% amplitude and does not survive a
look-elsewhere correction. This is the most sensitive chirality
measurement performed, with a minimum detectable dipole of $0.2\%$ at
$3\sigmaunit$. The $\sim\!3\%$ asymmetry reported by
Shamir~(2020, 2022)~\cite{Shamir:2020,Shamir:2022} is strongly disfavored within the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys footprint under the present pipeline: our
maximum regional asymmetry is 0.32\% (Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance}), a factor of~9 smaller. We further
show that raw survey systematics can masquerade as a $94.6\sigmaunit$
dipole from a classifier \CW{} bias of only 0.79\%, underscoring the
necessity of equivariant post-processing. All sky regions are balanced
to within 0.5\% of 50/50. The catalog is available at
\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/bamfai/galaxy-chirality-catalog}
under CC-BY-4.0.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================

The handedness (chirality) of spiral galaxies---whether their arms
trail clockwise (CW) or counter-clockwise (CCW) as projected on the
sky---is a simple observable with potentially profound implications for
fundamental physics. In a statistically isotropic and parity-symmetric
universe, the CW and CCW fractions should be exactly equal when averaged
over large angular scales. A significant departure from this null
expectation would signal parity violation at cosmological scales,
providing a window into physics beyond the Standard Model of
cosmology~\cite{Alexander:2009tp}.

Claims of such a signal have appeared intermittently in the literature.
Shamir~(2012)~\cite{Shamir:2012} reported a $2\text{--}4\sigmaunit$ chirality
dipole using $\sim\!10^4$ Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) galaxies
classified by the deterministic Ganalyzer algorithm.
Shamir~(2020)~\cite{Shamir:2020} extended this to $\sim\!10^5$ galaxies from
multiple surveys, reporting asymmetries of $\sim\!3\%$ with a
consistent dipole axis. Shamir~(2022)~\cite{Shamir:2022} further claimed
confirmation with DESI Legacy Survey data. Meanwhile,
Iye \textit{et~al.}~\cite{Iye:2020} analyzed Galaxy Zoo data and found no significant
signal after correcting for the known ``reading direction'' bias in
citizen science classifications. Tadaki \textit{et~al.}~\cite{Tadaki:2020} studied a
smaller sample with HSC-SSP imaging and likewise found null results.

The tension between these results has not been resolved, largely
because the classifiers used in positive-claim studies lack published
bias audits. The Ganalyzer algorithm~\cite{Shamir:2012} is
deterministic and by construction yields identical CW/CCW probabilities
for an image and its mirror reflection, but its classification accuracy
on ambiguous morphologies is validated only by small-sample manual
checks ($\sim\!400$ galaxies). More importantly, no study has tested
for brightness-dependent, position-dependent, or artifact-driven
chirality biases in a systematic and quantitative manner.

Recently, Jia \textit{et~al.}~\cite{Jia:2023} introduced CE-ResNet, a chirality-equivariant
convolutional neural network that guarantees, by architectural
construction, that horizontally flipping an input image exactly swaps
the CW and CCW output channels. This eliminates model-induced chirality
bias without post-processing. Their catalog of 1.95~million galaxies
from DESI Legacy pre-imaging yields $\CW/\CCW = 0.998$,\footnote{CE-ResNet's notation labels the second class \emph{ACW} (anti-clockwise); we use the equivalent \CCW{} (counter-clockwise) throughout this paper. The two are identical orientation conventions.}
consistent with parity. CE-ResNet represents the current state of the
art for unbiased chirality classification, but its catalog covers a
factor of~4 fewer galaxies than the full DESI Legacy footprint.

In this paper we present a new chirality catalog that advances beyond
CE-ResNet in three respects: (i)~survey-scale coverage of 8.47~million
galaxies classified, of which $3{,}201{,}160$ are equivariant-classified
spirals (1{,}592{,}107~\CW{} + 1{,}609{,}053~\CCW; the remaining
5{,}273{,}371 are \NS{} or edge-on, see Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}),
constituting the chirality-relevant subsample---this
spiral count alone is ${\sim}16\times$ larger than the largest prior
chirality catalog (Shamir~\cite{Shamir:2022}, ${\sim}200{,}000$
spirals) and $1.6\times$ larger than CE-ResNet~\cite{Jia:2023}
($1.95$~million galaxies, all classified as CW or CCW since
CE-ResNet lacks a not-spiral class);
(ii)~a dedicated \NS{} class that
prevents contamination from ellipticals and irregulars, and (iii)~the
first published multi-test bias hardening audit suite for any galaxy
chirality classifier. We use this catalog to perform the most
sensitive chirality measurement ever attempted, capable of detecting a
dipole asymmetry as small as $0.2\%$ at $3\sigmaunit$.  The result
is consistent with null parity violation at the sub-percent level: no
evidence for large-scale parity violation. The global (monopole) CW
offset is $0.26\%$ ($\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.4974$ vs.\ $0.5000$), which
is spatially uniform and produces no dipole signal; the dipole
amplitude upper limit is ${\sim}0.2\%$ (minimum detectable at
$3\sigmaunit$). This strongly disfavors Shamir's claimed
$\sim\!3\%$ asymmetry signal in the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys footprint
under the present pipeline by a factor of~9.  Perhaps equally important, we
demonstrate that uncorrected survey systematics can produce a
$94.6\sigmaunit$ spurious dipole from a classifier bias of
only $0.79\%$---a cautionary result for the entire field.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Galaxy Images}
\label{sec:images}

Our parent sample is the Smith42/galaxies dataset hosted on
HuggingFace\footnote{\url{https://huggingface.co/datasets/Smith42/galaxies}},
containing 8,474,688 galaxy images drawn from the DESI Legacy Imaging
Surveys Data Release~8~\cite{Dey:2019}. Each image is a
$224 \times 224$~pixel cutout in the $grz$ bands at a native scale
of $0.262''$/pixel. The dataset is distributed as 192 Parquet shards,
each containing $\sim\!44{,}000$ galaxies with unique \texttt{dr8\_id}
identifiers. Sky coordinates (right ascension and declination, J2000
ICRS) are obtained by cross-matching \texttt{dr8\_id} against the
Galaxy Zoo DESI predictions catalog~\cite{Walmsley:2023}.

Redshift estimates, when propagated through the Galaxy Zoo DESI
cross-match~\cite{Walmsley:2023}, are photometric (DESI Legacy Imaging
Surveys photo-$z$ catalog) with typical precision
$\sigma_z / (1+z) \approx 0.03$ for the magnitude range of our spiral
sample. No spectroscopic redshifts are used in this analysis; the
implied redshift-smearing floor of $\Delta z \sim 0.05$ at
$z \sim 0.5$ is carried forward as a limitation on future
redshift-binned dipole analyses (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).

\subsection{Training Labels}
\label{sec:labels}

We assemble training labels from three sources:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Galaxy Zoo~1}~\cite{Lintott:2008}: 6,637~galaxies
  with citizen-science CW/CCW labels at $>70\%$ vote confidence,
  spatially cross-matched to DESI Legacy cutouts (original GZ1 catalog:
  $\sim$14{,}000 objects with spiral classifications; reduced by our
  minimum-agreement threshold and magnitude cut). This is the only
  fully independent label source.

\item \textbf{CE-ResNet}~\cite{Jia:2023}: 17,153~galaxies with
  high-confidence ($>0.8$) spiral classifications from the
  chirality-equivariant ResNet catalog. An additional 846~galaxies
  confidently classified as non-spiral supplement the \NS{} class.

\item \textbf{Synthetic hard negatives}: 2,000~artificial images
  (blank sky, pixel-shuffled galaxies, uniform noise, gradient fields)
  serving as unambiguous \NS{} training examples.
\end{enumerate}

The combined training set contains 26,626~images, split 80/20 into
training and validation subsets with stratified class balance.
The ${\sim}27{,}000$-galaxy training set covers $< 0.32\%$ of the inference
catalog. While the DESI Legacy Survey imaging is photometrically
uniform across the footprint, the training set's morphological type
distribution may not fully represent the rarer galaxy types in the
8.47M sample; the \NS{} class mitigates this by explicitly routing
ambiguous objects away from chirality classification.
We note that 67.6\% of training labels (17,999 of 26,626) derive from
CE-ResNet predictions. Validation metrics computed against the full
training set therefore partially reflect agreement with CE-ResNet rather
than independent ground truth. To quantify the circular-labeling effect,
we ran the production catalog against the \emph{full} Galaxy~Zoo~1
Table~2~\cite{Lintott:2008} (667{,}944 objects), restricted to the
252{,}415 galaxies with a deterministic GZ1 class label
(\textsc{spiral} with $P_{\mathrm{CW}}\!\neq\!P_{\mathrm{CCW}}$ in our convention---GZ1's published schema labels this variable $P_{\mathrm{ACW}}$ (anti-clockwise), which is identical to $P_{\mathrm{CCW}}$ as established in the footnote at the start of Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}---or
\textsc{elliptical}). At a $1.0''$ sky-match radius, 240{,}919 GZ1 objects
($95.45\%$) cross-match into the catalog. The equivariant-head
three-class accuracy on this independent GZ1 sample is
$\mathbf{58.71\%}$ ($141{,}438\,/\,240{,}919$); the spiral-only CW versus
CCW accuracy on the 117{,}205 GZ1 spirals where the model also predicts
a chirality is $\mathbf{69.91\%}$. Both numbers are well below the
headline $93.7\%$ measured against the CE-ResNet-augmented training
validation set. The gap is the magnitude of the circular-labeling effect:
the headline figure measures internal consistency with a 67.6\%
CE-ResNet-derived training corpus, while the GZ1-only number measures
agreement with citizen-science labels on a fully independent sample.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Model Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The classifier consists of a \vit{} encoder~\cite{Dosovitskiy:2020}
(\texttt{vit\_small\_patch16\_224},
ImageNet-pretrained) with the last 6 of 12 transformer blocks
fine-tuned, followed by a custom classification head:
%
\begin{equation}
\text{LayerNorm} \to 384{\to}512~(\text{GELU}, d{=}0.3) \to
512{\to}256~(\text{GELU}, d{=}0.2) \to 256{\to}3~(\text{softmax}),
\end{equation}
%
where $d$ denotes dropout rate. The three-class output
$(\pcw,\,\pccw,\,\pns)$ is critical for full-survey deployment:
applying a binary CW/CCW classifier to data where $\sim\!70\%$ of
objects are elliptical or irregular produces a catalog dominated by
noise classifications.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}

The model is trained with AdamW optimization (head learning rate
$3 \times 10^{-4}$, encoder learning rate $2 \times 10^{-5}$,
weight decay $0.02$), batch size~64, and a cosine annealing
warm-restart schedule ($T_0 = 10$, $T_{\rm mult} = 2$). Early
stopping with patience of 15 epochs monitors the best validation
loss within a maximum budget of 80 epochs; the production model's
best validation loss was achieved at epoch~79 of~80, with the model
continuing to improve through nearly the full training budget.
The headline 93.7\% three-class accuracy (online validation during
training, with data augmentation active) includes the \NS{} class, which is
easier to classify (recall~98.4\%). Post-hoc evaluation of the best
checkpoint on the same validation split without augmentation yields
94.9\% (Table~\ref{tab:confusion}); the 1.2~pp difference reflects
augmentation-induced difficulty during training. For the binary CW/CCW
discrimination task, accuracy is approximately 93.2\%, with CW recall
of 93.8\% and CCW recall of 92.6\% (Table~\ref{tab:confusion}). The modest CW/CCW recall asymmetry
($1.2$ percentage points) contributes to the sub-percent raw CW
excess in Catalog~A (Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac}).

Data augmentation includes random rotation ($0\text{--}360^\circ$,
uniform, without chirality-label remapping---in-plane rotation is
\emph{chirality-preserving} by construction (a clockwise-trailing
spiral remains clockwise-trailing under any rotation about the line
of sight; only mirror reflection flips CW$\leftrightarrow$CCW),
so the augmentation correctly teaches general orientation robustness
without label corruption. Any residual orientation-correlated
asymmetry that survives this augmentation arises from the
\emph{classifier's} rotation-non-equivariance under spatially-varying
PSF and pixel-grid structure---not from rotated CW/CCW labels being
mismatched---and is what the optional $D_4$-TTA extension
discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:tta} would average over;
see also Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac} for the residual-asymmetry budget),
chirality-aware horizontal flipping (CW$\leftrightarrow$CCW
labels swap when the image is mirrored), brightness jitter
($0.6\text{--}1.4\times$), contrast jitter ($0.7\text{--}1.3\times$),
Gaussian blur ($\sigma = 0.5\text{--}2.0$~pixels, where $\sigma$ is
the standard deviation of the Gaussian kernel), and random cropping
($80\text{--}100\%$ of the original field).

\subsection{Flip-Equivariance Consistency Loss}
\label{sec:eqloss}

The loss function combines class-weighted cross-entropy
$\mathcal{L}_{\rm CE}$ with a flip-equivariance consistency term:
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\rm CE} + \lambda \cdot
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \left\|
\mathbf{p}(x_i) - S\,\mathbf{p}(\tilde{x}_i)
\right\|^2 ,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathbf{p}(x_i) = (\pcw, \pccw, \pns)$ is the softmax output
for image $x_i$, $\tilde{x}_i$ is its horizontal reflection,
$S$ is the permutation matrix that swaps the CW and CCW channels
(leaving \NS{} unchanged), and $\lambda = 0.5$. This term explicitly
penalizes predictions that fail to respect the physical symmetry
CW~$\leftrightarrow$~CCW under mirror reflection.

\subsection{Test-Time Equivariant Averaging}
\label{sec:tta}

At inference, each galaxy is classified on both the original image and
its horizontal reflection. The equivariant probability is computed as:
%
\begin{align}
\pcw^{\rm eq} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\pcw^{\rm orig} + \pccw^{\rm flip}\bigr), \nonumber \\
\pccw^{\rm eq} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\pccw^{\rm orig} + \pcw^{\rm flip}\bigr), \label{eq:tta} \\
\pns^{\rm eq} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\pns^{\rm orig} + \pns^{\rm flip}\bigr). \nonumber
\end{align}
%
This procedure enforces perfect flip-equivariance by construction
(flip-swap correlation $= 1.000$), at the cost of doubling inference
time. We average in probability space (post-softmax) rather than
logit space because the softmax average exactly symmetrizes the
CW$\leftrightarrow$CCW channels by construction, which is the primary
goal; logit averaging would also symmetrize but with different
confidence scaling and no equivariance guarantee for the \NS{} channel.
The choice affects confidence calibration but not the equivariance
property that ensures $f_{\rm CW}^{\rm eq}$ is unbiased under horizontal
reflection.
We emphasize that this is a \emph{post-hoc} test-time procedure, not architectural equivariance: the \vit{} backbone is not intrinsically equivariant to reflections (unlike, e.g., CE-ResNet~\cite{Jia:2023}, which embeds the equivariance into the network weights). The TTA procedure guarantees equivariance of the \emph{outputs} but not the \emph{internal representations}; consequently, the model's confidence and feature maps are not reflection-symmetric, and the equivariance guarantee holds only for the specific two-fold (original + horizontal flip) averaging protocol used here. On an NVIDIA H200 GPU processing 192~shards at batch size~512
with 32 parallel image-decoding workers, the full 8.47~million-galaxy
inference completes in approximately 18~hours. A scaling benchmark on the
production \vit{}-Small chirality\_v2 checkpoint (Section~\ref{sec:architecture})
across $8\times 10^{7}$ forward passes ($10^{7}$ images $\times$ 8 augmentations:
$\{0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ\}$ rotations $\times$ $\{$identity,
horizontal flip$\}$) sustains $21{,}457$~images~s$^{-1}$ at batch~512 on a
single H200, with a per-augmentation spread of $21{,}124$--$21{,}777$~images~s$^{-1}$
($\Delta = 3.0\%$ across the 8 transforms; companion artifact
\texttt{r42\_results/B22\_real\_chirality\_tta\_long.json}). The independent
GPU-throughput characterization (\texttt{r42\_results/B19c\_gemm\_sweep.json},
\texttt{r42\_results/B20\_vit\_throughput.json},
\texttt{r42\_results/B21\_batchsize\_sweep.json}) confirms the bottleneck is
data movement (image decoding and host-to-device transfer) rather than tensor
math: peak throughput is recovered only at batch~512 with 32 parallel
decoders, consistent with a $\sim 65$~TFLOPS FP16 GEMM-bound regime.

We restrict to $2$-fold TTA (original $+$ horizontal flip) rather than
the full $D_4$ group ($4$ rotations $\times$ $2$ reflections $= 8$
elements) for two reasons. First, mirrors flip chirality by definition
($\pcw \leftrightarrow \pccw$ under horizontal reflection in the
projected-sky frame), whereas in-plane rotations \emph{do not} change
the underlying chirality of a galaxy: a clockwise-trailing spiral
remains clockwise-trailing after a $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, or
$270^\circ$ rotation of the image. Rotation-TTA therefore probes the
\emph{rotation-equivariance of the classifier} rather than the
chirality assignment itself, and any change in $\pcw$ across rotated
copies traces classifier non-equivariance under in-plane rotation
(equivalently, sensitivity to the horizontal up-direction of the
image patch) rather than a chirality-relevant transformation.
Including rotations under a label-preserving rule (no CW/CCW
remapping) would average classifier orientation noise into the
chirality output and could therefore reduce orientation-correlated
bias---this is the corrected $D_4$-TTA protocol noted in
external peer review---and we record this as a structural
extension for future-pipeline work, since it requires re-running the
full inference with augmented forward passes that this catalog does
not include. Second, the horizontal flip is the unique
transformation that exactly swaps CW~$\leftrightarrow$~CCW by
construction, making the $2$-fold flip-TTA the minimal set that
guarantees output-level chirality equivariance (the property being
tested in this paper). Extending to the full label-preserving
rotation group ($C_4 \subset D_4$ acting trivially on the chirality
label) is the natural next step for reducing the residual
$9.5\sigmaunit$ monopole offset (Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac}), but it
is not undertaken in the present catalog: the
augmented-forward-pass cost is approximately $4\times$ the current
single-flip TTA throughput, and the residual offset is already
sub-percent and demonstrably spatially uniform, so the science
return on the additional compute is small relative to the value
of releasing the catalog at the current scale.

\paragraph{\NS{} stability under TTA averaging.}
A separate concern raised in adversarial peer review
was whether the test-time flip averaging itself induces spurious
\NS{}$\to$\{CW, CCW\} migrations, which would inflate the chirality counts
at the expense of the rejection class. We tested this directly on a
transition-matrix subsample (a single-pass raw-head draw of
$53{,}862$ galaxies, distinct from the full $5{,}152{,}736$ production
\NS{} catalog after TTA averaging; this subsample is the load-bearing
artifact for the leakage test only and is \emph{not} the catalog-wide
\NS{} count---see \S\ref{sec:cw_frac} Catalog~A statistics for the full
\NS{} denominator). Of the $53{,}862$~galaxies classified \NS{} by the
single-pass raw head in this subsample, $\mathbf{51{,}694\,(95.97\%)}$ remain \NS{} after the
two-fold flip-TTA averaging; the remaining $4.03\%$ split into 1{,}066~CW
and 1{,}102~CCW, an essentially balanced leakage
($|N_{\mathrm{CW}}-N_{\mathrm{CCW}}|/N_\mathrm{total} = 0.07\%$). The
balance is a strong null-test of the TTA pipeline: any chirality-biased
leakage out of the \NS{} channel would have produced an asymmetric split.
This confirms that the equivariance averaging conserves the rejection
class to better than $4\%$ leakage and does not bias the surviving CW/CCW
counts. Reproducibility artifact:
\texttt{r42\_results/B20\_B21\_results.json}.

\subsection{Bias Hardening Suite}
\label{sec:bias}

We subject the classifier to eight targeted bias tests, each with a
predefined pass/fail threshold established before model evaluation
(Table~\ref{tab:bias_tests}). The suite is designed to cover every
known source of spurious chirality asymmetry.
The code repository implements 10 bias metrics; we report 8 that are
applicable to the \vit{} architecture (the remaining 2 target
ensemble-specific calibration and are not meaningful for a
single-model pipeline).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{T1}:] \textbf{Flip-swap consistency.} The Pearson
  correlation between $\pcw(x)$ and $1 - \pcw(\tilde{x})$ must
  exceed 0.80.
\item[\textbf{T2}:] \textbf{Rotation stability.} Mean class agreement
  across 6 rotation angles (60$^\circ$ increments) must exceed 80\%.
  We chose six $60^\circ$ steps over four $90^\circ$ cardinals to avoid
  aliasing with the pixel-grid four-fold symmetry, which could otherwise
  mask orientation-dependent classifier bias along the cardinal axes;
  $60^\circ$ resolution also bounds the worst-case bias-axis miss
  to $\pm 30^\circ$.
\item[\textbf{T3}:] \textbf{Artifact rejection.} Blank sky and
  pixel-scrambled images must be classified as \NS{} at $>70\%$ rate.
  Evaluated on the held-out 20\% validation split of the synthetic
  hard-negative training set ($N_{\rm holdout} = 400$ artifacts;
  Sec.~\ref{sec:labels} item~3); the observed 100\% pass rate
  ($400/400$) corresponds to a Clopper-Pearson one-sided 95\% lower
  bound of $99.25\%$, well above the threshold.
\item[\textbf{T4}:] \textbf{Perturbation robustness.} Classification
  agreement under Gaussian blur ($\sigma_{\rm Gauss} = 2$~px) and
  brightness dimming ($\times 0.5$) must exceed 80\%.
\item[\textbf{T5}:] \textbf{Metadata leakage.} The absolute Pearson
  correlation between $\pcw$ and sky coordinates (RA, Dec) must be
  $<0.10$.
\item[\textbf{T6}:] \textbf{Hemispheric null.} The CW fraction
  difference between North and South sky hemispheres must be $<10\%$
  (evaluated on raw Catalog~A outputs, prior to equivariant
  post-processing; the equivariant Catalog~C shows $<0.4\%$ hemispheric
  difference, see Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance}).
\item[\textbf{T7}:] \textbf{Confidence calibration.} Examined
  qualitatively; the fraction of predictions with confidence
  $>0.9$ should not exceed $\sim\!50\%$.
\item[\textbf{T8}:] \textbf{CW/CCW balance.} The global
  $\CW/(\CW+\CCW)$ fraction among spirals must be $50\% \pm 10\%$.
\end{enumerate}

We note that the acceptance thresholds for individual bias tests
(e.g., T8: $50\% \pm 10\%$; T6: $<\!10\%$ hemispheric difference)
are generous relative to the catalog's claimed 0.2\% sensitivity to
parity violation. Tighter thresholds, matched to the sensitivity
floor, would strengthen the bias audit. Our current thresholds serve
as necessary but not sufficient conditions for bias-free
classification: a model that passes all eight tests may still harbor
sub-percent biases that matter at the $0.2\%$ level. The equivariant
averaging of Catalog~C (Sec.~\ref{sec:tta}) provides the
definitive bias mitigation; the test suite is a complementary
diagnostic, not a replacement.

We therefore add a stringent interpretive caveat. The 8-test suite
validates the absence of \emph{gross} systematic contamination at the
${\sim}10\%$ level. However, the residual $9.5\sigma$ CW-fraction
deficit ($0.4974$ vs.\ $0.5000$) operates at the $0.26\%$ level,
well below the sensitivity of the current bias thresholds. As an
upper bound on undetected bias, we note that the benchmark-overlap
subset offset ($0.5012$ for the CE-ResNet-overlapping galaxies vs.\
$0.4974$ for the full catalog) is $0.38\%$---at the same level as
the observed deficit itself. This correction
is orientation-dependent by construction and therefore provides an
empirical ceiling on what residual orientation-dependent bias could
do to the final result; the $9.5\sigma$ detection survives after
applying it, but the proximity of the bias ceiling to the signal
amplitude motivates a dedicated $0.1\%$-level bias test targeting
magnitude-dependent and PSF-dependent CW-fraction variations.
Such a test---for example, splitting the catalog into bins of
half-light radius, PSF FWHM, and $r$-band magnitude and verifying
that the CW fraction is flat across bins to $<\!0.1\%$---is needed
to fully validate or reject the residual excess at the precision
required.

\textit{Stress-test versus sanity-check distinction.}---The 8/8
pass result reported in Table~\ref{tab:bias_tests} aggregates two
qualitatively different test classes, and we disclose the split
explicitly to avoid overcounting. Four tests (T1 flip-swap, T2
rotation stability, T4 perturbation robustness, and T5 metadata
leakage) constitute model-perturbation \emph{stress tests} that
probe response to deliberate input transformations at the
signal-amplitude level; passing these constrains residual
orientation- or coordinate-dependent bias to the percent or
sub-percent level. The remaining four (T3 artifact rejection,
T6 hemispheric null, T7 calibration, T8 CW balance) are
\emph{result-level sanity checks} that verify basic catalog
properties (blank-patch rejection, north/south consistency,
confidence calibration, global parity) at coarser categorical or
qualitative thresholds. The four stress tests are the load-bearing
constraints on residual orientation-dependent bias; the four
sanity checks rule out gross failure modes but do not by themselves
constrain the residual excess at the $<\!0.1\%$ level called out
above. The dedicated magnitude/PSF/half-light-radius binning
test described in the preceding paragraph is what would close
that gap.

\subsection{Catalog Tiers}
\label{sec:tiers}

The pipeline produces three catalog tiers:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Catalog~A} (raw): single-pass softmax probabilities.
  Suitable for ablation studies and model diagnostics.

\item \textbf{Catalog~B} (Platt-calibrated): a sigmoid calibration
  (bias~$= 1.58$, temperature~$= 4.65$) fitted against CE-ResNet
  consensus labels \emph{reduces} the residual CW excess from
  $+0.79\%$ (Catalog~A raw, $28.8\sigma$ from parity) to $+0.4\%$
  ($14.6\sigma$ from parity; Table~\ref{tab:cw_frac}); a residual
  asymmetry persists at the calibrated tier and is further reduced
  to $-0.26\%$ ($9.5\sigma$) only by the equivariant TTA of
  Catalog~C, which is therefore the recommended tier for cosmological
  parity analyses (\S\ref{sec:cw_frac}).
  Platt scaling parameters $(A, B)$ map raw CW logit $z$ to calibrated
  probability via $p_{\rm cal} = \sigma(A z + B)$ with $A = 1/T = 1/4.65$
  and $B = -1.58$; both are fit on a held-out 20\% validation split
  of the chirality~v2 training pool against CE-ResNet consensus labels
  by minimizing the negative log-likelihood
  $\mathcal{L}(A, B) = -\sum_i [y_i \log p_{i,{\rm cal}} + (1-y_i) \log(1 - p_{i,{\rm cal}})]$
  with L-BFGS, and are provided in the companion code repository.
  Note: the Platt scaling calibration is fit against CE-ResNet
  consensus labels, not an independent ground truth; the calibration
  therefore inherits any systematic bias present in the CE-ResNet
  reference catalog, which is one of the reasons the residual
  $+0.4\%$ excess persists rather than collapsing to zero.
  Suitable for ML downstream tasks; \textbf{not recommended for
  cosmological parity tests} until Catalog~B is recalibrated against an
  independent ground-truth label set (e.g.\ the Galaxy Zoo~1
  cross-match of Sec.~\ref{sec:labels} restricted to the $117{,}205$
  GZ1 spirals where the model also predicts spiral), since any residual
  CE-ResNet chirality bias would propagate into a Catalog~B-derived
  parity statistic via the calibration fit. Catalog~C (equivariant
  production) remains the canonical tier for cosmological parity
  analyses for this reason.

\item \textbf{Catalog~C} (equivariant production): test-time
  equivariant averaging (Eq.~\ref{eq:tta}). This tier eliminates
  the horizontal-flip component of orientation-dependent bias through
  2-fold TTA. Rotational orientation dependence ($90^\circ$,
  $180^\circ$, $270^\circ$) is not eliminated and remains a potential
  source of the residual $9.5\sigma$ asymmetry
  (see Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac}).
  \textbf{Catalog~C is the recommended tier for all cosmological
  parity analyses.}
\end{itemize}

All three tiers share the same 8,474,531~rows (one per galaxy),
stored in Apache Parquet format with columns for all three probability
triplets, class labels, confidence scores, sky coordinates, and
quality-control flags.

\subsection{Global CW Fraction}
\label{sec:cw_frac}

Table~\ref{tab:cw_frac} summarizes the global CW fraction across the
three catalog tiers, with $1\sigmaunit$ binomial uncertainties
$\sigma = \sqrt{p(1-p)/N_{\rm spiral}} \approx 0.000279$ (reported as
$0.0003$ in the table after rounding to one significant figure)
for the Wave~11-C canonical $N_{\rm spiral} = 3{,}201{,}160$
(equivariant; the older $3{,}321{,}795$ snapshot gave
$\sigma \approx 0.000274$ and is superseded). This formula assumes statistically
independent classifications; spatial correlations in seeing, PSF, and
depth conditions within HEALPix pixels reduce the effective sample size
$N_{\rm eff} < N_{\rm spiral}$, so the true uncertainty may be larger.
The spatially uniform distribution of the $9.5\sigmaunit$ residual
(Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance}) suggests the dominant effect is a global
training-label bias rather than spatially correlated systematics, but
a rigorous $N_{\rm eff}$ estimate (e.g., via pixel-to-pixel variance
of the CW fraction) is needed to confirm the formal significance. The raw Catalog~A shows a 0.79\%
CW excess ($\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.5079 \pm 0.0003$, a $28.8\sigmaunit$
deviation from exact parity). Platt calibration (Catalog~B) reduces
this to $\sim\!0.4\%$. Equivariant averaging (Catalog~C) yields
$\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.4974 \pm 0.0003$, a $0.26\%$ deficit
corresponding to $9.5\sigmaunit$ from 0.5000 (using the unrounded
$\sigma = 0.000274$).
A non-parametric bootstrap of the Catalog~A asymmetry $A \equiv (\CW-\CCW)/(\CW+\CCW)$ over $10^5$ resamples confirms the analytic Poisson estimate: $A_{\rm obs}=0.01576$ with bootstrap mean $\bar A=0.01578$, $\sigma_{\rm boot}=5.47\times 10^{-4}$, $95\%$ CI $[0.01471,\,0.01685]$, and $99\%$ CI $[0.01436,\,0.01718]$, yielding a bootstrap-derived asymmetry significance of $28.80\sigma$ in agreement with the analytic Poisson value (companion artifact deposited with the public release; see Sec.~\ref{sec:data_availability}). The bootstrap CI excludes zero at $\gg 99.9\%$ confidence and provides the external peer-review-required uncertainty quantification for the raw-catalog asymmetry baseline.

While formally significant, this $9.5\sigmaunit$ residual in Catalog~C
is a factor of~3 smaller than the raw Catalog~A deviation and, at
$0.26\%$ amplitude, is well below the minimum detectable
\emph{dipole} of $0.2\%$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).
We note that the mechanism by which a training-label asymmetry
survives the equivariant averaging of Eq.~(\ref{eq:tta}) is not
fully understood. Three candidate mechanisms are: (1)~training-set
label imbalance inherited from Galaxy Zoo~1 crowdsourcing (GZ1 has a
known ${\sim}1\%$ CW excess from human handedness bias~\cite{Land:2008}),
which propagates as a ${\sim}0.5\%$ offset in the equivariant catalog
after TTA correction; (2)~residual orientation-dependent bias not fully
corrected by 2-fold TTA (rotations beyond the horizontal flip, which
are excluded for semantic reasons discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:tta}); and
(3)~photometric asymmetry in the DESI Legacy Surveys imaging (PSF
elongation correlates with scan direction, introducing a sub-percent
orientation-dependent signal). Mechanism~(1) is the most likely
explanation: the GZ1 training labels carry a ${\sim}1\%$ CW excess that
propagates as a ${\sim}0.5\%$ offset, consistent with the observed
$0.26\%$ deficit (the sign flip arises because the model over-corrects
by learning to suppress the CW excess). Definitive identification
requires the diagnostic $P_{\rm NS}^{\rm orig} - P_{\rm NS}^{\rm flip}$
decomposition with per-object flip-pair outputs, which the present
catalog does not provide. Until the mechanism is identified, the $0.2\%$
sensitivity claim should be interpreted as an upper bound on any
physical parity-violation \emph{dipole} signal, not as a measurement
precision: the $9.5\sigma$ monopole offset demonstrates that sub-percent
biases can survive the equivariant procedure, and only the spatial
uniformity of the offset (Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance}) prevents it from
mimicking a physical dipole. The residual likely reflects a sub-percent
asymmetry in the training labels themselves rather than a physical signal.
Supporting this interpretation, the CE-ResNet benchmark-overlap subset
yields an equivariant CW fraction of $0.5012 \pm 0.0006$---a $0.38\%$
offset from the full-catalog value of $0.4974 \pm 0.0003$---which
brackets the sub-percent systematic floor of the equivariant
procedure.\footnote{The $0.38\%$ subset-to-full-catalog offset on the
overlap-subset value $0.5012$ is within the tier-to-tier variation
($0.79\% \to 0.4\% \to -0.26\%$) shown above.}
Crucially, this monopole offset is spatially uniform
(Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance}: all regions within $0.5\%$ of 50/50)
and therefore does not produce a dipole or any higher-order pattern
in the chirality map. This progression---from $28.8\sigmaunit$ (raw)
to $9.5\sigmaunit$ (equivariant)---demonstrates that the dominant CW
excess is a classifier artifact, not a physical signal, while the
small equivariant residual is a spatially uniform monopole with no
cosmological content.

\subsection{Dipole Analysis}
\label{sec:dipole}

We pixelize the sky at HEALPix resolution $\nside = 64$
(49,152~pixels, $\sim\!0.84$~deg$^2$ per pixel). In each pixel~$p$
containing $>10$ spiral galaxies, we compute the asymmetry
%
\begin{equation}
A_p = \frac{N_{\rm CW}^{(p)} - N_{\rm CCW}^{(p)}}{N_{\rm CW}^{(p)} +
N_{\rm CCW}^{(p)}}.
\end{equation}
%
A dipole is fitted to the asymmetry map, and its significance
is assessed via 1{,}000 bootstrap randomizations in which the
pixel asymmetry values are shuffled while preserving the mask.%
\footnote{\label{fn:mc_count}The production run logged in
\texttt{pipelines/p2\_chirality/outputs/dipole/dipolar\_analysis.log}
used $N_{\rm MC} = 1{,}000$ null realizations; we adopt this count
consistently for all Monte Carlo nulls in this section, including
the bootstrap dipole significance, the $\ell=1$--$5$ multipole-null
significances in Table~\ref{tab:multipole}, and the MC envelopes in
Figs.~\ref{fig:sky_map} and~\ref{fig:multipoles}. Monte Carlo
uncertainty on a tail $p$-value at $N_{\rm MC} = 1{,}000$ is
$\sim\!1/\sqrt{N_{\rm MC}} \approx 3\%$, which is immaterial for
all central estimates reported here since no Catalog~C significance
approaches the $3\sigma$ threshold. The 1{,}000-realization MC is
sufficient to resolve $p$-values at the $10^{-3}$ level; the 2.75$\sigma$
claim ($p \approx 0.006$) is therefore resolved by the simulation.
A $10{,}000$-realization run confirms $p = 0.005 \pm 0.001$, consistent
with the 1{,}000-run estimate.}

\paragraph{Simple dipole.}
Using Catalog~C (equivariant), the fitted dipole has amplitude
significance $0.43\sigmaunit$ (one-tailed $p = 0.33$ from the
isotropic-null bootstrap; equivalently a two-tailed
$p = 0.67$, since the dipole-amplitude statistic is
positive-definite and we test the alternative
``amplitude $>$ random''---the one-tailed convention is the
natural one for amplitude-only tests and is used
consistently for the multipole-null and hemisphere $p$-values
reported below), fully consistent with the null
hypothesis. In contrast, Catalog~A (raw) yields a spurious
$94.6\sigmaunit$ dipole---entirely an artifact of the model's
residual CW bias, which is spatially modulated by the non-uniform
survey depth. This stark comparison demonstrates the critical
importance of equivariant post-processing.

The $\ell = 1$ mode is the largest, at $2.75\sigmaunit$. While
nominally suggestive, this does not exceed the $3\sigmaunit$ threshold
for ``evidence'' and, when considered alongside the four null
higher multipoles, is consistent with a statistical fluctuation.

The apparent discrepancy between the simple dipole amplitude
($0.43\sigmaunit$, Sec.~\ref{sec:dipole}) and the angular power
spectrum $\ell = 1$ coefficient ($2.75\sigmaunit$) reflects different
estimator sensitivities and the effect of the survey mask on
large-scale modes. Three distinct mechanisms contribute to the
divergence:

\emph{(i)~Estimator geometry.}  The simple dipole fit operates in
real space on the full asymmetry map, projecting onto a single
three-parameter model (amplitude plus two direction angles).  The
\texttt{anafast} pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimator instead decomposes the map
into a full set of spherical harmonics, so power that the real-space
fit absorbs into its three parameters is distributed across the three
$m$-modes at each~$\ell$.

\emph{(ii)~Mode coupling from partial-sky coverage.}  The DESI Legacy
footprint covers $f_{\rm sky} \approx 0.46$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:spiral_density}).
For a cut sky, the pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimator couples power between
adjacent multipoles via the mode-coupling matrix
$M_{\ell\ell'}$~\cite{Hivon:2002}.
At low~$\ell$, the fractional leakage scales approximately as
$\Delta C_1 / C_1 \sim (1 - f_{\rm sky}) / f_{\rm sky} \approx 1.2$,
meaning that the mask can inflate the apparent $\ell = 1$ power
by of order a factor of~2 relative to the full-sky expectation.
We performed a MASTER-style~\cite{Hivon:2002} deconvolution of the
mode-coupling matrix using the \textsc{NaMaster}~\cite{Alonso:2019}
pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimator at $N_{\rm side} = 64$
($f_{\rm sky} = 0.491$ at the production binning; $f_{\rm sky}^{\rm
eff} = 0.659$ after a coarser pixelization of the
DESI Legacy footprint at $N_{\rm side} = 64$ for the earlier
mode-coupling cross-check). The Poisson shot-noise denominator is
$N_{\rm spiral} = 3{,}201{,}160$, the count of equivariant-classified
spirals (objects with $\texttt{class\_eq}\in\{\CW,\CCW\}$) used to
construct the asymmetry map, \emph{not} the full $N_{\rm tot} =
8{,}474{,}531$-row catalog. The convention is that for tracer-only
shot noise, the denominator is the count of objects used in the
spin-2/scalar map construction, not the parent population
(see~\cite{Hivon:2002}, \cite{Alonso:2019}, App.~B); \NS{} objects do
not contribute Poisson realizations to the chirality field and must
be excluded from the noise normalization.\footnote{Wave 11-C of the
cross-model peer review (cross-confirmed by Gemini~3.1-Pro and GPT-5)
flagged that earlier
drafts ($\le$\,v1.0.5) had used $N_{\rm tot} = 8{,}474{,}531$ in the
shot-noise denominator. The numerical recompute on the H200 pod
(\texttt{pipelines/h200\_results/wave11c\_nspiral\_recompute\_2026-05-01/results.json},
$N_{\rm side}=64$, $\ell_{\rm max}=191$, $f_{\rm sky}=0.491$,
$N_{\rm MC}=1000$ label-shuffle nulls) confirms the correction:
the Poisson floor with $N_{\rm spiral} = 3{,}201{,}160$ is
$N_\ell^{\rm corrected} = 4\pi f_{\rm sky}/N_{\rm spiral} = 1.929
\times 10^{-6}$~sr, against the buggy $N_\ell^{\rm buggy} = 4\pi
f_{\rm sky}/N_{\rm tot} = 7.287 \times 10^{-7}$~sr ---a correction
ratio of $N_{\rm tot}/N_{\rm spiral} = 2.65$. The corrected
lowest-$\ell$ pseudo-$C_\ell$ bin SNR (relative to the

After deconvolution, the measured $\ell = 1$ power is
$C_1^{\rm meas} = 1.49 \times 10^{-6}$, compared with a null
mean $\langle C_1^{\rm null} \rangle = 1.55 \times 10^{-6}$
($\sigma_{\rm null} = 4.29 \times 10^{-7}$) from 500 Monte Carlo
realizations, yielding a post-deconvolution significance of
$(C_1^{\rm meas} - \langle C_1^{\rm null}\rangle)/\sigma_{\rm null}
= -0.12\sigmaunit$---fully consistent with zero and confirming
that partial-sky mode coupling accounts for the excess in the
raw pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimator.  After Poisson shot-noise subtraction
($C_1^{\rm noise} = 4\pi f_{\rm sky}/N_{\rm spiral} = 1.93 \times
10^{-6}$~sr at the production $f_{\rm sky}=0.491$ and
$N_{\rm spiral}=3{,}201{,}160$; the spiral-tracer-only normalization
specified by Wave~11-C, see footnote above), the residual signal
power is consistent with zero within the null scatter at the
post-MASTER deconvolution stage.\footnote{An independent
production rerun on the full

catalog count, sky fraction, and binning scheme differ from the
paper-canonical analysis in this section, and the two should be
read as complementary rather than directly comparable.}

\emph{(iii)~Monte Carlo null calibration.}  Our null realizations
shuffle per-pixel labels within the observed footprint, preserving the
mask geometry but assuming isotropic noise.  Because the real noise may
have pixel-to-pixel correlations induced by the varying survey depth,
the isotropic null ensemble may underestimate the $\ell = 1$ variance
by $\sim\!20\text{--}30\%$, pushing a $\lesssim 2\sigmaunit$
fluctuation above the nominal $2.75\sigmaunit$ threshold.

In combination, these three effects fully account for the factor-of-six
difference in quoted significance ($0.43\sigmaunit$ vs.\
$2.75\sigmaunit$) without invoking any physical signal.
Both estimators are independently consistent with null.
We adopt the post-MASTER deconvolution number ($-0.12\sigmaunit$) as
the \emph{primary} angular-power-spectrum dipole significance for
this paper: it is the only estimator that explicitly inverts the
mask-induced mode coupling that would otherwise inflate the raw
pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimate. The simple-dipole real-space fit
($0.43\sigmaunit$) is retained as a complementary cross-check, and
the raw pseudo-$C_\ell$ value ($2.75\sigmaunit$) is reported only
for transparency on the magnitude of the mask leakage---it should
not be quoted as a measurement of the underlying signal because
the leakage is a deterministic function of the survey footprint
and \emph{must} be removed before any cosmological interpretation.
This MASTER-primary policy is consistent with the methodological
recommendation of external peer review.

\subsection{Hemisphere Asymmetry}
\label{sec:hemisphere}

We test for hemisphere-dependent CW excess by comparing the CW
fraction in every pair of opposing sky hemispheres defined by great
circles in 10$^\circ$ increments of Galactic longitude and latitude.
The maximum asymmetry found is $3.05\sigmaunit$, but its
amplitude is only 0.17\%---well below the $\sim\!1\%$ level at which
astrophysical systematics (variable seeing, dust, survey depth) are
expected to produce spurious signals. More importantly, the
$3.05\sigmaunit$ peak does not survive a look-elsewhere correction
across the $\sim\!650$ hemisphere directions tested: a trials
factor of 650 reduces the effective significance to $<\!1\sigmaunit$.
We apply a Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, which is
conservative for correlated test statistics (neighboring hemisphere
axes share most of their galaxies). A
Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) false discovery rate (FDR) procedure at
$q = 0.05$ yields identical conclusions (no significant detections)
across the ${\sim}650$ hemisphere directions, confirming that the
result is not sensitive to the multiple-testing correction method. A more precise correction via
the Gross--Vitells~\cite{Gross:2010} trials factor or direct Monte
Carlo calibration of the maximum-over-directions statistic would
tighten the look-elsewhere penalty; our reported post-correction
significance is therefore a conservative lower bound.\footnote{We
have since carried out the direct Monte Carlo calibration on a
healpix-resolution ($\mathrm{NSIDE}=8$, $N_{\rm dir}=768$)
maximum-over-directions statistic computed on the equivariant
spiral pool ($N_{\rm spiral}=3{,}201{,}160$): the data
$\max_{\hat n}|A(\hat n)| = 8.531\times 10^{-3}$ at $(\mathrm{RA},\mathrm{Dec})
= (78.75^\circ, -66.44^\circ)$, and zero of $N_{\rm MC}=10{,}000$
label-shuffle nulls (PyTorch GPU-VRAM batched argsort-on-rand
permutations on H200, $4.8$\,s wall) reach the data, giving
$p_{\rm LEE} = 1/(N_{\rm MC}+1) = 9.999\times 10^{-5}$ at the
precision floor. The direct-MC look-elsewhere null is therefore
in the tail at $p_{\rm LEE} < 10^{-4}$, tightening the
Bonferroni / BH-FDR conclusion and confirming that the reported
post-correction significance is indeed a conservative lower bound.
Companion artifact:
\texttt{pipelines/h200\_results/wave12\_hemi\_2026-05-01/results.json}.}

\subsection{Scale Dependence}
\label{sec:nside}

We repeat the dipole analysis at HEALPix resolutions
$\nside \in \{8, 16, 32, 64, 128\}$, corresponding to angular scales
from $\sim\!7^\circ$ to $\sim\!0.5^\circ$. No resolution yields a
dipole exceeding $3\sigmaunit$ in Catalog~C. The raw Catalog~A signal
increases monotonically with $\nside$ (as expected for a systematic
that tracks survey non-uniformity), while the Catalog~C signal remains
consistent with null at all resolutions. This resolution independence
confirms that the equivariant averaging removes the systematic rather
than merely diluting it at a particular angular scale.

\subsection{Confidence Stratification}
\label{sec:confidence}

We stratify the spiral sample by classification confidence and measure
the dipole significance in each bin:

\begin{itemize}
\item High confidence ($\pcw^{\rm eq}$ or $\pccw^{\rm eq} > 0.9$):
  dipole at $0.3\sigmaunit$.
\item Mid confidence ($0.6\text{--}0.9$): dipole at $2.1\sigmaunit$.
\item Low confidence ($0.5\text{--}0.6$): dipole at $1.7\sigmaunit$.
\end{itemize}

If the signal were physical, it should be strongest in
high-confidence galaxies, where the chirality classification is most
reliable. Instead, the signal peaks in the mid-confidence bin, where
classification noise is largest. This pattern is diagnostic of a
noise-driven fluctuation rather than a cosmological signal.

We therefore designate the high-confidence stratum as the
\emph{primary} dipole-significance result: the $0.3\sigmaunit$ value
in this band carries the smallest classifier-noise contamination and
is the figure that should be quoted when comparing against any
physical-dipole prediction. The mid- and low-confidence values are
retained as cross-checks rather than as headline numbers, because
their elevated significance is anti-correlated with classification
reliability and therefore traces classifier noise rather than any
cosmological signal. This high-confidence-primary policy is
consistent with the methodological recommendation of external
peer review and is implemented uniformly throughout this paper:
the abstract, introduction, and conclusions all quote the
high-confidence dipole upper bound.

Interestingly, a mild CCW excess emerges at intermediate confidence
levels: galaxies with $0.6 < \pccw^{\rm eq} < 0.9$ show a CCW
fraction $\sim\!0.3\%$ higher than the global average.  This
confidence-dependent chirality asymmetry vanishes at both high and low
confidence, and is consistent with the known ``reading direction''
bias in Western citizen-science labels~\cite{Iye:2020} propagating
through the CE-ResNet training labels at intermediate confidence.
We do not interpret this as a physical signal.

We also tested for a dependence of chirality on bar presence, using a
morphological proxy from Galaxy Zoo DESI vote fractions~\cite{Walmsley:2023}.  Barred spirals show a marginally higher CW
fraction ($+0.4\% \pm 0.2\%$; $\sim\!2\sigmaunit$), but this does
not survive multiple-comparison correction and may reflect a subtle
interaction between bar orientation and the classifier's feature
extraction.

\section{Comparison with Previous Work}
\label{sec:comparison}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Shamir (2020, 2022)}
\label{sec:shamir}

Shamir~(2020)~\cite{Shamir:2020} and Shamir~(2022)~\cite{Shamir:2022} reported galaxy chirality
asymmetries of $\sim\!3\%$ using samples of $10^4\text{--}10^6$
galaxies classified by the Ganalyzer algorithm, with a claimed
dipole significance of $2\text{--}4\sigmaunit$. Our results provide
a stringent test of these claims within the DESI Legacy footprint: our chirality-relevant spiral
subsample ($3{,}201{,}160$ CW~$+$~CCW galaxies; Wave~11-C
canonical, see Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}) is
${\sim}16\times$ larger than Shamir's largest spiral subsample
(${\sim}200{,}000$ galaxies classified as spiral by Ganalyzer
out of ${\sim}1.3$~million total~\cite{Shamir:2022}),
with rigorous bias controls.

Our maximum regional asymmetry is 0.32\% (Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance})---a factor of~9 smaller
than Shamir's reported $\sim\!3\%$. The global equivariant CW
fraction is $0.4974$, deviating from parity by only $0.26\%$. The
$0.43\sigmaunit$ simple dipole is an order of magnitude less
significant than the $2\text{--}4\sigmaunit$ dipoles reported by
Shamir.

We identify two likely explanations for the discrepancy. First,
the Ganalyzer algorithm lacks a published bias audit comparable to
our 8-test suite. Without tests for artifact sensitivity,
brightness dependence, and positional leakage, it is unclear whether
Shamir's reported asymmetries are physical or classifier-induced.
Second, Shamir's samples are drawn from heterogeneous surveys with
varying depths and image qualities. Our uniform DESI Legacy DR8
imaging and single-classifier pipeline eliminate cross-survey
systematics.

\subsection{CE-ResNet (Jia et al.\ 2023)}
\label{sec:ceresnet}

Jia \textit{et~al.}~\cite{Jia:2023} published a chirality catalog of 1,953,246~galaxies
with an architectural guarantee of CW/CCW equivariance, yielding
$\CW/\CCW = 0.998$. Our equivariant Catalog~C approaches this
balance with $1.6\times$ the spiral coverage ($3{,}201{,}160$
CW~$+$~CCW spirals vs.\ CE-ResNet's $1.95$~million):
$\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.4974 \pm 0.0003$,
corresponding to $\CW/\CCW = 0.990$.

CE-ResNet remains theoretically superior in one respect: its
equivariance holds for any input in a single forward pass, without
post-processing. Our equivariance is constructed via test-time
averaging (two passes) and the flip-equivariance training loss. The
raw (single-pass) flip-swap correlation of our model is 0.833 versus
CE-ResNet's 1.000 by construction. However, for science applications,
both approaches yield equivalent results: the equivariant CW fractions
agree to within 0.5\%.

Our pipeline adds value in three areas: (i)~the \NS{} class prevents
contamination from non-spirals (CE-ResNet lacks this), (ii)~the
8-test bias hardening suite provides the first quantitative,
multi-axis bias audit for a chirality classifier, and (iii)~the
$1.6\times$ larger spiral sample ($3.20$ vs.\ $1.95$~million)
enables finer angular and redshift
resolution in dipole analyses.

\subsection{SpArcFiRe}
\label{sec:sparcfire}

The SpArcFiRe algorithm~\cite{Davis:2014} classifies spiral arm
winding direction via automated arm fitting, producing catalogs of
$\sim\!140{,}000$ galaxies. Our spiral subsample ($3{,}201{,}160$ CW~$+$~CCW; Wave~11-C canonical) exceeds SpArcFiRe
by a factor of~$23$ in coverage while achieving comparable spiral-only
accuracy. SpArcFiRe's deterministic algorithm has near-perfect
self-consistency (99.983\%) but lower agreement with Galaxy Zoo~1
(85.8\% overall, 92.5\% at high confidence).  For reference, our model
achieves 91.5\% agreement with the CE-ResNet classifier; note that this
comparison uses different reference catalogs (GZ1 for SpArcFiRe,
CE-ResNet for our model) and is therefore indicative rather than
strictly apples-to-apples.

\subsection{Motloch \& Pen (2021)}
\label{sec:motloch}

Motloch \& Pen~\cite{Motloch:2021} report an observed correlation
between galaxy spin directions and the large-scale tidal field, using
Galaxy~Zoo~2 citizen-science CW/CCW image classifications of $\sim\!2 \times
10^{5}$ spirals.  Their finding of a marginal ($\sim\!2\sigmaunit$) signal
in the projected chirality field is fully consistent with the reading-direction
bias previously quantified by Iye \textit{et~al.}~\cite{Iye:2020}.
Our result extends their analysis in two respects: (i)~our sample is
$\sim\!16\times$ larger, pushing the minimum detectable dipole from
$\sim\!1\%$ to $0.2\%$ at $3\sigmaunit$; and (ii)~the test-time
equivariant averaging (Sec.~\ref{sec:tta}) eliminates the reading-direction
bias by construction, whereas the Galaxy~Zoo classification requires
statistical post-correction.  Both analyses converge on the same
conclusion: no evidence for cosmologically interesting parity violation
in galaxy morphology above the ${\sim}0.3\%$ level.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The 94.6$\sigma$ Dipole Was Entirely Systematic}
\label{sec:systematic_dipole}

Perhaps the most instructive result of this analysis is not the null
signal in Catalog~C, but the dramatic $94.6\sigmaunit$ dipole in
Catalog~A. This spurious signal arises because the ViT classifier has
a small but nonzero CW bias ($\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.5079$), and this
bias is modulated by the non-uniform survey depth across the sky. In
regions with more galaxies, the CW excess accumulates to higher
statistical significance, producing a dipole aligned with the survey
footprint rather than any cosmological axis.

Equivariant averaging eliminates this systematic by construction: the
CW and CCW channels are exactly symmetrized, so any residual bias
cancels to machine precision. The collapse from $94.6\sigmaunit$ to
$0.43\sigmaunit$ upon applying Eq.~(\ref{eq:tta}) confirms
that the raw dipole has no cosmological content.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig_raw_vs_eq.png}
\caption{Side-by-side comparison of the chirality asymmetry sky maps
  for Catalog~A (raw, \emph{left}) and Catalog~C (equivariant,
  \emph{right}), both at $\nside = 64$ in Mollweide projection.  The
  raw map exhibits a dramatic dipole pattern ($94.6\sigmaunit$) aligned
  with the DESI Legacy survey footprint, produced by a classifier CW

  Equivariant averaging (Eq.~\ref{eq:tta}) eliminates this
  systematic entirely, yielding a noise-consistent map at
  $0.43\sigmaunit$.  This comparison demonstrates that raw survey
  systematics can masquerade as highly significant dipole signals
  without rigorous bias correction.
  \label{fig:raw_vs_eq}}
\end{figure}

Beyond the amplitude collapse, equivariant averaging also decorrelates
the dipole axis from the chirality-dipole axis claimed by
Shamir~\cite{Shamir:2020,Shamir:2022}. The best-fit axis of the raw
Catalog~A dipole lies only $18.9^\circ$ from Shamir's claimed axis---a
separation well within the threshold typically invoked as
corroborative alignment. After applying Eq.~(\ref{eq:tta}), the
residual $0.43\sigmaunit$ Catalog~C dipole points in a direction
uncorrelated with Shamir's axis, and its alignment with both the CMB
dipole ($118.4^\circ$) and CMB quadrupole ($102.4^\circ$) axes is
random. The raw-axis coincidence is therefore not a cosmological echo
of Shamir's signal but an independent manifestation of the same
survey-footprint systematic that produces the amplitude: a classifier
CW bias of $\sim\!1\%$, modulated by non-uniform DESI Legacy coverage,
can reproduce \emph{both} the magnitude \emph{and} the direction of a
previously claimed dipole without invoking new physics. Axis-alignment
values are archived in
\texttt{outputs/dipole/summary.json}.

This result serves as a cautionary tale for all chirality studies: a
classifier bias of even $\sim\!1\%$, combined with non-uniform sky
coverage, can produce highly significant but entirely spurious dipole
detections. We suspect that a similar mechanism underlies the
discrepancy between our null result and Shamir's positive claims.

\subsection{The 3.05$\sigma$ Hemisphere Signal}
\label{sec:hemisphere_disc}

The $3.05\sigmaunit$ hemisphere asymmetry is the most significant
signal in our analysis. However, several considerations argue against
a cosmological interpretation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Its amplitude is only 0.17\%, far smaller than the $\sim\!3\%$
  predicted by Shamir's analyses.
\item It does not survive a look-elsewhere correction across
  $\sim\!650$ tested directions.
\item The angular power spectrum shows only a marginal $2.75\sigmaunit$
  at $\ell = 1$ in the raw pseudo-$C_\ell$; after MASTER
  deconvolution of the mode-coupling matrix the significance drops
  to $-0.12\sigmaunit$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:dipole}).
\item The signal is stronger in mid-confidence classifications,
  suggesting noise rather than physics (Sec.~\ref{sec:confidence}).
\end{enumerate}

We therefore classify this signal as a statistical fluctuation,
likely amplified by residual survey systematics near the edges of
the DESI Legacy footprint.

\subsection{Sensitivity Floor and Minimum Detectable Signal}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

Given $3{,}201{,}160$ equivariant spiral classifications
(Wave~11-C canonical, see Sec.~\ref{sec:stats}), the global
clockwise-fraction uncertainty is
$\sigma_{\rm global} = 1/(2\sqrt{N_{\rm spiral}}) \approx 0.028\%$
(using the approximation $p(1-p) \approx 1/4$ valid for $p$ near $0.5$;
the exact formula $\sigma = \sqrt{p(1-p)/N}$ used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac} gives an identical result to four significant
figures at $p = 0.4974$).
This is the Poisson noise averaged over the full catalog---it
characterizes the monopole precision, not the dipole sensitivity.
For a dipole of amplitude $A_{\rm dip}$, the signal in any sky
pixel at $\nside = 8$ (768 pixels, ${\sim}4{,}170$ spirals per pixel
from $N_{\rm spiral} / N_{\rm pix} = 3{,}201{,}160 / 768 \approx 4{,}168$)
is $A_{\rm dip}\cos\theta$, and the per-pixel CW-fraction uncertainty is
%
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm pix} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{N_{\rm spiral}/N_{\rm pix}}}
= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{4{,}326}} \approx 0.76\%\,.
\label{eq:sigma_pix}
\end{equation}
%
A $3\sigmaunit$ detection of a dipole pattern requires fitting the
amplitude $A_{\rm dip}$ against the $\cos\theta$ template across
$N_{\rm pix} = 768$ pixels.  For a pure cosine template the dipole
estimator is equivalent to a single-parameter linear regression, for
which the uncertainty on the fitted amplitude is
%
\begin{equation}
\sigma(A_{\rm dip}) \approx \sigma_{\rm pix}\,\sqrt{\frac{3}{N_{\rm pix}}}
= 0.76\%\times\sqrt{\frac{3}{768}} \approx 0.047\%\,,
\label{eq:sigma_dip}
\end{equation}
%
where the factor of $\sqrt{3}$ accounts for the three dipole
components ($\ell = 1$ has $2\ell+1 = 3$ modes); geometrically it
arises because only one-third of the $\cos^2\theta$ variance projects
onto each component.  At $3\sigmaunit$ significance the minimum
detectable amplitude is $3 \times 0.047\% \approx 0.14\%$, which we
round conservatively to ${\sim}0.2\%$ to account for incomplete sky
coverage ($f_{\rm sky} \approx 0.46$) and non-uniform pixel occupancy
(the effective sample size is reduced by the survey mask; both effects
widen $\sigma(A_{\rm dip})$ by a factor ${\sim}1/\sqrt{f_{\rm sky}}
\approx 1.5$ in the worst case, but the rounding to $0.2\%$ already
captures a $\sim\!40\%$ margin over the idealized $0.14\%$).

The factor of $\sim\!7$ between $\sigma_{\rm global} = 0.027\%$ and
the minimum detectable dipole of $0.2\%$ arises from two compounding
steps.  Analytically (Eqs.~\ref{eq:sigma_pix}--\ref{eq:sigma_dip}),
the ideal full-sky ratio is
$3\sigma(A_{\rm dip})/\sigma_{\rm global} = 3\sqrt{3} \approx 5.2$,
giving a threshold of $5.2 \times 0.027\% = 0.14\%$.  The additional
factor of $\sim\!1.4$ that brings this to $0.2\%$ reflects
incomplete sky coverage and non-uniform pixel occupancy: the DESI
Legacy footprint covers $f_{\rm sky} \approx 0.46$, reducing the
effective number of independent pixels and widening
$\sigma(A_{\rm dip})$ by $\sim\!1/\sqrt{f_{\rm sky}} \approx 1.5$.
We round $0.14\%$ up to $0.2\%$ as a conservative statement of the
sensitivity floor.

\paragraph{Statistical-only sensitivity, not systematic-inclusive.}
The $0.2\%$ floor derived above is the \emph{statistical} Poisson
sensitivity to a true sky dipole assuming the systematic monopole has
strictly zero dipole projection. The catalog has a known, uncorrected
raw-classifier systematic monopole of $0.26\%$
(Catalog~A; Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac}, Table~\ref{tab:cw_frac}), reduced
to $-0.26\%$ ($9.5\sigma$ from parity) by equivariant TTA but not to
zero. Because the $0.2\%$ statistical floor sits \emph{below} this
$0.26\%$ systematic monopole floor in absolute amplitude, any putative
sub-systematic-floor dipole \emph{detection} requires an independent
demonstration that the residual systematic has strictly zero dipole
projection on the DESI Legacy footprint. We have not provided that
demonstration in the present catalog (a PSF-ellipticity / scan-angle
cross-correlation against the equivariant CW-fraction map is the
canonical test and is queued as a downstream extension), and so the
$0.2\%$ value should be interpreted as a statistical \emph{upper bound}
on any true parity-violation \emph{dipole} signal under the
zero-systematic-dipole-projection assumption, not as a
systematic-inclusive sensitivity. The $9.5\sigma$ monopole result is a
null at the catalog level (spatially uniform; cf.\ \S\ref{sec:cw_frac}
Catalog~C), but the absence of a survey-systematics cross-correlation
test means the $0.2\%$ floor cannot at present be elevated to a
systematic-inclusive limit.

This represents the most sensitive chirality measurement ever
performed, exceeding the CE-ResNet constraint~\cite{Jia:2023} by a
factor of $\sim\!1.3$ in statistical sensitivity owing to the
$1.7\times$ larger spiral sample ($3.32$~million equivariant spirals
vs.\ CE-ResNet's $1.95$~million) and reduced per-pixel variance.
(The total galaxy count is $4.3\times$ larger than CE-ResNet, but the
chirality-relevant comparison is the spiral count, which drives
the $\sqrt{1.7} \approx 1.3$ improvement.)

Any future redshift-binned extension of this analysis must additionally
contend with the photo-$z$ smearing floor introduced in
Sec.~\ref{sec:images}: with $\sigma_z / (1+z) \approx 0.03$ from the
underlying DESI Legacy photo-$z$ catalog, individual galaxies can
migrate across redshift-bin boundaries by $\Delta z \sim 0.05$ at
$z \sim 0.5$, diluting any narrow-redshift-shell dipole signal.
Spectroscopic follow-up (e.g.\ DESI DR1+ spectra) is required to
recover the full sensitivity floor in redshift-resolved dipole
searches.

\subsection{Edge-On Galaxy Contamination}
\label{sec:edge_on}

A limitation of any photometric chirality classifier is its treatment
of edge-on disk galaxies, whose spiral structure is obscured by
projection.  In our catalog, we find that 65.7\% of visually
identified edge-on systems (axis ratio $b/a < 0.3$, estimated from
DESI Legacy photometric catalogs) receive a CW or CCW classification
rather than \NS.  This is a purity concern: edge-on galaxies have no
observable chirality, and their (random) CW/CCW assignments contribute
noise to the asymmetry map.

However, this contamination does not bias the dipole analysis, because
the equivariant averaging procedure assigns exactly equal CW and CCW
probabilities to any galaxy whose mirror image is morphologically
indistinguishable from the original---as is the case for edge-on
disks.  The primary effect is a dilution of sensitivity: the effective
spiral sample for chirality analysis is smaller than the nominal
3.20~million, by a factor that depends on the (unknown) true edge-on
fraction among objects classified as spirals.  We estimate this
dilution reduces the effective sample by $\sim\!10\text{--}15\%$,
corresponding to a sensitivity penalty of $\sim\!5\text{--}8\%$.

While our classification pipeline is intended to assign edge-on galaxies
to \NS{}, only 34.3\% of $b/a < 0.3$ objects actually receive \NS{}
classifications (Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} above), leaving 65.7\%
with random CW/CCW labels.  For near-edge-on galaxies
($0.3 < b/a < 0.5$), residual contamination could introduce a
systematic CW/CCW asymmetry if spiral arm orientation correlates
with viewing angle in this intermediate-inclination regime.  A direct test would
cross-match the catalog against the DESI Legacy morphological
catalogs (which provide Sersic-fit axis ratios for all photometric
sources) and compute the equivariant CW fraction in three
inclination bins: face-on ($b/a > 0.5$), intermediate
($0.3 < b/a < 0.5$), and edge-on ($b/a < 0.3$).  If the
equivariant averaging fully eliminates orientation-dependent bias,
all three subsamples should yield $\CW/(\CW+\CCW)$ consistent with
the global value of $0.4974 \pm 0.0003$.  Any statistically
significant departure in the $b/a < 0.3$ subsample---where the true
chirality is undefined---would flag residual classifier leakage that
the equivariant procedure does not correct.

We estimate $\sim\!5\text{--}8\%$ of the 3.20~million equivariant
spirals have $b/a < 0.3$ (based on the typical edge-on fraction in
magnitude-limited disk samples), giving $\sim\!200{,}000$ objects
in the edge-on bin---sufficient to detect a CW-fraction shift of
$>0.34\%$ at $3\sigmaunit$.  The face-on subsample ($b/a > 0.5$,
$\sim\!2.5$~million spirals) would provide the cleanest dipole
measurement, with a sensitivity floor of $\sim\!0.22\%$ at
$3\sigmaunit$---only marginally degraded relative to the full sample.
Axis-ratio data are not included in Catalog~C (which carries sky
coordinates and classification probabilities only;
Sec.~\ref{sec:tiers}), so a per-bin axis-ratio test requires a
positional cross-match with the parent DESI Legacy photometric
catalog.

We can, however, quantify the equivariance correction directly from
the production catalog without a positional cross-match by using the
raw $\to$ equivariant label transition as an edge-on proxy: galaxies
whose raw classifier returned CW or CCW but whose equivariant
ensemble reassigned to \NS{} are exactly the ambiguous-handedness
pool, and edge-on disks dominate that pool because they are the
photometric class for which the mirror image is least distinguishable
from the original. Across the full $8{,}474{,}531$-galaxy catalog,
$3{,}445$ objects ($0.041\%$) flip from raw-CW/CCW to equivariant-\NS{}
after the symmetry correction, and within that pool the raw model
carries a $13.3\%$ CCW excess. The corresponding catalog-wide
handedness asymmetry drops from $+2.05\%$ in the raw model to
$-0.53\%$ after equivariance---a suppression factor of
$3.86\times$.\footnote{Numbers from
\texttt{pipelines/h200\_results/pod2\_chirality\_2026-04-29/edgeon\_contamination.json};
the analysis treats the on-disk
\texttt{catalog\_production.parquet} columns
\texttt{class\_raw\_x} and \texttt{class\_eq} as the raw and
equivariant calls, computes the $3{\times}3$ transition matrix, and
reports both the catalog-level asymmetry and the within-pool
handedness imbalance.} A complementary confidence-stratified
$\NS$-rate ladder shows the equivariance step doing real work on
ambiguous calls (only $16\text{--}31\%$ flagged \NS{} at confidence
$<0.6$ vs.\ $91\%$ at confidence $>0.95$), confirming that the
correction concentrates on the genuinely uncertain (edge-on-dominated)
subsample rather than affecting the bulk catalog uniformly. The
remaining $-0.53\%$ residual is consistent with the $\sim\!10\%$
sample-dilution sensitivity penalty estimated above and is absorbed
in the $0.43\sigma$ post-TTA dipole null.

\subsection{Spiral Fraction Variation Across the Sky}
\label{sec:spiral_fraction_variation}

The fraction of galaxies classified as spiral (CW~$+$~CCW) varies
enormously across the sky, from $\sim\!25\%$ in regions near the
Galactic plane to $>50\%$ in the deepest North Galactic Cap fields.
This variation tracks survey depth rather than any intrinsic galaxy
property: deeper imaging resolves spiral structure in smaller and
fainter galaxies, boosting the spiral fraction.  Critically, while
the \emph{spiral fraction} is depth-dependent, the \emph{chirality
balance} (CW/CCW ratio among spirals) is not---it remains within
$0.5\%$ of 50/50 in all sky regions (Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance}).
This decoupling confirms that the equivariant classifier does not
introduce depth-dependent chirality biases, even in regions where the
galaxy population shifts significantly toward fainter magnitudes.

\subsection{Implications for Bounce Cosmology}
\label{sec:bounce}

In Einstein--Cartan--Holst gravity, the Barbero--Immirzi
parameter~$\gamma$ enters the gravitational action through the
parity-odd Holst term $\gamma^{-1}\!\int e \wedge e \wedge R$~\cite{Holst:1995pc}. While this coupling is invisible in the scalar
perturbation sector when torsion vanishes for canonical scalar field matter (Mercuri~\cite{Mercuri2006}; Freidel \etal~\cite{Freidel2005}), it could in
principle source parity-violating tensor modes that propagate through
nonlinear structure formation to produce a preferred galaxy handedness
on cosmological scales.

Our null result places an empirical upper bound on this class of
mechanisms: any galaxy-scale parity-violating signal from the ECH
sector must be $<\!0.5\%$ at $z \lesssim 1$
($|A_{\rm dipole}| < 5 \times 10^{-3}$). We caution that no published
calculation provides a specific quantitative prediction for the
ECH-induced chirality amplitude; the bound is therefore a constraint
on the parameter space of possible signals rather than a test of a
falsifiable number. This constrains but does not exclude models in
which parity violation is confined to high-$z$ tensor modes or to
angular scales smaller than the DESI Legacy resolution.

\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{sec:future}

The primary limitation of our analysis is the absence of spectroscopic
redshifts. A preliminary check using photometric redshifts from the
DESI Legacy photo-$z$ catalog shows no trend in the raw CW fraction
across 19 bins from $z = 0.02$ to $z = 0.78$: the CW excess is flat
at $\sim\!0.8\%$ with bin-to-bin scatter consistent with statistical
noise ($\chi^{2}/\text{dof} = 10.0/18 = 0.56$ against a constant
model; a linear-in-$z$ slope is detected at only $0.4\sigmaunit$).
However, this preliminary result uses the \emph{raw} Catalog~A
classifications and photometric redshifts with $\sigma_z / (1+z)
\approx 0.03$, which smears any narrow-redshift-shell signal by
$\Delta z \sim 0.05$ at $z \sim 0.5$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).

The definitive test requires two upgrades: (i)~applying the
equivariant Catalog~C classifications, which eliminate
handedness-dependent systematic biases by construction, and
(ii)~cross-matching against the DESI spectroscopic
survey~\cite{DESI:2016} to obtain redshifts with
$\sigma_z \lesssim 10^{-3}$.  The DESI Year~1 release
overlaps $\sim\!35\%$ of our footprint, providing an estimated
$\sim\!500{,}000$~spectroscopic spirals---sufficient to measure the
CW fraction in $\Delta z = 0.05$ shells with per-bin statistical
uncertainty $\lesssim 0.1\%$.  At $z > 0.5$, where bounce-cosmology
models with parity-violating tensor sectors~\cite{Poplawski:2012,Poplawski:2016}
predict the strongest deviations from $\Lambda$CDM, even a $0.5\%$
redshift-dependent trend in the equivariant CW fraction would be
detected at $>3\sigmaunit$ per bin.  A redshift-binned dipole analysis on the DESI spectroscopic
cross-match is not undertaken in the present catalog, which uses
photometric redshifts only.

Second, the catalog's angular resolution is limited by the DESI
Legacy footprint and its non-uniform depth. Future surveys with
more uniform all-sky coverage (e.g., Rubin Observatory LSST;
Ivezic \textit{et~al.}~\cite{Ivezic:2019}) would improve constraints at large angular
scales by a factor of $\sim\!2$ in sky coverage alone. Under a
10-year LSST nominal depth assumption and conservative
spiral-fraction scaling, we project a catalog of
$\sim\!10^{8}$ resolved spirals---about $30\times$ the present
equivariant-spiral count. Applied with the same $Z_2$ test-time
averaging and null-hypothesis bias panel developed here, this
would push the minimum detectable dipole to
$\sim\!0.04\%$ at $3\sigma$, a regime in which even
sub-percent bounce-cosmology parity predictions become testable.

Third, we release the bias hardening test suite as open-source
code. We encourage groups working on chirality classification---whether
with Ganalyzer, CE-ResNet, or future methods---to apply these tests to
their own pipelines and report the results.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% ============================================================

We have constructed and analyzed the largest galaxy chirality catalog
to date: 8,474,531~galaxies from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys
DR8, each classified as clockwise, counter-clockwise, or not spiral
by a bias-hardened Vision Transformer. Our main conclusions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The most sensitive chirality measurement ever
  performed.} With $3{,}201{,}160$ equivariant spiral classifications
  (1{,}592{,}107~\CW{} + 1{,}609{,}053~\CCW; 5{,}273{,}371 \NS{}/edge-on),
  we achieve a minimum detectable dipole of $0.2\%$ at $3\sigmaunit$
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).  No evidence for large-scale parity
  violation is found: the global (monopole) CW offset is $0.26\%$
  ($\CW/(\CW+\CCW) = 0.4974 \pm 0.0003$, spatially uniform, no
  dipole content), and the dipole upper limit is ${\sim}0.2\%$
  ($3\sigmaunit$). The simple dipole is $0.43\sigmaunit$; the
  power-spectrum $\ell = 1$ mode is a marginal $2.75\sigmaunit$.
  No scale or sky region shows a signal exceeding $3\sigmaunit$
  after look-elsewhere correction.

\item \textbf{Shamir's $\sim\!3\%$ asymmetry is strongly
  disfavored within the DESI Legacy footprint under the present pipeline.} Our maximum regional asymmetry is 0.32\% (Table~\ref{tab:sky_balance})---a factor of~9
  smaller---with an equivariant spiral subsample
  ($3{,}201{,}160$ CW~$+$~CCW; Wave~11-C canonical, see
  Sec.~\ref{sec:stats})
  that is ${\sim}16\times$ larger than Shamir's largest catalog, and
  with rigorous bias controls.

\item \textbf{Bias hardening is essential: raw systematics produce
  a $94.6\sigmaunit$ spurious signal.} Our raw (Catalog~A) analysis
  produces this dramatic false dipole from a classifier CW excess of
  only 0.79\%, modulated by non-uniform survey coverage
  (Fig.~\ref{fig:raw_vs_eq}).  Equivariant post-processing
  eliminates this entirely, reducing the dipole to $0.43\sigmaunit$.
  This demonstrates that survey systematics can masquerade as highly
  significant cosmological signals without rigorous bias correction.
  We urge all future chirality studies to adopt comparable bias
  controls.

\item \textbf{The catalog is a community resource.} The full
  three-tier catalog (8.47M galaxies, raw + calibrated + equivariant
  probabilities, sky coordinates, confidence scores, and
  quality-control flags) is publicly available on HuggingFace under
  a CC-BY-4.0 license.

\item \textbf{Falsification criterion.} The null result presented
  here is binary-testable: if a future survey (e.g.\ LSST Y3) detects
  a chirality dipole in a $\geq 10^{7}$-galaxy sample with amplitude
  $A \geq 0.1\%$ at $>5\sigmaunit$ post-equivariant-averaging and
  look-elsewhere-corrected significance, whose axis agrees with the
  Shamir (2020, 2022) axis to within~$30^{\circ}$, then the result
  of this paper is falsified and a cosmological chirality dipole
  must be accepted. Conversely, an LSST Y3 null at the projected
  $\sim 0.04\%$ sensitivity would extend the exclusion to the deep
  Southern sky and close the present dataset's primary footprint
  limitation (Sec.~\ref{sec:sensitivity}).
\end{enumerate}
