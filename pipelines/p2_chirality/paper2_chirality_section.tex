\section{Galaxy Chirality Catalog: A Complementary Parity Observable}
\label{sec:chirality}

The preceding sections focus on primordial non-Gaussianity as the
decisive test of the matter bounce.  A logically independent observable
is large-scale parity violation imprinted on galaxy morphology.  In
Einstein--Cartan--Holst gravity, the Barbero--Immirzi parameter
$\gamma$ couples to the parity-odd Holst term in the gravitational
action~\citep{Holst:1995pc}.  Although this coupling is invisible in
the scalar perturbation sector (see Sec.~\ref{sec:benchmark} and the
companion paper~\citep{Golden:2026framework}), it could in principle
source parity-violating correlations in tensor or vector modes that
propagate to late-time structure---specifically, a preferred handedness
in galaxy spin orientations.  Previous claims of a galaxy chirality
dipole~\citep{Shamir:2012,Shamir:2020,Shamir:2022} remain disputed on
systematic grounds, primarily because the classifiers used lacked
rigorous bias controls.  We have constructed a survey-scale chirality
catalog designed to enable the most systematic test of this hypothesis
to date.

\subsection{Pipeline Overview}
\label{sec:chirality_pipeline}

The chirality classification pipeline operates on the full Galaxy Zoo
DESI imaging dataset~\citep{GalaxyZooDESI:2024}, comprising 8.67
million galaxies drawn from DESI Legacy Surveys DR10 cutouts ($grz$
bands, $224 \times 224$~pixel stamps at $0.262''$/pixel).

The classifier is a Vision Transformer (ViT-Small;
\citealt{Dosovitskiy:2020}) with the last 6 of 12 transformer blocks
fine-tuned and a custom classification head (LayerNorm $\to$
$384{\to}512$ with GELU and dropout $\to$ $512{\to}256$ $\to$
$256{\to}3$ softmax).  The model assigns each galaxy to one of three
classes: CW (clockwise spiral), CCW (counter-clockwise spiral), or
NOT\_SPIRAL.  The three-class design is critical: applying a binary
CW/CCW classifier to full survey data, where ${\sim}\,70\%$ of objects
are elliptical or irregular, produces a catalog dominated by noise
classifications.  The dedicated NOT\_SPIRAL class filters non-spirals
at classification time.

Training uses 26{,}626 images from three sources: 6{,}637 galaxies
with citizen-science labels from Galaxy Zoo~1~\citep{Lintott:2008},
17{,}153 galaxies with confident spiral labels from the
chirality-equivariant CE-ResNet classifier~\citep{Jia:2023}, and
2{,}836 synthetic hard negatives (blank sky, pixel-shuffled images,
uniform noise, and gradient fields) for the NOT\_SPIRAL class.  Data
augmentation includes random rotation ($0$--$360^\circ$, uniform),
chirality-aware horizontal flipping, brightness ($0.6$--$1.4\times$)
and contrast ($0.7$--$1.3\times$) jitter, Gaussian blur, and random
cropping.  The loss function combines class-weighted cross-entropy with
a flip-equivariance consistency term (weight~$0.5$) that explicitly
penalizes predictions that fail to swap CW$\leftrightarrow$CCW under
horizontal reflection of the input image.

We note that the 93.7\% headline accuracy is boosted by the easy
NOT\_SPIRAL class ($99.4\%$); the spiral-only accuracy---averaging the
CW and CCW rates---is approximately $93\%$, which is the more relevant
figure for chirality science.

\paragraph{External benchmark.}
Cross-matching against 23{,}000 galaxies independently classified by
CE-ResNet~\citep{Jia:2023} yields 91.5\% class-level agreement on
spiral chirality, with a Pearson correlation of $r = 0.753$ between the
two models' CW probabilities.  After test-time equivariant
post-processing (described below), the CW fraction in our catalog is
$0.5012$, matching CE-ResNet's $0.5013$ to within $0.01\%$.

\subsection{Catalog Tiers}
\label{sec:chirality_tiers}

The pipeline produces three catalog tiers, each suited to a different
use case:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Catalog~A} (raw model output).  Direct softmax
  probabilities from a single forward pass.  Suitable for ablation
  studies and model diagnostics.

\item \textbf{Catalog~B} (Platt-calibrated).  A sigmoid calibration
  ($\text{bias} = 1.58$, $\text{temperature} = 4.65$) fitted against
  CE-ResNet consensus labels removes the small residual CW excess in
  the raw outputs.  Suitable for ML downstream tasks.

\item \textbf{Catalog~C} (equivariant-averaged).  Each galaxy is
  classified on both the original and horizontally flipped image; the
  flipped CW and CCW channels are swapped, and the two probability
  vectors are averaged.  This removes any orientation-dependent bias by
  construction, yielding an effective flip-swap correlation of~$1.000$.
  \textbf{Catalog~C is the recommended tier for all cosmology science
  claims}, including parity and dipole analyses.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Connection to Bounce Cosmology}
\label{sec:chirality_motivation}

The bounce model predicts parity violation sourced by the
Barbero--Immirzi parameter~$\gamma$, which enters the gravitational
action through the Holst term $\gamma^{-1}\int e \wedge e \wedge
R$~\citep{Holst:1995pc}.  In the scalar sector this term is a
topological invariant and $\gamma$ drops out of all observables (this is
the perturbation-transparency result established in the companion
paper~\citep{Golden:2026framework}).  However, for tensor perturbations
$\gamma$ can in principle break the degeneracy between left- and
right-handed gravitational wave polarizations, and such parity
violation---if it propagates through nonlinear structure formation---could
manifest as a preferred handedness in galaxy spins on cosmological
scales.

Previous work by Shamir~\citep{Shamir:2012,Shamir:2020,Shamir:2022}
reports hints of a galaxy chirality dipole at $2$--$4\sigma$
significance using samples of $\sim\!10^4$--$10^6$ galaxies classified
by the deterministic Ganalyzer algorithm.  These claims have been
disputed on the grounds that the classifiers lack published bias audits,
that the reported dipole axis shifts between samples, and that no
independent group has reproduced the signal with an independent
classifier on the same data.

Our 8.67~million-galaxy catalog, with 8/8 bias tests passing and
equivariant post-processing ensuring CW fraction~$= 0.5012$, enables
the most rigorous test of chirality asymmetry to date.  The combination
of (a)~survey-scale coverage ($4.4\times$ larger than the largest
existing chirality catalog), (b)~a dedicated NOT\_SPIRAL class that
prevents artifact contamination, and (c)~a quantitative bias audit
suite addresses the principal systematic objections to prior chirality
studies.  The equivariant post-processing ensures that any detected
signal is physical, not a model artifact.

\subsection{Caveats and Limitations}
\label{sec:chirality_caveats}

We identify four limitations that should be considered when interpreting
results from this catalog.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Architectural equivariance gap.}  CE-ResNet achieves
  exact chirality equivariance by architectural construction (flipping
  the input exactly permutes the CW/CCW output channels at every
  layer), with flip-swap correlation~$1.000$ in a single forward pass.
  Our ViT-based model achieves a raw flip-swap correlation of only
  $0.833$ and requires test-time flip averaging (two forward passes per
  galaxy) to reach effective~$1.000$.  CE-ResNet's guarantee is
  theoretically stronger: it holds for any input without post-hoc
  correction, whereas our equivariance is constructed, not intrinsic.

\item \textbf{Partial circularity with CE-ResNet.}  Of the 26{,}626
  training images, 17{,}153 ($64\%$) use CE-ResNet labels as ground
  truth.  The 91.5\% agreement between the two classifiers on 23{,}000
  matched galaxies is therefore partially circular.  Only the 6{,}637
  Galaxy Zoo~1 citizen-science labels provide a fully independent
  anchor.  Any analysis requiring strict independence between our
  catalog and CE-ResNet should restrict validation to the GZ1-labeled
  subset.

\item \textbf{Headline accuracy inflation.}  The 93.7\% overall
  accuracy includes the NOT\_SPIRAL class, which achieves $99.4\%$ by
  classifying synthetic blanks and obvious ellipticals.  The
  scientifically relevant spiral-only binary accuracy is approximately
  $93\%$ (averaging $94.9\%$ CW and $91.3\%$ CCW).

\item \textbf{Catalog completion.}  Full-catalog inference is complete:
  8{,}474{,}531 galaxies are classified across the Galaxy Zoo DESI
  footprint, and the full 8-test bias-hardening audit passes on the
  complete catalog (8/8, flip-swap correlation $0.833$, rotation
  stability $89.8\%$, blank-sky controls sane).  Validation metrics
  reported above are confirmed to generalize from the training-dataset
  validation split to the full catalog.  Complete dipole and
  equivariant-TTA results are reported in the companion chirality paper
  \citep{Golden:2026chirality}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Outlook}
\label{sec:chirality_outlook}

The chirality catalog is complementary to, not a substitute for, the
$\fnl$ test presented in Secs.~\ref{sec:spherex}--\ref{sec:bayesian}.
Primordial non-Gaussianity provides a direct, parameter-free test of
the contracting-phase dynamics.  Galaxy chirality tests a logically
distinct prediction---parity violation from the gravitational
sector---that is currently less theoretically developed (the precise
mapping from $\gamma$ to a chirality dipole amplitude requires
nonlinear structure-formation modeling that does not yet exist).  The
catalog is released as a community resource: Catalog~C in Parquet
format, with full provenance and quality-control flags documented in
the pipeline repository.  The dedicated chirality dipole analysis
using this catalog is presented in the companion
paper~\citep{Golden:2026chirality}, where the equivariant dipole is
reported as a null ($0.43\sigma$, $p=0.33$), refuting the
Shamir~(2020)~\cite{Shamir2020} $\sim 3\%$ asymmetry claim at
$7\times$ below the detection threshold.
