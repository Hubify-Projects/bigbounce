# Row 16 — the in-domain `d(θ)` and the dilution bound `D`
### lane `bb-LS10-indomain-d180`, 2026-09-22

**Result in one line.** On the images the released catalogue was actually built
from, the released pipeline contradicts its own label between two lossless
rotations of the *same* image **more** often than it did on the viewer cutouts
row 16(ii-b) used, so the hold is **LIFTED WITH DIFFERENT NUMBERS**:
`D ≤ 0.5998 ± 0.0065` and `A₉₅^phys ≥ 1.63 %` on the catalogue's own
`primary_hc` cut, replacing the held `D ≤ 0.7166 ± 0.0047` and
`A₉₅^phys ≥ 1.37 %`.

Pre-registration: `PREREGISTRATION_2026-09-22.md`, commit `f45c9d1b`, committed
before any statistic below was computed. Every number is read from a committed
JSON in this directory.

---

## 1. What was held, and why

Lane `bb-LS6-row16-tta` found (`../row16_tta_2026_09_21/PROPAGATION_NOTE.md`
item Q-1) that rows 13 and 16 had measured `d(θ)` on **Legacy Survey viewer
cutouts** — the `image_url` column `run_eq_fast.py:265-270` appends to
`catalog_production.parquet` for display — while the catalogue's own inference
ran on `Smith42/galaxies` (`run_eq_fast.py:37`, keyed by `dr8_id`). On those
cutouts the released checkpoint reproduces the released catalogue's class for
only **43.9 %** of galaxies, so every `d(θ)` in row 16(ii-b) §6, the bound
`D ≤ 0.7166`, and `A₉₅^phys ≥ 1.37 %` were out-of-domain measurements. An
out-of-domain instability bounds the in-domain one in neither direction, so the
bound was held rather than retracted, with a named test to lift it.

This lane runs that test.

## 3. The controls — all three pass, and the first one is decisive

### C1 — catalogue scale, from artifacts already in the repo (no download, no GPU)

The 2026-07-11 full-catalogue e2e re-run
(`../../p2_chirality/outputs/canonical_provenance/e2e_fullrun/`, 192 shards,
A100, same checkpoint, same preprocessing) already pushed **every** in-domain
image through the released pipeline. Against the released catalogue's own class:

> **99.936 %** agreement on all **8,474,531** galaxies;
> **99.864 %** on the 3,201,160 catalogue spirals;
> **100.000 %** on the 949,584 `primary_hc` galaxies.
> Mean `|Δ eq_cw| = 2.9 × 10⁻⁴`.

(`c1_e2e_catalogue_agreement.json`. `NS` and `NOT_SPIRAL` are the same class
under two names; the comparison is on the class, not the string — mistaking the
two would have read as 37.3 % agreement.)

Against **43.9 %** on the viewer cutouts, at catalogue scale, this settles the
provenance question on its own: the released catalogue *is* reproducible from
the released checkpoint and the released preprocessing, on its documented image
source, and only on that source.

### C2 — this lane's own forward passes

This lane's `L(0°)` versus the released catalogue's class, on the 40,000 drawn
galaxies: **99.95 %** (99.895 % on catalogue spirals, **100.000 %** on
`primary_hc`); median `|Δ eq_cw| = 6.1 × 10⁻⁶`, max `0.037`. The pre-registered
in-domain threshold was **≥ 99.0 %**. Gate: **IN-DOMAIN**.

### C3 — the TTA parity identity

At `θ = 0°` and `θ = 180°` the two-fold flip TTA makes the parity operation
exact, so the identity residual must vanish. It does: `max |eq_cw(Y_θ) −
eq_ccw(X_θ)| = 0.0` to float32 at both angles, class-swap fraction excluding
ties `= 1.000000`. Asserted in code, not merely reported.

### Why the two image sets differ, measured rather than assumed

A post-hoc diagnostic (`posthoc_pixel_scale.py`, 30 galaxies, 180 viewer cutouts,
~10 MB, feeding no pre-registered statistic) correlates each in-domain image
against Legacy Survey cutouts of the same galaxy over a grid of pixel scales.
The median correlation is **0.992 at 0.262″/px** and ≤ 0.25 at every other grid
point. So a `Smith42/galaxies` v1.0 image is **512 × 512 px at 0.262″/px = a
134.1″ field**, and the viewer cutouts rows 13/16 used were a 39.3″ field
upsampled 150 → 224 — a **3.41×** difference in angular field, in the direction
the earlier zoom sweep could not explore (it could only crop *inward* from 39.3″).

This also corrects the manuscript: `main.tex:171` describes the parent sample as
"$224\times224$~px $grz$ cutouts at $0.262''$/pixel". The pixel scale is right;
the size is not — 224 is the **network input** after `Resize((224,224))`, while
the released cutouts are 512 × 512. See item R-2 of `PROPAGATION_NOTE.md`.

1,000-resample galaxy bootstrap, `rng(20260922)`. Identical to the held
statistic — nothing was redefined.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all drawn | 40,000 | 0.4762 | 0.4387 | 0.4750 | **0.5238 ± 0.0038** | 0.5613 ± 0.0044 | 1.87 % / 1.75 % |

`D` is bounded away from 1 at **61σ** on `primary_hc` and **127σ** on all

## 5. Reading it honestly

**The in-domain classifier is *less* rotation-stable, not more.** The natural
expectation was that the 43.9 % domain gap had inflated `d(θ)` and that the real
bound would be looser. It is the opposite: on its own images the released
pipeline contradicts itself across a lossless 180° rotation for **34.0 %** of
high-confidence spirals, against 21.1 % on the out-of-domain cutouts. The
correction therefore moves the bound in the **conservative** direction for P4′ —
`D` smaller means `A₉₅^phys = A₉₅^obs/D` larger, a weaker exclusion — which is
exactly the direction P-5a already says the paper adopts the bound in ("it is an
upper bound on `D`, so it can only weaken the exclusion, and we adopt it in that
direction only"). The held sentences survive; their numbers do not.

**What this is a bound on.** `d(θ) ≤ 2e` and `D = 1 − 2e` require only that
handedness does not depend on the orientation at which a galaxy is presented, so
`L(0) ≠ L(θ)` proves a label error in one of the two. `θ = 180°` needs no
position-angle-uniformity argument at all: position angle is defined mod 180°,
so the rotated cutout depicts the same galaxy at the same position angle. No
human truth labels enter anywhere.

**What it is not.** It is not a measurement on the 8,474,531-row parent — it is
40,000 galaxies drawn uniformly from it, 4,579 of them `primary_hc`, and the
`primary_hc` row carries a 0.0065 bootstrap SE accordingly. It is not a
detection of anything; a true population asymmetry of zero is diluted to zero
under any `D`.

**The θ = 0 labels are right; the rotated ones are the problem.** C2's 100.000 %
`primary_hc` agreement at `θ = 0°` and C1's 100.000 % at catalogue scale mean
the released catalogue's labels are exactly what the released pipeline produces.
The instability is entirely in what happens when the identical image is handed
over rotated, which is what makes it a statement about the pipeline's
rotation-equivariance rather than about the catalogue's reproducibility.

## 6. What still carries the old hold, and what does not

* **Lifted, re-measured here**: row 16(ii-b) item **P-5** (`D`, `A₉₅^phys`, the
  `d(θ)` table, the "21.1 %" sentence), and row-16 item **Q-2**'s `d(180°) =
  0.3090` — superseded by the in-domain `0.4388` (all spirals) / `0.3401`
  (`primary_hc`).
* **Still out-of-domain, not re-measured here**: row-16 item **Q-3** (the sky
  dipole in transfer efficiency, `a_δ = 0.090 ± 0.010`) and item **Q-4** (the D4
  / D8 TTA recovery fractions). Both were measured on viewer cutouts. Q-3's
  *mechanism* and its permutation significance do not depend on the image
  domain, but its amplitude does, and this lane did not re-measure it — the 16
  rotation angles Q-4 needs and the per-galaxy sky map Q-3 needs are both larger
  jobs than the four-angle pass run here. See the next step in
  `PROPAGATION_NOTE.md`.
* **Never affected**: row-16(ii-b) items **P-1…P-4**. Those are re-analyses of
  committed label-level quantities and of the exact identity
  `eq_cw(MI) = eq_ccw(I)`, which holds for any image whatsoever.
