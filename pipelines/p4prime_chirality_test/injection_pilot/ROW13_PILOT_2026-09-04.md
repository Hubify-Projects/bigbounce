## Part B — Euclid Q1 feasibility (no compute)

**Products (real, public, released 2025-03-19; papers published 2026-07 A&A special issue):**
- Data: ESA Euclid Science Archive (https://eas.esac.esa.int/sas), also
  IRSA mirror; catalogue + cutout DOI: Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.15002907.
- Key paper: "Euclid Quick Data Release (Q1) — First visual morphology
  catalogue" (Walmsley et al., arXiv:2503.15310 / A&A aa55140-25).
- Footprint: 63.1 deg^2 total (EDF-North 20, EDF-Fornax 10, EDF-South 23
  deg^2), first ~0.4% of Euclid's eventual ~100M-galaxy morphology reach.
- Morphology catalogue: 378,000 galaxies (I_E<20.5 or area>=700 px),
  classified by Zoobot (foundation CNN fine-tuned on 1.56M Euclid-specific
  Galaxy Zoo labels from an Aug-2024 campaign) for bars/spiral
  arms/mergers — includes spiral-arm and (with care) winding-direction
  features relevant to a CW/CCW chirality signal, though Zoobot's public
  vote fractions are for spiral-arm presence/count, not a direct
  CW/CCW label; a chirality read would need either a dedicated visual
  re-vote or applying our own DR9-trained classifier to VIS cutouts.
- Access: public, no proprietary period; catalogue query + cutout service
  via the Science Archive GUI or ESA Datalabs; astroquery-Euclid client
  available. Licence: ESA public open-data terms (Q1 special-release,
  no login-walled restriction found for the catalogue/cutout tables).

**Instrument mismatch — the real feasibility blocker:**
- Euclid VIS: single broad optical band (~550-900 nm), pixel scale
  0.1"/pix, diffraction-limited PSF FWHM ~0.16", space-based.
- Legacy Survey DR9 (our classifier's training/production domain): grz
  3-color JPEG composite, pixel scale ~0.262"/pix (DECam), ground-based
  seeing-limited PSF (~1").
- The pixel scale differs by ~2.6x and VIS is single-band (not the grz
  color composite the ViT was trained on) — the committed classifier
  CANNOT be applied to raw VIS cutouts without retraining or at minimum
  a resampling + band-matching preprocessing step, and even then colour
  information used by the current model would be missing. Direct
  zero-shot transfer is not expected to be reliable; this must be
  disclosed, not glossed over.

**Costed plan for a first Euclid chirality-dipole test:**
1. Retraining/domain-adaptation step (required): fine-tune (or retrain
   the head on top of a frozen encoder) using Zoobot's own Euclid-specific
   labels or a small hand-vetted CW/CCW re-annotation of a VIS subsample
   (~5-10k galaxies) — this is a genuine, disclosable science task, not
   a redo of an old mistake (per directive Q1). Est. compute: a few GPU-hours
   on a single RunPod A-class GPU (~$5-15).
2. Inference over the full Q1 morphology catalogue (378,000 galaxies) —
   CPU-feasible (ViT-small at ~50-100 img/s on CPU, VIS cutouts included);
   no GPU required for inference alone.
3. Sensitivity estimate via this paper's own committed illustrative
   $A_{95}\propto 1/\sqrt{N}$ ansatz, anchored at
   $N_{\rm support}=887{,}472 \to A_{95}^{\rm obs}=0.98\%$
   (`main.tex` Sec. "Robustness" / committed exclusion-script note).
   Applying the P4-catalogue high-confidence-spiral yield fraction
   ($887{,}472/8{,}474{,}531 \approx 10.5\%$) to Q1's 378,000 classified
   galaxies gives an illustrative $N_{\rm eff}\approx 40{,}000$ (bracket
   20k-100k depending on how much of Q1's spiral-arm-flagged population
   survives a high-confidence chirality cut):

   | N_eff | A_95 (illustrative, 1/sqrt(N) from the P4' anchor) |
   |---|---|
   | 20,000  | ~6.5% |
   | 40,000  | ~4.6% |
   | 75,000  | ~3.4% |
   | 100,000 | ~2.9% |
   | 150,000 | ~2.4% |

   i.e. Q1 alone gives roughly 3-7x WORSE amplitude sensitivity than the
   current 8.47M-galaxy DESI catalogue (0.98%) — expected, since Q1 is
   only the first 63 deg^2 / 0.4% of Euclid's eventual footprint. A
   competitive chirality-dipole test needs a later, wide-tier Euclid
   release (thousands of deg^2), not Q1 alone; Q1's value here is a
   pathfinder/validation run (proving the classifier transfers, or
   quantifying by how much it does not) rather than a new-sensitivity
   result.
4. Estimated wall-clock: retraining/adaptation 1-2 days elapsed (mostly
   labeling/QA, not compute); full-catalogue inference: under 2 hours
   CPU or under 20 min GPU.
5. Estimated cost: $0 data access (public) + ~$5-20 optional GPU compute
   if adaptation fine-tuning is done on RunPod; otherwise near-$0 (local
   CPU) since Q1 is CPU-tractable at this scale.

**Verdict:** Feasible as a validation/pathfinder study, NOT as a
sensitivity-improving result on its own. The binding blocker is the
VIS/DR9 instrument mismatch requiring a retraining or domain-adaptation
step before any inference is trustworthy — this is real, disclosable,
in-scope future work (not deferred indefinitely; recommend queuing as
its own ledger row once Part A's image-level-vs-label-level result is
closed).
