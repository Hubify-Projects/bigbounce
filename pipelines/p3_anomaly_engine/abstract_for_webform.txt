We present a unified autoencoder anomaly-detection pipeline applied to eight astronomical archives spanning optical, X-ray, infrared, microwave, and pulsar-timing data, comprising 37.3 million sources---the largest multi-survey anomaly sweep reported. BigAE (per-survey training) identifies 319,443 anomalies across DESI DR1 (195,829/22.5M), SDSS DR18 (77,905/2.3M), LAMOST DR10 (44,075/11.4M), eROSITA DR1 (298/930K), Planck CMB (200/20K), ACT DR6 (200/20K), Gaia DR3 (500/50K), and NEOWISE (436/43.5K). SIMBAD cross-match reveals 58.8% are uncataloged, with per-survey novelty ranging from 27% (Gaia) to 90% (SDSS). SDSS anomalies cluster into 3 latent groups dominated by M7-L9 ultra-cool dwarfs; LAMOST exposes training-set bias (98% blue-excess) rather than astrophysical rarity. Three DESI-SDSS cross-matches include a time-variable source (TIC 374313355, score = 49.5) and an uncataloged broad-absorption-line QSO candidate at z ≈ 0.86. A multi-tracer Fisher forecast shows that anomaly-recovered tracers improve σ(f_NL) by 6.1% (DESI) and 16.4% (DESI+SDSS), projecting a 4.38σ SPHEREx detection of the matter-bounce prediction f_NL = -35/8. PlanckxACT cross-correlation of CMB patch anomalies is null, consistent with a foreground/noise origin. Independent analysis of the NANOGrav 15-year pulsar-timing background yields γ = 3.20 ± 0.42, consistent with the matter-bounce γ = 3.0 at 0.48σ; ΔBIC = 7.0 favors bounce over the supermassive black hole binary model.
