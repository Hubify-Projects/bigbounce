We present the largest-scale application of autoencoder anomaly detection across seven astronomical archives, applying the BigAE framework to 37.3 million sources and CMB map patches (DESI DR1, SDSS DR18, LAMOST DR10, eROSITA DR1, Planck, Gaia DR3, NEOWISE; ACT DR6 quarantined as a cross-transfer artifact). After per-survey native retraining and 7-way positional deduplication at 5 arcsec (radius-sweep variation <0.1%), the catalog contains 378,280 unique anomalies: 378,080 point-source detections from six surveys plus 200 Planck CMB map-patch regions. The recommended catalog-grade subset is ~265,000 entries, excluding the LAMOST exploratory tier (~113,000 objects retained as a documented methodological lesson: 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact). The point-source tier is ~141x the largest prior single-survey anomaly catalog; the DESI-only subset (195,829) is a ~73x like-for-like increase. Archival cross-matching of the top-1,000 DESI anomalies against 20 curated all-sky catalogs yields a genuine novelty fraction of ~17.8% (top-score stratum point estimate). Cosmological applications include a multi-tracer scale-dependent-bias f_NL forecast using the anomaly catalog as a high-bias tracer, and a matter-bounce gravitational-wave-background template fit to the NANOGrav 15-yr HD-correlated free-spectrum posteriors (gamma = 2.567 +/- 0.382; Savage-Dickey B_MB/SMBHB = 7.1e3), with the bounce prediction gamma = 3.0 consistent at 1.1 sigma. The catalog, model weights, per-survey training records, and all reproducibility scripts will be publicly released with the arXiv posting.
