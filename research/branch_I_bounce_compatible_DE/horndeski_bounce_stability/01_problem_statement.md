# Branch I Test I-1: Problem Statement

**Date:** 2026-03-16

---

## Why Bounce→DE Derivation Routes Are Closed

Foundations A–G tested seven mechanism classes for connecting
the spin-torsion bounce to late-time dark energy. All failed
due to structural barriers:

1. Mass-coupling lock (A)
2. Topological-shift duality (B)
3. Scalar-tensor universality (C)
4. Planck suppression (D)
5. Scale separation (E)
6. Attractor-sensitivity dilemma (F)
7. Parameter immunity (G)
8. Parity-even effective interaction (H-parity)

Additionally, the Branch H tensor program found:
- Tensor amplitude P_T ~ 10⁻⁶⁴ (unobservable)
- Tensor chirality Δχ = 0 (exactly, in minimal model)

The bounce cannot PRODUCE dark energy, and the minimal model
has no distinctive tensor-sector signature.

---

## Why Branch I Is Different

Branch I asks a DIFFERENT question:

> Given that dark energy exists independently, does the bounce
> CONSTRAIN which dark energy models are viable?

This is a COMPATIBILITY test, not a derivation. The bounce
need not produce DE — it only needs to be incompatible with
certain DE sectors. If the bounce excludes some DE models,
that is a nontrivial physical constraint even though the
bounce doesn't determine Λ.

---

## Why Horndeski / Scalar-Tensor DE Is the First Test

Horndeski gravity is the most general scalar-tensor theory
with second-order equations of motion. It encompasses:
- Quintessence (canonical scalar + potential)
- K-essence (non-canonical kinetic terms)
- Kinetic gravity braiding (derivative coupling to gravity)
- Non-minimally coupled scalars (Brans-Dicke, f(R), etc.)
- Galileon models

These are the MOST COMMONLY studied DE models. If the bounce
constrains Horndeski, it constrains a large fraction of the
DE literature.

The specific concern: Horndeski stability conditions (ghost
freedom, gradient stability, sound speed bounds) involve
background quantities (H, Ḣ, φ̇, φ̈) that take EXTREME values
at the bounce. Could these extreme values violate stability?

## Success Criteria

A useful Branch I result would show at least one of:

### Strong success
1. A nontrivial EXCLUSION of a common DE model class
   (e.g., "cubic Horndeski with braiding scale M < M_crit
   develops ghost instability at the bounce")
2. A NARROWED viable parameter region
   (e.g., "non-minimal coupling requires |ξ| < ξ_max for
   bounce compatibility")
3. A SHARP stability criterion
   (e.g., "G₅ ≠ 0 implies c_T singular at H = 0 unless
   G₅X(φ, 0) = 0")

### Moderate success
4. A METHODOLOGICAL constraint
   (e.g., "standard EFT-of-DE parameterization breaks down
   at H = 0, requiring Lagrangian-level specification")
5. Identification of a specific model class that COULD fail
   bounce compatibility (requiring Phase 2 numerical work)

### Failure
6. All viable DE models are TRIVIALLY compatible with the bounce
   (DE sector negligible at Planck density, no interesting physics)
7. The analysis reduces to GENERIC known stability statements
   with no bounce-specific content

| ρ_DE/ρ_crit | ~ 10⁻¹²² | Negligible |
| a | a_b (minimum) | — |

The critical feature: **H = 0** at the bounce. Many DE/modified
gravity analyses assume H > 0 (expanding universe). The question
is whether H = 0 creates pathologies.

## The Scale Separation Concern

The DE energy density at the bounce is 10⁻¹²² of the bounce
energy density. This suggests the DE sector is a negligible
perturbation at the bounce, and all compatibility tests may
yield trivially positive results.

The analysis must be HONEST about this possibility. If scale
separation makes everything trivially compatible, that is the
correct answer — not a failure of effort but a structural fact.
