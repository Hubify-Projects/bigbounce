# Branch I: Independent DE Sectors Compatible With Bounce

**Date:** 2026-03-15

---

## The Question

Which dark energy models are COMPATIBLE WITH (not derived from)
a spin-torsion bounce cosmology?

## Why This Question Matters

Foundations A–G showed the bounce cannot PRODUCE dark energy. But
the universe has both a bounce (possibly) and dark energy
(certainly). These must coexist. The coexistence may be trivial
(any DE works with any bounce) or nontrivial (the bounce
constrains which DE sectors are viable).

## What "Compatible With" Means

A DE model is compatible with the spin-torsion bounce if:

1. **No dynamical conflict:** The DE sector does not prevent the
   bounce from occurring (e.g., by dominating at Planck density)
2. **No instability:** The combined bounce + DE system is stable
   (no ghosts, no gradient instabilities, no runaway modes)
3. **Observational consistency:** The combined model matches ΛCDM
   at late times and has a regular bounce at early times
4. **Theoretical consistency:** The DE sector respects the
   symmetries of Einstein-Cartan gravity

## What "Compatible With" Does NOT Mean

- NOT derived from the bounce
- NOT predicted by the bounce
- NOT uniquely selected by the bounce
- NOT geometrically connected to torsion

This is a COMPATIBILITY test, not a derivation program.

## The Interesting Case: Nontrivial Compatibility

If most DE models are compatible with the bounce, this branch
produces a null result (the bounce is invisible to DE). But if
some DE models are INCOMPATIBLE — if the bounce rules out certain
DE sectors — that is a genuine physical result.

The bounce could constrain DE by:
- Forbidding DE models that interfere with spin-torsion dynamics
- Requiring specific energy conditions at early times
- Constraining the DE equation of state through bounce regularity
- Limiting the DE field space through consistency at ρ ~ M_Pl⁴

## Success Criteria

Branch I succeeds if:
1. At least one mainstream DE model is INCOMPATIBLE with the bounce
   (gives a nontrivial constraint)
2. The incompatibility is physical, not fine-tuning-dependent
3. The constraint is testable (rules out a region of parameter
   space that experiments can probe)

## Failure Criteria

Branch I fails if:
- All reasonable DE models are trivially compatible
- Incompatibilities arise only for already-excluded DE models
- The constraints are too weak to be observationally relevant

Survey DE model classes and identify potential incompatibilities.

### Phase 2

### Phase 3
