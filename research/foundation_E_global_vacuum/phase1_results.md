# Foundation E — Phase 1 Results

**Date:** 2026-03-14

---

## Verdict: FOUNDATION_E_PARTIAL

---

## Summary

The vacuum energy sequestering mechanism (Kaloper-Padilla) is
**compatible with** but **not linked to** spin-torsion bounce
cosmology. The mechanism works — it absorbs constant vacuum energy
shifts through global constraints — but the residual cosmological
constant Λ_residual depends on global parameters (μ₁, μ₂) and the
full spacetime 4-volume, not on bounce-era physics.

---

## Strongest Surviving Mechanism

**Candidate A: Vacuum Energy Sequestering**

- Passes Test E1 (hidden tuning): MARGINAL. The scales μ₁, μ₂ are
  free parameters but Λ_residual is insensitive to vacuum shifts.
  The tuning is in μ₁, μ₂ — reduced from 10¹²² adjustments to 2
  parameters.

- Passes Test E2 (radiative stability): YES. The mechanism absorbs
  arbitrary constant vacuum shifts from any source.

- Passes Test E3 (bounce compatibility): YES. The bounce does not
  disrupt the mechanism and the mechanism does not interfere with
  bounce dynamics.

- Fails Test E4 (reduction to ΛCDM): PARTIAL FAIL. After resolving
  the global constraints, the local equations ARE standard Einstein
  equations with a specific Λ_residual. The mechanism is ΛCDM with
  a specific Λ-selection rule.

- Fails Test E5 (predictive power): FAIL for bounce-specific
  predictions. The only prediction (future recollapse requirement)
  is from sequestering alone, not from the bounce link.

## Why Other Candidates Fail

**Candidate B (4-form flux):** Not screened in detail. Requires a
landscape of vacua, which is an additional speculative assumption.
Deferred.

**Candidate C (global curvature constraint):** The constraint ∫√g R
= χ₀ has a natural bounce connection (the bounce contributes a
large curvature pulse). But χ₀ must be examined for tuning. Not
yet tested. **Remains open for Phase 2.**

**Candidate D (nonlocal action):** Fails screening — reduces to
scalar-tensor theory via auxiliary fields. Foundation C applies.

**Candidate E (boundary/topological):** Too speculative. No
calculational framework.

---

## The Core Difficulty

The sequestering + bounce combination fails to produce distinctive
predictions because of a **scale separation problem:**

```
V₄^bounce / V₄^total ~ 10⁻⁶⁰ (or smaller)
```

The bounce is too brief and too early to dominate any global
spacetime quantity. All global averages are controlled by late-time
cosmological evolution, not by bounce-era physics.

This is structurally analogous to Foundation C's scalar-tensor
universality: late-time FRW dynamics wash out early-time geometric
content.

---

## What Would Upgrade This to PROMISING

1. **Curvature-weighted sequestering:** If the global constraint
   involves ∫√g R × L_matter instead of ∫√g L_matter, the bounce's
   large R would enhance its contribution. This is a non-trivial
   modification of the Kaloper-Padilla mechanism.

2. **Cyclic cosmology:** If the universe bounces repeatedly, bounce
   contributions accumulate and could dominate global averages.

3. **Candidate C (global curvature constraint):** The constraint
   ∫√g R = χ₀ directly involves curvature, where the bounce
   contribution is enhanced. This candidate has the most natural
   bounce connection and should be tested in Phase 2.

## Recommended Next Step

### Option 1: Test Candidate C (global curvature constraint)
The global curvature constraint ∫√g R = χ₀ has the most direct
bounce connection. The bounce contributes a LARGE curvature pulse
that enters ∫R directly (not volume-suppressed). Test whether:
- χ₀ can be fixed by topology or theory
- The bounce curvature pulse constrains late-time Λ
- The mechanism has any predictive power

### Option 2: Test curvature-weighted sequestering
Modify Kaloper-Padilla to use curvature-weighted averages. This
is motivated by bounce cosmology (where curvature, not volume,
is the distinctive feature). Test whether this produces a
bounce-Λ connection.

### Option 3: Pivot to Foundation F
If the global vacuum approach is assessed as structurally incapable
of producing bounce-specific predictions (the scale separation
problem may be generic), pivot to Foundation F (bounce-linked
DE sector) where the connection is through initial conditions
rather than global averages.

**Recommended: Option 1 (test Candidate C), then Option 3 if it
fails.**
