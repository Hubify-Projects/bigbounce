## The No-Go Theorem

**Source:** Quintin, Chen & Brandenberger (2015), arXiv:1508.04141

### Statement

In a single-field matter bounce scenario with a general scalar field (with sound speed c_s and EOS w), the observables {n_s, r, f_NL} are coupled. Specifically, achieving both small r AND large |f_NL| simultaneously is impossible.

### Physical Origin

The no-go arises because both tensor and scalar perturbations are generated by the SAME field during contraction. The tensor-to-scalar ratio r = P_T/P_ζ is determined by the ratio of tensor to scalar amplification. In the simplest case:

$$
r = \frac{P_T}{P_\zeta} = 16 c_s \epsilon
$$

where ε is related to the contraction dynamics. Meanwhile, f_NL is set by the nonlinear evolution of ζ during contraction, which depends on the same parameters (c_s, w).

### The Scaling Relation

For a single field with EOS w and sound speed c_s:

**Scalar spectrum:**
$$
P_\zeta \propto \frac{H^2}{M_{\rm Pl}^2 \epsilon c_s}
$$

**Tensor spectrum:**
$$
P_T \propto \frac{H^2}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}
$$

**Ratio:**
$$
r = 16 c_s \epsilon
$$

**Non-Gaussianity:**
$$
f_{\rm NL} \propto \frac{1}{c_s^2} \quad \text{(dominant contribution for small } c_s\text{)}
$$

More precisely, for the equilateral shape: f_NL^equil ~ -1/c_s². For local shape in matter bounce:
$$
f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local} = -\frac{35}{8} \quad \text{for } w = 0, \, c_s \to 0
$$

The tension: making |f_NL| large requires small c_s (for equilateral) or growth of ζ (for local). But small c_s also makes r larger (r ~ c_s × ε, where ε is itself related to c_s through the dynamics).

### The Quantitative Constraint

From Quintin et al., for the single-field case:
$$
r \gtrsim 0.12 \quad \text{when } f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local} \approx -4.4
$$

This is **excluded by Planck** (r < 0.036 at 95% CL from BICEP/Keck 2021).

## Evasion Route 1: Curvaton (Multi-Field)

### Mechanism

Add a spectator curvaton field σ during contraction. The curvaton does NOT affect the background (w remains ~0). After the bounce, σ oscillates and decays, converting its perturbation δσ into curvature perturbation ζ_σ.

### Why It Evades the No-Go

The no-go assumes a SINGLE field sources both P_ζ and P_T. With a curvaton:
- P_T is still set by the background (matter contraction) alone
- P_ζ has TWO contributions: ζ_φ (from matter) and ζ_σ (from curvaton)
- If the curvaton dominates: P_ζ ≈ P_{ζ_σ} >> P_{ζ_φ}
- Then: r = P_T / P_ζ ≈ P_T / P_{ζ_σ} << P_T / P_{ζ_φ}
- r is suppressed by (ζ_φ/ζ_total)²

### The f_NL Cost

When the curvaton dominates ζ, the f_NL is no longer −35/8 from the matter contraction. Instead:

**Total f_NL with curvaton:**
$$
f_{\rm NL}^{\rm total} = f_{\rm NL}^{\rm matter} \times \left(\frac{\zeta_\phi}{\zeta_{\rm total}}\right)^4 + f_{\rm NL}^{\rm curvaton} \times \left(\frac{\zeta_\sigma}{\zeta_{\rm total}}\right)^4 + \text{mixed terms}
$$

**Standard curvaton f_NL (Lyth & Rodriguez 2005):**
$$
f_{\rm NL}^{\rm curvaton} = \frac{5}{4 r_{\rm dec}} - \frac{5 r_{\rm dec}}{6} - \frac{5}{3}
$$

where r_dec = 3ρ_σ/(3ρ_σ + 4ρ_r) at decay.

**Limiting cases:**
- r_dec → 1 (curvaton fully dominates before decay): f_NL^curv → 5/4 − 5/6 − 5/3 = −5/4 = −1.25
- r_dec → 0 (curvaton subdominant): f_NL^curv → 5/(4r_dec) → +∞

### Critical Analysis: Can We Preserve f_NL ≈ −4.4?

**Case 1: Curvaton fully dominates (r_dec → 1)**
- r is maximally suppressed: r << 0.036 ✓
- f_NL → −1.25 (from curvaton)
- The −35/8 prediction is LOST
- **Verdict: r is good, f_NL is wrong**

**Case 2: Curvaton partially dominates**
Let α = ζ_σ/ζ_total, so 1 − α² = (ζ_φ/ζ_total)²

- r = r_single × (1 − α²) → need α² > 1 − 0.036/r_single
- If r_single ~ 0.12: need α² > 0.7, so α > 0.84
- f_NL^total ≈ (−35/8)(1 − α²)² + f_NL^curv × α⁴

For α = 0.9 (curvaton provides 90% of ζ):
- r = 0.12 × (1 − 0.81) = 0.12 × 0.19 = 0.023 ✓
- f_NL^total = (−35/8)(0.19)² + (−1.25)(0.81)² = (−4.375)(0.036) + (−1.25)(0.656) = −0.158 − 0.820 = −0.98
- **f_NL ≈ −1.0** — too small, the distinctive prediction is washed out

For α = 0.7 (curvaton provides 70% of ζ):
- r = 0.12 × (1 − 0.49) = 0.12 × 0.51 = 0.061 **> 0.036** ✗
- r is too large!

**The fundamental tension:** Suppressing r requires the curvaton to dominate (α close to 1), but this dilutes the matter-bounce f_NL contribution. There appears to be no intermediate regime where BOTH r < 0.036 AND f_NL ≈ −4.4.

### Numerical Exploration of the α Parameter Space

| α (curvaton fraction) | r (if r_single = 0.12) | f_NL^total (approx) | Both satisfied? |
|----------------------|------------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| 0.0 | 0.12 | −4.375 | r TOO LARGE |
| 0.5 | 0.09 | −2.48 | r TOO LARGE |
| 0.7 | 0.061 | −1.38 | r TOO LARGE |
| 0.84 | 0.034 | −0.96 | r OK, f_NL ≈ −1 |
| 0.9 | 0.023 | −0.98 | r OK, f_NL ≈ −1 |
| 0.95 | 0.012 | −1.06 | r OK, f_NL ≈ −1 |
| 1.0 | 0 | −1.25 | r OK, f_NL ≈ −1.25 |

**Result: In the curvaton scenario, f_NL converges to approximately −1 to −1.25 for any α that satisfies r < 0.036.**

**The matter bounce f_NL = −35/8 is LOST in the curvaton scenario.**

## Evasion Route 2: Modified EOS (w ≠ 0)

### Does w = 0.003 Change the Quintin Relation?

For w = 0.003 (needed for n_s ≈ 0.965):
- The contraction is "nearly-matter" but not exactly matter
- The Quintin scaling r ∝ f_NL^{4/3} may have w-dependent corrections
- The tensor amplitude changes: P_T ~ H²/M_Pl² at horizon crossing
- The scalar amplitude changes: P_ζ depends on w through the growth of ζ

**For small w, the no-go relation should change continuously.** The question is whether the viable region (r < 0.036, |f_NL| > 1) opens up.

**Physical expectation:** For w > 0, the growth of ζ during contraction is slightly reduced compared to w = 0. This means:
- f_NL should be slightly LESS negative (smaller |f_NL|)
- P_ζ should be slightly smaller → r = P_T/P_ζ should be slightly LARGER

**This goes in the WRONG direction.** Non-zero w makes r larger AND |f_NL| smaller.

### Quantitative Estimate

For nearly-matter contraction with EOS w:
- The scalar spectral index: n_s − 1 = 12w/(1+3w)
- The f_NL depends on the superhorizon evolution of ζ during contraction

The superhorizon growth rate of the Bardeen potential during contraction:
$$
\Phi_{\rm growing} \propto a^{-(5+3w)/2}
$$

The non-Gaussianity f_NL is proportional to the square of the growth factor ratio. For w = 0, the growth exponent is −5/2, giving f_NL = −35/8.

For general w, the f_NL scales approximately as:
$$
f_{\rm NL}(w) \approx -\frac{5(5+3w)}{8(1+3w)} \approx -\frac{35}{8}\left(1 - \frac{4w}{5} + O(w^2)\right)
$$

For w = 0.003:
$$
f_{\rm NL} \approx -\frac{35}{8}\left(1 - 0.0024\right) = -4.375 \times 0.9976 \approx -4.365
$$

**f_NL is essentially unchanged for w = 0.003.** The correction is 0.24%.

**But the Quintin r-vs-f_NL relation still applies** — small w does not open a window.

## Evasion Route 3: Two-Phase Contraction

### The Idea

Split the contraction into two phases:
1. **Early phase:** Ekpyrotic (w >> 1), which resolves BKL instability
2. **Late phase:** Matter-dominated (w ≈ 0), which generates scale-invariant spectrum

The scalar perturbations that become observable CMB modes exit the Hubble radius during the matter phase. The ekpyrotic phase only affects modes that are already superhorizon by the time matter domination begins.

### Effect on Observables

This does NOT change the Quintin no-go for observable modes, because those modes are generated during the matter phase where w ≈ 0. The no-go applies to the matter phase, not the ekpyrotic phase.

### Verdict: Does NOT evade the no-go.

## Evasion Route 4: Reduced Sound Speed

### The Idea

In DBI-type models or k-essence, the sound speed c_s can be different from 1. For small c_s:
- Scalar perturbations are enhanced (P_ζ ∝ 1/c_s)
- r = P_T/P_ζ is suppressed (r ∝ c_s)
- f_NL gets additional contributions: f_NL^equil ∝ -1/c_s²

### Analysis

For a contracting phase with c_s < 1:
$$
r = 16 c_s \epsilon
$$

Reducing c_s directly reduces r. But it also generates large equilateral non-Gaussianity:
$$
f_{\rm NL}^{\rm equil} \sim -\frac{1}{c_s^2}
$$

For c_s = 0.1: f_NL^equil ~ −100. Planck constrains f_NL^equil = −26 ± 47. So c_s ≳ 0.1 is marginally OK.

**Can this work?**

With c_s = 0.2:
- f_NL^equil ~ −25 (consistent with Planck at 1σ)
- r ~ 16 × 0.2 × ε. Need ε to also be small.

But the local f_NL = −35/8 is a SEPARATE contribution from the superhorizon growth. The total f_NL has both local and equilateral shapes:
- f_NL^local = −35/8 (from growth) — unchanged by c_s
- f_NL^equil ~ −1/c_s² (from sound speed) — additional

**This gives r ∝ c_s, so reducing c_s reduces r. But it adds equilateral non-Gaussianity.**

Let's check if a viable window exists:

| c_s | r (relative) | f_NL^local | f_NL^equil | Consistent? |
|-----|-------------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| 1.0 | 0.12 | −4.4 | 0 | r too large |
| 0.5 | 0.06 | −4.4 | −4 | r too large |
| 0.3 | 0.036 | −4.4 | −11 | r MARGINAL, f_NL^equil OK |
| 0.2 | 0.024 | −4.4 | −25 | r OK, f_NL^equil marginal |
| 0.1 | 0.012 | −4.4 | −100 | r OK, f_NL^equil EXCLUDED |

**THERE MAY BE A WINDOW at c_s ≈ 0.2–0.3!**

With c_s ≈ 0.25:
- r ≈ 0.03 (below BICEP/Keck bound)
- f_NL^local ≈ −4.4 (preserved — this is the matter bounce prediction)
- f_NL^equil ≈ −16 (consistent with Planck: −26 ± 47)
- n_s: still need a tilt mechanism (w = 0.003 or curvaton)

**THIS IS A POTENTIAL EVASION OF THE QUINTIN NO-GO.**

The key insight: the no-go assumes c_s = 1 (or relates r and f_NL through c_s in a specific way). By treating c_s as an independent parameter (via DBI-type kinetic term for the contracting scalar), we can suppress r while preserving the local f_NL.

## Evasion Route 5: Non-Trivial Bounce (LQC Dressed-Metric)

In LQC, the dressed-metric approach introduces quantum-geometry corrections to the perturbation equation. Wilson-Ewing (2013) showed this suppresses r to ~10⁻⁴. The scalar spectrum is also modified but remains nearly scale-invariant.

**Effect:** r is suppressed by the LQC correction, independent of c_s or the contraction dynamics.

**But:** This is LQC-specific, not ECH. We noted ECH perturbations are equivalent to classical (no correction). However, since we're now working at the level of general bouncing cosmology, LQC-type corrections are worth considering.

**With LQC dressed-metric:**
- r ~ 10⁻⁴ (far below bound) ✓
- f_NL = −35/8 (preserved — LQC corrections don't affect superhorizon f_NL) ✓
- n_s: still needs a tilt mechanism

**But the r suppression is so strong that f_NL = −35/8 could be uniquely predicted.** This is because the no-go required the SAME field to source both P_T and P_ζ, and the LQC correction preferentially suppresses P_T.
