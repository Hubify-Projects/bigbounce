## Core Thesis (plain language)

The Einstein-Cartan-Holst framework provides a well-defined, nonsingular quantum bounce cosmology. We show that the minimal model cannot derive late-time dark energy from first principles (seven structural barriers close all routes), and the bounce produces no distinctive observable signatures at any energy scale (six additional barriers). However, the framework's Planck-scale parity-odd sector naturally motivates an ultralight axion-like particle with decay constant f_a ~ M_Pl and anomalous photon coupling. This spectator ALP predicts cosmic birefringence beta = C_{agamma} alpha theta_i / (4 pi) ~ 0.27 degrees, matching the observed 0.35 +/- 0.09 degrees within 1 sigma with no parameter tuning. We present MCMC constraints on the ALP parameter space using current birefringence data and forecast LiteBIRD sensitivity. Dark energy is treated as a separate cosmological constant; the ALP contributes negligible energy density.

### Abstract A (Closure-led)

We present a comprehensive assessment of cosmological predictions from the Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) framework. The nonsingular quantum bounce at rho_crit ~ 0.27 rho_Pl is well-defined, but systematic investigation of all routes from the bounce to late-time observables reveals thirteen structural barriers spanning five failure modes: the bounce is too high-energy for late-time observables, too generic at its own scale, and too decoupled from late-time vacuum energy. The minimal framework cannot derive dark energy from first principles, and the bounce produces no distinctive observable signatures. However, the Planck-scale parity-odd sector naturally motivates an ultralight spectator axion-like particle (ALP) with f_a ~ M_Pl and anomalous photon coupling C_{agamma} = 8 (SM fermion content). This ALP predicts cosmic birefringence beta = C_{agamma} alpha theta_i / (4 pi), giving beta ~ 0.27 degrees for theta_i ~ 1, consistent within 1 sigma with the observed 0.35 +/- 0.09 degrees (Planck PR4 + ACT DR6). MCMC analysis of the two-parameter spectator model (theta_i, m_a) yields [posterior constraints]. Dark energy is treated as a cosmological constant; the ALP contributes negligible energy density. LiteBIRD (sigma_beta ~ 0.01 degrees) will provide a decisive test.

### Abstract B (Prediction-led)

We study cosmic birefringence in spin-torsion cosmology. The Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) framework, which produces a nonsingular quantum bounce at Planck-scale densities, naturally motivates an ultralight axion-like particle with decay constant f_a ~ M_Pl through its parity-odd sector. With the Standard Model anomaly coefficient C_{agamma} = 8 and an O(1) initial misalignment angle, this spectator ALP predicts a cosmic birefringence angle beta ~ 0.27 degrees, matching the combined Planck PR4 + ACT DR6 measurement of 0.35 +/- 0.09 degrees within 1 sigma. MCMC constraints on the ALP parameter space (theta_i, log10 m_a) from birefringence data are presented, along with LiteBIRD forecasts. We simultaneously report the systematic closure of all first-principles routes from the ECH bounce to dark energy (seven structural barriers) and to distinctive observable signatures (six additional barriers across tensor, scalar, and relic channels). The bounce is a viable nonsingular cosmology; dark energy requires separate input (treated here as Lambda); cosmic birefringence via a Planck-scale ALP is the single surviving testable prediction.

### Abstract C (Balanced)

The Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) framework produces a nonsingular quantum bounce at rho_crit ~ 0.27 rho_Pl and generates a parity-odd four-fermion interaction through the Barbero-Immirzi parameter. We assess the framework's cosmological predictions across three domains. First, a systematic investigation of seven minimal and extended routes to deriving late-time dark energy from first principles yields clean negative results: seven structural barriers prevent any nontrivial bounce-to-dark-energy connection. Second, the bounce produces no distinctive observable signatures --- tensor, scalar, relic, and state-selection channels are all closed by six additional barriers. Third, the framework's Planck-scale parity-odd sector motivates an effective spectator axion-like particle with f_a ~ M_Pl and Standard Model anomaly coupling C_{agamma} = 8. This ALP predicts cosmic birefringence beta = C_{agamma} alpha theta_i / (4 pi) ~ 0.27 degrees for theta_i ~ O(1), consistent with the observed 0.35 +/- 0.09 degrees. MCMC analysis yields [constraints]. Dark energy is treated as Lambda; the ALP is a spectator with negligible energy density. LiteBIRD will be decisive. The paper establishes that spin-torsion bounce cosmology is theoretically viable but observationally inert, with cosmic birefringence via a Planck-scale ALP as the sole testable handle.

## Recommended Abstract

**Abstract C (Balanced)**

Reasons:
- Three-part structure matches the paper's actual content
- Leads with what the framework IS (bounce), not what it CLAIMS
- Negative results are presented as structural findings, not failures
- ALP prediction comes naturally as the surviving positive result
- "sole testable handle" is honest and compelling
- Leaves room for MCMC results to be inserted
- Does not overclaim uniqueness of the ALP (says "motivates," not "derives")
