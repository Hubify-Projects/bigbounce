# Branch O: Hidden-Sector Vacuum Selection via Irreversible Bounce Dynamics

**Date:** 2026-03-16

---

## Why Branch J Failed

Branch J asked whether the bounce could SELECT the state of an
independently existing dark-energy sector. It failed due to three
interlocking barriers:

### Barrier 9: Hamiltonian Phase-Space Conservation (Liouville)

The spin-torsion bounce is a Hamiltonian scattering event at H ≈ 0.
Liouville's theorem guarantees that the map from pre-bounce to
post-bounce states preserves phase-space volume. The bounce ROTATES
the dark-sector state vector but cannot CONTRACT it.

State selection requires phase-space contraction: many initial
conditions mapping to few outcomes. This demands dissipation or
irreversibility, both absent in the Hamiltonian bounce.

### The Naturalness Dilemma

Any curvature coupling ξRφ² strong enough to affect the dark sector
at the bounce (ξ ~ O(1)) generates Planck-scale radiative corrections
to DE masses (δm² ~ ξM_Pl²), destroying the DE potential. A coupling
weak enough to preserve the DE sector (ξ ~ 10⁻¹²⁴) is far too weak
for state selection.

### Scale Separation

The 10⁶¹ hierarchy between M_Pl and m_DE prevents parametric
resonance, meaningful field evolution during the bounce, and
nonadiabatic effects at DE frequencies.

**Branch J's verdict: ALL Hamiltonian state-selection mechanisms
are dead.**

## Why Branch O Is Logically Different

Branch O does NOT retry Hamiltonian state selection. It asks a
fundamentally different question:

> Can the bounce trigger or bias an IRREVERSIBLE process in a
> hidden sector, such that the late-time vacuum state is determined
> (or strongly constrained) by the bounce, through a genuinely
> non-Hamiltonian channel?

The key word is IRREVERSIBLE. Branch J proved that reversible
(Hamiltonian) dynamics cannot select states. Branch O asks whether
adding irreversibility — particle production, phase transitions,
thermalization, tunneling, dissipation, decoherence — changes the
conclusion.

### The logical distinction

| Branch J (CLOSED) | Branch O (THIS BRANCH) |
|---|---|
| Hamiltonian dynamics only | Explicitly requires irreversibility |
| Phase-space volume preserved | Phase-space contraction via dissipation/entropy |
| Bounce as scattering event | Bounce as trigger for phase transition |
| Direct curvature coupling to DE | Indirect: bounce → hidden sector → vacuum |
| DE sector must survive intact | Hidden sector can be destroyed/restructured |

### Why irreversibility might escape Liouville

Liouville's theorem applies to the FULL phase space of a closed
Hamiltonian system. It can be evaded if:

1. **Particle production** — New degrees of freedom are created,
   expanding the effective phase space. The original DOF lose
   information to the new DOF. (Schwinger pair production,
   parametric resonance into hidden-sector particles.)

2. **First-order phase transition** — Bubble nucleation is
   stochastic and irreversible. The nucleation rate is exponentially
   sensitive to the bounce conditions. The transition completes
   only if the nucleation rate exceeds the Hubble rate.

3. **Thermalization** — Energy transfer to many DOF creates entropy.
   The final state depends on the energy input (deterministic)
   but not on the microstate of the initial conditions (information
   loss).

4. **Tunneling** — Quantum tunneling rates are exponentially
   sensitive to barrier parameters. The bounce can modify
   barrier heights, exponentially biasing the tunneling outcome.

5. **Decoherence/coarse-graining** — Tracing over unobserved DOF
   produces entropy and effective phase-space contraction in the
   observed sector.

6. **Hysteresis** — First-order transitions with supercooling
   produce path-dependent final states.

## What Would Count as a Real Indirect Bounce→DE Link

A viable Branch O mechanism must demonstrate ALL of:

### Success Criterion O-S1: Genuine Irreversibility

The mechanism must contain a genuinely non-Hamiltonian or
hysteretic step. This could be:
- Particle production (Bogoliubov coefficients |β|² > 0)
- Bubble nucleation (Γ > 0)
- Thermalization (ΔS > 0)
- Quantum tunneling (P_tunnel ≠ 0)

The irreversibility must be QUANTIFIABLE, not merely asserted.

### Success Criterion O-S2: Bounce Essentiality

The irreversible process must be TRIGGERED or STRONGLY BIASED by
the bounce specifically. If the same process would occur in any
hot Big Bang (with or without a prior bounce), it is not bounce-
specific and provides no connection between bounce parameters and
late-time vacuum properties.

The test: compare the transition probability/rate WITH and WITHOUT
the bounce. The bounce must change the outcome qualitatively (not
just by an O(1) factor).

### Success Criterion O-S3: Vacuum Narrowing

The late-time vacuum state must be NARROWED by the bounce. If the
mechanism leaves arbitrary vacuum occupation (any of N vacua
equally likely), it has failed. The bounce must select a specific
vacuum or narrow the possibilities to a predictive subset.

### Success Criterion O-S4: Naturalness/Protection

The late-time vacuum-like state must be technically natural. If the
mechanism just moves the fine-tuning from Λ to another parameter
(hidden-sector potential shape, coupling constant, initial
temperature), it has failed.

Specifically: the DE-scale vacuum energy (2.3 meV)⁴ must emerge
without requiring any parameter to be tuned to 10⁻¹²² or 10⁻⁶⁰.

### Success Criterion O-S5: Predictive Relation

There must be at least one quantitative relation between bounce
parameters (ρ_crit, a_b, torsion coupling) and late-time vacuum
properties (ρ_DE, w, vacuum branch). This provides testable
content beyond "a bounce happened and DE exists."

## Failure Criteria

### FAIL_NO_IRREVERSIBILITY
The mechanism is secretly Hamiltonian. No entropy production,
no particle creation, no tunneling. This is Branch J recycled.

### FAIL_NOT_BOUNCE_SPECIFIC
The irreversible process occurs in any cosmology with a hot epoch
at the right temperature. The bounce is decorative. The mechanism
works equally well (or better) in standard inflationary cosmology.

### FAIL_ARBITRARY_BRANCH
The mechanism produces irreversible transitions but doesn't select
a specific vacuum. Multiple final states remain equally accessible.
The bounce provides energy but not direction.

### FAIL_NO_PROTECTION
The late-time vacuum energy requires fine-tuning. The mechanism
moved the CC problem to a hidden-sector parameter without solving
it. Typical signature: ρ_DE depends on a parameter that must be
tuned to 10⁻¹²² or comparable precision.

### FAIL_NO_NARROWING
The mechanism determines the vacuum branch but the vacuum energy
within that branch is unconstrained. The branch label is selected
but ρ_DE within that branch is arbitrary.

## The Central Challenge: Scale Separation

The elephant in every room:

```
ρ_bounce / ρ_DE ~ 10¹²²  (ECH)
ρ_bounce / ρ_DE ~ 10⁶⁰   (PGT with m_T ~ 10⁹ GeV)
```

Any mechanism must explain how a Planck-density (or m_T-density)
event at t ~ 10⁻⁴³ s determines a vacuum energy at (2.3 meV)⁴
WITHOUT fine-tuning a parameter to bridge this hierarchy.

Known mechanisms that bridge large hierarchies naturally:
- Exponential suppression (tunneling rates: Γ ~ exp(-S_E))
- Dimensional transmutation (Λ_QCD from α_s(M_Z))
- Seesaw mechanisms (m_ν ~ v²/M)
- Sequestering (protected sector decoupled from UV)

Branch O must employ one of these or identify a new one.
Otherwise the 10¹²² hierarchy is simply moved, not explained.
