## The Three Structural Outcomes

### 1. Irreversibility is real but not bounce-specific

Five of seven candidates (B, C, E, F, G) involve genuine
irreversibility (particle production, thermalization, nucleation).
But in every case, the irreversible process occurs in ANY hot
cosmology — standard Big Bang, inflationary reheating, or bounce.
The bounce provides energy/heat, but so does any other cosmological
heating mechanism.

**Branch O successfully evades the Liouville barrier (Barrier 9)
by introducing irreversibility. But it fails the bounce-specificity
test (O2). The irreversibility is generic, not bounce-specific.**

### 2. Bounce-marginal candidates die on naturalness

Two candidates (A, D) have marginal bounce-specificity: the ECH
bounce's Planck-scale curvature can trigger transitions that
lower-energy events cannot. But even for these, the resulting
vacuum energy is set by hidden-sector potential parameters, not
by the bounce. The bounce determines WHETHER a transition occurs,
not WHAT vacuum energy results.

**The outcome of the transition (ρ_DE) is structurally decoupled
from the trigger of the transition (the bounce).**

### 3. The hierarchy problem is untouched

No mechanism was found that connects the bounce energy scale
(M_Pl⁴ or m_T²M_Pl²) to the DE scale ((2.3 meV)⁴) without
introducing a new fine-tuning. The 10¹²² hierarchy must be
bridged by hidden-sector parameters (couplings, mass ratios,
or dimensional transmutation scales) that the bounce does not
set.

### Barrier 13: Decoupling of Bounce Scale from Vacuum Energy

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                             │
│  BARRIER 13: Bounce-vacuum energy decoupling                │
│                                                             │
│  The bounce operates at scale M (= M_Pl or m_T).           │
│  The late-time vacuum energy is at scale Λ_DE⁴.            │
│  Any formula ρ_DE = f(M, g_i) requires a suppression        │

│  bounce dynamics. Adding irreversibility determines          │
│  WHETHER a transition occurs but not the vacuum energy       │
│  scale. The bounce trigger and the vacuum energy are         │
│  structurally decoupled.                                    │
│                                                             │

│                                                             │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Barrier 13 is a generalization of the scale-separation barrier
(Barrier 5), extended from direct mechanisms to indirect ones.
It applies to any mechanism where the bounce triggers a
transition in a separate sector: the trigger is at M, but the
resulting vacuum energy is at Λ_DE, and no dynamics connects
the two scales.

## Is Branch O Genuinely Different from Branch J?

**YES, logically.** Branch O correctly identifies the escape
route from Liouville: irreversibility. Several candidates have
genuine irreversibility (particle production, nucleation,
thermalization).

**NO, in outcome.** The irreversibility evades Barrier 9 but
runs into Barrier 13 (decoupling). The bounce can trigger
irreversible transitions, but the vacuum energy of the resulting
state is set by the hidden sector, not the bounce.

**The distinction matters for understanding WHY the bounce→DE
program fails:**

- Branch J: fails because the bounce cannot SELECT a state
  (Liouville, Barrier 9)
- Branch O: fails because even if the bounce TRIGGERS a state
  change, the resulting vacuum energy is DECOUPLED from the
  bounce (Barrier 13)

These are complementary failures:
- Reversible mechanisms: Liouville prevents state selection
- Irreversible mechanisms: decoupling prevents vacuum energy
  determination

Together, they cover the full space of state-change mechanisms
(reversible + irreversible = everything).

## What Branch O Adds to the Program

### New barrier identified

Barrier 13 (bounce-vacuum energy decoupling) is genuinely new.
It extends the scale-separation barrier from direct to indirect
mechanisms, covering ALL hidden-sector transition routes. It
complements Barrier 9 (Liouville) by closing the irreversible
escape route.

### Completeness of the closure argument

Barriers 9 and 13 together close ALL state-change mechanisms:

```
State-change mechanisms
├── Reversible (Hamiltonian) → CLOSED by Barrier 9 (Liouville)
└── Irreversible
    ├── Bounce-specific → CLOSED by Barrier 13 (decoupling)
    └── Not bounce-specific → NOT A BOUNCE MECHANISM
```

There is no remaining category. ANY mechanism that changes the
dark-sector/hidden-sector state is either reversible (killed by
Liouville), irreversible and bounce-specific (killed by decoupling),
or irreversible and not bounce-specific (not relevant to this program).

### The mildly interesting Toy O4 result

Dimensional transmutation in a hidden gauge sector CAN produce
natural ρ_DE ~ Λ_h⁴ ~ (meV)⁴. This is a well-known mechanism
(hidden-sector QCD) that has nothing to do with the bounce. But it
confirms that the CC problem has potential solutions — they just
don't involve the bounce.
