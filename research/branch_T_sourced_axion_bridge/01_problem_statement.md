# Branch T: Sourced Axion Bridge — Problem Statement

**Date:** 2026-03-16
**Status:** Phase 1 Assessment
**Parent:** Post-A-G closure, Branches H through S

---

## Why prior branches failed

Every attempt to extract an observable signal from the spin-torsion bounce has hit a structural barrier:

| Branch | Strategy | Barrier |
|--------|----------|---------|
| H | Tensor spectrum from bounce | P_T ~ 10^{-64}, R-tilde-R = 0 on FRW |
| K | Scalar perturbation imprint | T(k) = 1 for all observable modes |
| M | Direct GW from vacuum amplification | Omega_GW proportional to (H/M_Pl)^2, 17 orders below detectors |
| N | Baryogenesis relics | Gravitational democracy: torsion is 1 of ~100 Planck-scale channels |
| O | Hidden sector vacuum | Bounce-vacuum energy decoupling: trigger != outcome |
| P | PGT pseudoscalar torsion mode | Z_2 parity: phi = 0 on FRW |
| S | ABJ triangle (photon-torsion vertex) | Planck-suppressed, 40 orders too weak |
| Q | Dynamical Immirzi field | Reduces to generic ALP after torsion elimination |

**Common thread:** torsion is non-propagating in minimal ECH. After algebraic elimination, all torsion effects reduce to (J^5)^2 contact interactions, suppressed by G^2. The bounce is violent but informationally opaque.

## Why Branch T is different (in principle)

Branch T does NOT try to make torsion itself carry the signal. Instead:

1. An EXTERNAL axion-like particle "a" exists independently of the torsion sector.
2. It couples to the fermionic axial current J^5_mu via the derivative coupling (partial_mu a / f_a) J^{5 mu}.
3. The bounce produces a transient spike in J^5_0 and its time derivative.
4. This KICKS the axion field: large da/dt during/after the bounce.
5. The kicked axion then amplifies gauge fields via the standard a X X-tilde coupling.
6. The amplified gauge fields produce observable signatures (GW, magnetic fields, birefringence).

The axion serves as an "information bridge" between the Planck-scale bounce and low-energy gauge fields. The bounce sources the axion; the axion sources the gauge fields. Two steps, each well-understood individually.

**The key advantage:** the axion coupling (partial_mu a / f_a) J^{5 mu} is dimension-5, not dimension-8 like direct torsion-gauge couplings. This gains (E/M_Pl)^3 relative to Branch S.

**The key risk:** the source strength still depends on the axial current at the bounce, which may be too small or require a free parameter (chiral asymmetry) that has nothing to do with the bounce geometry.

## Success/Failure criteria

**SUCCESS (Branch T promising):**
- At least one source term gives xi_b = da_dot / (2 f_a H) >= O(1) for physically motivated parameter choices
- The required f_a is in the phenomenologically allowed range (10^9 to 10^18 GeV)
- No new free parameters beyond {f_a, m_a, alpha_X}
- The signal has a distinctive bounce signature (not reproducible by generic ALP cosmology)

**FAILURE (Branch T closed):**
- xi_b << 1 for all source terms at all allowed parameter values
- OR xi_b ~ 1 requires n_5 (chiral asymmetry) as a free parameter unrelated to the bounce
- OR the bounce kick is indistinguishable from a generic ALP initial condition
- OR lower PGT bounce scale makes xi_b worse, not better

**MIXED:**
- xi_b ~ 1 achievable but only for f_a ~ M_Pl (no hierarchy) and only at Planck-scale bounce
- Signal exists but is degenerate with generic ALP cosmology
