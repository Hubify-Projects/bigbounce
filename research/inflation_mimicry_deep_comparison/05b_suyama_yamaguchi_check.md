# 05b: Suyama-Yamaguchi Trispectrum Check

## The Inequality

For ANY local-type non-Gaussianity generated by MULTIFIELD inflation:

τ_NL ≥ (6/5 f_NL)²

This is the Suyama-Yamaguchi inequality (arXiv:0804.4340). It is a THEOREM for multifield models.

## Application to Our Signal

If f_NL = -35/8 = -4.375:

τ_NL ≥ (6/5 × (-4.375))² = (-5.25)² = **27.5625**

So ANY multifield inflation model that produces f_NL = -4.375 MUST also produce τ_NL ≥ 27.6.

## What Does the Matter Bounce Predict for τ_NL?

The matter-bounce trispectrum has been computed in the literature (Cai et al. 2009, extended by later work). The generic prediction is:

τ_NL ~ (f_NL)² ~ (35/8)² ≈ 19

Wait — this is BELOW the Suyama-Yamaguchi bound of 27.6!

The S-Y inequality applies to multifield inflation. It does NOT necessarily apply to the matter bounce, because:
1. The bounce is NOT a multifield model — it's a single-field model with growing modes
2. The S-Y inequality is derived assuming the local template arises from the δN formalism, which doesn't apply when ζ is growing
3. The matter bounce can VIOLATE the S-Y inequality because it violates the assumptions

## Could τ_NL Distinguish Bounce from Multifield Inflation?

**POTENTIALLY YES.** If:
1. f_NL ≈ -4.4 is measured
2. τ_NL is measured to be < 27.6 (below the S-Y bound)

Then multifield inflation is EXCLUDED (it must satisfy τ_NL ≥ 27.6), and the bounce is favored (it can naturally have τ_NL < 27.6).

However:
- Current τ_NL constraints are very weak (σ(τ_NL) ~ 10⁴ from Planck)
- Even MegaMapper-class surveys won't reach σ(τ_NL) ~ 30 for decades
- This is a FUTURE discriminator, not a near-term test

## Verdict

**The S-Y inequality is a powerful THEORETICAL discriminator but NOT a practical near-term one.** If both f_NL and τ_NL were measured precisely, a violation of τ_NL ≥ (6/5 f_NL)² would definitively exclude multifield inflation and point to a single-field growing-mode mechanism (i.e., bounce). But τ_NL precision is far in the future.

**Include in the paper as a theoretical point, not as a near-term forecast.**
