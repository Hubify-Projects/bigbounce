# 02: Non-Attractor Single-Field Escape Hatch

## What Is Non-Attractor Inflation?

In non-attractor (or ultra-slow-roll) inflation, the inflaton temporarily leaves the slow-roll attractor. During this phase, ζ grows on superhorizon scales (similar to the matter bounce!), which can generate large local-type f_NL.

The classic result: during a pure non-attractor (ultra-slow-roll) phase, f_NL^local = 5/2 = 2.5 (positive).

## Can It Produce f_NL = -4.375?

### Sign Issue
The standard non-attractor result gives f_NL = **+5/2** (positive). The bounce gives **-35/8** (negative). The SIGN is opposite.

To get NEGATIVE f_NL from non-attractor inflation requires either:
- A decaying-mode contribution with specific initial conditions
- A transition from non-attractor to slow-roll that reverses the sign
- A non-standard potential shape (e.g., a bump or feature)

These are POSSIBLE but require engineering. The positive sign (+5/2) is the NATURAL non-attractor prediction.

### Magnitude Issue
Even the positive +5/2 = 2.5 is smaller than |-35/8| = 4.375 by factor 1.75. Getting to 4.375 requires either:
- Stronger non-attractor dynamics (more extreme potential flatness)
- Multiple non-attractor phases
- Fine-tuned transition effects

### Transition Sensitivity (from arXiv:1712.09998)
The key finding from Cai et al. (2018): "the transition process has important effect on the size of the local non-Gaussianity." Specifically:
- **Sharp transition:** preserves the O(1) non-Gaussianity from the non-attractor phase
- **Smooth transition:** can COMPLETELY ERASE the non-Gaussianity generated during non-attractor

This means non-attractor f_NL is **transition-dependent, not robust.** The final f_NL depends sensitively on how the inflaton returns to the slow-roll attractor.

### Stability
Non-attractor inflation is inherently UNSTABLE — the inflaton is not on an attractor, so perturbations grow. This is a feature (generates large f_NL) but also a bug (the model needs fine-tuned initial conditions to enter the non-attractor phase and fine-tuned transition to exit it).

## Scorecard for Non-Attractor Inflation

| Criterion | Non-Attractor Inflation | Matter Bounce |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------|
| Can produce |f_NL| > 1? | YES | YES |
| Natural sign | POSITIVE (+5/2) | NEGATIVE (-35/8) |
| Can produce f_NL = -4.375? | Only with engineering | **AUTOMATICALLY** |
| Transition-dependent? | YES (strongly) | NO |
| Number of tunable ingredients | ~3 (potential shape, non-attractor duration, transition sharpness) | 0 |
| Stability | UNSTABLE (requires fine-tuned initial conditions) | STABLE (attractor contraction) |
| Parameter-free? | NO | **YES** |

## Verdict

**Non-attractor inflation can produce large local f_NL but NOT naturally at -4.375.** The natural prediction is +5/2 (wrong sign). Getting to -4.375 requires:
1. Engineering the sign (via transition or potential features)
2. Engineering the magnitude (via non-attractor duration)
3. Fine-tuning the transition (sharp vs smooth)

This is NOT a clean competitor. It's a mechanism that CAN be tuned to hit any target, but doesn't PREDICT -4.375 the way the bounce does.

**Classification: MORE_ENGINEERED_THAN_BOUNCE**
