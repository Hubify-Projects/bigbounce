# 05: Hard-to-Mimic Core

## The Question: What Part of the Bounce Package Is Hardest to Fake?

## Candidate Assessment

### Candidate A: The precise amplitude (|f_NL| = 4.375)
Inflation CAN achieve this amplitude with tuning (multifield, self-interacting curvaton).
**Mimicry difficulty: MODERATE.** Achievable but requires tuning.

### Candidate B: The negative sign
Standard curvaton gives positive f_NL. Non-attractor gives positive f_NL (+5/2). Getting NEGATIVE f_NL in inflation requires either self-interacting curvaton with λ < 0 (unstable) or field-space curvature engineering.
**Mimicry difficulty: HIGH.** Natural inflationary constructions prefer positive sign.

### Candidate C: Amplitude + sign simultaneously
Achieving BOTH |f_NL| > 4 AND negative sign requires the most exotic constructions — self-interacting curvaton with carefully tuned negative λ, or strongly curved field space with specific turn parameters.
**Mimicry difficulty: HIGH.** The combination restricts the inflationary parameter space severely.

### Candidate D: The parameter-free nature
This is the STRONGEST card. The bounce doesn't just predict f_NL ≈ -4 as one value from a family — it predicts EXACTLY -35/8 with ZERO free parameters. Any inflationary competitor must introduce free parameters and then tune them to match.
**Mimicry difficulty: VERY HIGH.** Inflation can ACCOMMODATE but cannot PREDICT -35/8.

### Candidate E: The COMBINATION of all observables
f_NL = -4.375 (parameter-free) + n_s = 0.964 (one parameter) + r ~ 10⁻⁴ + consistency relation VIOLATION (f_NL >> (5/12)(1-n_s)).
**Mimicry difficulty: VERY HIGH.** No single inflationary model naturally produces this full package.

## WINNER: The Parameter-Free Prediction (Candidate D)

**The single hardest thing for inflation to fake is not any individual observable — it is the ZERO-PARAMETER nature of the f_NL prediction.**

If f_NL is measured to be -4.4 ± 0.7:
- The bounce explains it with 0 free parameters (it's an automatic consequence of w = 0 contraction)
- The best inflation competitor explains it with ≥ 2 free parameters (field self-coupling + decay fraction or field-space curvature + turn parameters)

The Bayesian penalty for introducing 2+ extra parameters is severe. In an evidence comparison, the bounce wins not because inflation CAN'T reach -4.375, but because inflation has to PAY for reaching it while the bounce gets it for free.

## The Precise Statement

**"The matter bounce provides the f_NL = -35/8 prediction kinematically, as a mandatory consequence of matter-dominated contraction. Inflation can accommodate f_NL = -35/8 only parametrically, requiring at least one additional field and at least two tuned parameters. If this specific value is measured, Bayesian model comparison would strongly favor the kinematic (bounce) explanation over the parametric (inflationary) accommodation."**

This is:
- True (not overclaimed)
- Sharp (identifies the exact asymmetry)
- Falsifiable (if f_NL ≠ -4.375, the argument doesn't apply)
- Publishable (standard Bayesian reasoning in cosmology)
