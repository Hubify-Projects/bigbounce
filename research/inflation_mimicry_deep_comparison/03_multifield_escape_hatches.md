# 03: Multifield Inflation Escape Hatches

## Standard Curvaton Model

The curvaton is the simplest multifield route to large local f_NL.

### The Formula
For a quadratic curvaton with decay fraction r_dec:
f_NL = (5/4r_dec) - 5r_dec/6 - 5/3

### Sign and Magnitude
- At r_dec → 0: f_NL → +∞ (large positive)
- At r_dec = 1: f_NL = 5/4 - 5/6 - 5/3 = -5/4 = **-1.25**
- Minimum achievable: f_NL ≈ **-1.25** (at r_dec = 1)

**The standard curvaton CANNOT reach f_NL = -4.375.** The most negative value possible with a quadratic curvaton potential is -1.25, which is 3.5× too small.

### What Would Be Needed
To reach f_NL = -4.375 requires either:
1. **Self-interacting curvaton** with V(σ) = m²σ²/2 + λσ⁴/4 where λ < 0 (UNSTABLE potential). The self-interaction modifies f_NL by adding terms proportional to g'' = V''/V evaluated at the curvaton field value. With sufficient negative self-coupling, f_NL can be pushed to -4.375 — but this requires tuning λ/m² to a specific value.

2. **Inhomogeneous reheating:** If the curvaton decay rate depends on the local field value, additional contributions to f_NL arise. These are model-dependent and require specific choices.

3. **Modulated preheating:** Another route to negative local f_NL, but requires specific coupling structures between the curvaton and the decay products.

**In all cases: reaching -4.375 requires at least ONE tuned continuous parameter beyond the minimal curvaton model.**

## Rapid-Turn / Field-Space Curvature Models

### Mechanism
In multifield inflation with curved field space, turns in the field trajectory can generate large local f_NL through the conversion of isocurvature to curvature perturbations.

### Can They Produce f_NL ≈ -4?
YES, in principle. Field-space curvature R_fs and the turning rate Ω can both contribute to f_NL:
f_NL ∝ -(R_fs × Ω² × N²_turn) (schematic)

where N_turn is the number of e-folds during the turn. With sufficiently curved field space and a sharp turn, f_NL of any desired magnitude and sign can be achieved.

### How Much Tuning?
- Requires at least 2 fields (adds at least 1 extra field + its potential)
- Requires specific field-space geometry (curvature R_fs)
- Requires a sharp turn at a specific time/field value
- f_NL is a FUNCTION of the curvature, turn rate, and timing — all tunable
- The SPECIFIC value -4.375 has no preferred status; it must be engineered

**The model can hit -4.375 but only as one point in a continuous parameter space. It does not PREDICT -4.375.**

## Multi-Field DBI / Gelaton Models

### Mechanism
In multi-field DBI inflation with "gelaton" spectator fields, large local f_NL can be generated from the reduced sound speed of the spectator coupled to the curvature perturbation.

### Can They Produce f_NL ≈ -4?
Potentially, but:
- The f_NL is typically positive in the simplest constructions
- Reaching negative requires specific choices of the DBI field-space metric
- The f_NL is not parameter-free — it depends on the DBI warp factor, field-space metric, and spectator coupling

**Classification: MORE_ENGINEERED_THAN_BOUNCE**

## Summary of Multifield Escape Routes

| Model | Can reach f_NL ≈ -4? | How many extra ingredients? | Parameter-free? | Natural? |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------------|----------------|----------|
| Standard curvaton (quadratic) | NO (max |-1.25|) | 1 field + 1 coupling | No | N/A |
| Self-interacting curvaton | YES (with λ < 0) | 1 field + 2 couplings | No | **Unstable potential** |
| Rapid-turn / curved field space | YES | 2+ fields + geometry | No | Tunable but not predicted |
| Multi-field DBI / gelaton | Possibly | 2+ fields + warp factor | No | Not natural for negative sign |
| Modulated reheating | Possibly | 1 field + decay coupling | No | Model-dependent |

**NO multifield model PREDICTS f_NL = -4.375. All can ACCOMMODATE it with tuning.**
