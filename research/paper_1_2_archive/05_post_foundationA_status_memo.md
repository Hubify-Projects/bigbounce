## 1. What is the strongest publishable contribution of Paper 1.2 now?

**The systematic closure program and its structural lessons.**

Specifically:
- Five closures (four minimal + PGT extension) with predefined gates
  and documented negative results
- Six structural lessons, including the mass-coupling lock (Lesson 6),
  which is a previously unrecognized constraint on geometric dark-energy
  theories
- Five decision rules (DR1-DR5) that constrain the space of viable
  geometric completions
- The mass-coupling lock itself is a concrete new finding: in the
  ghost-free PGT 0- mode, g_eff ~ 1/(M_Pl sqrt(|t_3|)) so that a
  cosmologically light mass automatically decouples the field. This is
  not in the existing literature as a constraint on geometric DE programs

The phenomenological analysis (MCMC fits, fine-tuning reduction, scaling
ansatz) is solid but not distinctive — it is standard Lambda CDM +
Delta N_eff analysis with spin-torsion motivation. The closure program
and its lessons ARE distinctive.

## 2. What is the biggest remaining weakness?

**Part I (phenomenology) and Part II+III (closures/requirements) are
two different papers sharing a manuscript.**

Part I is a phenomenological cosmology paper: MCMC fits, birefringence
consistency, galaxy spins, fine-tuning analysis. It is well-executed but
standard. It does not need the closures.

Parts II and III are a theory-landscape paper: systematic closure
methodology, structural lessons, decision rules, Foundation A assessment.
This is the distinctive contribution. It does not need the MCMC fits to
make its point.

The manuscript tries to be both. This makes it long (~20 pages in
two-column) and structurally awkward: a reader interested in the MCMC
fits must wade through four closure arguments and a PGT assessment; a
reader interested in the structural lessons must wade through galaxy
spin null tests and fine-tuning Monte Carlo.

## 5. Should Paper 1.2 be positioned as a phenomenological + structural
lessons paper, or still an internal bridge manuscript?

**Phenomenological + structural lessons paper. Not a bridge.**

The manuscript now has a coherent identity:
- Part I establishes the phenomenological framework and its observational
  consistency
- Part II systematically tests and closes minimal and near-minimal
  first-principles routes
- Part III extracts requirements that constrain the space of viable
  completions

This is a complete scientific narrative. It is not waiting for a future
result to give it meaning. The Foundation A closure strengthens it by
showing that even the most natural extension does not work — the closures
are not just "we tried four obvious things and they failed" but "we tried
four minimal routes AND the most natural upgrade, and all failed for
identifiable structural reasons."

The paper's honest framing — acknowledging phenomenological status,
reporting negative results, formulating requirements rather than claiming
solutions — is a strength, not a weakness. This is how responsible
theoretical physics papers should read.
