## 1. Does the mass-coupling lock read as a structural insight or a model-specific artifact?

**Structural insight.** After this revision, the lock is presented with:

- A general field-theory argument: any field where the mass and coupling
  both derive from a kinetic-normalization parameter Z exhibits m ~ 1/√Z,
  g_eff ~ 1/√Z. This is stated explicitly with the canonical-normalization
  formula.
- A cross-check against Brans-Dicke theory (ω_BD → ∞ IS the GR decoupling
  limit — same structure).
- A clear statement of what evades it: independent mass-generating mechanisms
  (Higgs VEV, gauge charge, environmental mass).
- The W boson as a concrete counterexample: m_W = gv/2 has two independent
  parameters, so m_W → 0 does not force g → 0.

A referee could still argue "this is just PGT" but would have to contend
with the explicit generality argument. The paper now makes it harder to
dismiss as model-specific because the mathematical structure
(L = -Z(∂φ)²/2 - μ²φ²/2 + gφJ, canonical normalization locks m and
g_eff together) is manifestly theory-independent.

**Remaining vulnerability:** The paper does not prove a no-go theorem.
It identifies a structural pattern and shows it applies to PGT
concretely, Brans-Dicke by analogy, and any Z-normalized theory
generically. A referee could ask for a formal proof that ALL geometric
dark-energy theories exhibit this lock. That proof does not exist and
the paper does not claim it — it claims the lock is a constraint that
must be checked and shows it is not trivially avoided.

This is the correct level of claim. Stronger would be overclaiming.

## 3. Remaining weaknesses before circulation

In decreasing order of importance:

1. **Acknowledgments are placeholder.** Must be written. (5 minutes)

2. **DiegoPalazuelos2025 citation has no arXiv ID.** Entry reads
   "(2025)" — needs the actual identifier. (5 minutes of lookup)

3. **Part I phenomenology is standard.** The MCMC analysis is a
   Λ CDM + ΔN_eff extension — solid but not distinctive. A referee
   could ask "what does Part I add beyond Liu et al. 2025?" Answer:
   the scaling ansatz, fine-tuning reduction, and the systematic
   closure context. This answer is in the paper but could be challenged.

4. **No new calculation.** The paper contains no new computation that a
   referee would call a "result" in the traditional theoretical physics
   sense. The mass-coupling lock argument is analytical but short —
   it is a structural observation, not a multi-page derivation. This is
   defensible (the observation IS the result) but a referee may want more.

5. **The five closures are summarized, not fully derived.** The detailed
   closures are in the companion note. A referee may want to see the
   companion note before accepting the closures. This is standard
   practice (supplemental material) but adds friction.

None of these is fatal. Items 1-2 are trivial fixes. Items 3-5 are
structural features of the paper that cannot be changed without changing
its nature — and changing its nature would be wrong.
