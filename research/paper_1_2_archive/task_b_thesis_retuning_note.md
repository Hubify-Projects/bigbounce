# Thesis Retuning Note

**Date:** 2026-03-14

## Title Options Considered

1. "Geometric Dark Energy: Phenomenological Viability, Systematic Closures, and Requirements for Completion" **[CHOSEN]**
2. "Dark Energy from Spin-Torsion Cosmology: A Phenomenological Framework and Its Structural Limits"
3. "Systematic Closure of Geometric Dark Energy Routes: Phenomenology, Structural Lessons, and the Mass-Coupling Lock"
4. "What Geometric Dark Energy Needs: Phenomenological Constraints, Five Closures, and Requirements for Completion"
5. "Spin-Torsion Dark Energy: Phenomenological Viability and the Structural Requirements for Geometric Completion"

### Why Option 1
- "Geometric Dark Energy" is the broadest accurate framing
- "Phenomenological Viability" signals the paper delivers working fits, not just theory
- "Systematic Closures" signals the negative results are disciplined, not failures
- "Requirements for Completion" signals forward-looking contribution without overclaiming
- Avoids "spin-torsion" in the title (too specific for a paper that now includes PGT)

## Abstract Options Considered

Three drafts were written. The chosen version:
- Opens with the framework and closures (both minimal and near-minimal)
- Introduces the mass-coupling lock as a concrete new result
- States the six structural lessons and requirements
- Ends with the paper's contribution: phenomenological model + theory landscape map
- Does NOT claim proximity to a first-principles result

## Thesis Shift

### OLD thesis (pre-Foundation A):
"We present a phenomenological framework, report four closures of minimal
routes, and identify three untested candidate foundations."

### NEW thesis (post-Foundation A):
"We present a phenomenological framework, report five closures
(four minimal + one near-minimal PGT extension), extract six structural
lessons including the previously unrecognized mass-coupling lock, and
formulate the requirements any viable geometric completion must satisfy."

### Key differences:
1. Foundation A is no longer a "future direction" — it is a tested and
   closed result within the paper
2. The mass-coupling lock is presented as a new structural finding, not
   just another failure
3. The requirements section (DR1-DR5) is the paper's theory-landscape
   contribution, not the candidate foundations list
4. The paper's identity shifts from "phenomenology + wishlist" to
   "phenomenology + systematic landscape mapping"
5. The ambition of geometric dark energy is preserved but reframed as
   an open challenge with explicit requirements, not a near-term expectation
