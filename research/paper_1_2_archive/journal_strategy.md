# Paper 1.2 — Journal Positioning Strategy

**Date:** 2026-03-14

## Journal Assessments

### 1. Physical Review D (PRD)

**Likelihood of acceptance:** 55-65%

**Strengths for PRD:**
- Paper format, length (~16 PRD pages), and style match PRD conventions perfectly
- revtex4-2 already used; compiles as PRD submission
- MCMC analysis with Cobaya/CAMB is standard PRD methodology
- PGT/ECH content is within PRD scope (gr-qc + hep-th + astro-ph.CO)
- PRD publishes negative results if the methodology is sound

**Risks for PRD:**
- A skeptical referee (Referee B profile) may argue insufficient original calculation
- The mass-coupling lock could be seen as "too simple" for PRD Regular Article
- The phenomenology section is standard ΛCDM+ΔNeff — not distinctive enough for PRD on its own
- PRD expects new calculations or significant new phenomenological results

**Required framing adjustments:**
- Emphasize the mass-coupling lock and DR framework as the primary contribution
- Consider a cover letter explicitly stating: "The contribution is the identification of a previously unrecognized structural constraint (the mass-coupling lock) and a systematic requirements framework, not the MCMC analysis"
- The companion technical note should be made available (supplemental material or separate arXiv post)

### 2. Journal of Cosmology and Astroparticle Physics (JCAP)

**Likelihood of acceptance:** 65-75%

**Strengths for JCAP:**
- JCAP publishes phenomenological cosmology papers routinely
- MCMC analysis with tension reduction is JCAP's core content
- JCAP is more tolerant of "survey + constraints" papers
- Negative results in the context of model building are valued
- The paper's mix of phenomenology + theory assessment fits JCAP well

**Risks for JCAP:**
- JCAP referees may be more phenomenology-focused and less interested in the structural lessons
- The mass-coupling lock may receive less attention if the referee focuses on the MCMC results
- JCAP papers with no distinctive observational prediction can struggle

**Required framing adjustments:**
- Lead with the MCMC results and fine-tuning reduction in the abstract (closer to Version A/C)
- Position the structural constraints as "theory requirements for future model building"
- The DR framework could be framed as a checklist for the JCAP audience

### 3. Classical and Quantum Gravity (CQG)

**Likelihood of acceptance:** 70-80%

**Strengths for CQG:**
- CQG is the natural home for PGT, Einstein-Cartan, and Poincaré gauge theory work
- The mass-coupling lock in PGT is directly relevant to CQG's readership
- CQG publishes structural analyses and constraint papers
- CQG is friendly to honest negative results in quantum gravity programs
- Karananas (2015) and Shapiro-Teixeira (2014) — two key references — were published in CQG

**Risks for CQG:**
- The MCMC phenomenology section (Part I) may be seen as tangential to CQG's core audience
- CQG may want more mathematical depth in the PGT analysis
- CQG has lower visibility/impact than PRD for cosmology results

**Required framing adjustments:**
- Lead with the structural analysis (Version B abstract)
- Shorten or defer the MCMC phenomenology
- Expand the PGT technical content (exact g_eff coefficient, detailed canonical normalization)
- Emphasize the mass-coupling lock as a constraint on PGT model building specifically

### 4. Journal of High Energy Physics (JHEP)

**Likelihood of acceptance:** 20-30%

**Strengths for JHEP:**
- JHEP publishes formal theory including gauge theory analyses
- The canonical normalization argument is the kind of structural observation JHEP values

**Risks for JHEP:**
- The paper is primarily cosmological phenomenology, not formal theory
- JHEP expects more mathematical depth and new calculations
- The MCMC analysis would be seen as out of scope
- JHEP referees would likely view the mass-coupling lock as trivial

**Required framing adjustments:**
- Would need major restructuring: drop most of Part I, expand Part II into a full PGT analysis paper
- Not recommended — the paper's structure is wrong for JHEP

## Recommended Strategy

### Primary target: **JCAP**

**Rationale:** JCAP offers the best combination of acceptance likelihood and audience fit. The paper's mix of MCMC phenomenology, theory assessment, and structural constraints maps directly to JCAP's scope. JCAP is more tolerant of "what doesn't work" papers than PRD, and the fine-tuning reduction + tension analysis will engage the phenomenology-focused readership. The mass-coupling lock will be seen as a useful model-building constraint.

### Backup: **CQG**

**Rationale:** If a JCAP referee objects to the MCMC phenomenology being standard, CQG is the natural fallback. The PGT content is CQG's core material, and the structural analysis would be valued. Would require shortening Part I and expanding the PGT technical details.

### Aspirational: **PRD**

**Rationale:** PRD is the highest-impact target but has the highest risk of a Referee B-type rejection. If submitting to PRD, the cover letter must explicitly frame the contribution as the mass-coupling lock and DR framework, not the MCMC analysis. Consider PRD if a trusted reader responds positively to the structural contribution.

### Not recommended: JHEP, PLB

**Rationale:** Wrong format (JHEP) or too short (PLB) for this paper's structure.
