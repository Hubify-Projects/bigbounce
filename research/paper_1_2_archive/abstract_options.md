## Version A: Conservative Theory Paper

We analyze the dark-energy phenomenology of Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) gravity with fixed Barbero-Immirzi parameter $\gamma = 0.274$ and minimal Dirac coupling. MCMC fits to Planck, DESI BAO, Pantheon+, and SH0ES data partially reduce the $H_0$ tension (from $4.9\sigma$ to $2.9\sigma$) and $S_8$ tension (from ${\sim}3\sigma$ to ${\sim}1\sigma$), while a scaling ansatz reduces vacuum-energy fine-tuning from $10^{120}$ to ${\sim}10^5$.

We test four minimal first-principles routes to deriving late-time $w = -1$ within this framework: NJL condensation, strict one-loop effective action, dynamical Immirzi field, and parity-sensitive CMB phenomenology. All four fail, sharing a common structural origin: algebraic torsion carries no information into the infrared after elimination. We further assess propagating torsion in Poincaré gauge theory (PGT), finding that in the ghost-free $0^-$ mode, canonical normalization locks the mass and matter coupling ($m \propto g_{\rm eff} \propto 1/\sqrt{|t_3|}$), suppressing the coupling ${\sim}10^{29}$ times below gravitational strength at cosmologically relevant masses.

From these closures we extract structural lessons and formulate five requirements (DR1--DR5) for viable geometric dark-energy completions. The phenomenological results are unaffected by the negative theoretical findings.

## Version B: Structural-Constraint Emphasis

Any geometric dark-energy theory built from a propagating torsion mode faces a structural obstruction we call the *mass-coupling lock*: when the field's kinetic normalization, mass, and matter coupling all depend on a single parameter, making the mode cosmologically light automatically decouples it from observable sectors. We demonstrate this concretely in the ghost-free $0^-$ axial mode of Poincaré gauge theory (PGT), where canonical normalization gives $g_{\rm eff} \sim 1/(M_{\rm Pl}\sqrt{|t_3|})$, suppressing the matter coupling ${\sim}10^{29}$ times below gravitational strength at meV-scale masses. No symmetry within PGT protects this mass.

The mass-coupling lock is the fifth of five requirements (DR1--DR5) we extract from a systematic closure program testing five first-principles routes to deriving $w = -1$ within Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) gravity and its PGT extension. All five routes fail. The four minimal-model closures share a common origin (algebraic torsion washes out after elimination); the PGT closure introduces a new mechanism (decoupling in the light-mass limit).

The ECH framework remains phenomenologically viable: MCMC fits partially reduce the $H_0$ and $S_8$ tensions, and a scaling ansatz reduces fine-tuning from $10^{120}$ to ${\sim}10^5$. The contribution is the combination of a well-tested phenomenological model with a systematic map of the geometric dark-energy landscape, identifying what does not work and why.

## Version C: Phenomenology + Theory-Landscape Emphasis

We present a phenomenological dark-energy framework rooted in Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) gravity, report the systematic closure of four minimal and one near-minimal first-principles route to deriving the late-time vacuum term, and extract the structural requirements that any viable geometric dark-energy completion must satisfy.

The minimal ECH framework---with algebraic torsion, fixed Barbero-Immirzi parameter $\gamma = 0.274$, and minimal Dirac coupling---provides a consistent phenomenological ansatz that reduces fine-tuning from $10^{120}$ to ${\sim}10^5$ and yields MCMC fits partially reducing the $H_0$ and $\sigma_8/S_8$ tensions. However, four systematic attempts to derive the late-time $w = -1$ behavior within the minimal model have all failed, sharing a common structural origin: algebraic torsion washes out after elimination, leaving no infrared fingerprint. Additionally, the most natural next-generation extension---propagating torsion in Poincaré gauge theory (PGT)---reveals a new obstruction: in the ghost-free $0^-$ axial mode, the mass and matter coupling are locked ($m \propto g_{\rm eff} \propto 1/\sqrt{|t_3|}$), so making the mode cosmologically light simultaneously drives its coupling ${\sim}10^{29}$ times below gravitational strength, rendering it observationally inert. No symmetry within PGT protects this mass.

From these closures we extract six structural lessons and formulate requirements that constrain the space of viable geometric dark-energy models: the theory must survive reduction, address scale naturalness, offer a distinctive observable, and---crucially---avoid the mass-coupling lock in which a light-mass limit automatically decouples the field from matter.

The framework's phenomenological viability (cosmological fits, scaling argument, fine-tuning reduction) survives all closures intact. Its scientific contribution is the combination of a well-tested phenomenological model with a systematic map of the theory landscape showing what does not work and why, narrowing the path for any future geometric completion.

### Recommendation: **Version B**

**Rationale:** Version B leads with the paper's strongest original contribution (the mass-coupling lock), immediately communicates what is new, and relegates the phenomenology to supporting context. This is the version most likely to survive a skeptical referee's first reading. It answers the question "why should I care?" in the first sentence.

Version C (the current abstract) is excellent but front-loads the phenomenology, which is the paper's weakest section from a novelty standpoint. A referee who is not engaged by paragraph 1 may not reach the mass-coupling lock in paragraph 2.

Version A is too conservative — it underplays the contribution and reads as a standard MCMC analysis paper.

**If adopting Version B:** The opening sentence should be slightly qualified to avoid overclaiming. The phrase "any geometric dark-energy theory built from a propagating torsion mode" is accurate but bold. Consider "geometric dark-energy theories in which a propagating torsion mode inherits its kinetic term from the gravitational action" for precision.
