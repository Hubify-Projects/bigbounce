# Current Paper Integration Decision

**Date:** 2026-03-13
**Program:** Early Structure from Bounce Cosmology

## 1. Can any of this be safely added to the current paper now?

**Not as main results or a phenomenology subsection.** The perturbation spectrum through the spin-torsion bounce has not been calculated. Any P(k) feature is a free phenomenological parameter disconnected from the paper's equations. Including forward-model constraint plots would misrepresent the framework's predictive power.

**YES — as a 2-3 sentence future-work paragraph.** The following is defensible:

> The bounce-to-inflation transition (Sec. XIV) may imprint features on the primordial scalar power spectrum at scales set by the pre-observable inflationary dynamics. If such features enhance small-scale power, they could affect early SMBH seed formation — a question made urgent by JWST discoveries of massive black holes at $z > 10$ \cite{Bogdan2024, Maiolino2024}. A full perturbation calculation through the spin-torsion bounce is needed to determine whether the framework produces such features and at what scales.

This is honest, forward-looking, and connects the framework to a timely observational question without overclaiming.

## 3. What is the strongest honest claim we could make today?

"The spin-torsion bounce framework modifies cosmological dynamics at Planck densities, which could in principle imprint features on the primordial perturbation spectrum. Forward modeling shows that even modest P(k) enhancements at scales k ~ 10^5-10^6 Mpc^{-1} could ease the formation of heavy SMBH seeds at z > 10, while remaining consistent with PBH constraints. However, whether the spin-torsion bounce actually produces such features — and at what scales — requires a perturbation calculation that has not yet been performed."

This is the maximum defensible claim. It:
- States the physical possibility
- Notes the observational motivation (JWST)
- Acknowledges the missing calculation explicitly
- Does not claim the framework predicts early SMBH seeds

**Timeline:** This paper could be drafted once the perturbation calculation exists, or as a purely phenomenological analysis with the bounce motivation clearly labeled as such.

## 5. The N_tot Problem (Must Be Addressed in Any Publication)

With N_tot = 92 (required by the dark energy constraint ρ_Λ ≈ (2.3 meV)^4), bounce-induced P(k) features are pushed to k ~ 10^{13} Mpc^{-1}, corresponding to M ~ 10^{-18} M_☉. This is:
- 21 orders of magnitude below SMBH seed scales
- In the asteroid-mass PBH window (weakly constrained)
- Potentially interesting for dark matter but not for early structure

For features at SMBH-relevant scales (k ~ 10^5-10^6 Mpc^{-1}), one needs only ~60-70 pre-observable e-folds, requiring N_tot ~ 75-80. This is inconsistent with the dark energy constraint unless the P(k) feature is broader than a single-scale estimate suggests.

**Any publication must state this tension explicitly.** It is not a fatal problem — the full perturbation calculation might show broader features, or the bounce-to-inflation transition might produce effects at multiple scales — but it is a significant caveat.
