# Foundation F — Problem Statement

**Date:** 2026-03-14

---

## The Alternative Strategy

Foundations A–D showed that local geometric dark-energy mechanisms
from spin-torsion gravity are structurally blocked on FRW. Foundation E
tests global vacuum mechanisms.

Foundation F takes a different approach: **dark energy may be a
separate sector whose parameters, initial conditions, or symmetry
breaking are influenced by the bounce phase.**

This concedes that dark energy is not directly geometric. Instead,
it asks whether the spin-torsion bounce CONSTRAINS or PREPARES the
dark-energy sector, even if that sector is itself a standard field
theory (quintessence, ALP, vacuum energy).

---

## Why This Route Avoids A–D

| Barrier | How F avoids it |
|---------|----------------|
| Lock (A) | DE sector has its OWN mass and coupling, not tied to G |
| Duality (B) | DE sector has its OWN symmetry protection |
| Universality (C) | Universality is ACCEPTED — the point is the link to bounce |
| Suppression (D) | DE sector is not coupled through the connection |

The key concession: the DE sector is generic EFT. The novelty comes
from the BOUNCE LINK, not from the DE sector itself.

## Examples to Consider

### 1. Bounce-induced vacuum selection
The bounce phase may select a specific vacuum from a landscape of
possibilities. The high energy density at the bounce (ρ ~ M_Pl⁴)
may trigger transitions that are forbidden at lower energies. The
selected vacuum determines the late-time Λ.

### 2. Bounce-prepared scalar attractors
A quintessence-like field that exists independently of the torsion
sector may be SET to specific initial conditions by the bounce
dynamics. Attractor solutions (tracking, scaling) may then drive
the field to the observed DE density without fine-tuning the initial
conditions — because the bounce FIXED the initial conditions.

### 3. Symmetry breaking triggered by bounce energy scale
A symmetry protecting a DE-sector field (e.g., shift symmetry of an
ALP) may be broken at the bounce energy scale M_bounce ~ M_Pl.
The resulting mass would be:
```
m ~ M_bounce × e^{-S_inst} ~ M_Pl × e^{-S_inst}
```
For S_inst ~ 280: m ~ H₀. This connects the bounce scale to the
DE scale through non-perturbative physics.

### 4. Torsion-mediated initial conditions
Even though torsion effects are Planck-suppressed at late times
(Foundation D), they are O(1) at the bounce. Torsion at the bounce
may set initial conditions for a separate scalar sector that then
evolves to produce late-time DE.

## Success Criteria

A viable Foundation F candidate must satisfy:

1. **Genuine bounce link:** The connection between bounce physics and
   DE sector must be structural, not incidental. "Any DE is compatible
   with any bounce" is not a result.

2. **Specificity:** The bounce must constrain at least one parameter
   of the DE sector (initial conditions, mass, coupling, vacuum
   selection). A generic statement is insufficient.

3. **Calculability:** The link must be computable, at least in
   principle. "The bounce somehow selects the right vacuum" is
   not a calculation.

4. **Consistency:** The DE sector must not interfere with bounce
   dynamics, and the bounce must not destroy the DE sector.

5. **At least one prediction:** The bounce-DE link must produce
   at least one prediction or consistency condition beyond matching
   the observed Λ.

---

## Failure Criteria

| Failure | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| FAIL_VACUOUS_LINK | Connection between bounce and DE is too weak to constrain anything |
| FAIL_GENERIC | Any DE sector is compatible; no specificity |
| FAIL_INCALCULABLE | Link requires non-perturbative physics that cannot be evaluated |
| FAIL_INTERFERENCE | DE sector disrupts bounce or vice versa |
| FAIL_NO_PREDICTION | No testable consequence of the bounce-DE link |

---

## Relation to Foundation E

Foundation E and Foundation F are complementary:

- **E asks:** Can the vacuum energy be controlled globally?
- **F asks:** Can the DE sector be prepared by the bounce?

If E succeeds (global vacuum mechanism), F becomes less urgent but
may still provide additional structure.

If E fails, F becomes the primary route.

If both fail, the program faces a harder question: whether the
spin-torsion bounce has ANY connection to dark energy, or whether
DE must be treated as an entirely independent problem.
