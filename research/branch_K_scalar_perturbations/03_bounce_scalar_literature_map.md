**Date:** 2026-03-16

## Purpose

Identify what would count as genuinely new versus already known
generic bounce behavior. The key question: does the spin-torsion

perturbations through bouncing cosmologies?

## Result 1: T(k) = 1 for symmetric bounces — KNOWN

### Literature

The conservation of the comoving curvature perturbation ζ
through a symmetric bounce is a standard result in bouncing
cosmology:

- **Wands (1999):** ζ conservation on super-Hubble scales for
  adiabatic perturbations holds through any smooth background
  evolution, including bounces.

- **Finelli & Brandenberger (2002):** Explicitly showed that
  for a radiation bounce, the constant mode of Φ passes through
  unchanged, with the growing mode mapping to the decaying mode.

- **Peter, Pinto-Neto, & Gonzalez (2007):** Detailed analysis
  of scalar perturbations through a quantum-gravity-inspired
  bounce, showing T(k) = 1 for super-Hubble modes.

- **Cai, Easson, & Brandenberger (2012):** General framework
  for perturbations through non-singular bounces, confirming
  the transfer function is trivial for symmetric bounces.

### What is known

For ANY time-symmetric bounce (same equation of state before
and after), the super-Hubble transfer function T(k) = 1. This
is a consequence of:
1. ζ conservation on super-Hubble scales (Weinberg 2003)
2. Time-reversal symmetry mapping growing → decaying modes

### What would be new

NOTHING about T(k) = 1 is new for the spin-torsion bounce. The
result is generic to all symmetric radiation bounces. The specific
bounce profile (a(t) = a_b(1+4α²t²)^{1/4}) doesn't matter for
super-Hubble modes.

### Novelty assessment: ZERO

## Result 2: Sub-Hubble oscillatory features — KNOWN STRUCTURE

### Literature

The transfer function T(k) for k ~ k_b has oscillatory features
(analogous to quantum mechanical scattering off a potential
barrier). This has been computed for:

- **LQC bounces** (Agullo, Ashtekar, Nelson 2012-2013):
  Oscillatory features in T(k) at k ~ k_LQC, with specific
  amplitude depending on the LQC effective potential.

- **Matter bounce** (Cai et al. 2014): Features at the bounce
  scale, suppressed for k ≪ k_bounce.

- **Generic bounces** (Battefeld & Peter 2015): Review of
  perturbation transfer through various bounce models.

### What is known

The EXISTENCE of oscillatory features at k ~ k_b is generic.
The specific SHAPE of the features depends on the bounce profile.

### What would be new

The specific oscillatory pattern for the spin-torsion bounce
profile IS new (the potential shape is determined by ρ_crit).
BUT: these features are at k ~ k_b ~ 10²⁵ Mpc⁻¹ (GHz
frequencies), completely unobservable. Computing the exact
oscillatory pattern at unobservable scales has zero scientific
value.

### Novelty assessment: ZERO (specific shape is new but unobservable)

## Result 3: Growing mode resolution — KNOWN FOR SYMMETRIC BOUNCES

### Literature

The growing mode problem in bouncing cosmologies is a major
topic:

- **Durrer & Vernizzi (2002):** Identified the growing mode
  as a key challenge for bouncing cosmologies.

- **Peter & Pinto-Neto (2002):** Showed that smooth bounces
  can resolve the growing mode problem (growing mode maps to
  decaying mode by continuity).

- **Xue, Steinhardt, & Steinhardt (2010):** Growing mode in
  ekpyrotic bounces — resolved by the specific bounce dynamics.

### What is known

For SYMMETRIC bounces: the growing mode is automatically
resolved by time-reversal symmetry. This is known and is the
primary reason symmetric bounces are considered well-behaved.

For ASYMMETRIC bounces (different equation of state before and
after): the growing mode can leak into the constant mode,
potentially spoiling the spectrum. This is a real problem for
some bounce models.

### What would be new

The spin-torsion bounce has EXACT symmetry (radiation on both
sides), so the growing mode resolution is guaranteed. This is
not new — it's the EXPECTED result for a symmetric bounce.

### Novelty assessment: ZERO

## Result 4: Vacuum-generated scalar spectrum — KNOWN AND EXCLUDED

### Literature

The scalar spectrum from vacuum fluctuations in a radiation
contraction:

- **Wands (1999):** P_S ∝ k⁴ for radiation contraction.
  Strongly blue-tilted, incompatible with observations.

- **Finelli & Brandenberger (2002):** Confirmed P_S ∝ k⁴ for
  radiation bounce from vacuum. This rules out a pure radiation
  contraction as the source of the observed scalar spectrum.

### What is known

A radiation contraction produces P_S ∝ k⁴ (from vacuum).
This is EXCLUDED by CMB observations (n_s ≈ 0.965 ≈ 1).

To get n_s ≈ 1, you need a MATTER-dominated or slow-contraction
phase before the radiation era. This is outside the minimal
spin-torsion bounce model.

### What would be new

Nothing. The k⁴ spectrum from radiation is standard.

### Novelty assessment: ZERO

### Literature

For bouncing cosmologies, r depends on the pre-bounce mechanism:

- Matter bounce: r ~ O(1) at CMB scales (problematic)
- Ekpyrotic: r ≈ 0 (tensors highly suppressed)
- LQC: r depends on model

### What would be new

The specific prediction r ~ 10⁻⁵⁵ from the spin-torsion bounce
(using P_T ~ 10⁻⁶⁴ from Branch H). This is model-specific but
trivially consistent with r < 0.03. It provides no useful
constraint.

### Novelty assessment: MARGINAL (specific number, but trivially below any bound)

## Result 6: ρ_crit-dependent features — POTENTIALLY NEW BUT UNOBSERVABLE

### Literature

Different bounce models have different ρ_crit (or equivalent):
- LQC: ρ_crit ~ 0.41 ρ_Pl (Ashtekar & Singh 2011)
- Spin-torsion: ρ_crit ≈ 0.21 M_Pl⁴ (this model)

The specific value of ρ_crit determines:
- The bounce scale k_b
- The shape of the effective potential near the bounce
- The oscillatory features in T(k) at k ~ k_b

### What would be new

The EXACT value of ρ_crit and its relation to the bounce
profile IS specific to the spin-torsion model. In principle,
if k_b were observable, it would distinguish EC torsion from
LQC or other bounce mechanisms.

### What is the problem

k_b corresponds to ~GHz frequencies. Current observations
reach ~nHz (PTA) to ~kHz (LIGO). The gap is ~10⁶ orders of
magnitude in frequency.

### Novelty assessment: GENUINE BUT UNOBSERVABLE

## Result 7: Torsion-modified perturbation equations — POTENTIALLY NEW

### Literature

Most bounce perturbation calculations use the effective fluid
approach (modified Friedmann equation as the only bounce input,
standard perturbation equations on the modified background).

In Einstein-Cartan theory, the torsion could ALSO modify the
perturbation equations directly:

- **Torsion perturbations:** The torsion tensor has its own
  perturbative degrees of freedom. In the minimal model
  (non-propagating torsion), these are algebraically determined
  by the matter perturbations. The constraint:
  T^λ_{μν} = (κ²/2) ε_{μνλρ} J^{5ρ}

  The perturbation of torsion is determined by the perturbation
  of the axial current: δT ~ δJ⁵.

  For scalar perturbations: δJ⁵₀ involves the velocity and
  density perturbations of the fluid. The effective Friedmann
  perturbation equations get corrections from δ(ρ²) terms.

### What would be new

A calculation that derives the scalar perturbation equations
DIRECTLY from the Einstein-Cartan field equations (not from
the effective fluid approach) and shows whether the torsion
perturbations modify the Bardeen equation.

For the MINIMAL model (non-propagating torsion): the torsion
is algebraically eliminated, and the perturbation equations
reduce to the effective fluid form with ρ → ρ(1-ρ/ρ_crit).
No new propagating degree of freedom.

The correction to the Bardeen equation from δ(ρ²):

```
δ(ρ²) = 2ρ δρ
δ(ρ²/ρ_crit) = 2ρ δρ / ρ_crit
```

This modifies the perturbation source term. At the bounce
(ρ = ρ_crit): the correction is O(1) relative to the leading
term. Away from the bounce: the correction is O(ρ/ρ_crit)
~ (a_b/a)⁴ → negligible.

**The torsion modification to scalar perturbations is
localized near the bounce (same as the background modification)
and affects only k ~ k_b modes (unobservable).**

### Novelty assessment: MARGINAL (new calculation, same physical conclusion)

## What Would Be Needed for an Observable Signal

For the bounce to leave an observable imprint on the scalar
spectrum, one would need:

1. **k_b at or below CMB scales** — requires a_b ≲ 10⁻²⁵ a_0,
   i.e., the bounce occurs at energy scales far below Planck.
   The spin-torsion bounce has ρ_crit ~ M_Pl⁴, so k_b is at
   GHz. A lower bounce scale would require a different theory.

2. **Asymmetric bounce** — different equation of state before
   and after the bounce would break T(k) = 1 and allow growing
   mode leakage. The spin-torsion bounce is exactly symmetric.

3. **Multi-field effects** — entropic perturbations could
   convert to adiabatic at the bounce, creating features. The
   minimal model has a single fluid.

4. **Pre-bounce mechanism** — a specific contraction phase that
   creates a scale-invariant spectrum with features near the
   bounce scale. This is outside the minimal model.

None of these apply to the minimal spin-torsion bounce.
