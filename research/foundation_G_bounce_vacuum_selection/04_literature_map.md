## Tier 1: Core Mechanisms

### Sequestering and Variants

1. **Kaloper & Padilla, "Sequestering the Standard Model Vacuum Energy" (2013)**
   - arXiv: 1309.6562
   - Informs: Candidate A (bounce-conditioned sequestering)
   - Relevance: Core sequestering framework. Need finite V₄.
   - For bounce: the finite-V₄ requirement connects to cyclic cosmology
   - Assessment: SUPPORTIVE of framework, NEUTRAL on bounce role

2. **Kaloper & Padilla, "Sequestration of Vacuum Energy and the End of the Universe" (2015)**
   - arXiv: 1502.07977
   - Informs: Candidate A
   - Relevance: Universe must eventually recollapse → cyclic connection
   - For bounce: directly motivates cyclic bounce cosmology
   - Assessment: SUPPORTIVE — recollapse requirement ↔ bounce cosmology

3. **Kaloper, Padilla & Stefanyszyn, "Sequestering in Field Space" (2016)**
   - arXiv: 1604.04000
   - Informs: Candidate A variants
   - Relevance: Field-space formulation may allow cycle matching
   - Assessment: NEUTRAL — more formalism, unclear on bounce role

### 4-Form and Flux Mechanisms

4. **Bousso & Polchinski, "Quantization of Four-Form Fluxes" (2000)**
   - arXiv: hep-th/0004134
   - Informs: Candidate B (flux branch selection)
   - Relevance: Large number of vacua allows fine-scanning of Λ
   - For bounce: bounce provides high-energy epoch for transitions
   - Assessment: NEUTRAL — framework exists but requires landscape

5. **Brown & Teitelboim, "Neutralization of the Cosmological Constant by Membrane Creation" (1988)**
   - Informs: Candidate B
   - Relevance: Dynamical flux adjustment through nucleation
   - For bounce: nucleation rates maximal at bounce energy
   - Assessment: SUPPORTIVE of mechanism, WEAK on bounce specificity

### Topological Aspects

6. **Eguchi, Gilkey & Hanson, "Gravitation, Gauge Theories and Differential Geometry" (1980)**

   - Informs: Candidate D (topological sector selection)
   - Relevance: Topological invariants in curved spacetime
   - Assessment: REFERENCE — establishes which invariants exist

7. **Alvarez-Gaumé & Witten, "Gravitational Anomalies" (1984)**
   - Informs: Candidate D
   - Relevance: Topological charges in gravitational theories
   - Assessment: REFERENCE — background for gravitational topology

## Tier 3: Critiques and No-Go Arguments

12. **Weinberg, "The Cosmological Constant Problem" (1989)**
    - Rev. Mod. Phys. 61, 1
    - Informs: ALL candidates
    - Relevance: Weinberg's no-go theorem. Any adjustment mechanism
      that uses only the equations of motion cannot determine Λ.
    - For bounce: the bounce provides BOUNDARY CONDITIONS, not just
      equations of motion. May evade Weinberg's theorem.
    - Assessment: CRITICAL — must verify each candidate evades this

13. **Padilla, "Lectures on the Cosmological Constant Problem" (2015)**
    - arXiv: 1502.05296
    - Informs: ALL candidates
    - Relevance: Comprehensive survey of what fails and why
    - Assessment: ESSENTIAL background

14. **Burgess, "The Cosmological Constant Problem" (2013)**
    - arXiv: 1309.4133
    - Relevance: EFT perspective on CC
    - Assessment: LIKELY FATAL for candidates that don't address
      radiative stability

15. **Polchinski, "The Cosmological Constant and the String Landscape" (2006)**
    - arXiv: hep-th/0603249
    - Informs: Candidate B (landscape)
    - Relevance: Why flux scanning requires enormous landscape
    - Assessment: CONTEXTUAL — landscape may be unavoidable
