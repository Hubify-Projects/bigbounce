# Foundation G — Problem Statement

**Date:** 2026-03-15

---

## Why Foundations A–F Failed

| Foundation | Route | Barrier |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| A | Local propagating torsion → DE | Mass-coupling lock: m and g tied by G |
| B | Geometric pseudoscalar (Nieh-Yan) | Topological-shift duality: protection ↔ content |
| C | Curvature-dependent mass | Scalar-tensor universality on FRW |

Three classes of mechanism are now exhausted:

1. **Local field mechanisms (A–D):** Every propagating geometric
   field that could drive DE is locked, unprotected, generic, or
   Planck-suppressed.

2. **Global spacetime-integral mechanisms (E):** Volume-weighted
   averages are dominated by late-time expansion. The bounce is
   negligible in any ∫√g(...) quantity.

3. **Initial-condition mechanisms (F):** Attractors erase bounce
   data; sensitive models require fine-tuning the bounce cannot
   provide.

## Why Bounce-Conditioned Vacuum Selection Is Logically Different

The mechanisms in A–F all attempt to use the bounce to SET a
CONTINUOUS quantity (field value, coupling strength, spacetime
integral) that determines dark energy. The structural barriers
show this fails: continuous quantities are either volume-diluted,
attractor-washed, or scale-separated from the DE scale.

Foundation G targets a different structure: **DISCRETE or GLOBAL
BRANCHING.**

The idea: the bounce does not determine a continuous field value.
Instead, it SELECTS among discrete vacuum branches, topological
sectors, or global constraint classes. The selection is made at
the bounce and is NOT subject to:

- **Volume dilution** (discrete labels don't have volume weights)
- **Attractor washout** (branch labels are not dynamical fields)
- **Scale separation** (the selection event is at the bounce, but
  the LABEL persists without needing to propagate through 10⁶⁰
  orders of magnitude in energy)

A discrete vacuum label is set ONCE and persists forever — like
a topological charge or a flux quantum. It does not evolve, is
not averaged, and is not erased by attractors. If the bounce
genuinely selects such a label, the connection survives.

---

## Why This Route Is Still Alive

The key distinction from E and F:

**Foundation E** asked: does the bounce contribute significantly to
global spacetime integrals? Answer: NO (volume dilution).

**Foundation F** asked: does the bounce set useful continuous initial
conditions? Answer: NO (attractor washout or fine-tuning).

**Foundation G** asks: does the bounce select a DISCRETE label or
constrain a GLOBAL variable through a MATCHING CONDITION (not a
volume integral)?

Matching conditions at the bounce are LOCAL IN TIME but GLOBAL IN
CONSEQUENCE. The bounce is a special surface (H = 0, ρ = ρ_crit)
where regularity conditions, junction conditions, or branch-
selection rules may apply. These conditions are evaluated AT the
bounce — not averaged over the spacetime — and therefore are not
diluted by late-time expansion.

This is structurally different from everything tested so far.

## The Target

Test whether the spin-torsion bounce can play a NONTRIVIAL role in
selecting or constraining a global vacuum variable, such that:

1. The bounce is ESSENTIAL (removing it changes the selection)
2. The selected value is NOT freely tunable
3. The resulting Λ is NARROWED relative to generic expectations
4. The mechanism is NOT equivalent to generic sequestering

---

## Success Criteria

A viable Foundation G candidate must satisfy ALL of:

1. **Genuine global variable or branch label:** There exists a
   quantity (flux number, topological charge, constraint parameter,
   branch index) that determines or constrains the effective Λ.

2. **Bounce plays a real role:** The spin-torsion bounce imposes a
   condition (regularity, matching, selection rule) that RESTRICTS
   the allowed values of the global variable. Removing the bounce
   changes or eliminates the restriction.

3. **No hidden tuning:** The selected value is genuinely
   DETERMINED or NARROWED by the bounce condition, not repackaged
   as a free parameter whose value must be chosen by hand.

4. **Not generic sequestering:** If the bounce is removed (replaced
   by a singularity or generic initial condition), the mechanism
   should change substantively. The bounce must be more than a
   narrative motivation.

5. **At least one consequence:** The mechanism produces at least
   one structural prediction, consistency condition, or observable
   consequence beyond "Λ exists."

6. **Internal coherence:** The action principle, variational
   structure, or branch-selection logic must be mathematically
   well-defined. No hand-waving about "the bounce chooses."

## The Honest Assessment

This is the last plausible route in the program. If Foundation G
fails, the conclusion is that the spin-torsion bounce has no
connection to dark energy at any level — local, global, continuous,
or discrete.

The prior probability of success is LOW:

- Every previous foundation encountered a structural barrier
- The cosmological constant problem has resisted all known approaches
- Vacuum selection mechanisms are generically speculative
- The bounce provides one scale (M_Pl) and dark energy requires
  another (10⁻³⁰ M_Pl)

But the route is worth testing because:
- Discrete/branching mechanisms have NOT been tested yet
- Bounce matching conditions are genuinely different from volume
  integrals or attractor dynamics
- A sharp negative result completes the program cleanly
- A positive result, even partial, would be significant
