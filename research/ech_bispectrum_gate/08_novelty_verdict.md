# 08: Novelty Verdict

## ECH_BISPECTRUM_PATH_DEAD

### The Argument (5 steps, each rigorous)

1. **No spin → no torsion.** A canonical scalar field φ has spin density S^λ_μν = 0. In Einstein-Cartan theory, torsion is algebraically determined by the spin density. Therefore T^λ_μν = 0 at ALL orders in scalar perturbations.

2. **No torsion → Holst is topological.** With T = 0, the full connection is the Levi-Civita connection. The Holst term ε^IJ_KL e^K ∧ e^L ∧ R^IJ(Γ̊) is the Pontryagin density, a topological invariant in 4D.

3. **Topological → no equations of motion.** A topological invariant is a total derivative. Its variation vanishes identically. It contributes no dynamics at any order in perturbation theory — including no cubic vertices.

4. **No cubic vertices → no bispectrum correction.** The bispectrum is determined by the cubic action. If the Holst term adds no cubic vertices, it adds nothing to f_NL.

5. **γ is invisible.** The Barbero-Immirzi parameter γ multiplies only the Holst term. Since the Holst term contributes nothing, γ drops out of all scalar observables.

### What Could Change This?

The ONLY ways to make the Holst term dynamical in the scalar sector are:

A. **Add fermions.** Fermion spin density sources torsion, making the Holst term non-topological. But the resulting torsion effects are Planck-suppressed and negligible during matter contraction.

B. **Make torsion dynamical (Poincaré gauge theory).** If torsion propagates (massive spin-2 modes), the algebraic elimination fails and torsion could contribute. But this is NOT Einstein-Cartan theory — it's a different theory entirely (and our Foundation A work showed it has the mass-coupling lock problem).

C. **Higher-derivative corrections.** If the gravitational action includes ∇T terms or R² terms, torsion can propagate and the Holst term can become dynamical. But these are UV corrections at the Planck scale, not relevant during matter contraction.

D. **Non-minimal scalar-torsion coupling.** Terms like φ²T² or φ∇T could source torsion from the scalar field. But these are ad hoc additions to the action that go beyond ECH, and our Foundation C work showed they reduce to standard scalar-tensor theory.

None of these rescue paths are viable within the ECH framework as defined.

### Does ECH Add ANYTHING to the Bispectrum?

**No.** The bispectrum is computed during the matter contraction phase, where:
- The background is standard FRW (torsion = 0)
- The perturbation equations are standard Mukhanov-Sasaki (no torsion correction)
- The cubic action is standard Maldacena (no Holst correction)
- The result is f_NL = -35/8 (independent of ECH parameters)

ECH's ONLY role is providing the bounce mechanism at high density. This affects:
- Whether the perturbations survive the bounce (assumed yes, standard in bounce cosmology)
- The normalization of the power spectrum (through the bounce transfer matrix)
- But NOT the shape function A_T or the f_NL ratio

### Comparison with LQC

In loop quantum cosmology, the situation is similar: the bispectrum from the contracting phase is generic. However, LQC provides a SPECIFIC bounce transfer matrix (computed from the effective Friedmann equation with quantum corrections). This allows LQC to make ADDITIONAL predictions:
- Bounce-generated non-Gaussianity (from the nonlinear dynamics at the bounce)
- Scale-dependent corrections to f_NL (from the finite bounce duration)
- Tensor non-Gaussianity (from quantum geometry effects)

ECH could in principle provide analogous bounce-generated corrections, but these would require a dedicated computation of perturbation theory AT the ECH bounce — not during the contraction phase.

### Verdict

**ECH_BISPECTRUM_PATH_DEAD for the contraction-phase bispectrum.**

The Holst term is inert for scalar perturbations. γ is invisible. The cubic action is unchanged. f_NL = -35/8 is purely generic.

The only ECH-specific bispectrum physics would be bounce-generated non-Gaussianity, which is a separate (and much harder) calculation.
