A matter-dominated contracting phase preceding a nonsingular bounce produces a minimally parameterized local-type non-Gaussianity f_NL = -35/8 = -4.375 (Cai et al. 2009; scalar-only matter-bounce class). The bounce-vs-inflation discrimination is dual-pronged: in the conventional local-template gauge convention the slow-roll value f_NL ~ 0.015 gives a contrast of ~290 in absolute value, while in the conformal-Fermi frame single-field slow-roll predicts f_NL -> 0 in the squeezed limit. We forecast tests of this prediction with SPHEREx (launched March 2025) and MegaMapper via the galaxy bispectrum and scale-dependent bias. We audit the Cai et al. bispectrum calculation, resolving the factor-of-two convention discrepancy with Cai & Brandenberger (2014) as an operator-ordering convention difference, and propagate a noise-weighted template-overlap correction r = 0.84 (rescaled from the Heinrich et al. 2024 multi-tracer Fisher forecast) plus a full systematic budget (epsilon-correction, photometric-z degradation, PNG bias, b_phi marginalization, relativistic projection): the realistic bispectrum-only detection significance for f_NL = -4.375 is 3-5 sigma. A separate joint (f_NL, n_fNL) scale-dependent-bias Fisher computation with documented SPHEREx public-product inputs (validated against Dore et al. 2014) gives sigma(n_fNL) = 0.30 (fixed-bias) to 0.60 (bias-marginalized): the running prediction n_fNL = 0 is testable but the SDB joint channel is subordinate to the bispectrum headline. Bayesian model comparison against tuned multifield alternatives gives BF ~ 10 at the recommended theory prior. The matter-bounce signature is decisively testable this decade.
