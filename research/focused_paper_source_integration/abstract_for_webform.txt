A matter-dominated contracting phase preceding a nonsingular bounce produces a parameter-free local-type non-Gaussianity f_NL^local = -35/8 = -4.375 (Cai et al. 2009)---approximately 300x the standard single-field inflationary prediction and opposite in sign. We forecast tests of this prediction with SPHEREx (first science data ~2028) and MegaMapper via scale-dependent bias and the galaxy bispectrum. Using Cai et al.'s intermediate vertex contributions, we derive the full-commutator shape polynomial algebraically, strengthening the case that -35/8 is the correct Planck-convention normalization. We quantify for the first time the template mismatch between the matter-bounce and local templates: a local estimator recovers 84% ± 2% of the bounce signal across all physically motivated weighting schemes (r ∈ [0.821, 0.879]; CMB Fisher signal-only r = 0.876, realistic LSS/SPHEREx noise-weighted r ≈ 0.83), validated via ℓ-space Fisher overlap, 200 injection-recovery realizations, and a 10,000-sample null-space scan. The SPHEREx multi-tracer bispectrum achieves σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.7 (Heinrich et al. 2023), giving template-corrected significance ~5-5.5σ after shape mismatch, ε-correction, photometric-z degradation, PNG bias, and relativistic projection uncertainties. MegaMapper could reach σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.5 ideally (3-7σ realistic, conditional on ultra-large-scale systematics modeling and survey funding). A Bayesian comparison over >6x10^5 Monte Carlo realizations across analytic, mock-based, and GR-aware frameworks finds that a detection near f_NL = -4.375 favors the bounce over tuned multifield competitors at Bayes factor ~8-17 (prior-dependent; see Sec. 6) and over single-field inflation at >>1. A SPHEREx null would disfavor the quasi-dust matter bounce benchmark at >4σ under assumptions (a)-(e).
