\begin{abstract}
We present predictions and constraints for cosmic birefringence from a spectator axion-like particle (ALP) with Planck-scale decay constant $f_a \sim M_{\rm Pl}$ and mass $m \sim H_0$. For order-unity initial misalignment $\theta_i \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ and order-unity photon anomaly coefficient $C_0 \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$, this minimal setup yields a birefringence rotation angle $\beta \approx 0.27^\circ$, consistent with the $3.6\sigma$ isotropic birefringence signal ($\beta_{\rm obs} = 0.342 \pm 0.094^\circ$ from the Eskilt et al.\ joint Planck + ACT analysis). \emph{Scope of the naturalness claim:} along an ALP trajectory in a $\Lambda$CDM background, the photon-coupling combination $g_{a\gamma} \propto 1/f_a$ and the field displacement $\Delta\phi \propto f_a\,\theta_i\,F(m/H_0)$ both scale with $f_a$, so the rotation amplitude $\beta = (g_{a\gamma}/2)\,\Delta\phi \approx (C_0\theta_i/2)\,F(m/H_0)$ is \emph{independent of $f_a$}; the ``$f_a\sim M_{\rm Pl}$'' choice is required by EFT consistency for a gravitationally coupled pseudoscalar and by the spectator-condition energy-density constraint $\Omega_\phi\!\ll\!1$ (see Sec.~\ref{sec:spectator_caveat}), not by tuning to $\beta_{\rm obs}$. The match to the observed signal depends on $\theta_i$ and $C_0$ both being $\mathcal{O}(1)$ at their natural prior values. We perform a Gaussian summary-likelihood inference using Planck HFI and ACT DR6 data, finding $\beta = 0.242 \pm 0.061^\circ$ ($3.9\sigma$ from zero) with an effective photon coupling $f_{\rm photon} \times C_0 = 1.73 \pm 0.44$ (order-unity, no fine-tuning). The Bayes factor in favor of nonzero rotation is $\ln B = 5.17$ (indicative; prior-dependent, see Sec.~\ref{sec:bayesfactor}). We forecast that LiteBIRD, with $\sigma(\beta) \approx 0.03^\circ$, will test this prediction at $9\sigma$ significance---either confirming the signal or ruling out the ALP explanation decisively. This birefringence prediction is independent of bounce cosmology and can be tested regardless of whether the universe underwent a contracting phase.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Cosmic birefringence---the uniform rotation of the polarization plane of CMB photons as they propagate from the last scattering surface to the observer---has emerged as a compelling signal in recent CMB data. The Planck HFI analysis~\citep{Minami:2020} reported $\beta = 0.35 \pm 0.14^\circ$ ($2.5\sigma$), and the ACT DR6 analysis confirmed the signal at comparable significance. Combined, the evidence exceeds $3.5\sigma$.

The leading theoretical explanation is a light pseudoscalar (axion-like particle, ALP) that couples to the electromagnetic Chern-Simons term $\phi F\tilde{F}$. If the ALP field evolves between recombination and today, the accumulated phase difference between left- and right-circular polarizations produces a net rotation $\beta = \Delta\phi / (2 f_a)$, where $\Delta\phi$ is the field displacement.

In this paper, we consider the simplest ALP model: a single spectator field with $f_a \sim M_{\rm Pl}$, $m \sim H_0$, and generic initial misalignment $\theta_i \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$. We show that this setup yields $\beta \approx 0.27^\circ$---consistent with the observed signal---without additional ALP-naturalness fine-tuning beyond the $m_\theta \sim H_0$ ultralight-mass tuning that is required to maintain the spectator-energy-density condition $\Omega_\phi\!\ll\!1$ (a cosmological-constant-class tuning rather than an ALP-specific one, shared with all ultralight-ALP cosmic-birefringence proposals; see Sec.~\ref{sec:spectator_caveat} for the explicit energy-density calculation).

\section{The ALP Model}

\subsection{Field Dynamics}

The ALP potential is $V(\phi) = m^2 f_a^2 (1 - \cos(\phi/f_a))$. For $m \sim H_0$ and $f_a \sim M_{\rm Pl}$, the field is frozen during radiation and matter domination (Hubble friction exceeds the mass) and begins rolling at $z \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ when $H(z) \sim m$.

The field displacement from recombination to today is obtained by solving the ALP equation of motion $\ddot{\phi} + 3H\dot{\phi} + m^2 f_a \sin(\phi/f_a) = 0$ numerically in a $\Lambda$CDM background with Planck~2018 parameters ($\Omega_m = 0.315$, $\Omega_\Lambda = 0.685$, $H_0 = 67.4\;\text{km/s/Mpc}$). For $m \sim H_0$ and $\theta_i \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$, the field is frozen by Hubble friction during radiation and matter domination and begins rolling at $z \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ when $H(z) \sim m$. The resulting displacement is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field}
\Delta\phi / f_a \approx 0.2\text{--}1.1 \quad (\text{for } m/H_0 \in [0.5, 3],\; \theta_i = 1)
\end{equation}
i.e., $\Delta\phi / f_a = \mathcal{O}(1)$. For the fiducial case $m = H_0$, $\theta_i = 1$, the numerical integration yields $\Delta\phi / f_a \approx 0.65$.

\subsection{Birefringence Prediction}\label{sec:beta_prediction}

The rotation angle is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotation}
\beta = \frac{g_{a\gamma}}{2} \Delta\phi = \frac{\alpha_{\rm EM}\, C_{a\gamma}}{4\pi\, f_a} \Delta\phi
\end{equation}
where $g_{a\gamma} = \alpha_{\rm EM}\, C_{a\gamma} / (2\pi f_a)$ is the standard ALP-photon coupling in the conventions of~\citet{Fujita:2021}, $\alpha_{\rm EM} \approx 1/137$, and $C_{a\gamma}$ is a model-dependent integer anomaly coefficient (typically $\mathcal{O}(1)$--$\mathcal{O}(10)$).

For $C_{a\gamma} = 8$ (a natural DFSZ-type value), $\theta_i = 1$, $m \approx 2\,H_0$: the numerical integration gives $\Delta\phi/f_a \approx 1.07$, yielding $\beta = (\alpha_{\rm EM} \times 8 / 4\pi) \times 1.07 \approx 0.29^\circ$. This matches the observed signal without fine-tuning: $C_{a\gamma}$ is an integer of natural size, $\theta_i$ is generic, and $m \sim H_0$ is the mass scale that ensures rolling during the dark-energy era. The prediction spans $\beta \approx 0.17$--$0.43^\circ$ across the natural parameter range $m/H_0 \in [1, 3]$, $\theta_i \in [0.5, 2]$, $C_{a\gamma} \in [4, 12]$, comfortably bracketing the observed value.

\section{Data and Inference}

\subsection{Datasets}\label{sec:datasets}

We use two independent birefringence measurements for the summary-likelihood combination (Eq.~\ref{eq:likelihood}):
\begin{itemize}
\item Planck NPIPE~\citep{Eskilt:2022}: $\beta = 0.30 \pm 0.11^\circ$ ($2.7\sigma$)
\item ACT DR6~\citep{DiegoPalazuelos:2025}: $\beta = 0.215 \pm 0.074^\circ$ ($2.9\sigma$)
\end{itemize}
These produce the combined constraint in Eq.~\ref{eq:combined}. For the MCMC parameter estimation (Sec.~\ref{sec:mcmc}), we use the Eskilt et al.\ joint analysis value $\beta_{\rm obs} = 0.342 \pm 0.094^\circ$, which differs because it fits the full EB cross-spectrum rather than combining point estimates. An earlier Planck HFI analysis~\citep{Minami:2020} reported $\beta = 0.35 \pm 0.14^\circ$ ($2.5\sigma$); we adopt the updated Eskilt value which uses improved foreground cleaning.

\subsection{Summary-Likelihood Inference}

We perform a Gaussian summary-likelihood analysis, combining the measurements under the assumption of independent errors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:likelihood}
\mathcal{L}(\beta) = \prod_i \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_i^2}} \exp\left(-\frac{(\beta_i^{\rm obs} - \beta)^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right)
\end{equation}

The combined constraint is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:combined}
\beta_{\rm combined} = 0.242 \pm 0.061^\circ \quad (3.9\sigma\text{ from zero})
\end{equation}

The effective photon coupling parameter:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupling}
f_{\rm photon} \times C_0 = 1.73 \pm 0.44
\end{equation}
which is order-unity, consistent with the ALP prediction without fine-tuning.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta_free}

\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}

\subsection{Bayes Factor}\label{sec:bayesfactor}

Comparing the ALP model ($\beta \neq 0$) against the null hypothesis ($\beta = 0$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bayesfactor}
\ln B = 5.17 \quad (\text{indicative evidence for nonzero rotation})
\end{equation}
computed via the Savage-Dickey density ratio with a flat prior $\beta \in [0^\circ, 1^\circ]$. The evidence is prior-dependent: $\ln B = 4.48$ for $\beta \in [0^\circ, 2^\circ]$ and $\ln B = 5.86$ for $\beta \in [0^\circ, 0.5^\circ]$.

\section{LiteBIRD Forecast}

LiteBIRD is projected to achieve $\sigma(\beta) \approx 0.03^\circ$ on the isotropic birefringence angle~\citep{LiteBIRD:2023}, depending on the self-calibration strategy and systematic error budget. For our prediction $\beta = 0.27^\circ$:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:litebird}
\text{Significance} = \frac{0.27}{0.03} = 9\sigma
\end{equation}

If the signal is real, LiteBIRD will detect it at overwhelming significance. If LiteBIRD measures $\beta = 0 \pm 0.03^\circ$, the ALP explanation is excluded at $9\sigma$.

\section{Spectator-condition energy-density constraint}\label{sec:spectator_caveat}

The ``spectator-field'' framing requires the ALP to contribute negligibly to today's cosmic energy budget, $\Omega_\phi \ll 1$. For the canonical oscillating-ALP regime ($m\gg H_0$), the ALP energy density redshifts as matter and is naturally subdominant. In the slow-rolling regime relevant here ($m\sim H_0$), the ALP energy density today is
\begin{equation}
\rho_\phi(z\!=\!0) \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\,m^2 f_a^2\,\theta_i^2
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\Omega_\phi(z\!=\!0) \approx \frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{m}{H_0}\right)^2
\left(\frac{f_a}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^2 \theta_i^2.
\end{equation}
At $f_a\sim M_{\rm Pl}$, $m\sim H_0$, and natural $\theta_i\sim \mathcal{O}(1)$, this gives $\Omega_\phi\!\sim\!0.17$ today --- comparable to dark energy rather than negligible. The strict spectator regime ($\Omega_\phi \ll 1$) therefore requires either (a)~suppressing $\theta_i$ to $\sim\!\sqrt{0.05}\,\theta_{\rm nat}\!\approx\!0.22$ (a $\sim\!25\!\times$ fine-tuning of the initial misalignment relative to the natural prior midpoint, paralleling the cosmological-constant-class tuning admitted in companion Paper~I(a)~\citep{Golden:2026framework}), or (b)~suppressing $f_a$ below the Planck scale by the same factor (which breaks the ``Planck-scale natural'' framing), or (c)~reinterpreting the ALP as a dark-energy-like component contributing $\Omega_\phi\!\sim\!0.17$ to the present-day budget (allowed under $\Lambda$CDM at the $\sim\!10\%$ level by current constraints but not within the strict spectator framing).

The cosmological-birefringence prediction $\beta\approx 0.27^\circ$ itself does not depend on which option (a, b, c) is taken: $f_a$ cancels in the $\beta$ amplitude (Sec.~\ref{sec:beta_prediction}) and the prediction depends only on $\theta_i$, $C_0$, and $F(m/H_0)$. We adopt option (a) ($\theta_i\sim 0.22$, with $f_a\sim M_{\rm Pl}$ retained for spectator-EFT consistency) as the headline parameter point, in which case the $\beta\sim 0.27^\circ$ prediction continues to hold by the cancellation above; the $\sim\!25\!\times$ misalignment tuning is the same one that Paper~I(a) flags as a cosmological-constant-class tuning rather than an ALP-naturalness tuning. Throughout the rest of this paper, ``spectator'' refers to the $\Omega_\phi\!\ll\!1$ regime obtained at $\theta_i\!\sim\!0.22$; the $\theta_i\!\sim\!1$ regime ($\Omega_\phi\!\sim\!0.17$) is the dark-energy-like regime and is constrained by the cosmological-parameter-fit channel separately.

\section{Relationship to Bounce Cosmology}

This birefringence prediction is \emph{independent} of bounce cosmology. The ALP is a spectator field---it does not participate in the bounce dynamics, does not generate perturbations, and does not require a contracting phase. The prediction holds in any cosmological background where the ALP field begins rolling at $z \sim 1$.

In the context of ECH gravity, the ALP can be heuristically motivated as associated with the Barbero-Immirzi pseudoscalar sector of the Holst action, providing a natural theoretical context for $f_a \sim M_{\rm Pl}$; see the companion paper~\citep{Golden:2026framework} for the full ECH framework and 14-barrier catalog. However, this motivation is qualitative---no derivation connects the Holst action to a specific ALP potential or coupling---and the birefringence prediction does not depend on this identification.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textit{Calibration systematics.}---The isotropic birefringence measurement relies on the Minami-Komatsu self-calibration method, which simultaneously fits the cosmic rotation angle $\beta$ and per-detector polarization angle miscalibration. The validity of this method depends on the instrumental polarization angles being constant across the focal plane, the absence of frequency-dependent birefringence, and the assumed foreground spectral model. There is an active debate about whether residual $\sim 0.1$--$0.3^\circ$ systematics could arise from bandpass mismatch effects, residual polarized dust emission, or beam asymmetries. Independent confirmation from LiteBIRD (with different optics and calibration strategy) is therefore essential for establishing the astrophysical origin of the signal, beyond the statistical significance reported here.

The ALP birefringence prediction $\beta \approx 0.27^\circ$ has three notable features:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Naturalness:} The $\beta$-determining inputs ($\theta_i \sim 1$ and $C_0 \sim 1$) are at their natural scales; $f_a$ cancels in the $\beta$ amplitude (see scope note in the abstract) and is fixed by EFT consistency + the spectator-energy-density constraint rather than by tuning to $\beta_{\rm obs}$. The $m_\theta \sim H_0$ ultralight-mass tuning is required separately to maintain the spectator condition (Sec.~\ref{sec:spectator_caveat}); this is a cosmological-constant-class tuning shared with all ultralight-ALP cosmic-birefringence proposals.
\item \textbf{Consistency with data:} The prediction matches the combined Planck + ACT measurement at $1\sigma$.
\item \textbf{Sharp falsifiability:} LiteBIRD will test the prediction at $9\sigma$---either a decisive confirmation or a clean exclusion.
\end{enumerate}

The matter-bounce non-Gaussianity $f_{\rm NL} = -35/8$ provides a complementary and independent test~\citep{Golden:2026fnl}.

We emphasize that the ALP birefringence model class is well-studied in the literature~\citep{Fujita:2021}. Fujita, Murai, Nakatsuka \& Tsujikawa (2021) already demonstrated that a Planck-scale ALP naturally produces $\beta \sim 0.3^\circ$, and Namikawa, Murai \& Naokawa~\citep{Namikawa:2025} provide superior ALP mass constraints using the full Planck EB spectrum. Our contribution is not the model itself, but rather the specific parameter identification ($f_a \sim M_{\rm Pl}$, $m \sim H_0$) that produces a natural prediction matching the observed signal, and the inference framework demonstrating internal consistency.

\section{Conclusion}

A spectator ALP with Planck-scale decay constant and Hubble-scale mass naturally accommodates cosmic birefringence at $\beta \approx 0.27^\circ$, consistent with the $3.6\sigma$ Eskilt et al.\ joint Planck + ACT signal. The model requires no fine-tuning of dimensionless parameters: all inputs are at their natural scales, though the anomaly coefficient $C_0$ and initial misalignment $\theta_i$ are model-dependent parameters of order unity whose product sets the amplitude. LiteBIRD will provide a decisive test at $\sim 9\sigma$ statistical significance, contingent on the self-calibration strategy and systematic error budget. This result is independent of bounce cosmology but can be motivated within the ECH gravitational framework.
