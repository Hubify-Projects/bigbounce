## What Foundation A Contributes to Paper 1.2

### 1. Strengthens the Closure Program

Foundation A provides a concrete example of what happens when you
try to fix the structural problems identified by the closure program:

- **Closure Lesson L1** (algebraic torsion washes out): PGT fixes this
  by making torsion propagate. RESOLVED.
- **Closure Lesson L2** (fixed gamma leaves no IR fingerprint): PGT
  replaces gamma with torsion-squared couplings that generate mass.
  RESOLVED.
- **Closure Lesson L5** (no scale protection): PGT does NOT fix this.
  The mass hierarchy is transferred, not solved. CONFIRMED.

This makes the closure lessons more powerful: they identify problems
that persist even in the most natural extension of the framework.

### 2. Provides a Concrete "What Would It Take" Result

Paper 1.2 can include a section showing: "Even in the most favorable
extension (PGT with propagating torsion), a geometric dark energy
mechanism requires:
1. A hierarchy |t_3| ~ 10^58 with no symmetry protection
2. Acceptance of a decoupling limit that erases distinctive signatures
3. Fine-tuning comparable to the CC problem itself"

This is a STRONG result. It quantifies what Paper 1.2 currently states
qualitatively.

### 3. Closes the "But What About PGT?" Objection

A reviewer of Paper 1.2 might ask: "You closed four specific models,
but what about propagating torsion in PGT? Isn't that the natural next
step?"

Foundation A pre-answers this: "Yes, it is the natural next step. We
investigated it. Here is what happens. The structural problems persist."

## What Foundation A Does NOT Contribute

### 1. No New Observable Prediction

Foundation A does not give Paper 1.2 a new testable prediction. The
decoupling limit kills the distinctive signatures.

### 2. No First-Principles Dark Energy

Foundation A does not derive w = -1 or any dark energy equation of state
from first principles. It shows that PGT can accommodate dark energy
(with fine-tuning) but cannot predict it.

### 3. No New Calculation for the Data Analysis

The MCMC analysis, birefringence constraints, and galaxy spin tests in
Paper 1.2 Part I are unchanged by Foundation A. The phenomenological
parameterization was never derived from PGT and Foundation A does not
change this.

Add a ~2-page section (Part III appendix or subsection 9.4) titled
"Propagating Torsion in Poincaré Gauge Theory" summarizing:
- The PGT setup (1 paragraph)

than a focused paper.

Keep Paper 1.2 as-is. Add a single paragraph in Section 9 noting:
"A systematic analysis of propagating torsion in PGT (Foundation A
program, in preparation) shows that while ghost-free torsion modes
exist with cosmologically relevant masses, the same parameter region
that produces light masses also decouples the torsion from matter,
eliminating distinctive observational signatures."

**Pros:** Clean. No structural changes to Paper 1.2.
**Cons:** The Foundation A analysis is not published, so "in preparation"
may not carry weight with reviewers.

Write Foundation A as a self-contained paper (or section of Paper 2)
and reference it from Paper 1.2.

**Pros:** Strongest scholarly approach.
**Cons:** Delays Paper 1.2 further.

paper-ready calculation. A single paragraph reference is appropriate.
