## Step 2 (cont.) — The ACTUAL Benedetti–Speziale β-function (from arXiv:1104.4028, JHEP 06(2011)107)

Verified against the source PDF (pdftotext of arXiv:1104.4028). Two regimes:

**(a) Pure gravity, off-shell scheme (§6, Eq. 6.8):**
  β_γ = μ ∂γ_R/∂μ = (4/3π) γ_R g_R,   with g_R ≡ κ²μ²/(16π).
  Fixed points γ=0 (IR-attractive) and γ=∞ (UV-attractive); γ=±1 has "no role."
  BS state explicitly this running is **scheme- and gauge-dependent, hence NOT physical.**

**(b) Gravity + fermions, physical on-shell scheme (§7, Eq. 7.24) — the physical result:**

  μ ∂γ_R²/∂μ  =  −(γ_R² − 1)² · [ μ²κ² / (8π)² ] · (23 γ_R² + 5).            (BS 7.24)

  - Driven by the **four-fermion interaction** generated by radiative corrections (∝ γ²/(γ²−1)).
  - **Sign depends on |γ|** via the (γ²−1)² and (23γ²+5) structure and the two sectors |γ|≷1
    (BS "two independent sectors, for |γ_R| larger or smaller than 1, with same UV limit and
    opposite IR limit").
  - **Only real fixed point is γ²=1** (|γ|=1), which is a UV fixed point — but it corresponds to
    a DIVERGENT four-fermion coupling, "out of the range of validity of perturbation theory."
    γ=0 and γ=∞ are NOT fixed points once fermions are present.
  - **Crucial structural feature:** the β-function is **non-autonomous** — it carries an explicit
    factor μ²κ² = (μ/M_Pl)² (up to 16π), which BS describe as "the renormalization scale measured
    in Planck units." This is NOT a log-running coupling; it is a power-suppressed Planck-scale effect.

Because β_γ² ∝ μ², the integral ∫ β_γ² d(ln μ) = ∫ β_γ² (dμ/μ) ∝ ∫ μ dμ = μ_UV²/2 is
