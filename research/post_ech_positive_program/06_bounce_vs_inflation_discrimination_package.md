## Supporting Discriminators

### Sign of f_NL
**ROBUST.** Standard slow-roll inflation gives f_NL = (5/12)(1-n_s) ≈ +0.02 (tiny positive). The matter bounce gives f_NL = -4.4 (large negative). The SIGN alone distinguishes the two.

### Maldacena Consistency Relation Violation
**ROBUST but hard to test.** In inflation: f_NL^sq = (5/12)(1-n_s). In the matter bounce: f_NL^sq = -35/8 ≠ (5/12)(1-n_s). The consistency relation is violated by a LARGE amount. This is detectable if both f_NL and n_s are measured precisely.

### Shape Information
**MODERATE.** The matter-bounce bispectrum is "loosely local" but not exactly local. The equilateral and folded amplitudes are specific:
- Equilateral: |B|_NL = -255/64 ≈ -3.98
- Folded: |B|_NL = -9/4 = -2.25

These ratios (eq/sq ≈ 0.91, fold/sq ≈ 0.51) are distinctive. With high-quality shape measurements, this could be tested — but it requires much better data than just the squeezed limit.

### Tensor-to-Scalar Ratio
**PROBLEMATIC.** The generic matter bounce predicts r ~ O(1), which is EXCLUDED by Planck + BICEP (r < 0.036). This is the most serious TENSION in the matter-bounce program.

Possible resolutions:
- A brief period of ekpyrotic contraction before the matter phase (suppresses tensors)
- Non-standard initial conditions
- Modified perturbation transfer at the bounce

This tension is a KNOWN ISSUE in the bounce literature and not specific to our program.

## What Inflation Has Trouble Mimicking

1. **Large negative f_NL:** Standard single-field slow-roll gives |f_NL| << 1. To get f_NL ~ -4, inflation needs multi-field dynamics, non-canonical kinetic terms, or other exotic mechanisms — all of which introduce free parameters and lose predictivity.

2. **Specific shape ratios:** The matter bounce gives specific equilateral-to-squeezed and folded-to-squeezed ratios. These are NOT generic for inflationary models with large f_NL.

3. **Parameter-free prediction:** The matter-bounce f_NL has NO adjustable parameters. Any inflationary model that matches it must explain WHY its parameters conspire to give -35/8.

## Weakness

**Tensor-to-scalar ratio.** If r ~ O(1) is really a generic prediction and cannot be suppressed by reasonable bounce dynamics, the matter bounce is in significant tension with current data. This is the most serious challenge to the bounce paradigm.

## Bottom Line

The discrimination package is:
1. **Primary:** f_NL = -35/8 (MegaMapper-testable, parameter-free)
2. **Supporting:** Sign of f_NL, consistency relation violation, shape ratios
3. **Challenge:** r ~ O(1) tension with r < 0.036 data

The program lives or dies on f_NL. If MegaMapper measures f_NL ≈ -4 → bounce wins. If f_NL ≈ 0 → bounce loses.
