## Branch N — Baryogenesis / Relic Production

### Strongest candidate
None survived. The closest was gravitational superheavy DM production
(Candidate C), but it is completely generic — any Planck-scale bounce
produces the same result. The torsion interaction is one of ~100
gravitational-strength channels at T ~ M_Pl, contributing ~1%.

### Why it failed
**Barrier 13 (Gravitational democracy):** At T ~ M_Pl, all
gravitational-strength interactions (including torsion (J⁵)²) have
comparable rates. The torsion channel:
- conserves B and L (cannot generate baryon/lepton asymmetry)
- is parity-even (no CPT violation)
- contributes ~1/g_* ~ 1% of any production effect
- is indistinguishable from generic gravitational scattering

## Branch O — Hidden-Sector Phase Transition

### Strongest candidate
First-order phase transition triggered by bounce curvature (Candidate A)
had genuine irreversibility and bounce-specificity. But the resulting
vacuum energy is set by hidden-sector potential parameters with zero
connection to bounce dynamics.

### Why it failed
**Barrier 13 (Bounce–vacuum energy decoupling):** The bounce determines
WHETHER a transition occurs, not WHAT vacuum energy results. The trigger
(M_Pl⁴) and the outcome (10⁻¹²² M_Pl⁴) are structurally decoupled.
No mechanism connects them without fine-tuning the hidden-sector
potential.

## New Barrier

Both branches independently identified the same structural issue,
which we label **Barrier 13** with two complementary faces:

**Face N (Gravitational democracy):** At T ~ M_Pl, torsion is one
channel among ~100 gravitational-strength interactions. It cannot
produce distinctive early-universe relics.

**Face O (Bounce–vacuum energy decoupling):** The bounce trigger and
the vacuum energy outcome are structurally decoupled. The bounce
determines WHETHER, not WHAT.

Together: the bounce is too generic at its own scale (Face N) and
too decoupled from late-time physics (Face O).
