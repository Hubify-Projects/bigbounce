## S1 — Label-resolved composition (RESOLVED, equations)

### S1.1 The two labels
The exact threading identity (threading note Eq. 2) assigns to each fluid worldline $\dot x^i=-N^i$ the number
$\delta N_c=\zeta(t_f,x_f)-\tfrac13\int\partial_iN^i\,dt$. A worldline can be **labelled** by
- its **final position** $x_f=x(t_f)$ (Eulerian label at the final comoving slice), or
- its **initial position** $x_i=x(t_i)=x_f+\xi$, $\xi^i\equiv\Delta^i(t_i)=\int_{t_i}^{t_f}N_L^i\,dt$ (Lagrangian label;
  the position of the fluid element on the initial slice where the separate-universe patches are laid out).

For the long mode $N^i_L=a^{-2}\partial_i\chi_L$, $\partial^2\chi_L=a^2\epsilon\dot\zeta_L$, so
$\xi(k_L)=-i\,\hat k_L\,\epsilon\,\zeta_L(k_L)/k_L$ at constant $\epsilon$: $k_L\xi=O(\epsilon\zeta_L)$, an $O(1)$ shift of the
label in units of the long wavelength. The two labelled fields are related by a **pure translation by a long-mode field**,
$\delta N_c^{(i)}(x_i)=\delta N_c^{(f)}(x_i-\xi(x_i))$, which at second order in $L\times S$ adds
$-\xi^j\partial_j\delta N_c^{(1)}$; its worldline companion (the displacement $\Delta(t)$ measured from $x_i$ instead of
$x_f$, `wl_initextra`) is of the same translation type.

### S1.2 Effect of the translation on the squeezed bispectrum
A translation by a *field* $\xi(x)\propto\zeta_L$ moves the two short legs by $\xi(x)$ and $\xi(y)$; the pair separation
changes by $(x-y)\!\cdot\!\nabla\xi$ — a shear (traceless part) plus a dilation (trace). In the squeezed bispectrum
(threading note Eq. 3) the $O(k_S/k_L)$ poles cancel between the legs, the trace gives $c\,(n_s-1)/3\to0$ at $n_s=1$,
and the shear gives the quadrupole: the translation term is $\propto(1-3\mu^2)$ with **zero monopole**. Summing the two
translation-type pieces (`lab_init` + `wl_initextra`, script `T_label`):
$$
T(\epsilon,\mu)\equiv f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}-f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}=\frac{5\epsilon}{4(3-\epsilon)}\,(1-3\mu^2),\qquad
\text{monopole }0\ \ (\text{all }\epsilon).\tag{S1.1}
$$

### S1.3 The map for each label and the corrected Eq. (3)
With $\lambda=1-\epsilon/3$ and $f^{\rm in\text{-}in}=\tfrac{5}{12}(\epsilon^2\mu^2-\epsilon^2+6\epsilon-12)$:
$$
f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}(\epsilon,\mu)=-\frac{5\epsilon}{4}\,(1-\mu^2),\qquad
f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}(\epsilon,\mu)=\frac{5\epsilon}{4(3-\epsilon)}\Big[(\epsilon-2)-\epsilon\,\mu^2\Big],\qquad
\text{both with monopole }-\frac{5\epsilon}{6}.\tag{S1.2}
$$
$$
\boxed{\;f_{\delta N}^{\rm init}=\frac{f^{\rm in\text{-}in}}{\lambda}+f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}=-5\ \ (\text{isotropic, every constant }\epsilon)\;}\qquad
\boxed{\;f_{\delta N}^{\rm fin}=\frac{f^{\rm in\text{-}in}}{\lambda}+f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}=-\frac{15(\epsilon-4)}{4(\epsilon-3)}+\frac{15\epsilon}{4(3-\epsilon)}\mu^2\;}\tag{S1.3}
$$
General $w$ ($\epsilon=\tfrac32(1+w)$): $f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}=\tfrac{5(1+w)}{8(w-1)}\big[1-3w+3(1+w)\mu^2\big]$,
$f_{\delta N}^{\rm fin}=\tfrac{5}{4(w-1)}\big[5-3w-3(1+w)\mu^2\big]$.

**Validation (sympy, `S1` block).** $\epsilon=3/2$: $f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}=-\tfrac{15}{8}+\tfrac{15}{8}\mu^2$,
$f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}=-\tfrac58-\tfrac{15}{8}\mu^2$; $f^{\rm in\text{-}in}/\lambda=-\tfrac{35}{8}+\tfrac{15}{8}\mu^2$;
totals $-5$ (initial) and $-\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac{15}{4}\mu^2$ (final). General $\epsilon$: initial total $\equiv-5$
(asserted symbolically); final total as in (S1.3); monopoles $-5$ for both; $T$ monopole $0$. USR corner
$\epsilon\to0$: $f_{\rm map}^{\rm init},f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin},T\to0$. Attractor ($\dot\zeta_L=0$): $\chi_L=0$, every piece
vanishes, map = identity for either label.

**What the paper's Eq. (3) did.** It composed $f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}$ with the initial-label total $-5$ of Table I. The
script confirms the printed composition evaluates to $-\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac{15}{4}\mu^2$ at $\epsilon=3/2$ (key
`printed_Eq3_composition`) — the three referees and the auditor's sympy were right; the number $-5$ was right too; the
label was mixed. Fix: print (S1.2)–(S1.3) with both labels, or print only the initial-label pair.

### S1.4 Which label the isotropic separate universe computes, and why
The separate universe evolves each patch as a homogeneous FRW region identified by its **fluid element**, i.e. by its
coordinates on the initial (flat, super-Hubble) slice; it has no shift, so it never learns where that element ends up.
Its output is therefore $\delta N_c$ **labelled by initial position** — and that is the label for which the composition
is exactly $-5$, isotropic, for all constant $\epsilon$. The final-position label is what one obtains by reading the same
field at the Eulerian point $x_f$; the two differ only by the label translation (S1.1), which has no monopole. Hence
the monopole $-5$ (and the gap $5\epsilon(9-\epsilon)/18$ to the in-in monopole) is label-independent; only the
quadrupole is a labelling convention.

## S2 — A well-defined criterion; the gradient term restored (RESOLVED, equations)

### S2.1 The reduction with the gradient term kept
Comoving gauge, first order, exact in $k$: $\partial_iN^i=\dfrac{\epsilon}{c_s^2}\dot\zeta-\dfrac{\nabla^2\zeta}{a^2H}$.
The separate universe counts e-folds of the fluid congruence from a **flat initial slice** $\Sigma_i$ to the comoving
final slice $\Sigma_f$. Splitting $N(\Sigma_i^{\rm flat}\!\to\!\Sigma_f)=N(\Sigma_i^{\rm flat}\!\to\!\Sigma_i^{\rm com})+N(\Sigma_i^{\rm com}\!\to\!\Sigma_f)$,
the first term is $\zeta_L(t_i)$ at linear order and the second is the identity integrated from $t_i$:
$$
\boxed{\;\delta N_c=\zeta_{L,f}-\frac13\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\frac{\epsilon}{c_s^2}\,\dot\zeta_L\,dt+\frac13\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\frac{\nabla^2\zeta_L}{a^2H}\,dt
=\zeta_{L,f}\Big[1-\frac{I}{3}+G\Big],\qquad
I\equiv\frac{1}{\zeta_{L,f}}\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\frac{\epsilon}{c_s^2}\,\dot\zeta_L\,dt,\quad
G\equiv-\frac{k_L^2}{3\,\zeta_{L,f}}\int_{\eta_i}^{\eta_f}\frac{\zeta_L}{\mathcal H}\,d\eta\;}\tag{S2.1}
$$
with $t_i$ any slice on which $k_L\ll a_iH_i$. $I$ is a **growth-weighted integral**, defined for every history
(the denominator is $\zeta_{L,f}$, never $\zeta_{L,f}-\zeta_{L,i}$). The note's "$\zeta$-growth-weighted mean"
$\langle X\rangle_\zeta=\int X\,d\zeta_L/(\zeta_{L,f}-\zeta_{L,i})=I\,\zeta_{L,f}/(\zeta_{L,f}-\zeta_{L,i})$ is a mean
only when $\zeta_{L,i}\neq\zeta_{L,f}$ — it is $0/0$ on the constant-mode rows, exactly as the audit found. The
criterion is a statement about $I$, not about the mean; the note's $-\infty$ lower limit with $\zeta_L(-\infty)=0$
was a growing-mode device that runs through horizon crossing, where the $G$ term is not small. **Corrected
criterion:** on super-Hubble scales, for any single-field history,
$\delta N_c=\zeta_{L,f}\,[1-I/3]+O(k_L^2/a^2H^2)$; the isotropic separate universe reproduces $\zeta$ at linear order
iff $I\to0$. "Exact for any history" becomes "exact on super-Hubble scales for any history, from a super-Hubble flat
initial slice, up to $O(k_L^2/a^2H^2)$".

### S2.2 Order and size of the dropped term (power-law backgrounds, script `S2`)
With $x=-\eta$, $a=x^p$, $p=1/(\epsilon-1)$, $\mathcal H=-p/x$, $k/aH=kx/|p|$, growing mode $\zeta_L\propto x^{-m}$,
$m=(3-\epsilon)/(\epsilon-1)$:
$$
I=\epsilon\Big[1-\Big(\frac{x_f}{x_i}\Big)^{m}\Big]\ \to\ \epsilon,\qquad
G=\frac{k_L^2\,(x_f^2x_i^{m}-x_f^{m}x_i^2)}{3p\,(m-2)\,x_i^{m}}=
\begin{cases}O\big((k_L\eta_f)^2\big)=O\big((k_L/a_fH_f)^2\big) & m>2\ (\text{e.g. dust }m=3:\ G\to\tfrac16(k_L\eta_f)^2)\\[2pt]
O\big((k_L\eta_i)^2\big)=O\big((k_L/a_iH_i)^2\big) & m<2\end{cases}\tag{S2.2}
$$
Constant mode ($m=0$, attractor or the dominant ekpyrotic mode): $I=0$ exactly and $G=k_L^2(x_i^2-x_f^2)/(6p)=O((k_L/a_iH_i)^2)$.
So $G$ is the standard gradient-expansion correction: it is $O(k_L^2/a^2H^2)$ evaluated at whichever end of the
interval dominates, never $O(k^0)$; it matters only (i) if the integral is run through horizon crossing (the note's
$-\infty$), or (ii) if the initial slice is not super-Hubble. On the constant-$\epsilon$ growing mode the $I$ term is
$O(k^0)$ and the $G$ term $O(k^2)$: the $O(1)$ statement survives unchanged.

## S3 — Discrepancy or change of variable? (the math; framing left to Houston, R3/R6)

At second order the two squeezed bispectra are related by
$B_{\delta N_c}=\lambda^3B_\zeta+\lambda^2P_L\big[\mathcal M(k_L,q)P(q)+\mathcal M(k_L,p)P(p)\big]$, i.e.
$f_{\delta N}=f^{\rm in\text{-}in}/\lambda+f_{\rm map}$ — **linear and invertible for $\lambda\neq0$** (script: inverting
the initial-label composition returns $f^{\rm in\text{-}in}$ identically and its monopole $-5(\epsilon-3)(\epsilon-6)/18$).
Therefore:
- **Variable-dependent:** the numbers. At $\epsilon=3/2$ the same physics reads $-\tfrac{35}{16}+\tfrac{15}{16}\mu^2$
  (comoving $\zeta$), $-5$ ($\delta N_c$, initial label), $-\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac{15}{4}\mu^2$ ($\delta N_c$, final label).
  None is "wrong"; each is the squeezed bispectrum of a stated gauge-invariant variable.
- **Variable-independent:** the map $(\lambda,\mathcal M)$ itself, and any observable once the variable that feeds it is
  stated. The squeezed bispectrum of comoving $\zeta$ is the observable *if* $\zeta$ is the variable matched across the
  bounce and propagated to late times (the standard assumption of the in-in and of every $\zeta$-based $f_{\rm NL}$
  forecast). $\delta N_c$ is a different variable — the e-fold count of the fluid congruence between a flat and a
  comoving slice.
- **Where the failure sits:** the standard $\delta N$ formula does not merely compute $\delta N_c$; it *identifies*
  $\delta N_c$ with $\zeta$ (Lyth–Malik–Sasaki, under $N_i=O(\nabla)$). That identification is off by $1-\lambda=I/3$ at
  linear order and by $f_{\rm map}$ at second order, and it is $O(1)$ iff $I=O(1)$. The "criterion" is precisely the
  condition $I\to0$ under which $\delta N_c=\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ and the identification is harmless. So: **no discrepancy in
  the physics (exact change of variable); an $O(1)$ error in the identification whenever $I=O(1)$.** Whether to headline
  that as "failure of the isotropic separate universe" or as "threading map with a quantified $\delta N$-usage caveat" is
  a framing decision (R3 lineup, R6 evidential strength); the math supports either wording provided the paper states
  which variable is being compared and that $\delta N_c$ is not $\zeta$ when $I=O(1)$. Not decided here: which variable is
  conserved through a given bounce — that is model-dependent and outside these gates.
