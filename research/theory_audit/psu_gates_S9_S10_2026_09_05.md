## S9 — the uniform-density slice at second order

### S9.1 Setup (what is continued, and how)

The 2026-09-04 threading note gives the fluid-congruence e-fold number as a **field**,
$N_c(t,x)=\bar N(t)+\delta N_c(t,x)$, $\delta N_c(t,x)=\zeta(t,x)-\tfrac13\int_{-\infty}^{t}\partial_iN^i\,dt'$ along the
fluid worldline through $(t,x)$ — the uniform-$\phi$ value is $N_c$ read on the coordinate slice $t=t_f$. The uniform-density
value is the *same scalar field* read on the surface $\rho(t,x)=\bar\rho(t_f)$, i.e. at $t=t_f+\delta t(x)$:
$$
\delta N_{c,\rho}(x_f)=N_c\big(t_f+\delta t(x_f),x_f\big)-\bar N(t_f)
=\delta N_c(t_f,x_f)+H\,\delta t-\tfrac{\epsilon}{2}H^2\delta t^2+\partial_t\delta N^{(1)}_c\,\delta t+O(3),\qquad(\text{S9.3})
$$
with $\partial_t\delta N^{(1)}_c=\dot\zeta-\tfrac13\partial_iN^i=\lambda\dot\zeta$ on super-Hubble scales. No label ambiguity
enters (S9.3): $N_c$ is a scalar and $\delta t$ is a surface. The initial-position label is applied afterwards exactly as in the
note (rigid translation by $\Delta_{\rm init}$ of the *full* linear $\rho$-field $\lambda'\zeta_S$, so the translation pieces scale by
$\lambda'/\lambda$; the second-order change of the endpoint, $N_L\delta t_S$, acts on the vanishing background gradient).

**The surface to second order.** In comoving gauge ($\delta\phi=0$, no field gradients) the fluid-frame density is exact,
$\rho=\dot\phi^2/(2N^2)+V$, so $\delta\rho=-\dot\phi^2\alpha+\tfrac32\dot\phi^2\alpha^2$ with $\alpha=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$,
$\alpha_2=A_2(\tau)\,\zeta_L\zeta_S$ the $L\times S$ lapse of the constraint solve. Solving $\rho(t_f+\delta t,x)=\bar\rho(t_f)$
order by order ($\bar\rho=3H^2$, $\dot{\bar\rho}=-6\epsilon H^3$):
$$
\delta t^{(1)}=-\frac{\delta\rho^{(1)}}{\dot{\bar\rho}}=-\frac{\dot\zeta}{3H^2},\qquad
\delta t^{(2)}_{LS}=-\frac{1}{\dot{\bar\rho}}\Big[-\dot\phi^2A_2+3\dot\phi^2\alpha_L\alpha_S+\delta\dot\rho_L\delta t_S+\delta\dot\rho_S\delta t_L
+\ddot{\bar\rho}\,\delta t_L\delta t_S\Big].\qquad(\text{S9.4})
$$
(S9.1) of the previous note is the first of these (asserted). The $\rho$-slice kernel is then
$M_\rho=M_\phi+M_{\rm extra}$, $M_{\rm extra}\zeta_L\zeta_S=\big[H\delta t^{(2)}-\epsilon H^2\delta t_L\delta t_S+\lambda(\dot\zeta_L\delta t_S+\dot\zeta_S\delta t_L)\big]_{t_f}$,
and the bispectrum is assembled with the linear factor of the new variable, $\lambda'\equiv\delta N_{c,\rho}/\zeta_\phi=2\lambda=2(1-\epsilon/3)$
(asserted from the same objects: $\lambda'=\lambda+H\delta t^{(1)}_S/\zeta_S$), $f^\rho=f^{\rm in\text{-}in}/\lambda'+f^\rho_{\rm map}$,
$f^\rho_{\rm map}=\tfrac{5}{12}\lambda'^{-2}[M_\rho(k_L,q)P(q)+M_\rho(k_L,p)P(p)]/P_S$.

### S9.3 Validations (all exact, sympy 1.14; script asserts)

- The frozen $\phi$-slice map is reproduced first ($f_{\rm map}=-\tfrac{5\epsilon}{4}+\tfrac{5\epsilon}{4}\mu^2$, $\lambda=1-\epsilon/3$) before any $\rho$-slice object is built.
- Linear order: $\delta t^{(1)}=-\dot\zeta/(3H^2)$ (S9.1 of the previous note) and $\lambda'=2\lambda$ (dust: $1$) recovered from the same code path.
- **Isotropy in the initial label for every constant $\epsilon$**: the $\mu^2$ coefficient of $f^\rho$ vanishes identically,
  $\tfrac{5\epsilon^2}{8(3-\epsilon)}$ (in-in$/\lambda'$) $+\tfrac{5\epsilon^2}{8(\epsilon-3)}$ (map) $=0$. This is a non-trivial check of
  $M_{\rm extra}$'s quadrupole — the separate-universe variable labelled by initial position must be isotropic, and it is only because the
  $A_2$/$\delta t^{(2)}$ terms carry exactly the right $\mu^2$ piece.
- Every $1/k_L$ pole cancels between the two short legs in all three assemblies (asserted in `f_of`).
- **Attractor** ($m\to0$, constant $\zeta$): $\delta t^{(1)}=0$, $A_2|_{k^0}=0$, $M_{\rm extra}=0$ — the two slices coincide and the map is the identity.
- **$\epsilon\to0$ at fixed $\dot\zeta/\zeta$** (USR-type): $M_{\rm extra}\to0$ and $\lambda'\to2$: the slices still differ at $O(1)$ at linear order
  (as S9.2 of the previous note found) but the *second-order* $\rho$-extra vanishes with $\epsilon$, like every other cross kernel.

### S9.4 The composition against the lab's $\delta N$ value — **does not close**

The lab's isotropic separate-universe integration on uniform-density slices (`fnl_matter_contraction_second_method_2026_09_02.md`,
$f^{\delta N}_{\rm NL}=5(\epsilon-7)/8=-55/16$ at dust) and the threading of the in-in result onto the same slice with the same label give
$$
f^{\rho}_{\rm threading}-f^{\delta N}_{\rm lab}=\frac{5(2\epsilon-15)}{24}-\frac{15(\epsilon-7)}{24}=\frac{5(6-\epsilon)}{24}
\;=\;\frac{15}{16}\ \text{at }\epsilon=\tfrac32\qquad(-\tfrac52\ \text{vs}\ -\tfrac{55}{16}).\qquad(\text{S9.5})
$$
So the third variable is **not** the same physics by this computation: the $\phi$-slice initial-label value ($-5$, previous note) matched
the Cai-lane $\delta N$ number, but the $\rho$-slice value ($-\tfrac52$) matches neither Cai's $-5$ nor the lab's $-\tfrac{55}{16}$. Two
things are worth recording rather than resolving here: (i) the $\rho$-slice **final**-label quadrupole is exactly $\tfrac{15}{8}\mu^2$ at dust,
the Bianchi-I lane-(C) quadrupole that the previous note flagged as matching neither $\phi$-label; (ii) $5(6-\epsilon)/24$ vanishes at
$\epsilon=6$, not at any physical point, so it is not a normalisation slip of a single factor.

**Candidate missing step (named, not asserted).** The threading map is an exact change of variable applied to the full in-in bispectrum,
which includes the intrinsic non-Gaussianity of the field perturbation at *every* time; the lab's $\delta N$ integration takes the
initial flat-slice data $(u_i,s_i)$ Gaussian. On a power-law background the ratio $\langle u^3\rangle/\langle u^2\rangle^2$ is
self-similar and need not vanish as $t_i\to-\infty$, so the $\delta N$ route may be missing an intrinsic term of exactly this
kind — the standard caveat of $\delta N$ in non-attractor phases (Namjoo–Firouzjahi–Sasaki 2012; literature, not verified here).
Deciding between "intrinsic initial-data NG omitted in the $\delta N$ integration" and "an error in one of the two computations"
requires the flat-slice in-in three-point function of $u$ at finite $t_i$ on this background — a new mode-function computation.
