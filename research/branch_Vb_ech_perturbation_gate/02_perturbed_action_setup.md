## The ECH Action in First-Order Form

The Einstein-Cartan-Holst action is:

$$
S_{\rm ECH} = \frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}\int \left(e^a \wedge e^b \wedge R_{ab} + \frac{1}{2\gamma}\,\epsilon_{abcd}\,e^a \wedge e^b \wedge R^{cd}\right)
$$

or equivalently in component form:

$$
S_{\rm ECH} = \frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}\int d^4x\, e\, e^\mu_a\, e^\nu_b \left(R^{ab}{}_{\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{2\gamma}\,\epsilon^{ab}{}_{cd}\, R^{cd}{}_{\mu\nu}\right)
$$

where:
- $e^a_\mu$ is the tetrad (vierbein), $e = \det(e^a_\mu)$
- $\omega^{ab}_\mu$ is the spin connection (independent variable in first-order formalism)
- $R^{ab}{}_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \omega^{ab}_\nu - \partial_\nu \omega^{ab}_\mu + \omega^a{}_{c\mu}\omega^{cb}_\nu - \omega^a{}_{c\nu}\omega^{cb}_\mu$ is the curvature of $\omega$
- $\gamma = 0.274$ is the Barbero-Immirzi parameter
- The first term is the Palatini action; the second is the Holst term

### Matter sector

For the dust contraction phase, we model matter as a scalar field φ:

$$
S_\phi = -\int d^4x\, e\, \left[\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi + V(\phi)\right]
$$

where $g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{ab}e^\mu_a e^\nu_b$.

**Critical point:** The scalar field does not couple to the spin connection $\omega^{ab}_\mu$ directly — only through the metric (via the tetrad). Scalars have no spin current.

For completeness, if fermions are present (relevant for the actual bounce where spin-torsion coupling generates the ρ² term):

$$
S_\psi = \int d^4x\, e\, \bar{\psi}\left(i\gamma^a e^\mu_a D_\mu - m\right)\psi
$$

where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + \frac{1}{4}\omega^{ab}_\mu \gamma_{ab}$ is the covariant derivative with spin connection. **Fermions couple directly to ω.**

## Background: FRW

On the FRW background:

$$
\bar{e}^0_0 = 1, \quad \bar{e}^i_j = a(t)\delta^i_j
$$

$$
\bar{\omega}^{0i}_j = H a\, \delta^i_j, \quad \bar{\omega}^{ij}_k = 0
$$

The background torsion is:

$$
\bar{T}^a_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \bar{e}^a_\nu - \partial_\nu \bar{e}^a_\mu + \bar{\omega}^a{}_{b\mu}\bar{e}^b_\nu - \bar{\omega}^a{}_{b\nu}\bar{e}^b_\mu
$$

For FRW with scalar field matter only: **the background torsion vanishes** ($\bar{T}^a_{\mu\nu} = 0$) because scalars don't source torsion. The background spin connection equals the Levi-Civita connection.

**Wait — this contradicts the modified Friedmann equation.** How does the ρ² correction arise if background torsion vanishes for scalar fields?

### Resolution: the ρ² correction comes from the fermionic sector

The modified Friedmann equation H² = (ρ/3M²)(1 − ρ/ρ_crit) arises from the spin-torsion coupling of **fermions**, not from scalar fields. In the cosmological bounce scenario:

1. The matter content includes fermions (quarks, leptons) whose spin current sources torsion
2. On the FRW background, the spin-averaged contribution of a thermal bath of fermions produces an effective energy-momentum correction proportional to ρ²
3. This ρ² term is what modifies the Friedmann equation and generates the bounce

For a pure scalar field on the ECH background: **there is no ρ² correction.** The scalar field sees standard GR. The bounce does not occur.

**This is a fundamental issue for Branch V:** If the dust phase is modeled as a scalar field (which is standard for the matter bounce), the scalar field does not source torsion, and the ECH correction is absent for the perturbations of that field.

The resolution in practice is that the bounce occurs due to the **total** energy density (including fermionic contributions) reaching ρ_crit, while the perturbation spectrum is generated by the scalar field during contraction (when ρ ≪ ρ_crit and the torsion correction is negligible anyway).

## Perturbation Variables

### Tetrad perturbations

$$
e^a_\mu = \bar{e}^a_\mu + \delta e^a_\mu
$$

For scalar perturbations in longitudinal gauge:

$$
\delta e^0_0 = \Psi, \quad \delta e^0_i = a\,\partial_i B, \quad \delta e^i_0 = 0, \quad \delta e^i_j = a\,\Phi\,\delta^i_j
$$

(The tetrad has more degrees of freedom than the metric — 16 vs 10 — but the extra 6 correspond to local Lorentz transformations that can be gauge-fixed.)

### Connection perturbations

$$
\omega^{ab}_\mu = \bar{\omega}^{ab}_\mu + \delta\omega^{ab}_\mu
$$

The connection perturbation $\delta\omega^{ab}_\mu$ has 24 independent components. It decomposes into:
- **Riemannian part** (determined by the tetrad perturbation via metric compatibility + zero torsion)
- **Contorsion part** (sourced by torsion perturbations)

$$
\delta\omega^{ab}_\mu = \delta\omega^{ab}_\mu\big|_{\rm LC} + \delta K^{ab}_\mu
$$

where $\delta K^{ab}_\mu$ is the contorsion perturbation.

### Torsion perturbations

$$
\delta T^a_{\mu\nu} = \delta\omega^a{}_{b\mu}\bar{e}^b_\nu - \delta\omega^a{}_{b\nu}\bar{e}^b_\mu + \bar{\omega}^a{}_{b\mu}\delta e^b_\nu - \bar{\omega}^a{}_{b\nu}\delta e^b_\mu + \text{(tetrad pieces)}
$$

The torsion tensor has 24 independent components, decomposing into three irreducible parts under the Lorentz group:
- **Trace vector** $T_\mu = T^\nu{}_{\mu\nu}$ (4 components)
- **Axial vector** $S_\mu = \epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}T^{\nu\rho\sigma}$ (4 components, pseudovector)
- **Tensor part** $q^a_{\mu\nu}$ (16 components, traceless)

For scalar perturbations, only the scalar parts of $T_\mu$ and $S_\mu$ contribute.

## Gauge Choices

We work in **longitudinal (Newtonian) gauge** for the metric sector:
- $B = 0$ (no off-diagonal scalar metric perturbation)
- Local Lorentz gauge: fix the antisymmetric part of $\delta e^{[a}_\mu \bar{e}^{b]\mu} = 0$

The remaining physical scalar perturbations are:
- $\Psi$ (lapse perturbation)
- $\Phi$ (spatial curvature perturbation)
- $\delta\phi$ (scalar field perturbation)
- Scalar parts of torsion: $\delta T_0$ (trace-time), $\delta T_i = \partial_i \mathcal{T}$ (trace-spatial), $\delta S_0$ (axial-time), $\delta S_i = \partial_i \mathcal{S}$ (axial-spatial)

## Key Structural Observation

**The scalar field φ does not couple to torsion.** Its action depends only on the metric (via the tetrad), not on the connection. Therefore:

1. The scalar field equation of motion is the standard Klein-Gordon equation on the (perturbed) metric.
2. The scalar field perturbation $\delta\phi$ does not source torsion perturbations.
3. Torsion perturbations, if present, are sourced only by spinning matter (fermions).

**If the matter sector during dust contraction is a scalar field, the torsion perturbations are zero.** The connection perturbation reduces to the Levi-Civita connection of the perturbed metric, and the perturbation action reduces to the standard GR result.

This is the critical finding that determines the gate.
