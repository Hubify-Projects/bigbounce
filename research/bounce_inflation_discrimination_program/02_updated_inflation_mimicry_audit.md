# 02: Updated Inflation Mimicry Audit

## The Core Question

For each bounce prediction, can inflation reproduce it without exotic engineering?

## Observable-by-Observable Assessment

### f_NL^local = -4.375

**Single-field slow-roll:** f_NL = (5/12)(1-n_s) ≈ 0.015. Off by factor 300 and wrong sign. **CANNOT mimic.**

**Standard curvaton:** f_NL = 5/(4r_dec) - 5r_dec/6 - 5/3. Minimum f_NL ≈ -1.25 (at r_dec → 1). **CANNOT reach -4.375.**

**Self-interacting curvaton:** Could reach -4.375 with negative self-coupling λ < 0 (unstable potential). **Requires exotic engineering.** Not a natural model.

**DBI inflation:** Produces equilateral-shape f_NL, NOT local. Different observable channel. **Cannot mimic the local squeezed signal.**

**Multi-field (general):** Can produce negative local f_NL in principle via curvature of field space or isocurvature conversion. But:
- Requires specific field-space geometry (not generic)
- Typically has additional observables (isocurvature) that break the degeneracy
- The value -4.375 with NO free parameters is extremely hard to engineer

**EFT of inflation / excited states:** Initial state modifications (Bogoliubov coefficients) can produce non-Gaussianity, but typically equilateral or folded, not local. **Unlikely to mimic local f_NL = -4.375.**

**VERDICT: f_NL = -4.375 is the HARDEST prediction for inflation to mimic.** Inflation CAN produce negative local f_NL, but only through contrived multi-field or self-interacting models that introduce free parameters. The PARAMETER-FREE nature of the bounce prediction is what makes it distinctive.

### Sign of f_NL (negative)

**Single-field:** f_NL ≈ +0.015 (positive). **Opposite sign.**
**Standard curvaton:** f_NL > 0 for r_dec < 0.9 (typical). **Mostly positive.**
**Multi-field (general):** Both signs possible. **Not uniquely discriminating by sign alone.**

**VERDICT:** Negative local f_NL is unusual in inflation but not impossible. The sign combined with the magnitude is the real discriminator.

### n_s = 0.964

**VERDICT: TRIVIALLY mimicked.** Every serious inflation model matches n_s = 0.964.

### r ~ 10⁻⁴

**VERDICT: EASILY mimicked.** Small-field inflation models naturally predict r ~ 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻¹⁰.

### Shape ratios (equil/sq = 0.91, fold/sq = 0.51)

**VERDICT: POTENTIALLY distinctive** but requires very high-precision shape measurement. Currently beyond observational reach.

### Consistency relation violation

**VERDICT: STRONGLY discriminating IF f_NL is detected.** If f_NL = -4.375 and n_s = 0.964, then f_NL / [(5/12)(1-n_s)] = -4.375 / 0.015 ≈ -300. This enormous violation is IMPOSSIBLE in single-field inflation and requires exotic multi-field constructions in any inflationary model.

## Mimicry Difficulty Ranking

| Observable | Inflation Mimicry Difficulty |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| f_NL = -4.375 (parameter-free) | **VERY HARD** (requires exotic multi-field) |
| Consistency relation violation | **VERY HARD** (impossible in single-field) |
| Sign(f_NL) = negative | MODERATE (unusual but possible) |
| Shape ratios | UNKNOWN (insufficient data) |
| n_s = 0.964 | TRIVIAL |
| r ~ 10⁻⁴ | TRIVIAL |

## The Bottom Line

**The SINGLE hardest-to-mimic prediction is f_NL = -4.375 as a parameter-free local-type non-Gaussianity.** Inflation can produce it only through exotic engineering. The combination of:
- Large magnitude (~300× slow-roll)
- Negative sign (opposite to standard curvaton)
- Parameter-free (no tuning knobs)
- Local shape (not equilateral or folded)

creates a distinctive signature that would be very difficult to replicate in any natural inflationary scenario.

The one caveat: a sufficiently contrived multi-field inflation model with engineered field-space curvature COULD produce f_NL ≈ -4. But such a model would introduce free parameters and lose the predictive power that the bounce achieves naturally.
