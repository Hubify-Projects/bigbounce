## Key Scenario: MegaMapper Detects f_NL = -4.375 ± 0.5

| Bounce vs... | Bayes Factor | Interpretation |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| Standard single-field inflation | **5.5 × 10¹⁶** | DECISIVE |

| Tuned multifield (prior [-15,+15]) | **23.9** | STRONG |

## The Hierarchy Is Clear

**Against standard models (SSFSR, standard curvaton):** The bounce wins DECISIVELY — Bayes factors of 10⁵ to 10¹⁶. These models simply cannot produce f_NL = -4.375, so any detection crushes them.

**Against the best exotic loopholes (non-attractor, tuned multifield):** The bounce wins STRONGLY — Bayes factors of 10-25. This is the Occam penalty: these models CAN produce -4.375, but only by tuning free parameters over a wide prior range. The bounce predicts it exactly with zero parameters.

## The Critical Insight

**Even against the WEAKEST competitor (tuned multifield with flat prior [-15,+15]), the bounce wins at 17:1 for SPHEREx and 24:1 for MegaMapper.** This is entirely from the Occam penalty — the multifield model wastes prior probability across the wide range [-15,+15] while the bounce concentrates all its probability at the correct value.

For the NARROWEST sensible prior ([-5,+5]), the advantage drops to 7:1 — still moderate. For very broad priors ([-50,+50]), it rises to 57:1. The Occam argument is ROBUST to reasonable prior choices.

## When the Bounce Loses

If f_NL ≈ 0 is measured, ALL competitors crush the bounce (Bayes factor << 1). This is correct — a null result kills the bounce hypothesis regardless of the comparison framework.

If f_NL ≈ -6 (overshooting), the bounce is mildly favored against SSFSR but INCONCLUSIVE against tuned multifield. This makes sense — -6 is NOT the bounce prediction, so the bounce doesn't get the Occam advantage.
