# Publication Packaging Options

How to package the research program into publishable papers.

---

## Option 1 — One Mega Synthesis Paper

**Title concept:** "Spin-Torsion Bounce Cosmology: A Systematic Assessment of Dark Energy Derivation, Observable Signatures, and Surviving Predictions"

### What goes in
- Part I: ECH framework and bounce mechanism (4pp)
- Part II: Structural closure — 13 barriers across 15 branches (8pp)
- Part III: ALP birefringence prediction and MCMC constraints (6pp)
- Part IV: Forward-looking — Branch V matter bounce prospects (2pp)
- Appendices: derivation details, claims table, reproducibility (6pp)
- Total: ~26-30 pages

### Strengths
- Tells the complete story in one place
- Closure + positive result in same paper is intellectually honest and compelling
- Reviewers see the full scope of effort
- Single authoritative reference for the program

### Weaknesses
- Very long for a single paper; may exceed typical journal limits
- Mixed reception: reviewers may not know whether it's a theory paper, phenomenology paper, or review
- The positive result (ALP birefringence) risks being buried under 8 pages of negative results
- The closure program and ALP prediction serve different audiences

### Novelty profile
- Closure: N3 (systematic barrier map)
- ALP: N2 (known phenomenology, new MCMC implementation)
- Combined: N2-N3

### Publication readiness
- 70% — claims locked, figure plan exists, but writing not started
- Estimated 3-4 weeks to submission-ready draft

### Emotional / strategic fit
- Satisfying as a capstone but may feel like an admission of failure if the closure dominates
- Risk of "they tried to derive dark energy and couldn't" framing by readers

## Option 2 — Closure Paper + ALP Phenomenology Paper

### Paper 2A: Structural Closure

**Title concept:** "Why Spin-Torsion Bounces Cannot Derive Dark Energy: A Systematic Barrier Analysis"

**Content:**
- ECH framework (3pp)
- 13 structural barriers with formal statements (10pp)
- Implications for modified gravity DE programs (2pp)
- Appendix: detailed derivations (4pp)
- Total: ~19 pages

**Strengths:** Clean focus; negative-result paper with genuine intellectual content; broad applicability beyond ECH
**Weaknesses:** Hard to publish pure negative results; needs strong framing as "saving others time"
**Novelty:** N3 — barrier map is original
**Venue:** PRD, CQG, JCAP
**Readiness:** 60% — barrier catalog exists but needs unification into paper prose

### Paper 2B: ALP Birefringence

**Title concept:** "Cosmic Birefringence from a Planck-Scale ALP: Predictions, MCMC Constraints, and LiteBIRD Forecasts"

**Content:**
- ALP model specification (2pp)
- Birefringence prediction derivation (2pp)
- MCMC methodology and results (4pp)
- LiteBIRD forecast (2pp)
- Discussion: ECH motivation, comparison with literature (2pp)
- Total: ~12-14 pages

**Strengths:** Focused positive result; testable prediction; LiteBIRD timeline gives urgency; matches existing literature conversation
**Weaknesses:** Not unique to ECH (any Planck ALP gives same prediction); crowded field (Fujita et al., Obata, Eskilt)
**Novelty:** N2 — implementation and MCMC are new; prediction is known
**Venue:** JCAP, PRD, possibly PRL (if MCMC results are sharp enough)
**Readiness:** 50% — Phase 2 MCMC needs completion; figures need generation

### Combined assessment
- Cleaner separation of audiences
- Closure paper may be hard to place without a positive result as companion
- ALP paper competes directly with existing literature unless differentiated by MCMC quality or LiteBIRD forecast precision

**Option 3A — the current Paper 1.2 framework paper.**

Reasons:

- Claims are locked (`research/final_phase/03_claims_lock.md`)

- Balances closure and positive result honestly
- Can be written in 2-3 focused sessions
- Does not depend on any future calculation succeeding

### Strongest "smaller focused paper" candidate right now

**Paper 2B — ALP birefringence standalone.**

Reasons:
- Branch R Phase 2 MCMC is in progress
- Prediction matches data at 1-sigma
- LiteBIRD falsifiable within 5 years
- Focused, testable, timely
- Lower barrier to publication than closure paper

### Recommended strategy

1. **Immediate:** Write and submit Paper 3A (the framework paper with compact closure + ALP + MCMC). This is the main deliverable. Target: PRD or JCAP.

2. **Parallel:** Complete Branch R Phase 2 MCMC. If results are publication-quality, either fold into Paper 3A or publish as standalone Paper 2B.

3. **Near-term:** Compile Paper 3B (companion technical note with full barrier catalog). Post to arXiv as companion to Paper 3A. Low effort if barrier derivations are compiled from existing branch results.

4. **Medium-term:** If Branch V Phase 1 succeeds, write Paper 4 (matter bounce + ECH). This would be the highest-impact paper in the program. But do not delay Paper 3A for this.

These branches consumed significant time but are unlikely to contribute to any publication:

|--------|--------------|------------|--------|
| Paper 1 original claims (H_0 = 69.2, tension reduction) | Weeks | Based on unverified MCMC with SH0ES prior contamination | Verify MCMC independently before claiming results |

| Branch W (curvaton tilt) | Days | Superseded by Branch V immediately after opening | Check whether the question has already been answered |
| Next-gen signals assessment | Days | Confirmed expected result (Planck-scale signals are Planck-scale) | Don't invest in systematic assessment of obvious conclusions |
| Galaxy spin dipole analysis | Weeks (early phase) | 9-12 OOM coupling gap; data provenance issues | Order-of-magnitude estimate before detailed analysis |

**Note:** These are characterized as "wasted" in hindsight only. Several (particularly the galaxy spin work and original Paper 1 claims) were reasonable starting points that required investigation to disconfirm. The lesson is about efficiency, not judgment.
