# Branch J: Bounce-Triggered Dark-Sector State Selection

**Date:** 2026-03-16

---

## Why All Previous Routes Failed

### Foundations A–G: Direct derivation closed

Seven structural barriers prevent the spin-torsion bounce from
PRODUCING dark energy:

1. Mass-coupling lock (A) — propagating torsion
2. Topological-shift duality (B) — pseudoscalar protection
3. Scalar-tensor universality (C) — FRW reduction
4. Planck suppression (D) — connection coupling
5. Scale separation (E) — global integrals
6. Attractor-sensitivity dilemma (F) — initial conditions
7. Parameter immunity (G) — vacuum selection

The bounce cannot derive Λ, w(z), or any DE equation of state.

### Branch H: Tensor signatures closed

8. Parity-even effective interaction — tensor chirality

- P_T ~ 10⁻⁶⁴ (unobservable at all frequencies)
- Δχ = 0 exactly (no parity violation in minimal EC on FRW)
- Tensor sector fully exhausted

### Branch I: Generic DE compatibility is weak

- 4/6 Horndeski classes trivially compatible (DE frozen at bounce)
- 2/6 have EFT breakdowns (not stability failures)
- Scale separation (ρ_DE/ρ_crit ~ 10⁻¹²²) dominates
- The bounce and DE are "ships passing in the night"

### The common thread: energy-budget reasoning fails

All previous approaches implicitly asked: does the bounce
ENERGETICALLY produce or constrain DE? The answer is no, because
the 122-order-of-magnitude hierarchy between bounce and DE
energy scales prevents any interesting energy-level interplay.

## Why Branch J Is Logically Different

Branch J asks an EVENT-BASED question:

> Does the bounce, as a specific dynamical event (H → 0 → H,
> Ḣ ~ M_Pl², R ~ M_Pl²), SELECT or PREPARE the state of an
> independently existing dark sector?

The key distinction:

| Previous approaches | Branch J |
|---|---|
| Bounce PRODUCES DE energy | Bounce SELECTS DE state |
| Energy budget must match | State selection is discrete/topological |
| Suppressed by ρ_DE/ρ_bounce | Could be O(1) effect on state label |
| Continuous quantity (Λ) | Discrete/angular quantity (branch, θ) |

The analogy: a coin flip doesn't create energy, but it selects
a state (heads/tails). The bounce might "flip a coin" in the
dark sector even though it doesn't contribute DE energy.

## What Counts as State Selection

### Types of state selection that could work

1. **Vacuum branch selection** — A dark sector with multiple
   degenerate or near-degenerate vacua. The bounce selects which
   vacuum the universe ends up in. The selection is discrete
   (branch label, not continuous field value).

2. **Symmetry restoration and re-breaking** — The Planck-scale
   curvature at the bounce restores a dark-sector symmetry that
   is broken at low energy. After the bounce, the symmetry
   re-breaks. The bounce determines the pattern of re-breaking.

3. **pNGB misalignment setting** — An axion-like DE field has a
   misalignment angle θ = φ/f. The bounce, through curvature
   coupling, drives θ toward a specific value. The post-bounce
   θ determines the late-time ρ_DE.

4. **Metastable vacuum trapping** — A dark sector has multiple
   local minima with different cosmological constants. The bounce
   deforms the effective potential (via curvature coupling),
   trapping the field in a specific minimum. After the bounce,
   the field remains trapped.

5. **Nonadiabatic excitation / occupation selection** — The
   bounce is a nonadiabatic event (timescale ~ t_Pl). It creates
   specific occupation numbers in a dark sector. If the ground
   state occupation determines late-time behavior, the bounce
   sets the DE state.

### What does NOT count

- **Arbitrary initial conditions** — Simply saying "the field
  starts at φ_i after the bounce" with no mechanism to determine
  or narrow φ_i. This is Foundation F (attractor-sensitivity
  dilemma) recycled.

- **Generic spectator-field behavior** — A DE field that is
  merely present during the bounce but unaffected by it. This
  is Branch I (trivially compatible).

- **Fine-tuned coupling** — A mechanism that requires coupling
  constants tuned to the DE scale (g ~ H₀/M_Pl ~ 10⁻⁶⁰) without
  symmetry protection.

## Success Criteria for Branch J

A viable mechanism must satisfy ALL of:

### S1. Event strength
The bounce must actually CHANGE the dark-sector state. The
interaction coupling × bounce scale must exceed the dark-sector
potential barrier or energy splitting. A purely negligible
perturbation fails.

### S2. Non-collapse to arbitrary initial conditions
The post-bounce state must NOT be a generic function of the
pre-bounce state. There must be NARROWING: a range of pre-bounce
states must map to a smaller range of post-bounce states. Ideally,
an ATTRACTOR: all pre-bounce states map to the same post-bounce
state.

### S3. Technical naturalness
The late-time dark sector must be technically natural. The bounce
coupling must not introduce fine-tuning problems worse than the
existing cosmological constant problem.

### S4. Predictive narrowing
The bounce must determine or significantly constrain at least one
late-time observable: ρ_DE, w(z), θ_i, branch label, or another
measurable quantity. "The bounce did something but we can't measure
what" is not useful.

### S5. Late-time viability
The resulting state must actually produce acceptable dark energy
(w ≈ -1, ρ_DE ~ 10⁻¹²² M_Pl⁴, no ghost, no gradient instability).

## Failure Criteria

- **FAIL_TOO_WEAK** — The bounce coupling to the dark sector is
  negligible (suppressed by scale separation, Planck suppression,
  or other hierarchy).

- **FAIL_ARBITRARY_INITIAL_CONDITIONS** — The post-bounce state
  depends sensitively on the pre-bounce state with no narrowing.
  The bounce is decorative, not functional.

- **FAIL_NO_PROTECTION** — The mechanism requires fine-tuned
  couplings or potentials that are not technically natural.

- **FAIL_NO_NARROWING** — The bounce modifies the state but
  doesn't constrain any observable. Many post-bounce states are
  equally viable.

- **FAIL_SPECTATOR_COLLAPSE** — The mechanism reduces to
  "DE field sits there during the bounce unchanged." This is
  Branch I, already assessed.

## The Central Tension

Branch J faces an inherent tension:

**For the bounce to affect the dark sector:**
The coupling must be strong enough at Planck curvature to
change the state. This requires:
  coupling × R_bounce ≳ V_barrier (potential barrier in dark sector)

**For the dark sector to be technically natural:**
The coupling must not generate radiative corrections that
destabilize the DE potential. This requires:
  coupling ≲ V_DE / M_Pl² ~ 10⁻¹²² (for direct mass corrections)

**The tension:** Strong enough to act, weak enough to be natural.

This tension has a POSSIBLE resolution: if the coupling is to
curvature (ξRφ²) with ξ ~ O(1), it is:
- Strong at bounce: ξR ~ M_Pl² ≫ V_DE
- Weak at late times: ξR ~ H₀² ~ V_DE/M_Pl² (negligible correction)
- Technically natural: ξ ~ O(1) is radiatively stable

The curvature coupling is the ONLY coupling that is both
strong at the bounce and naturally weak at late times, thanks
to the 10¹²² drop in R between the bounce and today.

**This singles out curvature coupling as the unique viable
channel for bounce state selection.**
