# Foundation Map v2 — Bounce-First Cosmology

**Date:** 2026-03-14

---

## Context

Foundations A–D established that local geometric dark-energy mechanisms
from spin-torsion cosmology are structurally blocked. The program now
pivots to mechanisms that are **compatible with** (rather than derived
from) the spin-torsion bounce.

---

## Foundation E — Global / Nonlocal Vacuum Mechanisms

### Mechanism
Control vacuum energy through global constraints, nonlocal action
terms, or auxiliary sectors that enforce cancellation without local
fine-tuning. Examples: vacuum energy sequestering (Kaloper-Padilla),
4-form flux adjustment, global Lagrange multiplier constructions.

### Why it avoids A–D
- **Not a local propagating field** → no mass-coupling lock (A)
- **Not a pseudoscalar coupled to a 4-form** → no topological-shift duality (B)
- **Not a scalar-tensor EFT on FRW** → evades universality collapse (C)
- **Not coupled through the connection** → no Planck suppression of distinctive effects (D)

### Biggest theoretical risk
Hidden fine-tuning relocation: the mechanism may appear to solve the
CC problem while secretly moving the tuning to boundary conditions,
initial conditions, or the choice of global variables.

### Possible observable consequence
Modified vacuum energy evolution across cosmological eras. Potential
relation between bounce-era parameters and late-time Λ. Constraints
on the number of phase transitions contributing to vacuum energy.

---

## Foundation F — Bounce-Prepared Late-Time DE Sector

### Mechanism
A separate late-time sector (quintessence, ALP, or other) whose
parameters, initial conditions, or symmetry-breaking pattern are
determined or constrained by the bounce phase. The bounce acts as
a cosmic "preparation" mechanism rather than a direct DE source.

### Why it avoids A–D
- DE sector is **not derived from torsion** → no lock (A)
- Mass protection comes from the **DE sector's own symmetry** → no duality issue (B)
- The sector is acknowledged as **generic EFT** → universality (C) is accepted, not fought
- Geometric content enters through **initial conditions**, not coupling → suppression (D) irrelevant

### Biggest theoretical risk
Arbitrariness: the "link" between bounce and DE sector may be too weak
to be meaningful. If any DE sector is compatible with any bounce, the
connection is vacuous.

### Possible observable consequence
Correlations between bounce-era observables (CMB anomalies, tensor
modes from bounce) and DE properties (w, equation of state evolution).
Specific initial conditions for DE field set by bounce energy scale.

---

## Foundation G — Bounce-Driven Vacuum Selection

### Mechanism
The bounce phase itself selects the vacuum through dynamical processes:
bubble nucleation during contraction, landscape selection by bounce
energy scale, or torsion-driven phase transitions that fix the vacuum
sector before the expanding phase begins.

### Why it avoids A–D
- Selection happens **at the bounce**, not during late-time evolution → different energy scale
- No need for light propagating geometric modes → no lock (A)
- Selection mechanism is **non-perturbative** → evades EFT arguments (C, D)

### Biggest theoretical risk
Requires a landscape or vacuum multiplicity, which is speculative.
The selection mechanism may not be calculable. May reduce to anthropic
reasoning.

### Possible observable consequence
Discrete predictions for Λ from bounce energy scale. Possible
correlations between bounce-era curvature and late-time vacuum.
Constraints on the number of accessible vacua.

---

## Foundation H — Non-Homogeneous Geometric Signatures (Secondary)

### Mechanism
Abandon dark energy as the primary target. Instead, seek geometric
signatures that are distinctive in perturbations, propagation,
screening, or dense environments — places where FRW universality
does not apply.

### Why it avoids A–D
- **Not on FRW background** → universality (C) does not apply
- **Perturbation-level effects** → may have different suppression scaling
- **Dense environments** → torsion effects enhanced, not Planck-suppressed

### Biggest theoretical risk
Effects may be too small or too local to be cosmologically relevant.
May already be excluded by GW170817, fifth-force, or solar system tests.

### Possible observable consequence
Modified GW propagation, parity-dependent fermion effects in dense matter,
neutron star structure modifications, screening phenomenology distinct
from generic scalar-tensor.
