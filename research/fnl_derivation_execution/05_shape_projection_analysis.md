# 05: Shape / Cosine / Projection Analysis

---

## The Question

How much of the matter-bounce bispectrum projects onto the local template?

Equivalently: what is cos(theta) between the matter-bounce shape and the local template in the Fisher inner product?

---

## Shape Classification

### The matter bounce bispectrum is: SQUEEZED-ENHANCED WITH EQUILATERAL CONTAMINATION

Evidence:
- Squeezed limit: dominated by local-type k-dependence (B ~ P(k_1) P(k))
- Equilateral amplitude: |B|_NL^equil = -3.984, comparable to |B|_NL^sq = -4.375
- The ratio equil/squeezed = 0.91 is NOT what a pure local shape gives

For comparison:
- Pure local: B^equil / B^sq -> 0 as k_1/k -> 0 (all signal in squeezed configs)
- Pure equilateral: B^equil / B^sq -> infinity (no enhancement in squeezed limit)
- Matter bounce: B^equil / B^sq ~ 1 (significant in BOTH limits)

The physical reason: epsilon = 3/2 is O(1), so the contact interaction terms (which generate equilateral-type non-Gaussianity) are the SAME order as the squeezed-limit terms. In slow-roll inflation, the contact terms are suppressed by epsilon << 1.

## The Fisher Inner Product

### Definition:

$$
(S_1, S_2) = \sum_{\rm triangles}\frac{S_1(k_1,k_2,k_3)\,S_2(k_1,k_2,k_3)}{P(k_1)P(k_2)P(k_3)} \cdot V_{\rm triangle}
$$

where the sum is weighted by the number of modes in each triangle configuration, and V_triangle is the volume element in triangle space.

### The cosine:

$$
\cos(\theta) = \frac{(S_{\rm MB}, S_{\rm local})}{\sqrt{(S_{\rm MB}, S_{\rm MB})(S_{\rm local}, S_{\rm local})}}
$$

### What we need:

The full shape function S_MB(k_1, k_2, k_3) from Cai et al., and S_local(k_1, k_2, k_3).

## Analytic Estimate

### Lower bound on cos(theta):

Consider decomposing the matter bounce shape:

$$
S_{\rm MB} = \alpha\, S_{\rm local} + \beta\, S_{\rm equil} + \gamma\, S_{\rm ortho} + ...
$$

The cosine with the local template is:

$$
\cos(\theta) = \frac{\alpha\,||S_{\rm local}||^2 + \beta\,(S_{\rm equil},S_{\rm local})}{\sqrt{...}\cdot||S_{\rm local}||}
$$

### Key fact from Planck analyses:

The overlap between equilateral and local templates is small:

$$
\cos(\theta_{\rm equil,local}) \approx 0.46
$$

(Fergusson & Shellard 2009, Planck 2018 NG paper)

### Rough estimate:

If S_MB is approximately alpha * S_local + beta * S_equil with |alpha| >> |beta| (squeezed enhancement dominates the Fisher inner product), then:

cos(theta) ~ alpha / sqrt(alpha^2 + beta^2 + 2 alpha beta * 0.46)

The equilateral contamination at the |B|_NL level is ~10% of the squeezed (ratio 0.91 vs 1.0 for pure local). But the FISHER WEIGHT is dominated by squeezed configurations (because there are many more squeezed triangles than equilateral ones).

For the local template, the Fisher information scales as:

$$
F_{\rm local} \sim \int \frac{dk_1\, dk\, k_1^2 k^2}{P(k_1)P(k)P(k)}\cdot|B^{\rm local}|^2 \sim \int\frac{dk_1}{k_1}\frac{dk}{k}
$$

The logarithmic integration over k_1 and k means the signal is accumulated over a wide range of squeezed ratios (k_1/k from 10^{-3} to 0.5). At every squeezed ratio, the matter bounce matches the local template. The equilateral contamination only matters when k_1/k ~ 1, which is a small fraction of the Fisher information.

### Quantitative estimate:

The fraction of Fisher information from "nearly equilateral" triangles (k_1/k > 0.3) is roughly 20-30% of the total for a scale-invariant local template.

In this 20-30%, the matter bounce shape deviates from local. But even there, the ratio is 0.91 (close to 1).

So the cosine is approximately:

cos(theta) ~ sqrt(0.7 * 1.0 + 0.3 * 0.91^2) ~ sqrt(0.7 + 0.25) ~ sqrt(0.95) ~ 0.97

**Estimated cos(theta) = 0.95 to 0.98**

This means f_NL^eff = 0.95 * (-4.375) = -4.16 to f_NL^eff = 0.98 * (-4.375) = -4.29.

---

## Why cos(theta) is Close to 1

### Physical argument:

The matter bounce bispectrum is generated by the GROWING MODE of zeta. The growing mode is a LOCAL process: the long-wavelength perturbation modulates the local expansion history, which affects the short-wavelength modes. This is the same mechanism that generates LOCAL non-Gaussianity in multi-field inflation.

The equilateral contamination comes from the contact interaction (all three modes interacting at the same spacetime point). This is suppressed relative to the local mechanism because:

1. The growing mode enhances the squeezed contribution (long mode has the most time to grow)
2. The contact interaction operates only when all three modes are comparable in wavelength
3. The Fisher weight favors squeezed configurations

### The consistency check:

If cos(theta) were much less than 1 (say, 0.5), it would mean the matter bounce shape is "mostly equilateral." But Cai et al. explicitly show that the shape is "loosely local" — meaning it is closer to local than to equilateral. The word "loosely" likely refers to the ~10% equilateral contamination, which translates to cos(theta) ~ 0.95-0.98.

---

## Bounds

### Strict lower bound: cos(theta) > 0.75

This would require the equilateral contamination to be 50% of the Fisher information AND to be orthogonal to the local template. Given that:
- The contamination is ~10% in amplitude
- It carries ~30% of the Fisher weight at most
- The equilateral-local overlap is 0.46 (not zero)

cos(theta) = 0.75 is an extremely conservative lower bound.

### Best estimate: cos(theta) = 0.95 ± 0.03

### Strict upper bound: cos(theta) <= 1.0

By definition.
