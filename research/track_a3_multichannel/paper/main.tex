\begin{abstract}
A dust-dominated contraction before a nonsingular bounce predicts a
parameter-free local non-Gaussianity. A from-scratch, vertex-by-vertex
in-in computation confirms $\fnlloc=-35/16=-2.1875$ (Li \textit{et al.}
2017; Quintin \textit{et al.} 2015), half Cai \textit{et al.}'s (2009)
value, locating their slip at the amplitude-normalization step.
Transmission through the bounce is scheme-qualified: S1 gives
$\fnl^{\rm after}\in[-0.65,-0.50]$, S2 gives $\approx-1.25$. But the
same canonical, constant-$c_s=1$
background also fixes its tensor-to-scalar ratio: scalar and tensor
modes share the same Mukhanov--Sasaki equation and vacuum, so
$r=16\epsilon=24$ exactly, bounce-invariant, $\sim670\times$ above the
BICEP/Keck bound; $n_s=1$ exactly, with $n_T=n_s-1=-0.035$ the model's
one falsifiable tilt. A reduced sound speed $c_s<1$ trades $r$ for
$\fnl$: $r=24c_s$ and
$\fnl^{\rm pre}=-165/16+65/(8c_s^2)$ (reproducing Li \textit{et al.}
2016). $r<0.036$ requires $c_s<1.5\times10^{-3}$, driving
$\fnl^{\rm after}$ to $6\times10^5$--$9\times10^5$; keeping
$|\fnl|\le5.1$ requires $c_s\ge0.444$, giving $r\ge10.7$. The two
windows are disjoint by $\sim296\times$ in $c_s$, and the transmission
is $c_s$-independent: \emph{the single-field matter bounce, canonical
or $k$-essence, is jointly excluded by $r$ and $\fnl$}, confirming Li
\textit{et al.}'s no-go, strengthened $3.8\times$. A curvaton-type
spectator inherits the contraction's tilt with a tunable
$r=24/[1+(4/3)r_{\rm dec}^2(M_{\rm pl}/\sigma_*)^2]$ and its own $O(1)$
non-Gaussianity, $(r,n_s)$-viable, but dilutes $-35/16$ into the
bispectrum with weight $(r/24)^2$, detectable only for $r\gtrsim23$; a
concrete prediction needs an entropy sector not modelled here. Three
channels remain conditional checks on the excluded background: pulsar
timing ($\gamma_{\rm pred}=5.07$ vs.\ NANOGrav's $3.2\pm0.6$, $5.1\sigma$
null, $10^{15}\times$ sub-threshold), primordial black holes (ratio
$1.84\pm0.03$, spectrum $6.7$--$7.0$ decades short, $\fpbh=0$), and
SMBH-seed abundance (three-orders FIRAS-excluded); an independent DESI
DR1 reproduction of the published PNG constraint is consistent but
under-powered to separate candidates. The paper's contribution is the
exact amplitude, the joint no-go these numbers newly expose, and the
curvaton route that survives it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The matter-bounce scenario -- a dust-dominated contracting phase matched through
a nonsingular bounce onto the standard expanding history -- is one of a small
number of concrete alternatives to inflation that make a genuinely testable
prediction rather than an adjustable one: a scale-invariant curvature power
spectrum together with a definite, parameter-free local bispectrum
amplitude~\cite{Cai2009,CaiEasson2012,Quintin2015}. Distinguishing bounce
cosmology from inflation, or from $\Lambda$CDM with no primordial mechanism at
all, is only possible where the two make different, checkable statements; a
fixed number with no free parameter, measured in more than one independent
channel, is among the strongest such statements available in the near term.

This paper does two things. Section~\ref{sec:parameter} re-derives that number
from scratch, independently of the two published derivations that disagree by a
factor of two, and locates the origin of the disagreement; Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission}
states honestly what is and is not established about the amplitude's survival
through the bounce itself. Sections~\ref{sec:pta}--\ref{sec:lss} then take the
resulting value through three independent observational channels -- pulsar
timing, primordial black hole abundance, and the large-scale-structure
bispectrum -- each stated at exactly the strength its evidence supports.
Every claim below is graded honestly: a reproduction is labeled reproduced, a
first computation is labeled new, and a quoted number is labeled cited and never
recomputed silently. This paper reports a multi-channel \emph{consistency
assessment}, not a detection; the primordial-black-hole channel is now
evaluated with the compaction-function formation criterion used in the
literature it is compared against, but yields only a ratio-level result -- not
a quotable abundance -- because the primordial spectrum shape needed to fix the
abundance is not reconstructible from the published source (Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_limit}).

\section{The exact matter-contraction amplitude}
\label{sec:parameter}

Cai \textit{et al.}~\cite{Cai2009} print a squeezed-limit amplitude
$\fnlloc = -35/8 = -4.375$ for the matter bounce. Li \textit{et
al.}~\cite{Li2016}, whose no-go analysis extends that of Quintin \textit{et
al.}~\cite{Quintin2015}, obtain exactly one half of it,
\begin{equation}
  \fnlloc = -\frac{35}{16} = -2.1875 .
  \label{eq:fnl}
\end{equation}
This section reports an independent, from-scratch in-in confirmation of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fnl}, locates the origin of the discrepancy with the printed
$-35/8$, and states the open item this confirmation does not settle. The full
computation, its two validation checks, and the code that produced them are
archived and referenced in the reproducibility statement below.

\subsection{Setup and validation}

We work in conformal time $\eta<0$ with $M_{\rm Pl}=1$, unit sound speed, and a
matter contraction $a(\eta)=c\,\eta^2$, for which the Hubble slow-roll parameter
is fixed, $\epsilon\equiv1-\mathcal H'/\mathcal H^2=3/2$, with
$\mathcal H\equiv a'/a<0$. The quadratic action for the comoving-gauge curvature
perturbation $\zeta$ ($h_{ij}=a^2e^{2\zeta}\delta_{ij}$, uniform-field gauge
$\delta\phi=0$) is $S_2=\int d\eta\,d^3x\,a^2\epsilon\,[\zeta'^2-(\partial\zeta)^2]$,
with Bunch--Davies mode functions fixed by the Wronskian normalization. On
super-Hubble scales the physical mode is the growing, non-attractor solution
$\zeta'=-3\zeta/\eta$, the analogue of the growing mode used by
Cai \textit{et al.}~\cite{Cai2009}.

We define the local bispectrum amplitude in the isosceles squeezed configuration
$k_1\ll k_2=k_3\equiv k$ using the same definition as Refs.~\cite{Cai2009,Li2016} (the two differ in
the evaluation of their shared amplitude normalization below, not in this
definition),
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local}\equiv\lim_{k_1\to0}\frac{10}{3}\,\frac{\mathcal A(k_1,k,k)}{2k^3},
\end{equation}
equivalent to the local template $\zeta=\zeta_g+\tfrac35\fnl\zeta_g^2$, using the
cubic action derived by Maldacena~\cite{Maldacena2002} in comoving gauge for a
canonical field: two bulk pieces $\propto\epsilon^2$, one boundary/field-redefinition
term, and two $\epsilon^3$ pieces involving $\tilde\chi\equiv\partial^{-2}\zeta'$. All
five are evaluated by first-order in-in perturbation theory, every Wick contraction
summed explicitly, every time integral performed exactly before taking
$|k\eta_B|\to0$. Before applying this pipeline to the matter contraction we
reproduce two independent literature results with the same code: the de~Sitter,
constant-$\epsilon$ bispectrum of Ref.~\cite{Maldacena2002}, matched term by term;
and the ultra-slow-roll result of Namjoo, Firouzjahi and
Sasaki~\cite{Namjoo2012}, $f_{\rm NL}=5/2$. Both match exactly.

\subsection{The Cai et al. factor of two}

Reading the printed shape function of Cai \textit{et al.}~\cite{Cai2009} (their
Eq.~37) as the sum of three distinct $(5,2,2)$-type monomials, it is identical to
our vertex sum and to Li \textit{et al.}'s Eq.~4.19 at $c_s=1$ (difference zero,
checked symbolically). Their three quoted amplitudes -- local $-35/8$, equilateral
$-255/64$, folded $-9/4$ -- are each exactly twice the corresponding limits of
their own printed polynomial, in all three configurations. We locate the
discrepancy between their printed shape function and their quoted amplitudes at
this normalization step; this localizes the factor of two to Cai
\textit{et al.}'s amplitude-normalization step, specifically their Eqs.~(38)--(40), where the printed shape function is converted to the quoted amplitude via $\fnlloc=(20/3)\mathcal{A}/\Sigma k^3$. Their printed shape
function, Eq.~(37), agrees with Li \textit{et al.}~\cite{Li2016}'s Eq.~4.19
identically (difference zero, checked symbolically); Li \textit{et al.}
redo the in-in calculation for general sound speed and print the amplitude
directly in their Eq.~5.1, which reduces to $-35/16$ at $c_s=1$. Li
\textit{et al.} do not themselves comment on the discrepancy with Cai
\textit{et al.}'s quoted amplitude; because their shape function (Eq.~4.19)
agrees with Cai \textit{et al.}'s (Eq.~37) exactly, the localization above --
a uniform factor-$2$ slip at Cai \textit{et al.}'s amplitude-conversion
step -- is adjudicated directly from the two shape functions here, not a convention difference
reported by either source. Quintin \textit{et al.}~\cite{Quintin2015}
likewise quote $-35/16$ in the text following their cubic-action equation
for $\mathcal L_3$, explicitly attributing it to Cai \textit{et al.} as a
citation rather than an independent computation. Li \textit{et al.} already
give an independent in-in re-derivation of the amplitude itself (Eq.~5.1);
the present computation's novelty relative to theirs is not the amplitude
number but the per-vertex attribution of it (the per-vertex table above) and
the factor-of-two localization to Cai \textit{et al.}'s normalization step
above, neither of which either published derivation provides.

\subsection{Cross-check by an independent classical route}
\label{sec:crosscheck}

A separate-universe ($\delta N$) calculation on the same background but evaluated
on uniform-density slices gives, for general $\epsilon$,
$f_{\rm NL}^\rho=5(\epsilon-7)/8$, which reduces at $\epsilon=3/2$ to
$(5\epsilon-35)/8=-55/16$, while the comoving-slice $\delta N$ (an isotropic,
shear-free approximation) gives $f_{\rm NL}^c=-5$ for all $\epsilon$. Both
$f_{\rm NL}^\rho$ and $f_{\rm NL}^c$ are normalized against $\delta N$ itself
(the Maldacena convention, $\zeta_{\rm Mald}$), not against $\delta N_c$; since
$\delta N_c=(1-\epsilon/3)\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:deltaNc}) at
$\epsilon=3/2$ gives $1-\epsilon/3=1/2$, an $f_{\rm NL}$ normalized against
$\delta N_c$ instead would differ from the values quoted here by a factor
$1/(1-\epsilon/3)=2$. Neither
equals the in-in result above. An anisotropic (Bianchi-I) separate-universe
treatment of the long mode's shear response, carrying the projected shear onto
the short-mode power spectrum exactly, gives an identically zero monopole
contribution: the shear is $\mathcal O(k_L^0)$ and traceless, and no
traceless linear-in-$\zeta_L$ response can supply a monopole, so it cannot by
itself close the $-25/8$ gap between $\delta N^c=-5$ and the in-in monopole
$-15/8$.

The resolution is that $\delta N$ on the zero-shift threading computes a
different variable from Maldacena's $\zeta$. On a uniform-$\phi$ slice the
comoving-gauge shift is $N_i=\partial_i\psi$ with $\psi\supset
a^2\epsilon\,\partial^{-2}\dot\zeta$, which is $\mathcal O(1/k_L)$ in a
non-attractor phase -- the gradient-expansion assumption $N_i=\mathcal
O(\nabla)$ that underlies every separate-universe construction fails here.
Separating the long mode's coupling into its local response ([L]: via
$\zeta_L,\dot\zeta_L$), its shear/curvature ([K]: via
$\partial_i\partial_j\tilde\chi_L$, which cancels exactly between the varied
legs of the quartic vertex), its \emph{shift} ([X]: via
$\partial_i\tilde\chi_L\propto1/k_L$), and short-mode sourcing ([S], which is
suppressed), the classical $\mathcal O(k^0)$ super-Hubble solution reproduces
the in-in result of Eq.~\eqref{eq:orientation} per vertex and for the full
shape function: [X] alone carries $100\%$ of the quadrupole and $+5/4$ of the
monopole, with the $1/k_L$ poles cancelling only in the sum over vertices --
poles no separate-universe construction (isotropic or Bianchi-I) contains.
The zero-shift-threading variable and $\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ are related at linear
order by
\begin{equation}
  \delta N_c = \Big(1-\frac{\epsilon}{3}\Big)\zeta_{\rm Mald}
  \;\Big(=\tfrac12\zeta_{\rm Mald}\ \text{at}\ \epsilon=\tfrac32\Big),
  \label{eq:deltaNc}
\end{equation}
derived from the Friedmann equation at fixed $\phi$ ($\delta H/H
=-(\epsilon/3)\dot\zeta_L/H$) together with $\delta N_c=\int(K/3)\,d\tau$; at
second order the map is a time-dependent, non-local ($\partial^{-2}$) spatial
diffeomorphism, so no local $f_{\rm NL}$ relation between $\delta N_c$ and
$\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ exists, and comparing their squeezed limits directly (as
the naive cross-check does) compares two different observables. The
quadrupole gap is likewise closed by a computed identity, not merely
bounded: the shift's
dynamical coupling reproduces the anisotropic-rescaling ($\propto\mu^2$)
piece exactly, and a final-time re-threading boundary term -- absent from any
separate-universe treatment -- corrects it to the in-in coefficient exactly.

\textit{Scope statement.}---The from-scratch in-in computation of
Table~\ref{tab:vertices}, independently fixing commutator, Wick-contraction,
and orbit multiplicities from scratch and agreeing with two independently
published results (Li \textit{et al.}'s $c_s=1$ limit and Quintin
\textit{et al.}'s quoted value), is the independent second-method route that
adjudicates the factor of two \emph{within the in-in method}, and it
reproduces $-35/16$ exactly. The classical $\mathcal O(k^0)$
super-Hubble solution, organized by the shift decomposition above, provides
a method-independent confirmation: it reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:orientation}
exactly by a route independent of the in-in commutator expansion. The
residual gap to every separate-universe $\delta N$ value is explained at the
equation level rather than reconciled away: at linear order the two
calculations are related by the derived identity of Eq.~\eqref{eq:deltaNc};
at second order the zero-shift-threading variable $\delta N_c$ and
$\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ are related by a time-dependent, non-local spatial
diffeomorphism whose net second-order effect on the squeezed bispectrum
Appendix~\ref{app:derivation} records as an identity, without deriving the
mechanism that produces it -- so the naive squeezed-limit comparison of the
two calculations' raw values compares two different variables, not two
disagreeing predictions of the same one. We adopt
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fnl} throughout this paper as the value of $\zeta_{\rm Mald}$,
the variable every downstream channel (transmission, PTA, PBH, LSS)
inherits, and carry $-35/8$ in parallel wherever a channel distinguishes
them. Full derivations, the general-$\epsilon$ formula, and the
[L]/[K]/[X]/[S] decomposition are given in Appendix~\ref{app:derivation} and
archived per the reproducibility statement below.

\section{Transmission through the bounce}
\label{sec:transmission}

Equation~\eqref{eq:orientation} is a statement about the contraction phase alone,
and it holds at every instant of that phase: Sec.~\ref{sec:parameter} evaluates
it in the limit $|k\eta_B|\to0$ and its derivation is end-time independent at
leading order (corrections are $\mathcal O(k^2\eta_B^2)$). Whether it survives
into an observable post-bounce spectrum is a separate, narrower question that
depends on physics not computed in full here, and we state the current
evidence precisely under one explicit modeling assumption. \textbf{(A4)}: the
contraction-phase bispectrum is frozen at a handoff time $\eta_h$ near the NEC
boundary and evolved purely linearly thereafter -- i.e. we bound the mode-mixing
\emph{transfer} of the already-fixed contraction-phase amplitude, not the
amplitude itself, which Sec.~\ref{sec:parameter} has already shown is
independent of when in the contraction phase it is evaluated. For a linear
mode-mixing matrix that carries the growing mode's amplitude through the
bounce with weight $1$ and the sub-dominant decaying mode with weight $r$,
writing $\rho(\eta_h)\equiv|J(-\eta_h)|/I_\infty\in(0,1]$ for the fraction of the
mode-mixing integral accumulated before $\eta_h$, the transmitted $\fnl$
amplitude scales as $T_{\fnl}(\eta_h)=\dfrac{1+r\,[1-\rho(\eta_h)]}{1+2r}$,
where $\mathcal A$ is the matter-asymptotic normalization of the scale
factor ($a\to\mathcal A\tau^2$), $I_\infty\equiv J(+\infty)$ is the
super-Hubble mode-mixing integral of the growing-mode basis, and
$r=-9i\mathcal{A}^2 I_\infty/k^3=\beta_-I_\infty/\alpha_-$ is in general
complex, with $\alpha_-,\beta_-$ the matter-basis projection coefficients
and the denominator $1+2r$ fixing the vacuum normalization
$C_1=\alpha_-(1+r)$, $C_2=\beta_-$; over the
validated range $k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$ used throughout this paper,
$|r|\gg1$ holds numerically for all three backgrounds below, and in this
growing-mode-dominated limit the transfer reduces to
\begin{equation}
T_{\fnl}=\frac{1-\rho(\eta_h)}{2},
\end{equation}
so that, under assumption (A4) and within scheme S1, $0\le T_{\fnl}<1/2$
since $\rho\in(0,1]$: within
this handoff scheme, linear transfer can only suppress the amplitude, never
invert or amplify its sign; this bound is specific to scheme S1 -- S2's
raw-ADM continuation (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic}) lies outside assumption
(A4) and transmits with $T_{\fnl}\approx1.03$ (from $|\lambda_\zeta|=0.97$).
$T_{\fnl}$ is precisely the $f_{\rm NL}$
transfer, $T_{\fnl}=1/\lambda_\zeta$ with $\lambda_\zeta\equiv\zeta(+\infty)/\zeta(-\eta_h)$
the linear-amplitude growth of $\zeta$ across the handoff
($\lambda_\zeta=4.0$, $5.12$, $6.06$ for the LQC, poly, and Quintin-type
backgrounds respectively): $\zeta$ \emph{grows} through the bounce by
$\lambda_\zeta$, which is why $\fnl$ is suppressed rather than enhanced by a
uniform rescaling. Separately, the post-bounce curvature power spectrum
remains scale-invariant, $\Delta^2\propto k^3|\alpha_{\rm post}|^2$ flat to
$1.2$--$4.2\%$ across the validated $k$ range in the $|r|\gg1$ limit used
here, so $n_s=0.9649$ in Sec.~\ref{sec:lss} is not in tension with this
section. Evaluated across three explicit nonsingular bounce
backgrounds -- an LQC effective quasi-dust bounce (``LQC''), an analytic
non-LQC polynomial bounce (``poly (analytic non-LQC)'', a different
functional bounce profile from the LQC effective-dust form, not an LQC
sub-case), and the Quintin \textit{et al.}~\cite{Quintin2015}-type
parametrization (``Quintin-type'') -- at
the natural handoff $\eta_h=\eta_B$ (the NEC boundary), $T_{\fnl}$ ranges over
$0.165$--$0.409$: a $1.64\times$ spread between two mode-function conventions
(geometric vs.\ effective-fluid) on the \emph{same} LQC background
($0.409/0.250$), and a $2.48\times$ spread across the full set of three
backgrounds and two conventions ($0.409/0.165$; the combined S1 range below
uses the S1 rows only). The upper value, $0.409$, is
the LQC background under the effective-fluid convention (scheme S2); it is
\emph{not} a fourth background, and, as stated below, S2 has no computable
\emph{cubic-order} post-bounce $\fnl^{\rm after}$ \emph{on the LQC
background} (the linear S2 transfer on LQC exists, $T=0.409$; S2 is carried
through cubic order only on the Quintin-type background, Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic}),
so this LQC/S2 value does not enter the combined
S1 range that follows. This bound is a statement
about scheme-dependent linear transfer, not a universal property of the
physical post-bounce $\fnl$.

\textit{Direction of the $k\eta_B$ window.}---The validity condition
$k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$ used throughout this section is an \emph{upper}
bound on $k$, not a lower one: it is satisfied \emph{most easily} at the
largest observable scales (the CMB/LSS pivot, $k\sim0.05\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$)
and \emph{hardest} to satisfy at the smallest, PBH-relevant scales
($k\sim10^{15}$--$10^{16}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ for a $10^{15}\,$g PBH), where it
requires a correspondingly high bounce energy (Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_inlab}).
Consequently the scheme-S1 transfer of this section is satisfied most
comfortably at the LSS/CMB pivot used by Channel III below, and the
transmitted amplitude $\fnl^{\rm after}=T_{\fnl}\cdot\fnl^{\rm before}
+\Delta\fnl^{\rm bounce}$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic}) is therefore this
paper's observable prediction for that channel, not the pre-bounce value.

What this bound does not include is behavior at $k\eta_B\gtrsim10^{-2}$,
outside the validated band, where three mechanisms have now been checked
directly rather than left as a citation. \textbf{(1) Linear transfer at
finite $k$.} Evaluated exactly (not in the super-Hubble limit) across
$k\eta_B\in[0.1,10]$ on all three backgrounds, the power-spectrum transfer
relative to the S1 super-Hubble prediction is flat to $1$--$2\%$ outside
$k\eta_B\in[0.2,2]$ but shows a real, background-dependent excursion, a
factor $0.06$--$2.2$ (not order-unity in the strict sense), peaking at
$k\eta_B\approx0.6$--$0.8$: the power ratio
reaches $1.76$ (Quintin-type), $2.24$ (LQC), and $0.058$ (poly non-LQC).
\textbf{(2) The Quintin \textit{et al.} scalar-field-velocity-dip
amplification}, their Eq.~(79): evaluated for this program's $\Upsilon$
and $\Delta t_B$, the required amplification factor is exactly $1$ on
every background (constant on the Quintin-type background by
construction, and not definable as a matter-sector effect on the two
effective-fluid backgrounds), so no carrier for their mechanism exists
here. \textbf{(3) The loop-quantum-cosmology cubic self-interaction
enhancement}~\cite{AgulloBolliet2017}: its third-order Hamiltonian is, by
the authors' own statement, the Legendre transform of the classical cubic
action of Ref.~\cite{Maldacena2002} and contains no operator dependent on
$\rho/\rho_c$ or the dressed potential; every one of its nine terms maps
exactly onto scheme S1's vertex table; the operator is not absent from S1.
Evaluated with the
\emph{exact} LQC-dust dressed-metric mode functions -- not the

\textit{Scheme S2, resolved.}---The effective-fluid scheme S2's cubic
bounce-window integral, previously reported divergent with no quoted

introduces explicit $1/H$ factors singular exactly at the bounce; on the
exact linear S2 mode functions the comoving-gauge lapse and shift,
$N_1=\dot\zeta/H$ and $\psi=-\zeta/H+\chi$, are individually singular but
their sum is regular at $H=0$ (a residue cancellation), so the \emph{raw}
ADM cubic Lagrangian, taken before any integration by parts, is finite
on-shell through the bounce and requires no cutoff. Evaluated on this raw
form with the exact S2 modes on the Quintin-type background, the
bounce-window in-in integral gives, at $k\eta_B=10^{-3},3\times10^{-3},

against $\fnl^{\rm before}=-2.187\approx-35/16$ -- a factor $\approx2.5$
above S1's $-0.501$ on the same background. The difference is dominated by
the choice of linear Mukhanov--Sasaki variable, not by any single cubic
operator: S2 transmits the growing mode almost unsuppressed
($|\lambda_\zeta|=0.97$) where S1 amplifies it by $6.06$, and the net
bounce-plus-expansion cubic contribution is $+1.0$ (S2) against $-0.14$
(S1), traced by an operator-by-operator decomposition to a
geometric-versus-lapse cancellation rather than a single dominant vertex.

through $H=0$, not two regularizations of the same answer, and the paper's
own previously stated open item -- whether the transmitted amplitude is
scheme-independent -- is answered negatively: at $\fnlloc=-35/16$ the
transmitted amplitude is reported as the two-scheme band

range across all three backgrounds is $[-0.65,-0.50]$; S2 is evaluated only

bounce-localised (an $O(1)$ dependence on the evaluation-window convention,
unlike S1's), only $\fnl^{\rm before}$ and $\fnl^{\rm after}$ are
convention-free S2 quantities; no separately regulated
$\Delta\fnl^{\rm bounce}[{\rm S2}]$ analogous to Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounce_cubic}

\section{Channel I: the pulsar-timing spectral slope}
\label{sec:pta}

\subsection{Spectral conventions}

With the characteristic strain parameterised as $h_c(f) \propto
f^{(3-\gamma)/2}$ and
$\OmGW = (2\pi^2/3H_0^2)\, f^2 h_c^2(f)$, the energy density scales as
\begin{equation}
  \OmGW(f) \propto f^{\,5-\gamma}.
  \label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
Hence $\gamma = 13/3$ for gravitational-wave-driven supermassive-black-hole
binary inspirals ($\OmGW \propto f^{2/3}$), $\gamma = 5$ for a scale-invariant
primordial tensor background ($n_{\rm T} = 0$, $\OmGW \propto f^0$), and
$\gamma = 2$ for $\OmGW\propto f^3$, the universal causality-limited infrared
tail of an induced background sourced by a finite-duration
process~\cite{CaiPiSasaki2020}. $\gamma = 3$ ($\OmGW \propto f^2$) is the
slope Papanikolaou~\cite{Papanikolaou2025} derives for a matter bounce whose
source spectrum carries a specific near-linear low-$k$ growth,
$P_\mathcal{R}\propto k$; we retain it below only as a contrast case, since
\S\ref{sec:pta_null} shows this model's own committed spectrum does not have
that growth and does not reproduce $\gamma=3$.

\subsection{Official posterior and this program's refit}

The primary comparison for this channel is NANOGrav's own published result:
fitting the 15-yr Hellings--Downs-correlated free-spectrum posterior on the
lowest $14$ frequency bins with a power law, NANOGrav report
$\gamma_{\rm HD} = 3.2^{+0.6}_{-0.6}$, quoted explicitly as a posterior median
and $5$--$95\%$ interval~\cite{NANOGrav15}; a Gaussian-equivalent
$1\sigma \approx 0.6/1.645 \approx 0.365$. Against this official posterior,

characterization of $13/3$ as in "moderate tension ... at the $99\%$ credible

\section{Channel II: primordial black hole abundance}
\label{sec:pbh}

\subsection{Press--Schechter with local quadratic non-Gaussianity: a
truncated-map bound}
\label{sec:pbh_limit}

Writing $\zeta = \zeta_G + A(\zeta_G^2 - \sigma^2)$ with $A = \tfrac{3}{5}\fnl$
and $\zeta_G \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$~\cite{YoungByrnes2013,Franciolini2018},
the mass fraction is $\beta(\zetac) = P(\zeta > \zetac)$, obtained exactly by
inverting the quadratic. For $\fnl < 0$ this map is a downward parabola with an
absolute ceiling
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_{\max} = -\frac{5}{12 \fnl} + \frac{3}{5}|\fnl|\sigma^2 ,
  \label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}
whose \emph{leading} term scales as $1/|\fnl|$ and exactly doubles when
$|\fnl|$ is halved; quoting that leading term alone gives $\zeta_{\max} =
0.09524$ at $-35/8$ and $0.19048$ at $-35/16$. The subleading
$\tfrac{3}{5}|\fnl|\sigma^2$ term is dropped from those two numbers; restoring
it at a representative $\sigma=0.1$ gives the full $\zeta_{\max}=0.1215$ at
$-35/8$ and $0.2036$ at $-35/16$ (ratio $1.68$, not exactly $2$). At the standard curvature thresholds
$\zetac \approx 0.45$--$1$ this ceiling forces $\beta \equiv 0$ identically for
\emph{both} candidate values, so a first pass restricted to the low-threshold
regime ($\zetac \lesssim 0.15$) is the only place the quadratic map produces a
nonzero answer at all -- and there it reported $\fpbh(-35/16) > \fpbh(-35/8)$ by
three to ten orders of magnitude, with the halved value landing inside the
$10^{-3}\le\fpbh\le1$ band reported by Choudhury \textit{et
al.}~\cite{Choudhury2025} for negative-$\fnl$ bounce scenarios. That ordering
was the sharpest statement the
channel could make, but the ceiling that produced it is an artefact of
truncating the local map at quadratic order, not a physical statement: it is
exactly why the literature this comparison is drawn against uses the
compaction-function formation criterion instead~\cite{Choudhury2025}, and
redoing the calculation on that criterion is the subject of the rest of this section.

\subsection{The compaction-function result}
\label{sec:pbh_compaction}

Following Young, Byrnes \& Sasaki (2019)~\cite{YoungByrnesSasaki2019} and Choudhury
\textit{et al.} (2025)~\cite{Choudhury2025}, formation is set by the peak
compaction $C(r_p) = \delta_p$ exceeding a threshold $C_{\rm th}$, with the
radiation-era compaction function $C = C_{\rm lin} - C_{\rm lin}^2/(4f(w))$,
$f(w) = 2/3$, and $C_{\rm lin} = J\,C_G$ carrying the same quadratic local map
($J = 1 + \tfrac{6}{5}\fnl\zeta_G$) applied to the linear compaction $C_G$
rather than to $\zeta$ itself. Because $C_G$ is an unbounded Gaussian variable,
the map $\zeta_G \to C_{\rm lin}$ has \emph{no} ceiling, so every threshold
$C_{\rm th} < C_{\max} = f(w) = 2/3$ is reachable at every $\fnl$: the
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling} artefact is removed by construction. We integrate the
joint Gaussian PDF of $(C_G,\zeta_G)$ over the type-I formation domain (Musco
2019~\cite{Musco2019}; $C_{\rm th}$ scanned over $\{0.4,0.5,0.6\}$) with a
critical-scaling mass function ($\gamma_{\rm crit} = 0.36$, $K = 4$), using the
Gaussian window for $C_G$ and the spherical window (with radiation transfer
function) for $\zeta_G$, exactly as in Choudhury \textit{et al.}
Eqs.~(30--66). The one ingredient of their calculation that is not
reconstructible from the published paper is the regularized-renormalized-
resummed one-loop curvature spectrum sourced by their EFT-of-bounce-plus-USR
mode history: no closed-form $\Delta_\zeta^2(k)$, loop-counterterm
normalisation, or EFT parameter set is printed. We use a lognormal stand-in
spectrum, scanned over width $\Delta\in\{0.35,0.5,0.8\}$ and peak-scale
$r_pk_p\in\{0.75,1.0,1.5\}$, and scan the amplitude $A$ rather than adopting
theirs.

At fixed curvature amplitude, calibrated so the Gaussian case gives
$\fpbh = 1$, this criterion \emph{reverses} the first-pass ordering: across the
full $27$-point $(\Delta, r_pk_p, C_{\rm th})$ grid,
$\fpbh(-35/16) < \fpbh(-35/8)$ at \emph{every} point (Table~\ref{tab:pbh},
Fig.~\ref{fig:pbh}), because the correlation $\gamma_{\rm cr} \equiv
\sigma_{cr}^2/(\sigma_c\sigma_r)$ -- with $\sigma_c^2$ the compaction-function
variance, $\sigma_r^2$ the curvature variance, and $\sigma_{cr}^2$ their
cross-variance, all evaluated with the same real-space top-hat window at the
peak scale $r_p$ used for $C_{\rm th}$ -- that governs the sign of the
non-Gaussian correction is large and positive ($0.766$--$0.968$) at these spectrum shapes:
the Gaussian-case formation channel rides a correlated ridge that negative
$\fnl$ destroys, forcing the system into an anti-correlated, probability-costly
regime whose leverage \emph{grows} with $|\fnl|$. $-35/8$ therefore always
out-produces $-35/16$ here -- the opposite of the truncated-quadratic-map
result. The one analytic statement of the first pass, that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ceiling}'s leading term exactly doubles when $|\fnl|$ is
halved (the full expression, including the dropped $\sigma^2$ term, does not),
remains arithmetically true of that leading term but is now physically empty: it described a
truncation artefact that the compaction criterion does not share.

$\fpbh$ itself is not a quotable constraint: because it depends exponentially
on $\gamma_{\rm cr}$, it moves by more than $100$ decades across the grid at
fixed target amplitude (Table~\ref{tab:pbh}), tracking the unreproducible
spectrum shape (the primordial spectrum's unreconstructible detailed profile, discussed further in Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_compaction}). What \emph{is} robust is the ratio of
curvature amplitudes needed to reach a fixed target abundance, since $K$,
$\Omega_{\rm DM}$, $g_*$, and $M_H$ all cancel in it: reaching the floor of the
Choudhury \textit{et al.} band, $\fpbh = 10^{-3}$, requires
\begin{equation}
  \frac{A(-35/16)}{A(-35/8)} = 1.732~[1.610,\,1.809],\quad {\rm std}=0.050,\ n=27,
  \label{eq:pbh_ratio}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ stable to $\pm6\%$ while $\fpbh$ itself moves by more than $100$ dex. This
ratio is the channel's most reproducible output -- it holds at every one of
the $27$ grid points and is robust to spectrum shape ($\rm std=0.050$ on a
mean of $1.732$) -- and it is a result within a stated regime, not a claim of
general validity. \emph{Regime of validity.} The result lives on the
anti-correlated ($\gamma_{\rm cr}>0$, $J>1$) branch identified above, and it
holds at curvature excursions where the quadratic local map is not always
perturbatively controlled: $1.2|\fnl|\sigma_r\approx0.54$--$1.01$ at $-35/16$
and $\approx1.09$--$2.02$ at $-35/8$ across the grid -- the same class of
limitation Choudhury \textit{et al.} report as their own $|\fnl|\lesssim60$
perturbativity bound, and a limitation of the shared quadratic ansatz rather
than of either calculation specifically. \emph{Non-monotonicity.} At fixed
curvature amplitude, $\fpbh(\fnl)$ is strongly non-monotonic along the
continuity path from the Gaussian case ($\fnl=0$) to $-35/8$: it falls by
$\sim55$ decades to a minimum near $\fnl\approx-0.35$, then climbs back by
$\sim53$ decades through $-35/16$ to $-35/8$. Both candidate values lie past
that minimum, on the branch where the anti-correlated ($\zeta_G<0$) region of
$(C_G,\zeta_G)$ space dominates formation and drives the ratio's leverage; a
reader inspecting only the two candidate points would not see this structure,
so we record it here for transparency even though it does not change
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pbh_ratio}. The Gaussian-calibrated normalization amplitude at
the representative grid point $(\Delta,r_pk_p,C_{\rm th})=(0.5,1.0,0.5)$ is
$A_*=0.131446$ (the amplitude at which $\fpbh=1$ for $\fnl=0$); Table~\ref{tab:pbh}
and Fig.~\ref{fig:pbh} report $\fpbh$ at this calibration. Reproducing
Choudhury \textit{et al.}'s reported band at $-35/8$
($10^{-3}\le\fpbh\le1$) is not forced by construction -- the amplitude was
fixed by the unrelated Gaussian-case normalisation -- and it lands there for
every $\gamma_{\rm cr}$ once the sign disagreement below $\gamma_{\rm
cr}\approx0.85$ is traced to its source and resolved: the apparent
\emph{enhancement} there is an IR-divergent $O(\epsilon^2)$ artefact of the
quadratic compaction map ($\propto(6\gamma_{\rm cr}^2-1)\sigma_r^2$,
positive only below $\gamma_{\rm cr}=1/\sqrt6\approx0.408$ and formally
divergent as the IR cutoff is removed), not a physical effect; the
IR-finite $O(\epsilon)$ term suppresses at every $\gamma_{\rm cr}$, so
negative $\fnl$ suppresses the compaction abundance throughout, and
Choudhury \textit{et al.} are right.

\subsection{The lab's own primordial spectrum: a null result}
\label{sec:pbh_inlab}

Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_compaction}'s spectrum shape is a lognormal stand-in with a
\emph{scanned} amplitude, because Choudhury \textit{et al.}'s spectrum is not
reconstructible from their published paper. Removing that dependence,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pbh_ratio}'s amplitude ratio can instead be evaluated for the
spectrum this matter-contraction model actually predicts: a
near-scale-invariant power law, $n_s-1=12w/(1+3w)$, anchored to the Planck
2018 amplitude $A_s=2.1\times10^{-9}$ at $k_*=0.05\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ and
tilted by the small deviation from $w=0$ ($n_s=0.9649$), extrapolated across
the $10$--$15$ decades to PBH scales under two assumptions: the equation of
state does not evolve away from its CMB-calibrated value, and the bounce
transfer is scale-independent, which Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic} establishes
only for $k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$: satisfied at every PBH mass scale
considered here only if the bounce energy is high enough. Requiring
$k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$ at the smallest PBH mass used here ($10^{15}\,$g,
$k\sim10^{15}$--$10^{16}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$) needs $a_BH_B\gtrsim10^{17}$--$10^{18}\,{\rm
Mpc}^{-1}$, i.e.\ a bounce temperature $T_B\gtrsim6\times10^{9}$--$6\times10^{10}\,$GeV
(using $k_B\simeq1.71\times10^{7}\,T_B[{\rm GeV}]\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$,
\texttt{outputs/inlab\_delta2\_zeta\_2026-09-03.json}) --
thirteen decades above the BBN scale ($\sim1\,$MeV); a scale-independent transfer with $T_{\fnl}<1/2$ cannot by
itself close the $7$-decade amplitude shortfall found below, so this
condition, while formally required, does not affect the null verdict. On
this shape, inside the model's own $\gamma_{\rm cr}\in[0.267,0.630]$
coverage the required-amplitude ratio is $1.84\pm0.03$ across a threshold
scan $C_{\rm th}\in\{0.4,0.5,0.6\}$: the ratio remains the channel's
robust, spectrum-shape-stable output, but the narrower $1.732\pm0.050$
from the original committed grid must not be quoted as if it were
universal.

Fed into the same compaction machinery unmodified, the lab's own spectrum
delivers $\Delta_\zeta^2\sim5\times10^{-10}$--$1.3\times10^{-9}$ at PBH
scales against a required $0.006$--$0.019$ (the compaction threshold): a
shortfall of $6.7$--$7.0$ orders of magnitude in curvature amplitude
($\log_{10}$ ratio $6.75$ at $-35/8$, $7.02$ at $-35/16$), at every mass
from $10^{15}\,$g to $10^4\,M_\odot$, over
the same threshold scan and a four-decade IR-cutoff scan. $\fpbh$ is exactly
zero in double precision at every point (Gaussian-limit
$\log_{10}\beta\approx-1.7\times10^9$ at $M_H=10^{20}\,$g, i.e.\ an
$n_\sigma=89{,}149$ Gaussian excursion; $89149^2/(2\ln10)=1.73\times10^9$;
\texttt{outputs/inlab\_delta2\_zeta\_2026-09-03.json}). \textbf{The PBH
channel is therefore a null for this model's own predicted spectrum}: it
neither constrains nor is evidenced by primordial black holes, and any
quotable PBH signal would require a small-scale amplification mechanism the
model does not contain -- a survey of four such candidate
mechanisms~\cite{Quintin2015,AgulloBolliet2017} finds none applies within the
model's validated ($k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$) domain, and this is no longer
only a literature survey: at $k\eta_B\approx3$ (an illustrative bounce
energy \emph{below} \S\ref{sec:pbh_compaction}'s validity condition,
$T_B=10^8\,$GeV, $k_B\approx1.714\times10^{15}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$, so
$k\approx5.1\times10^{15}\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$,
inside this paper's own PBH band), the extreme small-scale
end of the PBH band, the scheme-S1 bispectrum has now been evaluated
directly on exact (non-super-Hubble) LQC-dust mode functions
(Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission}) and gives $|\fnl|\approx0.9$--$1.9$, which
\emph{worsens} rather than relieves the required Gaussian excursion
(the required-excursion threshold, at the $M_H\sim10^{15}$--$10^{16}\,$g
end where the $7.0$-decade $\Delta_\zeta^2$ deficit above applies --
a $10^{3.5}=3162$ Gaussian-$\sigma$ excursion -- moves to
$3364\sigma$); even the largest number produced anywhere in that scan
(equilateral configuration, $|\fnl|\approx1.2\times10^3$) leaves the
threshold at $408\sigma$ against the $7.0$-decade amplitude deficit
established here, so no configuration, initial state, or evaluation time
tested reopens this channel.

The same spectrum is checked against the FIRAS $\mu$-distortion bound
($|\mu|<9\times10^{-5}$): the lab's own prediction gives
$\mu=1.65\times10^{-8}$, safely allowed at $1.8\times10^{-4}$ of the bound.
For the early-supermassive-black-hole-seed channel (a possible use of PBH
formation this ratio was originally motivated by), the \emph{required}
curvature amplitude for $10^3$--$10^6\,M_\odot$ seeds is FIRAS-excluded by
three orders of magnitude if broadband, and remains excluded for seeds
$\ge10^4\,M_\odot$ even as a narrow ($\Delta=0.5$) spectral peak; only the
lightest ($10^3\,M_\odot$) seeds evade the bound with a sufficiently narrow
peak. Combined with the null above, the honest statement is two-part: the
required SMBH-seed amplitude is excluded by three orders of magnitude
(broadband, and for $\ge10^4\,M_\odot$ narrow-peak seeds) independent of
this model, and this model's own spectrum falls far short of supplying it
either way.

\subsection{Current constraints}

The DESI DR1 analysis of $1{,}631{,}716$ luminous red galaxies
($0.6<z<1.1$) and $1{,}189{,}129$ quasars
($0.8<z<3.1$)~\cite{Chaussidon2024} gives
$\fnlloc = -3.6^{+9.0}_{-9.1}$ (asymmetric $9.0$/$9.1$) at $68\%$ confidence

$\fnlloc = +3.5^{+10.7}_{-7.4}$ (asymmetric $10.7$/$7.4$) assuming universality.
These two numbers come from mutually exclusive theoretical priors on the same
DESI DR1 data (merger-response vs. universality) and are not directly
comparable to each other; each is quoted here against its own prior. This
section compares against the pre-bounce amplitude $\fnlloc=-35/16$, since
that is the value the DESI DR1 analysis constrains directly, using the upper

$|\fnl|/\sigma = 0.24$: DESI DR1 is consistent with the prediction under
either prior and cannot test it.

re-analyze the public DESI DR1 QSO sample using DESI's own published
window matrix, full-randoms $P_\ell$, and EZmock covariance

\section{The tensor amplitude of the modelled background}
\label{sec:tensor}

Every result above is conditional on the canonical, constant-sound-speed
dust contraction used for the amplitude and its transmission. That
background is not free of its own tensor spectrum. In the dressed-metric
scheme the tensor and scalar canonical variables obey the identical
equation $\mu''+(k^2-a''/a)\mu=0$ from the identical Bunch--Davies
vacuum, so $r=16\epsilon$ identically for this class of background, and
the dust contraction's $\epsilon=3/2$ fixes $r=24$. Integrating the
tensor mode independently through each of the three bounce backgrounds
of Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission} returns transfers equal to the scalar
transfers to better than $8\times10^{-5}$, so $r$ is unchanged by the
bounce, $r_{\rm after}=23.996$--$24.000$: this exceeds the BICEP/Keck
bound $r<0.036$~\cite{BicepKeck2021} by a factor of $\sim670$. This is the
long-known matter-bounce large-$r$ problem~\cite{CaiEasson2012,
Quintin2015}, applied here to the lab's own background rather than
assumed from the literature: because tensors and scalars share the same
geometric potential, no bounce in the class considered relieves it.
Similarly, the exact dust contraction gives $n_s=1$; the observed
$n_s=0.9649$ is an anchor fixing the contraction's equation of state to
$w=-0.0029$ via $n_s-1=12w/(1+3w)$, not a prediction of the model. The
model's one genuinely predictive tilt statement is the consistency
relation $n_T=n_s-1=-0.035$, sharply different from single-field
inflation's $n_T=-r/8$, and a cheap, falsifiable discriminator that
survives everything else in this section. Propagating this
first-order tensor background to nHz through the same kernel used in
Sec.~\ref{sec:pta_null} gives $\OmGW h^2(f_{\rm yr})=1.7\times10^{-14}$,
still $10^{5.3}$ below the NANOGrav band: the PTA null of
Sec.~\ref{sec:pta_null} is unaffected by this section's own tensor
amplitude. A tensor-sense $r=0.84$ appeared in earlier drafts of this
program; it is withdrawn here, having been traced to the
noise-weighted bispectrum shape-overlap coefficient of
Sec.~\ref{sec:lss}, a shape correlation and not a tensor-to-scalar
ratio.

\section{The joint $(r,\fnl)$ no-go and the curvaton route}
\label{sec:nogo}

The textbook remedy for a large tensor-to-scalar ratio in a contracting
background is a reduced constant scalar sound speed $c_s<1$, which
suppresses $r$ while enhancing the scalar bispectrum. For the dust
contraction with constant $c_s$, two independent symbolic routes (a
general-$\nu$ Hankel limit and the exact $q=2$ mode functions) give
\begin{equation}
r=16\epsilon\,c_s^{2\nu-2}=24\,c_s,
\end{equation}
reproducing Li, Quintin, Wang \& Cai (2016)~\cite{Li2016} Eq.~(3.18) and
row 10's $r=24$ at $c_s=1$. The squeezed local amplitude, from their
Eq.~(4.19), is
\begin{equation}
\fnl^{\rm pre}(c_s)=-\frac{165}{16}+\frac{65}{8c_s^2},
\end{equation}
whose $c_s\to1$ limit reproduces this paper's own from-scratch
$-35/16$ exactly (Sec.~\ref{sec:parameter}). The transfer through the
bounce is $c_s$-independent to $\lesssim5\times10^{-11}$ across all
three backgrounds, because lowering $c_s$ only rescales the mode's
effective wavenumber and the transfer is already flat over
$k\eta_B\ll1$.

The two regions are disjoint by a factor $\sim296$ in $c_s$ (BICEP/Keck-viable
$c_s<1.5\times10^{-3}$ against Planck-viable $c_s\ge0.444$): no constant-$c_s$
value keeps both the tensor ratio and the local non-Gaussianity within
current bounds. This independently reproduces and strengthens Li
\textit{et al.}'s (2016) own no-go statement (their $r<0.07$ era bound
gives $c_s\lesssim0.0029$; the tighter current $r<0.036$ moves this to
$c_s<1.5\times10^{-3}$ and $\fnl^{\rm pre}=3.6\times10^{6}$, a factor
$3.8$ tighter). The alternative route of Quintin \textit{et al.}
(2015)~\cite{Quintin2015} -- a scalar-only super-Hubble amplification
through the bounce rather than a sound-speed change -- also fails on
the three backgrounds used throughout this paper: their Eq.~(31)
requires an amplification $\lambda\gtrsim49$ for $r<0.12$, while the
velocity-dip evaluation of Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission} (Eq.~79 of that
reference, evaluated on these backgrounds) finds $\lambda=4.0$--$6.1$
with no net $r$ suppression. \emph{The single-field matter bounce,
canonical or $k$-essence, is therefore excluded jointly by $r$ and
$\fnl$ on the backgrounds and channels used in this paper.} This
sharpens, rather than contradicts, the paper's earlier statements: the
exact amplitude $-35/16$ and its transmitted values remain correct
statements about this background, but that background is now shown to
be observationally excluded on tensor grounds independent of $\fnl$
itself, and no sound-speed fix rescues it.

A route that survives is a light curvaton-type spectator riding the
same contraction. Because it obeys the same Mukhanov--Sasaki operator
as the adiabatic mode at constant $\epsilon$, it inherits the identical
tilt $n_s-1=12w/(1+3w)$, and for a quadratic curvaton with sudden decay
fraction $r_{\rm dec}$,
\begin{equation}
r=\frac{24}{1+\frac{4}{3}r_{\rm dec}^2(M_{\rm pl}/\sigma_*)^2},\qquad
\fnl^{\rm curv}=\frac{5}{4r_{\rm dec}}-\frac{5}{3}-\frac{5r_{\rm dec}}{6},
\end{equation}
(Lyth--Ungarelli--Wands; Sasaki--Valiviita--Wands, applied here to
Cai--Xue--Brandenberger's 2011~\cite{CaiXue2011} matter-bounce
curvaton), which is $(r,n_s)$-tunable to observational viability and
gives an $O(1)$ non-Gaussianity in its own right ($+9.30$ to $-1.25$,
negative for $r_{\rm dec}>0.581$). The contraction's own intrinsic
$-35/16$ does not disappear in this picture; it enters the observable
bispectrum suppressed by the curvaton weight $(r/24)^2\simeq2\times10^{-6}$
at $r=0.036$, below any planned survey's reach for any tensor-viable
$r<11.5$ ($<23$ after this paper's own bounce transfer), and only
becomes detectable for $r\gtrsim23$ -- essentially the single-field
value this section excludes. Cai--Xue--Brandenberger's own quoted
$-320/\pi^4\approx-3.29$ is arithmetic on their order-of-magnitude
estimate, not an independently derived result, and is not adopted here.
Converting this route into a concrete, checkable prediction requires an
entropy-sector kinetic amplification mechanism and a demonstration that
$\sigma_*$ stays constant across the bounce backgrounds used in this
paper; neither is modelled here (Sec.~\ref{sec:next}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{The joint statement}

The matter-bounce prediction $\fnlloc=-35/16$ -- independently re-derived here
within the in-in formalism, confirming Li \textit{et al.} (2017) and
localizing the factor-of-two slip in Cai \textit{et al.} (2009), and now
confirmed by a method-independent route (Sec.~\ref{sec:crosscheck}) -- is a
correct statement about a background that Secs.~\ref{sec:tensor}
and~\ref{sec:nogo} show is jointly excluded by its own tensor-to-scalar
ratio and this same non-Gaussianity: no constant sound speed relieves both
at once. Conditional on that background, the amplitude
is not contradicted by any channel currently able to speak, but three of the
four channels tested are honest nulls rather than supporting consistencies.
Pulsar timing: propagating this model's own committed curvature spectrum
through the validated induced-GW kernel gives $\gamma_{\rm pred}=5.07$
($5.00$, dust bracket), a Gaussian-equivalent $5.1\sigma$ ($4.9\sigma$)
$z$-distance from NANOGrav's measured
$\gamma_{\rm HD}=3.2\pm0.6$, at an amplitude $10^{15.2}$ below the NANOGrav
band (Sec.~\ref{sec:pta_null}); the $\gamma=3$ slope quoted for a matter
bounce elsewhere in the literature~\cite{Papanikolaou2025} requires a
small-scale spectral enhancement this model does not have, and is retained
only as a flagged contrast case. This channel is also insensitive to the
value of $\fnl$ and therefore does not test it either way.
Primordial black holes yield a reproducible amplitude ratio
($1.84\pm0.03$ within this model's own $\gamma_{\rm cr}$ coverage)
between the two candidate values, but the lab's own predicted spectrum falls
$6.7$--$7.0$ orders of magnitude short of the required amplitude at every mass
scale considered (Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_inlab}): the channel is a clean null for
this model, not an open promise. The same lab spectrum falls three orders of
magnitude short of the amplitude an early-supermassive-black-hole-seed
channel would require (Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_inlab}): a third honest null.
Transmission through the bounce is now a
definite, scheme-qualified prediction rather than an open bound: within
scheme S1 and $k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$,
$\fnl^{\rm after}\in[-0.65,-0.50]$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic}). As
Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission} states, this $k\eta_B$ window is an upper bound
on $k$ and is satisfied \emph{most easily}, not hardest, at the CMB/LSS pivot
scale used by Channel~III below, so this transmitted range is precisely the
number that channel should be compared against. Galaxy surveys are the only
channel that discriminates between the two candidate amplitudes at all, but
at the transmitted amplitude that discrimination is weak: DESI DR1 is
consistent with $\fnl^{\rm after}$ at well under $1\sigma$ under either
prior, and SPHEREx reaches only $0.7$--$0.9\sigma$ ($-35/16$) or
$1.2$--$1.7\sigma$ ($-35/8$) bare significance (Table~\ref{tab:reach}), and
combining power spectrum and bispectrum widens the candidate separation to
only $0.5$--$1.1\sigma$ at current or forecast
LSS sensitivity, not the $3.13\sigma$ forecast detection significance quoted
for the pre-bounce
amplitude a naive reading of Channel III alone would suggest. No channel
observationally contradicts $\fnlloc=-35/16$; the honest summary is three
nulls (pulsar timing, PBH abundance, SMBH-seed abundance) and one channel
(large-scale structure) that is reachable in principle but does not yet
separate the two candidates.

\subsection{Where the consistency is weak}

The pulsar-timing channel is now a definite null rather than an open
consistency: as Sec.~\ref{sec:pta_null} shows, this model's own committed
spectrum predicts $\gamma_{\rm pred}\approx5$, not the $\gamma=3$
associated elsewhere in the literature with a small-scale-enhanced
matter-bounce source~\cite{Papanikolaou2025} this model does not have --
and it is identical at both candidate $\fnl$ values regardless. The
black hole channel now uses the literature's own formation criterion and the
lab's own spectrum rather than an external, unreconstructible one, and
returns a definite verdict (null) rather than an open amplitude scan; the
amplitude ratio remains the channel's only shape-robust, spectrum-independent
output, and does not by itself separate $-35/16$ from $-35/8$ observationally
-- only compares how hard each is to produce -- and $\fpbh(\fnl)$ itself was
shown to be strongly non-monotonic along the path to both candidates on the
earlier lognormal grid (Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_compaction}). The transmission
result is scheme-qualified and validated only for $k\eta_B\lesssim10^{-2}$;
scheme-independence, previously an open question, is now answered
negatively rather than left unresolved: the effective-fluid scheme S2,
evaluated on its raw (pre-integration-by-parts) cubic action with exact
mode functions, is finite -- the earlier ``S2 diverges'' statement traced
to a total-derivative artefact, not a physical pathology -- but gives
$\fnl^{\rm after}\approx-1.25$ on the one background tested (Quintin-type),
a factor $\approx2.5$ from S1's $-0.50$ on the same background
(Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic}). The transmitted amplitude is therefore
reported as a two-scheme band rather than a single scheme-S1 number, and
which scheme's linear variable is physical at $H=0$ remains an open
theory question this paper does not resolve. At the transmitted amplitude
the survey channel's discriminating power is itself the weak link: SPHEREx
separates the two candidates by $|\Delta\fnl^{\rm after}|\approx0.35$--$0.55$
against $\sigma_{\fnl}=0.5$--$0.7$, i.e.\ $0.5$--$1.1\sigma$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:lss}), so no currently forecast LSS survey can distinguish
$-35/16$ from $-35/8$ once scheme-S1 transmission is applied; a
not-yet-derived shape-overlap projection $r<1$ would only narrow this
separation further, and since $r$ has not been computed at this paper's
fiducial no quantitative statement about its effect is made here.

\subsection{Next steps}
\label{sec:next}

In priority order: (i)~settling which linear variable ($z=a$ or the
effective-fluid $z^2\propto\epsilon/c_s^2$) is the physically correct
continuation through $H=0$ -- a theory question, not a numerical one, since
both schemes are now finite and give genuinely different answers rather
than differing by a regularization choice (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic});
(ii)~an evaluation-time (freeze-out) prescription for the bispectrum at
$k\eta_B\gtrsim0.1$, where this model's absence of a post-bounce
inflationary phase leaves $\zeta$ unfrozen and the bounce-window cubic
integral dependent on when it is evaluated by a factor of several, so that
the $k\eta_B\approx0.6$--$0.8$ transfer feature and the $k\eta_B\sim1$--$3$
exact-mode bispectrum of Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission} can be quoted as
single numbers rather than ranges; (iii)~re-derivation of the shape
overlap $r$ at the $-35/16$ fiducial; (iv)~extending the S2 raw-ADM
computation to the LQC and poly backgrounds, and to the $-35/8$ amplitude,
neither of which is computed here (Sec.~\ref{sec:bounce_cubic},
Table~\ref{tab:reach}); and (v)~a direct-collapse (rather than
horizon-re-entry) primordial-black-hole formation channel during the dust
contraction phase itself, identified but not evaluated in
Sec.~\ref{sec:pbh_inlab}. The propagation of the post-bounce amplitude to
the CMB/LSS pivot and of the scalar spectrum through the induced-GW kernel
to the nHz amplitude are carried
out in Secs.~\ref{sec:lss} and~\ref{sec:pta_null}; the bounce-scale
enhancement mechanisms at $k\eta_B\sim1$ that would be the remaining route
to reopen Channel~I or the black-hole channel have been checked directly
(velocity dip, transfer excursion, and LQC
cubic-operator enhancement, Sec.~\ref{sec:transmission}) and found not to
reopen either. Two further items follow directly from
Secs.~\ref{sec:tensor}--\ref{sec:nogo}: (vi)~converting the curvaton route
of Sec.~\ref{sec:nogo} into a concrete, checkable prediction requires an
entropy-sector kinetic-amplification mechanism realized on the A2 bounce
backgrounds used throughout this paper, and a demonstration that
$\sigma_*$ stays constant across them -- neither is modelled here, and
Cai--Xue--Brandenberger's own Eqs.~(62)--(64) (the $\zeta_{NL}$ integral
through deflation and bounce) remain un-re-derived by this lab; and
(vii)~the consistency relation $n_T=n_s-1=-0.035$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:tensor})
is a surviving, cheap discriminator of this class of background from
single-field inflation independent of the $(r,\fnl)$ no-go, worth a
dedicated forecast.

\section{The $\delta N_c$--$\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ derivation}
\label{app:derivation}

This appendix transcribes, without new science, the derivation archived in
the monopole-adjudication note cited in the reproducibility statement below
(\S\S1--4) that underlies Eq.~\eqref{eq:deltaNc} and the [L]/[K]/[X]/[S]
decomposition of Sec.~\ref{sec:crosscheck}.

\subsection{The linear-order identity, Eq.~\eqref{eq:deltaNc}}

On a uniform-$\phi$ slice the lapse is $N=1+\dot\zeta/H$, so the Friedmann
equation at fixed $\phi$ gives $\delta H/H=-(\epsilon/3)\dot\zeta_L/H$: the
local expansion rate on comoving slices is perturbed at $O(k^0)$, carried by
the trace of the shift gradient, $\delta K=-\partial^2\psi/a^2\to
-\epsilon\dot\zeta_L$. The separate-universe $\delta N$ is defined on the
zero-shift threading, $\delta N_c=\int(K/3)\,d\tau$, so writing
$\delta N_c=\zeta+b$ with $\dot b=\delta K/3$ gives, on the growing mode,
$b=-(\epsilon/3)\zeta_L$, i.e.\ Eq.~\eqref{eq:deltaNc},
$\delta N_c=(1-\epsilon/3)\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ at linear order (sign convention:
$\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ and $\delta N_c$ share the sign of the comoving-gauge
curvature perturbation used throughout this paper).

\subsection{The $[L]/[K]/[X]/[S]$ decomposition}
\label{app:decomp}

Separating the long mode's coupling to the short-mode pair by how it enters:

\begin{center}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{lp{2.6cm}p{2.1cm}}
\hline\hline
class & long mode enters as & squeezed value at $\epsilon=3/2$ \\
\hline
$[L]$ & $\zeta_L,\dot\zeta_L$ (lapse/curvature; local response) & $-25/8$, pure monopole \\
$[K]$ & $\partial_i\partial_j\tilde\chi_L$ ($\delta K$ and shear) & $0$ (cancels between varied legs of $T_4$) \\
$[X]$ & $\partial_i\tilde\chi_L$ (the \emph{shift}, $\propto1/k_L$) & $\tfrac{15}{16}+\tfrac{15}{16}\mu^2$ (monopole $5/4$) \\
$[S]$ & short-mode sourcing of the long mode & $0$ (suppressed) \\
\hline
total & & $-\tfrac{35}{16}+\tfrac{15}{16}\mu^2$ (= in-in exactly) \\
comoving $\delta N$ (lanes A/B/C) & different variable, no $[X]$ & $-5$, isotropic \\
\hline\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $1/k_L$ poles in $[X]$'s two contributions -- the $T_3$ bulk coupling
and the boundary term $f_b$ below -- cancel only in their sum, leaving a
finite $O(k_L^0)$ remainder; no separate-universe construction (isotropic or
Bianchi-I) contains this term, because it originates in the comoving-gauge
\emph{shift} $N_i=\partial_i\psi\supset a^2\epsilon\,\partial^{-2}\dot\zeta$,
which is $O(1/k_L)$ in this non-attractor phase and so violates the
gradient-expansion assumption $N_i=O(\nabla)$ every separate-universe
construction relies on. The final-time re-threading boundary term,
\begin{equation}
f_b=\frac{\epsilon}{2\mathcal H}\Big[\partial\zeta\,\partial\tilde\chi
-\partial^{-2}\partial_i\partial_j(\partial_i\zeta\,\partial_j\tilde\chi)\Big],
\end{equation}
subtracts $(1-\epsilon/3)\times\tfrac{5\epsilon}{4}\mu^2$ from the $T_3$
bulk's $\mu^2$ coupling and adds the $+15/16$ monopole, reproducing the
$\mu^2$ coefficient exactly and supplying the [X] monopole. In-in monopole minus
$\delta N_c$: $-\tfrac{15}{8}-(-5)=\tfrac{25}{8}=[X]_{\rm mono}\,(5/4)+
([L]-\delta N_c)\,(15/8)$; in general $\epsilon$,
$5\epsilon(9-\epsilon)/18=5\epsilon(9-2\epsilon)/36+5\epsilon/4$. The
zero-shift, fluid-worldline threading identity underlying $\delta N_c$ is
$\delta N_c(x_f)=\zeta(t_f,x_f)-\tfrac13\int\partial_iN^i\,dt$ along the
worldline; expanding it to second order at constant $\epsilon$ gives
$f_{\delta N_c}=f^{\rm in\text{-}in}/\lambda+f_{\rm map}$ with
$\lambda=1-\epsilon/3$ and $f_{\rm map}=-\tfrac{5\epsilon}{4}
+\tfrac{5\epsilon}{4}\mu^2$, an isotropic monopole of exactly $-5$ for
every constant $\epsilon$. A pure rigid translation of the short-mode
field is isotropic and therefore cannot by itself supply a monopole; the
numerical match of $f_{\rm map}$'s coefficient to a translation-like form
is a coincidence of this threading map, not evidence that translation is
the mechanism -- the position-dependent piece doing the work is the
shear/dilation of the fluid displacement field, not its rigid part.

\subsection{General-$\epsilon$ formulas}

The comoving separate-universe results, for general $\epsilon$, are
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm NL}^\rho=\frac{5(\epsilon-7)}{8},\qquad f_{\rm NL}^c=-5\quad(\text{all }\epsilon),
\end{equation}
and the in-in squeezed bispectrum, general $\epsilon$, is
\begin{equation}
f^{\rm sq}(\mu,\epsilon)=\frac{5}{12}\big(\epsilon^2\mu^2-\epsilon^2+6\epsilon-12\big),
\end{equation}
with monopole $-5(\epsilon-3)(\epsilon-6)/18$, isosceles ($\mu=0$) value
$-5(\epsilon^2-6\epsilon+12)/12$, and $\mu^2$ coefficient $5\epsilon^2/12$;
at $\epsilon=3/2$ these reduce to the monopole $-15/8$, the isosceles value
$-35/16$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:orientation}'s $\mu=0$ limit, and the
$+15/16\,\mu^2$ coefficient (Legendre $\ell=2$ coefficient $5/8$) quoted
throughout this paper. These general-$\epsilon$ expressions hold on the
non-attractor growing branch ($\dot\zeta=-3\zeta/\eta$) that this paper's
entire construction uses, and do \emph{not} reduce to the slow-roll
attractor (Maldacena) result $(5/12)(1-n_s)\to0$ as $\epsilon\to0$; they are
not claimed to.

\subsection{Bianchi-I: why an anisotropic separate universe does not close the gap}

An anisotropic (Bianchi-I) separate-universe treatment, carrying the long
mode's projected shear onto the short-mode power spectrum exactly, gives an
identically zero monopole contribution: the shear is $\mathcal O(k_L^0)$ and
traceless, and no traceless linear-in-$\zeta_L$ response can supply a
monopole. It therefore cannot by itself close the $-25/8$ gap between
$\delta N^c=-5$ and the in-in monopole $-15/8$; the gap is closed only by
$[X]$, the shift term no separate-universe construction contains
(Sec.~\ref{app:decomp} above).
