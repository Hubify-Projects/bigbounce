## Question

Channel I compares only the **second-order** (scalar-induced) background, while
Table II's `γ = 5` row is labelled "prim. tensors". The model's own **first-order**
tensor amplitude at nHz is never stated. Which of the two dominates in the
NANOGrav band, and does that change the PTA null?

## Method

Primordial tensor spectrum on the same near-dust background,
`P_T(k) = r A_s (k/k_*)^{n_T}` with `A_s = 2.1e-9`, `k_* = 0.05 Mpc⁻¹`.
Propagated to today with the standard radiation-era result for a first-order
tensor mode deep inside the horizon (Watanabe & Komatsu 2006, PRD 73, 123515;
Caprini & Figueroa 2018 review §2):

    Ω_GW(k)|_production = P_T(k)/24 ,
    Ω_GW,0 h²           = 1.62e-5 · (g_*/106.75)^{-1/3} · Ω_GW|_production ,

using the **same** `f ↔ k` map and the **same** transfer prefactor already used
for the induced background in `sigw_nhz_from_lab_spectrum_2026_09_04.py`, so the
two channels are compared on one convention. Baseline `g_* = 106.75`; the
`g_*(T ≈ 0.2 GeV) ≈ 20` variant appropriate to nHz horizon entry raises every
number by ×1.75 and is carried in the JSON.

**`r` is reported in two cases**, because the matter-bounce tensor-to-scalar ratio
is an open item in this program (A3-4, the `r = 0.84` re-derivation):

- **CASE A (adopted, conservative):** the CMB observational bound `r < 0.036`
  (95% CL, BICEP/Keck + Planck; Ade *et al.* 2021, PRL **127**, 151301,
  arXiv:2110.00483). This is an **upper limit**, so the resulting `Ω_GW^{(1)}` is
  an upper limit.
- **CASE B (scenario, not adopted):** `r = 0.84` (Cai *et al.* 2009 matter-bounce
  value; A3-4 open). Already excluded at `k_*` by CASE A; quoted only to bracket
  the scenario.

Tensor tilt from the same background: `n_T = n_s − 1 = −0.0351` (Planck-anchored
branch) and `n_T = 0` (pure-dust bracket — the paper's own "prim. tensors,
`n_T = 0`, `Ω_GW ∝ f⁰`" convention at `main.tex :550, :569`, Table II).

## Result

| case | `n_T` | `Ω_GW^{(1)} h²(f_yr)` | vs induced | vs NANOGrav |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, `r < 0.036`, anchored | −0.0351 | **< 2.54e−17** | 10^{+6.24} | 10^{−8.15} |
| A, `r < 0.036`, dust      | 0       | < 5.10e−17 | 10^{+5.94} | 10^{−7.85} |
| B, `r = 0.84`, anchored   | −0.0351 | 5.94e−16 | 10^{+7.61} | 10^{−6.79} |
| B, `r = 0.84`, dust       | 0       | 1.19e−15 | 10^{+7.31} | 10^{−6.48} |

Comparators: induced `Ω_GW h²(f_yr) = 1.4545e−23` (anchored) / `5.8764e−23`
(dust); NANOGrav 15-yr HD power law `Ω_GW h²(f_yr) = 3.6235e−9`.

**Which dominates:** the **first-order** tensor background, by **6.2 decades**
even at the CMB upper bound, and by 7.6 decades in the excluded `r = 0.84`
scenario. Channel I's induced background is therefore *not* the model's largest
nHz signal — but it is also not the one that decides the null.

**Does the PTA null change: NO.** Even the first-order upper limit sits
`10^{8.2}` below the NANOGrav band (`10^{6.5}` below in the excluded `r = 0.84`
scenario). The slope test is likewise unchanged: `n_T = n_s − 1` gives
`γ_first-order = 5.035`, within `0.04` of the induced `γ_pred = 5.070`, so both
channels sit on the same side of the measured `γ_HD = 3.2 ± 0.6`.

## Statement the paper may make (at evidential strength)

> The model's own first-order tensor background dominates the scalar-induced one
> at nHz by roughly six decades — at the CMB bound `r < 0.036` (BICEP/Keck,
> arXiv:2110.00483) and the same background's `n_T = n_s − 1`,
> `Ω_GW^{(1)} h²(f_yr) < 2.5×10⁻¹⁷` against the induced `1.45×10⁻²³` — but it is
> still eight orders of magnitude below NANOGrav's `3.62×10⁻⁹`, and its slope
> (`γ = 5.04`) is within `0.04` of the induced value, so Channel I remains a null
> in both amplitude and slope whichever channel is used. In the unresolved
> `r = 0.84` matter-bounce scenario (A3-4) the shortfall narrows only to
> `10^{6.5}`, and that `r` is itself excluded at `k_*` by the same CMB bound.
