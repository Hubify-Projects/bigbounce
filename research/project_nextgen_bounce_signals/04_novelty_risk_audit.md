# 04: Novelty Risk Audit

**Created:** 2026-03-17
**Status:** COMPLETE

---

## A. Bounce + Entropy/Curvaton

**How much is already in the literature?**
Fully explored. Cai & Brandenberger (2011), Alexander et al. (2014), Cai et al. (2009, 2011). The entire curvaton-in-matter-bounce program is 10+ years old.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
Yes — any curvaton tilt mechanism gives the same generic result (n_s < 1 from mass, f_NL from curvaton fraction). The bounce is transparent to the relevant modes.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
Only if the curvaton interacts with the bounce non-trivially (e.g., conversion AT the bounce, not after). But this requires non-transparency, which belongs to Class H.

**Novelty type:** None of the above (existing work covers all angles)

**Rating: LIKELY_ALREADY_SATURATED**

---

## B. Bounce + Sourced Parity/Chiral GW

**How much is already in the literature?**
Partial. Zhu & Cai (2023) showed parity-violation enhancement at the bounce in a Chern-Simons context. Jiang et al. (2024) used the Nieh-Yan term in an inflationary NEC-violation context. Nobody has computed the quantitative chiral GW spectrum from a torsion bounce.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
No. The chiral GW spectrum from a torsion bounce is not a known result. Inflationary Chern-Simons birefringence is well-studied, but the bounce-specific version (parity violation maximal at H = 0, curvature ~ Planckian) is different: the enhancement mechanism (H reversal rather than slow-roll) is new.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
- **Theory construction:** Connecting the Holst/Nieh-Yan parity-odd gravitational structure to chiral GW production at the bounce
- **Observable prediction:** Quantitative circular polarization fraction V/I as a function of frequency
- **Parameter-space exclusion:** Using GW detector upper limits to constrain the bounce-scale parity coupling

**Novelty type:** Theory construction + observable prediction

**Rating: HIGH_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

---

## C. Bounce + Time-Asymmetric / Dissipative

**How much is already in the literature?**
Mostly conceptual. Sahni & Toporensky (2012) and others studied cosmological hysteresis at the background level. Peter et al. (2020) studied asymmetric quantum bounces. de Cesare (2024) discussed arrows of time. Nobody has computed perturbation observables from an asymmetric bounce.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
No. The perturbation-level consequences of time asymmetry at the bounce are unexplored. The question "what does an asymmetric transfer function look like?" is open.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
- **Observable prediction:** Asymmetric transfer function T(k) that differs from the symmetric T(k) = 1
- **Theory construction:** A specific dissipation mechanism that breaks time symmetry at the bounce
- **Parameter-space exclusion:** Constraining the degree of asymmetry from CMB or GW data

**Novelty type:** Theory construction + observable prediction

**Rating: MODERATE_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

Downgraded from HIGH because: (1) specifying the dissipation mechanism adds significant theoretical overhead, and (2) the observable may be degenerate with other sources of spectral features.

## D. Bounce + PBH Window

**How much is already in the literature?**
Heavily occupied. Papanikolaou et al. (2024) computed PBH + SIGW from matter bounce. Ye et al. (2026) found negative results for dust-radiation bounce. Choudhury et al. (2024) connected bounce non-Gaussianity to PTA via PBH. Gaztanaga (2026) proposed nonlinear bounce relics.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
Mostly yes. Any PBH calculation from a bounce will overlap with Papanikolaou et al.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
Only in a very specific feature mechanism not yet studied (e.g., torsion-induced resonance enhancing specific scales).

**Novelty type:** Exact implementation only (if feature mechanism is new)

**Rating: LOW_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

---

## E. Bounce + Modified SIGW Kernel

**How much is already in the literature?**
The standard SIGW calculation applied to bounces exists (Papanikolaou 2025, Li 2025, Lai & Li 2025). But nobody has modified the SIGW kernel to account for the non-standard bounce-phase dynamics.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
Partially. If the kernel modification is small (likely for super-Hubble modes), the result is a small correction to existing work — useful but not flagship-worthy. If the kernel modification is large for some frequency range, that's genuinely new.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
- **Theory construction:** Deriving the SIGW kernel in a NEC-violating background
- **Observable prediction:** Modified SIGW spectral shape that could be distinguished from the standard kernel in PTA or LISA data

**Novelty type:** Exact implementation → observable prediction

**Rating: MODERATE_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

## F. Bounce + Phase Transition

**How much is already in the literature?**
Essentially absent. Nobody has studied cosmological phase transitions in a bouncing background. The entire literature on first-order phase transition GWs assumes expanding FRW.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
No — the result would be genuinely new. However, the theoretical overhead is very high: need to specify both the bounce model AND the phase transition model.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
- **Theory construction:** Phase transition dynamics in a contracting-then-expanding background
- **Observable prediction:** Modified GW spectrum from bubble collisions in a bouncing background
- **Parameter-space exclusion:** Constraints on dark-sector phase transitions from bounce GW observations

**Novelty type:** Theory construction + observable prediction

**Rating: MODERATE_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

Downgraded from HIGH because: (1) massive theoretical overhead, (2) must specify the phase transition sector, (3) the bounce-specific modification may be subdominant if the phase transition occurs far from the bounce.

---

## G. Bounce + GW Memory

**How much is already in the literature?**
No prior work on GW memory specifically from a cosmological bounce event. Bieri et al. (2024) and Unal & Veske (2025) treat memory generically but not from the bounce.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
No — this would be the first computation of bounce-sourced GW memory.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
- **Theory construction:** Formulating the nonlinear GW memory calculation in a bouncing background
- **Observable prediction:** A specific low-frequency SGWB contribution from bounce memory

**Novelty type:** Theory construction + observable prediction

**Rating: MODERATE_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

Kept at MODERATE because: the memory signal is likely confined to very high frequencies (near the bounce scale), making it unobservable. The calculation would be theoretically interesting but may not produce a measurable signal.

---

## H. Bounce + Prolonged/Non-Transparent Phase

**How much is already in the literature?**
Partial. Zhu & Cai (2026) computed resonant GW echoes from a double-peak bounce potential (very recent, March 2026). Steinhardt distinguishes fast vs slow bounces conceptually. But systematic exploration of non-transparent bounce bands is absent.

**Would the result reproduce known stories?**
Partially overlaps with Zhu & Cai (2026) for GW echoes. But the scalar perturbation transfer through a prolonged bounce with EOS-dependent non-transparency is not in the literature.

**Where could novelty still exist?**
- **Observable prediction:** Frequency-dependent transfer function producing observable features in the SGWB
- **Theory construction:** Identifying which bounce structures (EOS profiles, potential shapes) produce maximally non-transparent transfer

**Novelty type:** Theory construction → observable prediction

**Rating: MODERATE_NOVELTY_POTENTIAL**

Potential overlap with very recent Zhu & Cai (2026) work is a risk.
