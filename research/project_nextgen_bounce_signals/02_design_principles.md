# 02: Design Principles for Next-Generation Bounce Theory

**Created:** 2026-03-17
**Status:** COMPLETE

---

## Required Properties

### P1: Must beat transparency
The bounce must imprint something on the perturbation spectrum that was not already present in the pre-bounce state. This requires at least one of:
- Modes that are NOT super-Hubble at the bounce (k ~ k_b) and evolve non-trivially
- A new degree of freedom that is excited at the bounce and couples to observable modes
- A time-asymmetric process that converts pre-bounce information into a post-bounce signal with different character

### P2: Must preserve nonsingular bounce logic
The theory must still resolve the cosmological singularity. The bounce must be regular, with finite curvature and well-defined perturbation evolution. No singularity-hiding or formal matching across a singular surface.

### P3: Must not rely on arbitrary parameter patches
The observable signal must arise from the theory's structure, not from a free parameter tuned to produce the desired output. One or two free parameters is acceptable (like amplitude normalization). Five or more is not a theory — it's a fit.

### P4: Must produce an observable not generic to prior literature
The prediction must survive a novelty audit. If the same signal is predicted by generic matter bounce, generic curvaton, or generic bouncing cosmology with unspecified bounce mechanism, it is not novel.

### P5: Must be testable or constrainable
The signal must target a real observable:
- CMB power spectrum, bispectrum, or polarization
- Stochastic GW background (PTA, LISA, ET, DECIGO)
- GW polarization (circular polarization / V-mode Stokes)
- Large-scale structure features
- PBH abundance constraints

### P6: Must use minimal new ingredients
Each new field, coupling, or symmetry-breaking mechanism adds theoretical overhead. The best theory is the one that produces the largest observable signal with the fewest new inputs beyond the bounce background.

## Forbidden Moves

### F1: Pure phenomenological knob-turning
No: "We add parameter X and tune it until we get n_s = 0.965." The tilt (or other observable) must emerge from the theory's dynamics, not from parameter adjustment.

### F2: Adding a field with no real dynamical role
No: "We include field σ but it doesn't affect the bounce, and the bounce doesn't affect it." The new ingredient must interact with the bounce in a way that produces the signal.

### F3: Reproducing standard results without a new angle
No: "We compute the matter-bounce curvaton spectrum using Model X instead of Model Y." If the answer is the same (generic curvaton tilt), the model label doesn't matter.

### F4: Rebranding generic ALP cosmology as bounce physics
No: "The ALP exists in our framework, so any ALP cosmology result is a bounce prediction." The bounce must actually do something to the ALP dynamics that it doesn't do elsewhere.

### F5: Reopening closed ECH branches
No: dark energy from ECH, ECH perturbation corrections, or minimal torsion at the perturbation level. These are permanently closed by the structural barriers.

### F6: Claiming novelty from computational details
No: "We solved the equation numerically instead of analytically." If the physics is the same, the method doesn't create novelty.

---

## What Counts as Genuine Novelty

A genuinely novel bounce prediction satisfies ALL of:
1. The observable depends on bounce-phase dynamics (not just the contraction phase)
2. The observable cannot be reproduced by inflation or by a generic bounce with different bounce mechanism
3. The theory construction identifies a specific signal that is computable and falsifiable
4. The literature search confirms no prior computation of this specific signal in this specific framework
