\title{The separate universe computes a different variable: an exact criterion\\
for $\delta N=\zeta$ in non-attractor phases}

\author{Houston Golden}
\email{houston@hubify.com}
\altaffiliation[ORCID: ]{\href{https://orcid.org/0009-0008-5616-5994}{0009-0008-5616-5994}}
\affiliation{Independent Researcher, Los Angeles, California, USA}

\date{\paperTimestamp}

\begin{abstract}
The isotropic separate universe ($\delta N$) computes $\delta N_c$, the e-fold count of a
fluid worldline labelled by its position on the initial slice; the standard $\delta N$
formula identifies $\delta N_c$ with Maldacena's $\zeta$. We show this identification is an
exact linear-order, super-Hubble change of variable,
$\delta N_c=\zeta_{L,f}[1-I/3+O(k_L^2/a^2H^2)]$, with
$I=\zeta_{L,f}^{-1}\int(\eps/c_s^2)\dot\zeta_L\,dt$ the $\zeta$-growth-weighted mean:
$\delta N_c=\zeta$ at linear order iff $I\to0$. At second order the worldline label matters:
the initial-position label (the separate universe's own label) composes the in-in
bispectrum back to the isotropic separate-universe value, $-5$, for every constant $\eps$;
the final-position label differs by a translation term with zero monopole. We present one
nontrivial check (matter-dominated contraction, $I\to\eps$, an $O(1)$ identification error)
plus three consistency limits -- attractor slow roll and ekpyrosis ($I=0$, identity map)
and ultra-slow-roll ($I=O(\sqrt{\eps_s\eps_f})$, not computed here) -- and place the
criterion against the non-attractor consistency-relation and beyond-$\delta N$
gradient-expansion literature. There is no discrepancy in the physics, only in which
variable $\delta N_c$ is compared to.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation}

The squeezed limit of the primordial bispectrum is governed, in single-clock inflation, by
Maldacena's consistency relation, and can equivalently be computed by the separate-universe
($\delta N$) formalism, which treats the long-wavelength mode as a local shift of the
background evolution~\cite{Maldacena2003}. In a non-attractor phase, where $\dot\zeta\neq0$
on super-Hubble scales, two independent difficulties arise. First,
Namjoo, Firouzjahi \& Sasaki showed that ultra-slow-roll (USR) inflation violates the
consistency relation, $\fnl=5/2$, and that $\delta N$ built from $N(\phi)$ alone misses
this: the growing mode is an independent initial datum, requiring $N(\phi,\pi)$ to be
correctly captured~\cite{Namjoo2013}. This initial-data requirement was extended to
$P(X)$ non-attractors by Chen, Firouzjahi, Namjoo \& Sasaki, who found
$\fnl=5(1+c_s^2)/(4c_s^2)$ and confirmed $\delta N$ with momentum
agrees~\cite{Chen2013}. Pajer, Schmidt \& Zaldarriaga separately argued that in genuine
attractors the squeezed bispectrum is an unobservable coordinate artifact of the long
mode~\cite{Pajer2013}, a statement later sharpened with conformal Fermi coordinates by
Dai, Pajer \& Schmidt~\cite{Dai2015}. Cai \etal\ tracked $\fnl$ through smooth and sharp
USR transitions and found $\delta N$ and in-in agree throughout~\cite{Cai2018}. Bravo,
Mooij, Palma \& Pradenas derived a generalized consistency relation for non-attractor
backgrounds and showed the canonical single-field local $\fnl$ is removable by a change of
physical coordinates~\cite{Bravo2018a,Bravo2018b}, and Passaglia, Hu \& Motohashi confirmed
$\delta N(\phi,\pi)$/in-in agreement for PBH-motivated USR
scenarios~\cite{Passaglia2019}. Separately, Artigas, Grain \& Vennin, and Jackson
\etal, identified a phase-space separate-universe breakdown localized to a finite window of
scales at slow-roll-to-USR transitions, traced to a momentum/initial-data
mismatch~\cite{Artigas2022,Jackson2023}.

Second, and logically independent, a companion theory-audit note on this program found
that the isotropic separate universe applied to a matter-dominated cosmological
contraction ($w=0$, $\eps=3/2$) returns $f_{\delta N}=-5$ (initial-position label) while
the from-scratch in-in squeezed bispectrum of $\zeta$ is
$-35/16+(15/16)\mu^2$~\cite{Golden2026Monopole}, a monopole gap of $25/8$ -- an $O(1)$
discrepancy, not a small correction. Cai, Xue, Brandenberger \& Zhang quote a squeezed (isoceles) amplitude of $-35/8$ for the
same background~\cite{Cai2009}; the from-scratch shape is uniformly a factor of $2$
smaller ($-35/16+\tfrac{15}{16}\mu^2$, monopole $-15/8$), and \cite{Golden2026Monopole}
locates the slip in their amplitude step rather than in any vertex or commutator,
reproducing their shape function monomial by monomial (Li, Quintin, Wang \& Cai
reproduce the same polynomial~\cite{LiQuintinWangCai2017}). Were the from-scratch shape
uniformly twice as large instead, the monopole would be $-15/4$ and the gap to
$f_{\delta N}^{\rm init}=-5$ would be $-5/4$, a ratio of $4/3$ rather than $8/3$. The
linear criterion $\lambda=1-\eps/3$ is independent of any bispectrum; only the
second-order $-5$ composition uses the in-in input. A second companion
note derived the exact
second-order map between the two variables and showed the gap decomposes into a linear
rescaling and an explicit second-order term, both proportional to
$\eps$~\cite{Golden2026Threading}. Every prior test of $\delta N$/in-in agreement in the
literature above is an inflationary non-attractor, where $\eps\ll1$ throughout the growth
of the long mode. This note asks the question directly: is there a single criterion,
applicable to both inflationary and contracting non-attractors, that predicts when the
two constructions agree and by how much they differ when they do not.

\section{The threading identity and the map}

Comoving gauge, ADM variables $N=1+\alpha$, $N_i=\partial_i\psi+\tilde N_i$. The
separate universe evolves each patch as a homogeneous FRW region identified by its fluid
element's position $x_i$ on a flat, super-Hubble initial slice $\Sigma_i$ ($k_L\ll a_iH_i$);
its output is the e-fold count $\delta N_c$ of the fluid (normal) congruence, labelled by
$x_i$, from $\Sigma_i$ to the comoving final slice $\Sigma_f$. Along a fluid worldline
$\dot x^i=-N^i$ the fully nonlinear relation between the local expansion and the volume
element gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\delta N_c(x_i)=\zeta(t_f,x_f)-\frac13\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\!\partial_iN^i\big(t,x(t)\big)\,dt,
\quad x_f=x(t_f),
\label{eq:threading}
\end{equation}
with $\delta\rho_c/\rho\propto\partial_iN^i$ up to a gradient term; $\partial_iN^i$ is the
coordinate divergence of the contravariant shift $N^i$, and $u^\mu\parallel n^\mu$
(fluid four-velocity along the ADM normal) presumes a single scalar degree of freedom,
i.e.\ no vorticity. On super-Hubble scales,
$\partial_iN^i=(\eps/c_s^2)\dot\zeta-\nabla^2\zeta/(a^2H)$ for the $P(X)$ class (a genuine
fluid with non-adiabatic pressure adds a term)~\cite{Maldacena2003,ChenHuang2007}. Splitting
the interval at the comoving slice through $x_i$ and integrating,
\begin{equation}
\delta N_c=\zeta_{L,f}\Big[1-\frac{I}{3}+O\!\Big(\frac{k_L^2}{a^2H^2}\Big)\Big],\qquad
I\equiv\frac{1}{\zeta_{L,f}}\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\!\frac{\eps}{c_s^2}\,\dot\zeta_L\,dt,
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
exact on super-Hubble scales for any single-field history from a flat, super-Hubble initial
slice; the dropped gradient term is $O(k_L^2/a_iH_i^2)$ or $O(k_L^2/a_fH_f^2)$, whichever
end of the interval dominates. $I\to0$ (identity map) on any constant mode, including the
attractor and the dominant ekpyrotic mode; $1-\lambda=I/3$ reduces to $\eps/3$ for constant
$\eps$ (i.e.\ $I\to\eps$), so $\lambda\equiv1-I/3=1-\eps/3$, and to
$\lambda_{\rm USR}=1+\eps_f/3-\sqrt{\eps_s\eps_f}/3$ for USR with $\eps=\eps_s(a/a_s)^{-6}$
from a slow-roll-frozen $\zeta_{L,i}\neq0$ at $a_s$, $\zeta_L\propto a^3$ thereafter. At second
order, for constant $\eps$ and $c_s=1$, assuming $\zeta_L(t_i)=0$ (a pure growing mode, no
constant piece on the initial slice), solving the exact ADM constraints to
$O(\zeta_L\zeta_S)$ and integrating Eq.~\eqref{eq:threading} gives
$f_{\delta N}=f^{\rm in\text{-}in}/\lambda+f_{\rm map}$, where $f_{\rm map}$ depends on how
the fluid worldline is labelled. Labelled by \emph{initial} position $x_i$ -- the label the
separate universe uses, since it never learns where the fluid element ends up --
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}(\eps,\mu)=\frac{5\eps}{4(3-\eps)}\big[(\eps-2)-\eps\mu^2\big],
\label{eq:fmap}
\end{equation}
and $f_{\delta N}^{\rm init}\equiv f^{\rm in\text{-}in}/\lambda+f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}=-5$,
isotropic, for every constant $\eps$. Labelled instead by \emph{final} position
$x_f=x(t_f)$, the two labels differ by a pure translation $\xi^i=-\int N_L^i\,dt$ along the
long mode, adding a shear-plus-dilation term with zero monopole:
\begin{equation}
f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}(\eps,\mu)=-\frac{5\eps}{4}(1-\mu^2)=f_{\rm map}^{\rm init}-
\frac{5\eps}{4(3-\eps)}(1-3\mu^2),
\label{eq:fmapfin}
\end{equation}
giving $f_{\delta N}^{\rm fin}=-\tfrac{15(\eps-4)}{4(\eps-3)}+\tfrac{15\eps}{4(3-\eps)}\mu^2$
($-\tfrac{25}{4}+\tfrac{15}{4}\mu^2$ at $\eps=3/2$); $\mu=\hat k_L\!\cdot\!\hat k_S$. Both
labels give the same, label-independent monopole $-5\eps/6$; only the quadrupole is a
labelling convention.\footnote{Each of the five geometric contributions to
$f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}$ carries an overall factor of $\eps$: the lapse, the second-order
metric perturbation, the gradient term, the final-position worldline displacement, and the
initial-label translation~\cite{Golden2026Threading}.} In terms of a constant equation of
state $w$ ($\eps=\tfrac32(1+w)$, $c_s=1$),
\begin{equation}
\lambda=\frac{1-w}{2},\qquad
f_{\rm map}^{\rm fin}=-\frac{15}{8}(1+w)(1-\mu^2),
\label{eq:generalw}
\end{equation}
with label-independent monopole $f_{\rm map}^{\rm mono}=-\tfrac54(1+w)$. Fig.~\ref{fig:lambda}
shows both across $-1\le w\le1$: at $w=-1$ ($\eps=0$, de~Sitter/attractor limit) $\lambda\to1$
and $f_{\rm map}^{\rm mono}\to0$, so the map is the identity; $\lambda\to0$ at $w=1$
(kination, $\eps=3$, formally the $m=3/\eps-1=0$ constant mode, $\dot\zeta_L=0$, $I=0$ --
$\lambda\to0$ is a formal limit of the constant-$\eps$ formula rather than a physical
loss of linear response), where the general-$\eps$ in-in monopole
$-\tfrac{5}{18}(\eps-3)(\eps-6)$ itself vanishes.

\section{The criterion}

Equations~\eqref{eq:lambda}--\eqref{eq:fmapfin} give a single statement:

\emph{The map $\delta N_c=\zeta_{L,f}[1-I/3+O(k_L^2/a^2H^2)]$, $I=\zeta_{L,f}^{-1}\int(\eps/c_s^2)\dot\zeta_L\,dt$,
is exact and invertible for any single-field history ($\lambda\equiv1-I/3\neq0$); it is not
a discrepancy in the physics, only a statement about which variable $\delta N_c$ is. The
isotropic separate universe ($\delta N$, with $N(\phi,\pi)$) computes $\delta N_c$, and the
standard $\delta N$ formula identifies $\delta N_c$ with $\zeta$. That identification is
exact iff $I\to0$; it carries an $O(1)$ error in the identification -- not in either
variable separately -- whenever $I=O(1)$, e.g.\ every constant-$\eps$ contraction with a
growing $\zeta$. When $I\ll1$ (attractor and dominant ekpyrotic mode: $\dot\zeta_L=0$,
$I=0$ exactly; USR: $\eps\ll1$ throughout the growth) the identification holds to $O(I)$.}

Via the Hamiltonian constraint on comoving slices, $I=O(1)$ is equivalent to the long
mode's Bardeen potential growing as $\Psi_L=O((aH/k)^2)\,\zeta_L$, the textbook signature
of a non-attractor contraction~\cite{Wands1999,Finelli2002}, and $I\to0$ recovers the
gradient-expansion assumption of Lyth, Malik \& Sasaki~\cite{Lyth2005}, following Salopek \&
Bond~\cite{Salopek1990}. The final slice used throughout is uniform-$\phi$ (comoving), which
coincides with uniform-$\rho$ only at $I\to0$; at $I=O(1)$ the two differ already at
$O(k^0)$, so Lyth--Malik--Sasaki's $\delta N=\zeta_{ud}$ (uniform-density $\to$ uniform-density)
is not the object compared here, and this is a second, independent $O(1)$ slice ambiguity of
the same order as the effect this note isolates. Two logically distinct requirements are thus needed for the
standard $\delta N$ formula to give $\zeta$ in a non-attractor phase: (i) $N(\phi,\pi)$, to
capture the growing mode as an independent initial datum~\cite{Namjoo2013}; (ii) $I\to0$,
so that $\delta N_c=\zeta$. USR satisfies (ii) approximately because $\eps\ll1$ while
$\zeta$ grows -- not because the comoving shift is small, which it is not: the shift itself
is $O(1/k_L)$ in every non-attractor, USR included, but its coefficient $\eps\dot\zeta_L$ is
what controls $I$, and this coefficient is small in USR precisely when its growing-mode
initial-data problem (i) is largest. Every inflationary test cited in Sec.~I checked (i)
with (ii) automatically small; none tested a regime with $I=O(1)$.

Table~\ref{tab:validation} summarizes one nontrivial check plus three consistency limits by
exact sympy (script and manifest in the reproducibility statement). The $w=0$ scalar case
(a $w=0$, $c_s=1$ field, not true dust: genuine dust has $c_s\to0$, where
$I=\zeta^{-1}\int(\eps/c_s^2)\dot\zeta\,dt$ diverges and the criterion as stated does not
apply) is the one already reported in
Ref.~\cite{Golden2026Monopole}: the isotropic separate universe gives
$f_{\delta N}^{\rm init}=-5$ for every constant $\eps\in(1,3)$, while the in-in monopole is
$f^{\rm in\text{-}in}_{\rm mono}=-\tfrac{5}{18}(\eps-3)(\eps-6)=-15/8$ at $\eps=3/2$, so the
identification error, $-5-(-15/8)=-25/8$, is a factor $8/3$; the growing-mode monopole and
the separate-universe value coincide only in the attractor limit $\eps\to0$, where both are
computed on the constant mode ($I=0$) rather than by extrapolating this $\eps=3/2$ result.
USR agrees because $\eps\ll1$ throughout the growth of the long mode --
its row tests the size of the $O(\eps)$ correction to the map, not a constraint from
Namjoo-Firouzjahi-Sasaki's leading-order $\fnl=5/2$ itself~\cite{Namjoo2013}. Attractor slow
roll is the identity map, consistent with Maldacena's consistency
relation~\cite{Maldacena2003}. Ekpyrotic contraction ($\eps\gg3$) has $I=0$ because the
physical long mode sits on the constant-$\zeta$ solution, reproducing the attractor
behavior of single-field ekpyrosis found by Creminelli, Nicolis \&
Zaldarriaga~\cite{Creminelli2004}; with $\dot\zeta_L=0$ the row is a consistency check on
the definition of $I$ rather than a nontrivial test of the criterion. The non-dominant,
growing-$\Psi$ ekpyrotic mode would have $I=O(1)$ by the same criterion, but never sets
$\zeta_L(t_f)$ and so is not physically realized. $\lambda_{\rm USR}$ assumes $\zeta_L$ is
purely the growing $a^3$ mode from a slow-roll-frozen value at $a_s$; a slow-roll-to-USR
transition with a non-negligible decaying piece is not covered.

\section{What is new}

Relative to Takamizu, Mukohyama, Sasaki \& Tanaka and Naruko, Takamizu \& Sasaki, who
computed the $O(\eps_{\rm grad}^2)$ (gradient-expansion, $m=2$) correction beyond the leading-order
$\delta N$/separate-universe approximation for the superhorizon
non-Gaussianity~\cite{Takamizu2010,Naruko2012}, the term this note restores,
$-\nabla^2\zeta/(a^2H)$ in $\partial_iN^i$, is the same known next-to-leading-order
gradient-expansion object; the point here is that in a non-attractor contraction the
\emph{other}, retained term, $(\eps/c_s^2)\dot\zeta$, is already $O(k^0)$ and $O(1)$ in
$\eps$, so the leading-order ($m=0$) identification $\delta N_c=\zeta$ can fail at $O(1)$
without needing the $m=2$ correction at all; the two effects are independent and this note
isolates the first one with a single closed-form criterion $I$. Relative to
Refs.~\cite{Namjoo2013,Chen2013}, this note identifies a second, independent source of
identification error between $\delta N_c$ and $\zeta$ in non-attractors -- controlled by
$I$ rather than by initial data -- and shows it is $O(\eps)$ in USR (hence invisible to
their test) and $O(1)$ in a matter-like contraction. Relative to
Ref.~\cite{Pajer2013}, the attractor case reduces to the vanishing of the non-attractor
parameter $\Theta\equiv\eps\dot\zeta_L/(H\zeta_L)$, i.e.\ $\Theta=0$
and the identity map, so the two curvature variables coincide there and the only remaining
question is observability; away from the attractor they already differ at $O(1)$ before any
observability argument, by an explicit, time-dependent, non-local map along the fluid
worldline. Relative to Ref.~\cite{Dai2015}, this note supplies the input a conformal-Fermi-coordinate
treatment of a genuine non-attractor would need: the $O(k^0)$ divergence of the long
mode's comoving shift and the resulting displacement and dilation of the local frame.
Relative to Dai, Pajer \& Schmidt's ``On separate universes''~\cite{DaiSeparateUniverses2015},
which states the conditions under which a long-wavelength mode is locally a separate FRW
universe, the assumption that fails once $I=O(1)$ is theirs (b): the long mode is not
adiabatic on the fluid worldline used here once $\eps/c_s^2\,\dot\zeta_L$ is $O(k^0)$, so the
local patch is still a separate universe but the map from its clock to the global $\zeta$
picks up the $I$-dependent rescaling and translation of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:lambda}--\eqref{eq:fmapfin}
rather than reducing to the identity.
Relative to Refs.~\cite{Cai2018,Passaglia2019}, the agreement found there is explained as
the $I\ll1$ corner of the criterion rather than a coincidence of the USR transition.
Relative to Refs.~\cite{Bravo2018a,Bravo2018b}, the coordinate-removal argument for the
canonical single-field local $\fnl$ is $O(\eps)$-controlled in inflation; whether an
analogous physical-coordinate argument removes the $O(1)$ in-in monopole in a matter-like
contraction is a distinct, open question, not addressed here. Relative to
Refs.~\cite{Artigas2022,Jackson2023}, the mechanism and order differ: their breakdown is
localized to a finite window of scales at a slow-roll-to-USR transition and traced to a
momentum mismatch, while the criterion here applies to all super-Hubble scales at $O(k^0)$,
at constant $\eps$, with no transition required.

\section{Limits}

The second-order map, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:fmap}--\eqref{eq:generalw}, is exact for constant
$\eps$ and $c_s=1$ (the scalar-field realization of a given $w$); it is not the
second-order map for a genuine fluid with $c_s^2=w\neq1$, which needs the $c_s$-dependent
second-order ADM constraints -- an open, bounded extension. The USR second-order statement
in Sec.~III is structural (every frozen second-order kernel is proportional to $\eps$, and
vanishes as $\eps\to0$), not a re-solve with a time-dependent $\eps(t)=\eps_s(a/a_s)^{-6}$;
a full time-dependent-$\eps$ second-order calculation was not attempted. The linear
criterion, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambda}, holds on super-Hubble scales for any single-field history
and any $c_s$, from a flat, super-Hubble initial slice, up to the dropped
$O(k_L^2/a^2H^2)$ gradient term bounded in Sec.~II; it is not literally exact at all $k_L$.

\subsection{The second-order lapse/shift solution and the exact worldline identity}

Comoving gauge ($\delta\phi=0$), Maldacena variables $h_{ij}=a^2e^{2\zeta}\delta_{ij}$,
$N=1+\alpha$, $N_i=\partial_i\psi+\tilde N_i$; at first order $\alpha=\dot\zeta/H$,
$\psi=-\zeta/H+\chi$, $\partial^2\chi=a^2\eps\dot\zeta$. The zero-shift threading is the
fluid (normal) congruence, $n^\mu=(1/N,-N^i/N)$, $dx^i/dt=-N^i$. The expansion of the normal
congruence is $K=\nabla_\mu n^\mu=\frac{1}{N\sqrt h}[\partial_t\sqrt h-\partial_i(\sqrt h\,N^i)]$;
along a fluid worldline this gives the exact (fully nonlinear) identity
\begin{equation}
NK=\frac{d}{dt}\ln\sqrt h\Big|_{\rm worldline}-\partial_iN^i.
\end{equation}
With $\delta N_c=\int(K/3)N\,dt-\bar N$ and $\ln\sqrt h=3\ln a+3\zeta$, this is Eq.~\eqref{eq:threading}
of the main text. Every difference between $\zeta_{\rm Mald}$ and $\delta N_c$ is the divergence of
the shift integrated along the fluid worldline: (i) $\partial_iN^i$ itself at second order, from
the second-order lapse and shift solving the exact ADM Hamiltonian and momentum constraints;
(ii) its evaluation along the worldline displaced by the long mode; (iii) the choice of label
(final position $x_f$ vs.\ initial position $x_i=x_f+\xi$), a rigid translation of the final
field.
