We assess four enumerated minimal Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) spin-torsion channels as candidate sources of late-time dark energy and find that each fails at the amplitude level under stated assumptions. This is a channel-level assessment, not an operator-level theorem: the four routes (NJL, one-loop effective action, Immirzi running, parity-CMB) are not proven to be a complete operator basis, and missing operators (Jackiw-Pi gravitational Chern-Simons, the parity-odd four-fermion partner) are acknowledged explicitly. Through 7 foundation studies and 6 observational branches we report 13 logically-independent mechanism-class constraints. The central result is a perturbation-transparency theorem: for canonical scalar matter, torsion vanishes at all perturbation orders and the Holst sector decouples from all scalar/tensor perturbation equations of motion. A structural tension exists between the dark-energy mechanism, which requires N_tot ~ 92 post-bounce e-folds, and the matter-bounce f_NL = -35/8 signature, which would be erased at SPHEREx-accessible wavenumbers by that many e-folds; the minimal-ECH channel set is therefore tightly constrained as both a dark-energy generator and a matter-bounce host. Two surviving observables are bounce-class and GR+ALP-class signatures the assessment does not forbid, not ECH predictions: (i) f_NL = -35/8, a parameter-free property of the matter-bounce class; and (ii) spectator-ALP birefringence beta ~ 0.27 deg, a benchmark consistency point inside the WMAP+Planck 1-sigma band beta_obs = 0.342 +/- 0.094 deg and comparable to ACT DR6 beta = 0.215 +/- 0.074 deg. Technical verification (MCMC, NaMaster pipeline validation, ALP fitting) is documented in a companion paper.
