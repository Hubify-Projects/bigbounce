This is a referee report for Physical Review D. 

The manuscript presents a rigorous, multi-channel analysis of the matter-bounce $f_{NL}^{local} = -35/16$ prediction. The from-scratch in-in derivation is commendable, and the careful propagation of this amplitude through pulsar timing, PBH formation, and LSS channels is exactly the kind of cross-disciplinary consistency check the field needs. The grading of claims (reproduced vs. new) and the explicit tracking of assumptions are excellent practices. 

However, the manuscript requires major revisions before it can be accepted. There is a critical standalone-reader violation where a load-bearing parameter is imported from an unpublished draft, an abstract-last drift issue that misrepresents the DESI DR1 constraints by omitting a mutually exclusive prior, and a severe numerical inconsistency in the PBH abundance formula. 

Below is the detailed list of findings.

### ESSENTIAL (Must be fixed for acceptance)

**A3M-E1: Abstract-last drift on DESI DR1 constraints**
* Location: Abstract, page 1
* Problem: The abstract quotes only the DESI DR1 merger-model constraint ("$f_{NL}^{local} = -3.6^{+9.0}_{-9.1}$ at $0.16\sigma$") and completely omits the universality prior result ($+3.5$ at $0.77\sigma$). In the body (Sec VI.A), the text explicitly and correctly states that these come from "mutually exclusive theoretical priors" and are "not directly comparable to each other." Presenting only one side of a mutually exclusive prior split in the abstract without the "not directly comparable" caveat violates the requirement that abstract claims must match the body's calibrated statements.
* Required fix: Include both constraints in the abstract with their respective priors, or explicitly state that the quoted $0.16\sigma$ value assumes the merger-response prior.

**A3M-E2: Standalone-reader test violation**
* Location: Section VI.B, page 6 and Reproducibility Statement, page 7
* Problem: The shape overlap $r = 0.84$ is "imported from this lab's companion Fisher-forecast draft". This violates the standalone-reader test, as the companion draft is not a published paper or an accessible preprint. A PRD paper must be self-contained; load-bearing parameters cannot be cited to inaccessible internal documents.
* Required fix: Provide the derivation for $r=0.84$ in an appendix, or cite a publicly available preprint/dataset where this value is derived.

**A3M-E3: Missing frozen-release DOI**
* Location: Reproducibility Statement, page 7
* Problem: The code and data are linked via a GitHub commit hash (`68309c8`) but lack a persistent frozen-release DOI (e.g., Zenodo) or an arXiv ancillary file submission. GitHub trees are subject to repository deletion or access changes.
* Required fix: Mint a persistent DOI (via Zenodo or similar) for the exact commit used for this paper and update the reproducibility statement.

### MAJOR (Significant revisions required)

**A3M-M1: Dimensional/numerical inconsistency in PBH abundance**
* Location: Section V.B, page 6, Eq. (10)
* Problem: The prefactor in the $f_{PBH}$ equation appears numerically inconsistent with standard literature (e.g., the cited Sasaki et al. 2018). Using the given values ($M_H = 10^{20}$ g, $\Omega_{DM} = 0.674$), the term $(M_\odot / M_H)^{1/2} \approx 4.47 \times 10^6$. If $\beta_{NG} = 7.9 \times 10^{-10}$, the formula yields $f_{PBH} \approx 6.6 \times 10^6$, which is unphysical ($>1$). Standard derivations usually carry a mass ratio exponent of $(M_H / M_\odot)^{-1/2}$ and a different normalization constant.
* Required fix: Correct the mass ratio exponent and verify the $7.9 \times 10^{-10}$ normalization constant against the cited source [13]. Ensure the equation yields a physical fractional abundance.

**A3M-M2: Uncomputed quantitative claims**
* Location: Section V.B, page 5
* Problem: The text makes specific quantitative claims about the non-monotonicity of $f_{PBH}$ ("falls by ~ 55 decades to a minimum near $f_{NL} \approx -0.35$, then climbs back by ~ 53 decades") and states that the anti-correlated region "dominates formation". These claims are not supported by any figure or table in the paper, but rather point to an external JSON artifact (`outputs/pbh_compaction_fnl.json`). 
* Required fix: Include a plot (e.g., adding these curves to Figure 1) or a table demonstrating this 55-decade dip, and provide a quantitative threshold/number for the "dominates formation" claim.

## PASS 2 — self-critique findings (what initial review missed)

Here are the additional findings from a fresh-eyes re-evaluation of the manuscript, focusing on arithmetic, parameter consistency, and statistical claims. 

### ESSENTIAL (Must be fixed for acceptance)

**[A3M-E4] Critical Parameter Confusion ($\Omega_{DM}$ vs. $h$)**
* Location: Section V.B, page 6, below Eq. (10)
* Problem: The text explicitly defines the dark matter density parameter as "$\Omega_{DM} = 0.674$". This is a severe parameter confusion: $0.674$ is the standard Planck 2018 value for the dimensionless Hubble parameter ($h$), not the dark matter density. The actual fractional dark matter density today is $\Omega_{DM} \approx 0.26$. Using $0.674$ artificially suppresses the computed PBH abundance by nearly a factor of 3. 
* Required fix: Correct $\Omega_{DM}$ to the proper physical value ($\sim 0.26$) and re-evaluate any downstream numerical results that depend on it.

### MAJOR (Significant revisions required)

**[A3M-M3] Unquantified Hedge / Failed Injection Test**
* Location: Section IV.C, page 4
* Problem: The author claims the 30-bin refit pipeline is "validated by injection" and "approximately unbiased" because it recovers $\gamma = 3.19 \pm 0.42$ for an injected signal of $\gamma = 13/3 \approx 4.333$. However, the injected true value is $2.72\sigma$ away from the recovered mean ($|4.333 - 3.19| / 0.42 = 2.72$). In statistical data analysis, a $2.7\sigma$ offset on a controlled injection test indicates a systematic bias or a failed test, not an "approximately unbiased" pipeline. 
* Required fix: Remove the claim that the pipeline is "approximately unbiased," explicitly state the $2.7\sigma$ bias in the text, and discuss why the pipeline systematically underestimates the slope (e.g., prior volume effects, red noise leakage) before applying it to real data.

**[A3M-M4] Arithmetic Contradiction in Truncated Map Scaling**
* Location: Section V.A, page 5, Eq. (8) and subsequent text
* Problem: The author writes the maximum curvature as $\zeta_{max} = -\frac{5}{12f_{NL}} + \frac{3}{5}|f_{NL}|\sigma^2$. Immediately below, the text claims this "scales as $1/|f_{NL}|$" and that the values at $-35/8$ and $-35/16$ ($0.09524$ and $0.19048$) are "exactly a factor of 2". This is mathematically false. The exact factor of 2 only applies to the first term; the second term scales as $|f_{NL}|$, which breaks both the $1/|f_{NL}|$ scaling and the exact doubling. The author silently dropped the $\sigma^2$ term they explicitly included in Eq. (8) to compute the quoted numbers.
* Required fix: Either explicitly state that the $\sigma^2$ term is being neglected (and justify why, given $\sigma$ cannot be zero for PBH formation), or recalculate the values including the $\sigma^2$ term and remove the claim that it is "exactly a factor of 2".
