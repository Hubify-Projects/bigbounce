1. Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONS

This paper is publishable in principle as a clearly scoped sensitivity recast, not as an independent SPHEREx/MegaMapper forecast or a derived matter-bounce prediction. The current draft has done the most important thing correctly: it repeatedly and visibly discloses that the headline result depends on an imported Heinrich et al. SPHEREx bispectrum forecast, a template-overlap recast, heuristic systematic budgeting, and the unproven cubic-order transmission of the bispectrum through the bounce. That honesty materially lowers the severity of several issues that would otherwise be blockers.

I would recommend minor revisions before acceptance, mainly to tighten framing, reduce over-complexity, and prevent readers from misreading conditional sensitivity envelopes as forecasted measurement precisities.

2. BLOCKERS

None. I do not see a fatal internal inconsistency, unacknowledged assumption, or visibly wrong rendered equation/table that prevents publication. The major theoretical weakness—faithful third-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce—is explicitly identified as the weakest link in the abstract, Sec. II C, and the conclusion. The lack of an independent SPHEREx bispectrum Fisher matrix is also repeatedly acknowledged.

3. MAJORS

Major 1 — The paper remains very close to the boundary between "forecast paper" and "annotated recast"
The manuscript says, correctly, that it is a recast of Heinrich et al. rather than an independent forecast. However, the title, abstract, and repeated "SPHEREx can test at 2.6–5.5σ" language may still lead readers to treat the result as a new forecast. The authors should keep the word "recast" in the title and ensure that the first quantitative headline always reads something like: "conditional recast of the Heinrich et al. local-template bispectrum forecast" rather than simply "SPHEREx forecast."

Major 2 — The systematic budget is transparent but still heuristic
Table IV is useful and honest, but the quadrature combination of template mismatch, bϕ, GR projection, photo-z degradation, and other effects is not a substitute for a joint Fisher/marginalized covariance. The best wording is "a scoping envelope of roughly 2.6–5.5σ" not "a realistic forecast of 2.6–5.5σ."

Major 3 — The Bayesian model-comparison section is over-elaborate and risks obscuring the main result
The Bayes-factor section is technically careful, but extremely long relative to the paper's central contribution. For journal readability, I recommend compressing Sec. VI substantially and moving some derivational detail to an appendix.

Major 4 — The fNL=−35/8 normalization audit is useful but should be positioned as a consistency audit, not a full derivation
Appendix A does a good job separating the local-template normalization convention from the in-in commutator/time-ordering factor. However, the manuscript should avoid implying that it has independently rederived the full Cai et al. bispectrum.

Major 5 — The manuscript is very long for the actual incremental result
The paper's core result is compact: matter-bounce prediction fNL≃−35/8, template overlap r≃0.84, recast significance 2.6–5.5σ after heuristic budget. This could be made much sharper with trimming of repetitions across the abstract, Secs. IV, VI, VII, IX, X, and Table IV captions.

4. MINORS

m1. Clarify "realistic" vs "conservative." Define these once and use consistently.
m2. Figure 2 caption is overloaded. Shorten it and move details to Table IV.
m3. Table II is too dense. Consider splitting into two smaller tables.
m4. Avoid "overwhelming evidence" language. Replace with "strong evidence against standard single-field slow-roll inflation."
m5. MegaMapper should remain clearly speculative. Include "proposed / not yet funded / illustrative" with every MegaMapper headline.
m6. The auxiliary cosmic-birefringence paragraph is optional and tangential.
m7. Check repeated terminology around "parameter-free." Remove any remaining language that implies the prediction is strictly parameter-free.
m8. Data/code availability should not overpromise reproducibility of external forecasts.

5. Strengths

S1. Excellent scope discipline. The paper repeatedly states that the result is a sensitivity recast, not an independent forecast.
S2. The template-mismatch treatment is valuable. The r≃0.84 result, with checks across weighting schemes and null-space scans, is one of the strongest parts of the manuscript.
S3. The main theoretical caveat is honestly foregrounded. The paper does not hide that faithful cubic-order transmission through the bounce is unproven.
S4. The systematic budget is unusually transparent. Even though the quadrature combination is heuristic, the paper itemizes the assumptions.
S5. The factor-of-two convention audit is useful. The discussion of Cai et al. vs. Li et al. is a helpful clarification for readers.
S6. The paper gives falsifiable observational thresholds. The decision-threshold framing is clear.
S7. The authors separate bispectrum and SDB channels correctly.

Bottom line: This is a strong, careful conditional sensitivity recast. With modest tightening of the abstract, Bayesian section, and "forecast" language, I would support publication as MINOR REVISIONS.
