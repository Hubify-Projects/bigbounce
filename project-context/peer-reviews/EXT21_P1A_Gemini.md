Title: Channel-Level Closure of Four Minimal Einstein-Cartan-Holst Dark-Energy Routes and Perturbation Transparency for Scalar Matter

General Overview

This manuscript presents a detailed channel-level assessment of four minimal Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) spin-torsion pathways as potential sources for late-time dark energy. The author demonstrates that three of these routes (R1–R3) are strongly amplitude-suppressed under explicit scaling ansätze, while the fourth (R4, involving a spectator axion-like particle or neutrino current) simply reparameterizes or relocates the cosmological constant problem by requiring an extreme ultralight-mass fine-tuning (mθ ∼ H0).

A major theoretical highlight of the work is the perturbation-transparency result. The author proves that for canonical scalar matter, torsion vanishes at all classical perturbation orders around the torsion-free branch, causing the Holst dual contraction to vanish identically via the first algebraic Bianchi identity. Consequently, the Holst sector decouples entirely from the scalar and tensor perturbation equations of motion. Furthermore, the paper identifies a severe structural tension between generating dark energy (which demands Ntot ≈ 92 post-bounce e-folds) and preserving the testable matter-bounce non-Gaussianity signature (fNL = −35/8), which gets completely erased under that many e-folds.

The manuscript is exceptionally rigorous, highly self-aware of its phenomenological boundaries, and structurally well-organized. It provides a valuable "no-go" catalog that reframes how geometric spin-torsion formulations map to observable late-time cosmology. The manuscript is well-suited for publication in MNRAS after addressing a few minor revisions, clarifications, and typographical issues.

Key Strengths

- Clarity of Scope: The author explicitly states that this is a channel-level enumeration rather than a complete operator-level theorem. This clear demarcation protects the paper from over-generalizing its no-go conclusions.

- Elegant Use of Symmetry: The derivation of the perturbation-transparency result via the first algebraic Bianchi identity is a clean, mathematically sound proof that simplifies the scalar/tensor perturbation analysis.

- Candor on Fine-Tuning: Rather than claiming a full resolution to the dark energy puzzle, the paper transparently admits that the framework relocates the fine-tuning liability into inflationary initial conditions (Ntot).

Points for Revision and Clarification

1. Heavy Dependence on Unreviewed Companion Papers

The manuscript frequently relies on data methods, pipeline validations, and quantitative constraints sourced from companion papers listed as "in preparation" or "posted concurrently" (specifically Paper I(b), Paper II, Paper III, and Paper IV). For instance:

The reader is asked to accept cosmological values (H0 = 67.68 ± 1.06 km/s/Mpc) from an internal MCMC analysis that is not yet independently peer-reviewable.
Galaxy spin dipole null configurations rely heavily on the pipeline descriptions in Paper IV.

Revision Request: The author should explicitly clarify the publication or pre-print status of these companion papers. If they are concurrently uploaded to arXiv, providing their active identifiers would greatly assist the community. Alternatively, the author should briefly expand Appendix A or the relevant data sections to ensure the current manuscript remains functionally self-contained for a reader evaluating the structural proofs.

2. Physical Grounding of the Washout Rate Constraint

In Section II.C.1, the paper discusses a parallel thermodynamic erasure channel. It notes that a thermal unpolarized fermion bath in approximate CP equilibrium will force the axial current expectation value to zero (⟨Jμ5⟩ → 0), provided that the chirality-flipping rate exceeds the Hubble rate (Γwash(Treh) > H(Treh)).

Revision Request: While a full Boltzmann computation is explicitly deferred to future work, the author should add one or two sentences contextualizing the physical plausibility of this inequality remaining satisfied throughout the post-bounce plasma cooling phase, especially given the rapid dominance of the Standard Model top-Yukawa channel highlighted in the text.

3. Guidance for the MNRAS Readership

MNRAS serves a broad astrophysics and cosmology audience. Portions of the text — such as the differential-form identity eI ∧ eJ ∧ RIJ = −NY + TI ∧ TI — are highly specialized.

Revision Request: To maximize readability, please ensure that whenever complex differential geometry or loop quantum holonomy expressions are introduced, they are immediately accompanied by a brief sentence detailing their direct physical or observational consequence (as elegantly done in Figure 1's flow chart).
