Gemini said Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONSGeneral CommentsThis manuscript presents the "BIGAE" autoencoder framework applied to a massive multi-survey dataset comprising 37.3 million sources and CMB map patches (including DESI DR1, SDSS DR18, LAMOST DR10, eROSITA DR1, Planck, Gaia DR3, and NEOWISE). The work is highly commendable for its sheer scale, representing perhaps the largest single multi-archive unsupervised anomaly detection campaign in the astronomical literature to date.What sets this paper apart from typical machine learning applications in astrophysics is its exceptional transparency and self-aware skepticism. Rather than sweeping pipeline failures, lost scripts, or data leaks under the rug to present a artificially perfect catalog, the author explicitly documents and quantifies these flaws (e.g., the 98% blue-excess artifact in LAMOST, validation leakage in tabular preprocessing, and the unrecoverable scoring axis for eROSITA). This candid approach transforms what could have been a fragile machine-learning catalog into a robust, well-caveated benchmark data product. The cosmological tracer applications (fNL​ forecasts and NANOGrav spectral index checks) are appropriately framed as secondary model audits rather than overstated claims of new physics.The manuscript is highly suited for publication in MNRAS after addressing a few major methodological concerns and cleaning up minor text/figure rendering artifacts.StrengthsUnprecedented Processing Scale: The application of a single multi-archive autoencoder framework to over 37 million raw objects represents a massive leap over prior single-survey anomaly catalogs, providing a valuable all-sky resource for downstream target selection.Methodological Honesty and Transparency: The author displays a level of scientific integrity that should serve as a model for astroinformatics papers. Disclosing that a key preprocessing script was lost (Gaia), that the final score axis was non-reproducible (eROSITA), and that an entry passed a gate by construction (NEOWISE) ensures that downstream users know exactly how far to trust the data tiers.Clear Distinction Between True Novelty and Database Incompleteness: The paper carefully contextualizes why a 58.8% SIMBAD-unmatched rate is a symptom of database coverage heterogeneity rather than actual astrophysical novelty. Data Leakage in Tabular Preprocessing (§II B)The author notes that for the three tabular catalogs (eROSITA, NEOWISE, and Gaia), standardizing and scaling metrics (robust median/IQR, mean/variance) were fit on the full sample rather than the training split alone. This constitutes a classic data leakage flaw, allowing validation and tail-end information to influence the normalization constants.Required Action: While the author is commended for performing a bounded robustness check on the load-bearing eROSITA tier (showing a 15% extreme-tail churn that sits at the retrain reproducibility floor), this practice must be explicitly flagged as a warning in the final data release documentation. In the text, please explicitly quantify how much this validation leakage alters the absolute validation MSE scale compared to a strict split-only baseline.2. eROSITA Score Axis Irreproducibility (§III E & Table IV)The production scoring run's threshold of 0.259 could not be reproduced on any of the 16 tested monotone rescalings due to an uncommitted post-hoc step. While treating the n=298 list as a canonical membership list is a reasonable recovery strategy, it severely limits the utility of the published SBigAE​ values for subsequent statistical workflows (e.g., score-weighted stacking or threshold re-derivation).Required Action: The author must include a explicit warning in the abstract or target catalog schema table clarifying that the eROSITA SBigAE​ column is non-monotonic relative to the raw reconstruction artifacts. Downstream users must be directed to use the raw-score artifact or the rank indices for any meta-analyses.Minor Revisions1. NEOWISE Mask Gate Verification (§III H / §VI D)The NEOWISE masking-geometry QA gate achieved a 100% recovery rate, but as disclosed in §III H, this was because synthetic sources were planted outside the mask boundaries and then evaluated using the fixed mask cut itself. The gate therefore passes by construction and evaluates code implementation rather than detector sensitivity.Required Action: While the text is transparent about this, labeling it simply as a "PASS" alongside the genuine detector-sensitivity gate of SDSS in summary lists can be misleading.
