Referee Report: MNRAS / PRD / JCAP
Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS
The paper is a serious, technically ambitious contribution that synthesizes loop quantum cosmology, Einstein–Cartan–Holst (ECH) gravity, torsion, and bounce cosmology in an attempt to systematically close phenomenological routes from a quantum bounce to late-time dark energy. It contains one genuinely sharp technical result (the perturbation-transparency theorem) and performs a useful service by cataloguing failure modes. However, the headline claim of "channel-level closure" of the four enumerated routes is not yet supported at the strength required for these journals. The closures for Routes 2 and 3 rest on author-chosen phenomenological ansätze rather than derivations from the cited literature or a demonstration that any operator in those channels is suppressed. The dark-energy mapping itself is introduced as an on-shell scaling ansatz whose off-shell mass dimension is +1 rather than +4. These points are acknowledged in the text but are not given sufficient prominence in the abstract, introduction, and conclusions relative to the strength of the language used ("closure", "no-go", "constrain the enumerated channels").
The manuscript is therefore not yet at the acceptance threshold. With targeted revisions to calibrate claims to the actual derivations versus ansatz-dependent assessments, to strengthen the operator justification for R2/R3, and to better separate theorems from naturalness arguments, it could become a solid contribution to PRD or JCAP.
BLOCKERS
None. There are no fatal technical errors, contradictions, or unstated assumptions that invalidate the core logic. The perturbation-transparency argument (Sec. X) is correctly grounded in the algebraic Bianchi identity and the algebraic nature of torsion when spin density vanishes. No math rendering errors rise to the level of a blocker.
MAJORS (in rough order of importance)
Overstated strength of the "four-route closure" (abstract, Sec. I, Sec. IV, Sec. IX, Sec. XV).
The paper repeatedly and correctly states that it performs a channel-level assessment under "explicitly-labeled scaling ansätze" (R2, R3) and a "phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz" (Appendix B), and that it does not claim a complete diffeomorphism-invariant operator basis (Jackiw–Pi gravitational Chern–Simons term and the parity-odd four-fermion partner of R1 are explicitly omitted). However, the abstract, introduction, and executive summary headline "channel-level closure" and "14 constraints … close those routes at amplitude-budget granularity" without sufficient qualification. Routes 2 and 3 are closed only for the specific phenomenological operators and coefficients adopted in Eqs. (14) and (16), which the text itself describes as "motivated by but not literally derived" from Mercuri (2015), Shapiro & Teixeira (2009), and Date–Kaul–Sengupta (2006). A true channel closure would require showing that the leading operators in each class are inevitably Planck- or loop-suppressed, or that the ansatz is the most optimistic case still allowed by the literature.
Action: Rewrite the abstract, Sec. I (Introduction), and Sec. IV opening paragraphs to lead with the actual logical status: "We assess four commonly discussed minimal-ECH channels … and find each is constrained under the following explicitly stated assumptions/ansätze …" Move the strong scoping language currently in Sec. IV ("Scope" paragraph) and the abstract footnote into the main abstract and introduction. Add a short "Status of claims" subsection in Sec. IV or IX that tabulates, for each route/barrier, whether the constraint is (a) a first-principles derivation, (b) an amplitude bound under a stated phenomenological ansatz, or (c) a naturalness/qualitative argument.
Justification of the specific operators and coefficients used for R2 and R3 (Sec. IV B–C).
The one-loop parity-odd operator in Eq. (14) and the chiral-count running ansatz in Eq. (16) are presented as conservative upper bounds. This is acceptable for an order-of-magnitude closure exercise, but the paper must demonstrate why these choices are sufficient to close the channels rather than merely the specific parametrizations chosen. The text already notes that the full perturbative β-function of Benedetti & Speziale (2015) is |γ|-dependent and more complicated; the simple chiral-count form is an EFT bound.
Action: Either (i) derive (or cite a derivation of) the leading parity-odd operator generated by integrating out fermions in the Holst sector at one loop, or (ii) argue explicitly why any operator that could source observable birefringence or dark energy from the Holst sector must carry at least the suppression shown (M_Pl^{-1} plus loop factor, or mass-dimension lock). If the latter, state the assumption clearly: "Any parity-odd operator built from the Holst curvature and the axial current that is generated at one loop or by RG running will be suppressed by …". The current phrasing ("we adopt the phenomenological one-loop parity-odd operator … as an upper-bound EFT ansatz") leaves the closure conditional on the author's modeling choice.
Status of the dark-energy mapping ansatz (Sec. II C, Appendix B, abstract).
The entire motivation for treating the four routes as candidate dark-energy sources rests on the phenomenological identification ρ_Λ = Ξ M_Pl^4 with Ξ constructed from the dimension-+1 operator evaluated on-shell at bounce densities. The paper is admirably explicit that this is an ansatz, not a derivation, and that the off-shell mass dimension is +1. However, the abstract and introduction still present the framework as "a phenomenological framework that connects ECH spin-torsion cosmology to dark energy" without foregrounding that the connection itself is imposed by hand via the scaling ansatz.
Action: In the abstract and Sec. I, state at the outset: "We introduce a phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz (Appendix B) that maps a dimension-+1 parity-odd operator to a late-time vacuum energy density, and then assess whether any of the four standard minimal-ECH channels can realize this ansatz at the required amplitude." This makes the logical structure transparent: the paper closes routes to a specific phenomenological model, not routes to dark energy from ECH in general.
Calibration of the 14-barrier catalog (Sec. IX, Table III).
The catalog is a valuable organizing device, but it mixes sharp results (R1 torsion-elimination derivation; B14 perturbation transparency) with naturalness arguments, scale-separation observations, and one explicitly heuristic closure (B9 Liouville conservation, conditional on no particle production/entropy injection). Several barriers (B5, B6, B7, B10, B13) apply broadly to many bounce or modified-gravity models, not specifically to minimal ECH. The paper notes that B8 is subsumed by B14, which is good, but does not clearly flag the varying logical status of the entries.
Action: Add a column or footnote to Table III indicating the type/strength of each barrier (first-principles calculation / amplitude bound under ansatz / naturalness argument / heuristic). In Sec. IX, explicitly state that the catalog maps failure modes rather than constituting thirteen independent rigorous no-go theorems. This does not weaken the paper; it increases its credibility.
Reliance on in-preparation companions for contextual claims (throughout, especially Sec. III, VII, XIII, and Table II).
The structural claims (perturbation transparency, four-route amplitude closures, 14-barrier catalog) are stated to be self-contained. This is correct and helpful. However, the paper repeatedly invokes numerical results from "Paper I(b)", "Paper II", and "Paper IV" (in preparation) for MCMC posteriors, Fisher forecasts (2.6–5σ), galaxy-spin catalog construction, and bias audits. While coordinated multi-paper submission is acceptable, a standalone reader cannot verify the robustness of the "confirmed null" on galaxy spin or the precise significance forecast without those works.
Action: Either (a) move the minimal supporting material needed to understand the quoted significances into appendices or supplementary material of this paper, or (b) rephrase all numerical forecasts as "as forecasted in the companion Paper II (in preparation)" and ensure the present manuscript's conclusions do not logically depend on the exact numbers. The current Table II is already a good step toward self-contained readability; extend it if necessary.
MINORS (selected; many are presentational and easily fixed)
Abstract and introduction contain very long sentences; several could be split for readability.
Minor typographical/LaTeX artifacts remain (e.g., "ans¨atze", occasional broken math delimiters in the extraction). Proofread carefully.
Sec. X E ("What Would Break the Transparency") is excellent; move a one-sentence version of it into the abstract or the statement of the central result so readers immediately understand the scope.
In R2, the paper notes an alternative "deliberately loose ∼10^{-33}" bound but adopts the canonical ∼10^{-60} contraction. State explicitly which bound is used for the closure conclusion and why it is the appropriate one.
Figure 1 caption distinguishes γ_PTA (GWB spectral index) from the Barbero–Immirzi γ; this is already clear in the text but worth a parenthetical reminder in the figure itself.
Some repetition between Sec. IV Scope paragraph and the abstract footnote; consolidate.
Ensure every equation referenced in the text (especially in Sec. X) has a number and that cross-references are accurate.
STRENGTHS (at least three)
The perturbation-transparency theorem (Sec. X) is the paper's strongest and most rigorous result. It correctly shows that, for canonical scalar matter, spin density vanishes identically, torsion is algebraically zero at all orders, the connection reduces to Levi-Civita, and the Holst dual contraction ϵ^{μνρσ} R_{μνρσ} vanishes by the first (algebraic) Bianchi identity R_{μ[νρσ]} = 0. This is a clean, first-principles extension of the classic Hehl et al. (1976) result to the Holst sector and to perturbative orders. The scoping ("excluding propagating-torsion, dynamical Immirzi, fermion-loop, and non-minimal-matter sectors") is precise. This result alone is a valuable technical contribution to the ECH literature.
Intellectual honesty and careful scoping throughout. The author repeatedly flags that (a) the dark-energy mapping is a phenomenological on-shell ansatz, (b) R2/R3 closures use upper-bound EFT ansätze rather than derived operators, (c) the four routes do not constitute a complete operator basis, and (d) B8 is subsumed by B14. This level of explicit qualification is rare and increases trust in the analysis. The structural tension identified in Sec. XIV D between N_tot ≈ 92 (required for DE dilution) and survival of the matter-bounce f_NL = −35/8 signature is a sharp, observationally falsifiable insight that stands independently of the ansatz details.
Systematic organization of failure modes. Even if individual barriers vary in rigor, the 14-entry catalog (Table III) provides a useful map of why connecting a minimal ECH bounce to observable dark energy is difficult. It separates amplitude suppression, thermal washout, operator decoupling, and naturalness issues in a way that will be helpful to subsequent workers in bounce cosmology and torsion gravity. The executive summary table (Table I) is also well-constructed.
Clear separation between ECH-specific closures and ECH-independent class tests. The paper correctly emphasizes that the surviving predictions (f_NL = −35/8 and spectator-ALP birefringence β ≈ 0.27°) are properties of the broader matter-bounce / ALP landscape, not distinctive ECH signatures. This prevents overclaiming and keeps the focus on what the channel-level analysis actually constrains.
Summary
This is a dense, serious manuscript that identifies a genuine structural limitation in a corner of modified-gravity cosmology. The perturbation-transparency result and the honest scoping are real strengths. The principal obstacle to acceptance is that the headline "closure" language is not yet matched by the logical status of the supporting arguments for Routes 2 and 3 and the dark-energy ansatz. With the revisions outlined above—primarily recalibrating claims to derivations versus ansatz-dependent assessments, strengthening the justification for the R2/R3 operators, and clarifying the varying strength of the 14 barriers—the paper would meet the high bar of PRD or JCAP.
