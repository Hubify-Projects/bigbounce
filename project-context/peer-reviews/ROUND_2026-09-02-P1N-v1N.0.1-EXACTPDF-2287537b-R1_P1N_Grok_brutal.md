**P1N-E1 (ESSENTIAL, Abstract + p.1)**  
The abstract states the contact term “plausibly supports Poplawski’s bounce mechanism” while being “unable to generate the late-time acceleration independently attributed to dark energy.” No quantitative mapping between the derived \(G_s = -3\kappa/16\) and the observed \(\rho_{\Lambda,\rm obs}\) is supplied in the abstract or body; the only numerical benchmark (Eq. 3) is 68 orders of magnitude below \(\rho_{\Lambda,\rm obs}\). The abstract claim is therefore stronger than any calibrated statement in the text.

**P1N-E2 (ESSENTIAL, p.1–2 and throughout)**  
The Note repeatedly imports load-bearing results by citation to two Zenodo-only companions ([6], [7]) whose DOIs are given but whose content is not reproduced. Examples: “the Note’s sole Tier-I rigorous result” (perturbation transparency), the 14-barrier catalog, the operator-basis rank proof, and the on-shell torsion-irrep decomposition all reside in the companions. A CQG Note must be standalone; this is not.

**P1N-E3 (ESSENTIAL, p.2, Eq. 2 and text after Eq. 4)**  
The central claim that the NJL channel “has no nonzero solution” rests on a “declared direct-channel, hard-cutoff, standard mean-field NJL scalar projection.” No demonstration is given that other truncations (e.g., beyond mean-field, parity-odd channels, or non-minimal couplings) are excluded by the minimal ECH action itself. The “no-go” is therefore truncation-dependent and not a theorem of the theory stated in Eq. 1.

**P1N-E4 (ESSENTIAL, p.3, Table I and Sec. IV)**  
Table I lists 14 “mechanism-class constraints” whose logical independence and completeness are asserted to “jointly close” all four dark-energy channels. The justifications for several entries (B8–B14) are one-sentence citations to the companions or to “general naturalness.” A reader cannot evaluate the catalog without the source manuscripts; the table therefore functions as an index rather than evidence.
