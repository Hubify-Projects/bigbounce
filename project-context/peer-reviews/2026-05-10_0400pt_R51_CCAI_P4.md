## M9 closure — verify rank-based p_MC ≈ 0.45 is internally consistent

**Status:** PARTIAL. The numerical value 0.45 is plausible/correct as a *one-sided rank percentile*; the "two-sided" label attached to it is wrong.

**The math.** The paper retains Gaussian-equivalent |z|=0.12 (signed -0.12σ) "for cross-comparison" with the simple-dipole 0.43σ and pseudo-C_ℓ 6.48σ figures. Treating the post-MASTER null at ℓ=1 as approximately Gaussian for sanity-check purposes (which the paper invites by quoting |z|=0.12 at all):

- One-sided fraction of null draws at-or-below the measured C_1 (i.e., empirical CDF rank): Φ(-0.12) ≈ **0.452**.
- Two-sided p-value: 2·min(Φ(-0.12), 1-Φ(-0.12)) = 2·0.452 ≈ **0.905**.

The number 0.45 in the paper matches the *one-sided* rank percentile. But the paper labels it "rank-based, two-sided" in three independent locations:

- Abstract line 99: "rank-based p_MC ≈ 0.45 post-MASTER"
- §dipole line 106: "rank-based, two-sided; the measured C_1 falls within the bulk..."
- §dipole line 1239-1240: "rank-based, two-sided; the measured C_1 sits within the bulk of the null distribution and below the median by a small amount"
- §conclusions line 2315: same value, no two-sided qualifier here, presented as "rank-based empirical p-value"

A two-sided p-value cannot be 0.45 when the corresponding |z|=0.12 — a value that close to the median has a two-sided p ≈ 0.9. The reviewer cannot tell whether the authors:

1. Computed a one-sided rank percentile and mislabeled it "two-sided" (labeling fix only); or
2. Computed something genuinely two-sided (e.g., 2·min(...) on the empirical rank) and got a number that disagrees with the Gaussian sanity-check for reasons the paper does not explain (e.g., the 1-dof χ² shape pulls the two-sided definition somewhere unusual).

Footnote `fn:mc_count` confirms N_MC = 500 for this null, so the rank resolution is 1/500 = 0.2%, which is fine. The issue is the *definition*, not the precision.

**Recommended fix (one of):**

(a) If 0.45 is the empirical CDF rank (one-sided "fraction of null at or below data"): re-label all three sites to drop "two-sided" and call it "one-sided rank percentile p_MC^{≤} ≈ 0.45" or equivalently "the measured C_1 falls at the 45th percentile of the 500-MC null distribution." Add one sentence: "The two-sided rank-based p-value is ≈ 0.91, also fully consistent with null."

(b) If 0.45 is a genuinely two-sided rank-based statistic on a non-Gaussian null where the |z|=0.12 cross-comparison is not Gaussian-faithful: add one sentence in the dipole section explaining *why* the rank-based two-sided p (0.45) and the Gaussian-equivalent two-sided p (0.91) disagree by 2× — the explanation must reference the 1-dof χ² shape concretely (not just "non-Gaussian"). Without this, the reader is forced to reverse-engineer the disagreement.

Either fix is small (<10 lines of LaTeX). The science-level conclusion ("fully consistent with null") is robust to the fix. **But the current text is internally inconsistent and an external reviewer will flag it.**

## M11 closure — GZ1 attribution caveat doesn't undermine null parity claim

**Status:** CLEAN. Verified at both sites.

**Site 1 — abstract (lines 90-101).** The text now reads as a clearly-flagged working hypothesis: "spatially uniform... attributed to the ~1% human-handedness bias inherited from the GZ1 training labels under the working hypothesis; an independent verification would require a non-GZ1, non-CE-ResNet chirality reference at scale (≥10⁶ galaxies), which does not currently exist." The italicized clause then makes the parity-violation logical separation explicit: "the parity-violation observable is the *dipole*, not the monopole, so our null dipole result (p=0.30, σ=0.43 in real space; rank-based p_MC ≈ 0.45 post-MASTER) rules out a cosmological parity signature at the empirical level regardless of the monopole's origin."

This is the correct rhetorical posture. A hostile reader cannot collapse the GZ1 attribution and the null parity claim into a single fragile inferential chain — the dipole bound is logically prior to and independent of the monopole's origin.

**Site 2 — §conclusions item 1 (lines 2329-2346).** The text mirrors the abstract: working-hypothesis framing, ≥10⁶-galaxy reference dataset gap acknowledged, then the alternative hypothesis ("monopole reflects a genuine cosmological parity-violation signature") is explicitly addressed with: "the parity-preference observable would be the dipole component (which we measure consistent with null at p=0.30 and σ=0.43), not the monopole; the parity-violation hypothesis is therefore tested by the dipole bound, not the monopole offset, and our null dipole result rules it out at the empirical level regardless of the monopole's origin."

Note item 1 in conclusions does NOT carry the "two-sided" qualifier on p_MC ≈ 0.45 (it just calls it "rank-based empirical p-value"). This is the cleanest of the three M9 sites and could serve as the model for the abstract and §dipole if option (a) above is chosen.

**Cross-claim test — does the M11 caveat survive when you push on it hostile-style?**

- *Q: If GZ1 attribution is wrong, does the null parity claim collapse?* No. The dipole bound is measured directly from Catalog C; it does not depend on knowing the *cause* of the monopole offset. The paper now says this in three places (abstract, §dipole, §conclusions item 1).
- *Q: Could a position-dependent classifier bias produce both a monopole offset AND mask itself in the dipole?* The paper addresses this in the abstract (lines 130-136): "We do not claim the orthogonality is structural... rather, we treat the dipole null as the load-bearing empirical test." Honest framing.
- *Q: Is the ≥10⁶-galaxy non-GZ1/non-CE-ResNet gap a paper-killer?* No, because it bounds only the *attribution* of the monopole, not the *measurement* of the dipole. The conclusions item correctly punts the attribution question to future work without compromising the headline.

**M11 verdict:** the closure is sufficient and the abstract+conclusions language is the right posture for an external review. No further edits required on M11.
