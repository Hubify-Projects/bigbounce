## Most concerning theory issue (the BLOCKER)

**Routes I (NJL) and IV (parity-odd CMB coupling via spectator ALP or fermion axial current) are not independent channels; they share the same axial current as the operator anchor.** The paper's §IV writes R1 as a parity-even four-fermion contact from torsion elimination (the Hehl-Datta NJL term, `(J^5)^2` parity-even) and R4 as a parity-odd `(α/M) ∂_μθ J^{5μ}` (Lue-Wang-Kamionkowski-style, with θ either a spectator ALP or a derived Nieh-Yan pseudoscalar). The paper treats these as separate routes (lines 508–519, 711–732). But:

1. Both operators are built from the same fermion axial current `J^{5μ} = ψ̄γ^μγ^5ψ`.
2. R4 written with θ ↔ Nieh-Yan pseudoscalar (Mercuri 2009; the paper *uses* this identification at Eq. (10) / §II.B Step 3) reduces, after `∂_μθ` is integrated by parts and the Nieh-Yan structure expanded, to a quadratic-in-J^5 form that is the *parity-odd* partner of the R1 NJL term. R1 (parity-even) and R4 (parity-odd) are *the two parity sectors of the same dimension-six four-fermion operator* generated by torsion elimination in the Holst-extended Einstein-Cartan action — see Freidel-Minic-Takeuchi 2005 Eq. (4.21) and Alexandrov 2008 (the canonical reference is missing from references.bib).
3. This means the "four-route closure" is not the partition the paper claims. If R1 closes the parity-even sector by `M_Pl^{-2}` suppression, then by the *same* operator-level argument R4 is automatically closed for the J^5-J^5 axial current channel — but R4 is *not* closed for the spectator-ALP channel where θ is genuinely independent of fermion bilinears. The paper then quietly switches R4 from the torsion-internal channel to the spectator-ALP channel at line 686 ("R4 is therefore *not* closed by amplitude mismatch (as earlier-draft analyses claimed); it is closed by the observation that the same coupling that produces β_obs requires an ultralight-mass tuning m_θ ∼ H_0 to also produce ρ_Λ") — which is a *different* no-go argument entirely (the ALP fine-tuning relabel), not the parity-odd closure the §IV header advertises.

The consequence is structural: the no-go theorem as written rests on *three* genuinely independent closure arguments (R1 NJL amplitude, R2 one-loop Planck suppression, R3 mass-dimension lock from γ-running) plus *one* relabel of the cosmological-constant problem (R4). The "4-route exhaustion" claim that "Routes R1–R4 between them exhaust the parity-odd / dark-energy channels available to a minimal ECH sector" (line 713) is not established at the operator level — it is established only at the *phenomenological-channel* level (NJL operator, graviton loop, β-function running, late-time ALP-photon coupling). An adversarial referee will press exactly here: *which dimension-six and dimension-eight operators built from `{R^{ab}, e^a, J^{5μ}, F^{μν}, θ}` does the four-route exhaustion cover, and which does it skip?* The paper does not produce the operator inventory.

**Concretely, two operators are missing from the four-route closure:**
- The Holst-induced Chern-Simons gravity term `(γ/M_Pl) R∧R̃` (parity-odd in the *graviton sector*, distinct from R2's one-loop induced parity-odd fermion-graviton coupling) — this is the Jackiw-Pi 2003 Chern-Simons gravity channel, which has its own published cosmic-birefringence and chiral-GW closure (Lue-Wang-Kamionkowski 1999, Saito-Ichiki-Taruya 2007). The paper cites LWK at R4 but assigns it to the ALP-photon coupling channel, not the R̃R̃ gravitational Chern-Simons channel.

The Freidel-Minic-Takeuchi 2005 decomposition shows this term has coefficient `(γ/(γ²+1)) × (8πG)`, distinct from the parity-even coefficient `(γ²/(γ²+1)) × (8πG)` that the paper *does* derive at Eq. (5) / line 299. The parity-odd partner is in Freidel 2005 but does not appear anywhere in this manuscript.

**The fix is structural, not cosmetic.** Either:
(a) Expand §IV from four routes to six by adding R5 (R∧R̃ Chern-Simons gravity, closed by Jackiw-Pi / Alexander-Yunes amplitude bound) and R6 (the Freidel-Minic-Takeuchi parity-odd four-fermion partner of R1, closed by `M_Pl^{-2}` and chiral-asymmetry running), explicitly tabulate the dimension-six and dimension-eight operator inventory, and demonstrate exhaustion at the operator-classification level. This is the *honest weaker* fix.

## MAJOR findings

### MAJOR-1: The Holst action setup (Eq. (1), §II.A) silently drops the parity-odd `T^{abc}T̃_{abc}` piece.

**Eq. (1) writes:**
```
S_ECH = (1/16πG) ∫ d⁴x e [e^μ_a e^ν_b R^{ab}_{μν} + (1/γ) ε^{abcd} e_a^μ e_b^ν R_{cd μν} + (1/4) T^{abc} T_{abc}] + S_matter
```

This is the parity-even torsion-squared kinetic term `T^{abc} T_{abc}` only. The footnote at line 263 says this term "is a shorthand for the four-fermion contact interaction obtained after integrating out the non-propagating torsion; it is not an independently specified kinetic term." Fine for the Einstein-Cartan piece. **But the Holst extension — `ε^{abcd}` contracted with `R_{cdμν}` — generically *also* generates a parity-odd torsion-squared partner `T^{abc} T̃_{abc} = ε_{abcd} T^{abc} T^d`** (the Nieh-Yan invariant in the torsion sector, distinct from the Nieh-Yan invariant in the curvature sector). This term is what gives the *parity-odd* Hehl-Datta-Mercuri NJL partner that BLOCKER §IV.R6 above relies on. It is in Freidel-Minic-Takeuchi 2005 Eq. (3.7) and in Mercuri 2006 Eq. (15). The paper's Eq. (1) drops it.

**Why this matters for the no-go:** the perturbation-transparency theorem (§X, line 992-1011) proves that for canonical scalar matter, S=0 ⇒ T=0 ⇒ Holst term reduces to a topological Nieh-Yan ⇒ vanishes on first Bianchi identity. *This is correct for the parity-even Holst term* (Eq. (1) third term). It is *also* correct for the parity-odd Nieh-Yan-of-curvature `ε^{abcd} R_{ab}∧R_{cd}` because that, too, vanishes on first Bianchi. **But the parity-odd Nieh-Yan-of-torsion `T^{abc}T̃_{abc}` is *not* a topological Nieh-Yan and is not killed by the first Bianchi identity** — it is a propagating parity-odd contact term whose coefficient is `γ/(γ²+1) × 8πG`, and it is dynamically nontrivial when fermions activate torsion even infinitesimally.

The paper's perturbation-transparency theorem hinges on the dropped term being zero. It is — for scalar matter. It is *not* zero for fermionic matter. The "What Would Break the Transparency" subsection at line 1039 correctly notes that "matter includes fermions with nonzero spin density" breaks the transparency. But the action Eq. (1) does not transparently expose where the parity-odd content lives. For a no-go paper, the action setup must make the parity-odd content *manifest* and then close it explicitly — not implicitly via the matter-content assumption.

**Fix:** Rewrite Eq. (1) to include both Nieh-Yan torsion-squared partners explicitly:
```
S_ECH = (1/16πG) ∫ d⁴x e [R + (1/γ) ε^{abcd} R_{abcd} + (1/4) T^{abc} T_{abc} + (1/2) (γ/(γ²+1)) T^{abc} T̃_{abc}] + S_matter
```
with the fourth term identified as the parity-odd torsion-squared partner that vanishes when integrated out *if* fermion spin density vanishes. This makes the perturbation-transparency theorem's domain-of-validity explicit (scalar matter only) and makes BLOCKER §IV.R6 above a one-paragraph closure rather than an operator-level surprise.

### MAJOR-2: The "perturbation transparency" theorem (§X.B) proves less than it claims when read carefully against published Mercuri-2006 / Freidel-2005.

The theorem statement (line 986-990): "In minimal ECH gravity with canonical scalar field matter, the Holst term is dynamically inert for both scalar and tensor perturbations at all orders. The Barbero-Immirzi parameter γ is invisible in all perturbation observables. This generalizes Hehl et al. (1976) to the Holst sector and to all perturbation orders."

The proof (line 994-1011) walks through five steps:
1. Canonical scalar field has zero spin density.
2. T = 0 at all orders.
3. Connection reduces to Levi-Civita.
4. Holst term becomes `ε^{μνρσ} R_{μνρσ}(Levi-Civita)`, which vanishes by first Bianchi identity.
5. Total derivative ⇒ no EOM contributions at any order.

**This is correct as a statement about the Holst term in Eq. (1). But it is not the strongest statement of γ_BI invisibility that Mercuri-2006 and Freidel-2005 actually establish.** Mercuri-2006 (Phys Rev D 73, 084016, the "Fermions in the Holst formulation" paper) establishes — and Freidel-Minic-Takeuchi 2005 confirms — that γ_BI becomes *physical and observable* in the four-fermion contact-interaction sector, with a parity-even coefficient `γ²/(γ²+1) × 8πG` and a parity-odd coefficient `γ/(γ²+1) × 8πG` (the latter being exactly the term dropped from Eq. (1); see MAJOR-1).

The paper's perturbation-transparency proof therefore establishes:
- *For canonical scalar matter:* γ_BI is invisible — correct, but only because spin density is zero, which trivially kills the torsion sector. This is a property of the *matter content*, not a property of the Holst sector.
- *For fermionic matter:* γ_BI is *visible* in the four-fermion contact sector (Mercuri-2006). The paper's §IV.R1 (line 520) acknowledges this for the parity-even partner; §IV.R6 (proposed in BLOCKER fix) would handle the parity-odd partner.

The "all perturbation orders" claim (line 988) is therefore *trivially true* for scalar matter (because T=0 exactly) and *false in general* for fermionic matter (because the four-fermion contact survives at one-loop in any cosmological fluid with nonzero ψ̄ψ density). The "What Would Break the Transparency" subsection at line 1039 says exactly this, but its placement at the *end* of §X buries the load-bearing caveat. The headline claim in the abstract (line 75-78), the §I.A novel-contributions list (line 195-202), and the conclusions (line 1293-1299) all advertise the result as if it were unconditional.

**Fix:** Re-state the theorem in the abstract and §X as conditional: "For *canonical scalar-field matter only*, the Holst sector decouples completely from all scalar and tensor perturbation observables at all orders. For fermionic matter, the four-fermion contact terms enumerated in §IV (R1, R6) carry the surviving γ_BI dependence, all amplitude-suppressed by `M_Pl^{-2}`." This is a one-line change that converts a slightly-overclaimed theorem into a precisely-stated one. Mercuri 2006 and Freidel 2005 read the modified statement as exactly their result; the unmodified statement reads as a generalization neither author proved.

### MAJOR-3: The 35-order ρ_θ vs ρ_Λ hierarchy is reported incoherently across §IV.R4 (line 678-694) and §V (Table V), and the load-bearing direction of the inequality is unstated.

Line 678-679: "plugging in α/M = 10^{-21} GeV^{-1}, β = β_obs ≈ 6×10^{-3} rad, and m_θ = H_0 ≈ 1.5×10^{-33} eV gives ρ_θ ≈ 2.8×10^{-11} eV⁴ ≈ ρ_Λ to within a factor of unity".

The prompt asks me to verify "ρ_θ ≲ 10^{-46} eV⁴ vs ρ_Λ ~ 10^{-11} eV⁴ — the 35-order ratio is presented correctly." **The paper as written does *not* contain a 10^{-46} eV⁴ figure** — it contains the *opposite* claim, that ρ_θ ≈ 2.8×10^{-11} eV⁴ ≈ ρ_Λ when `m_θ = H_0`. The 35-order hierarchy (between m_θ = H_0 ≈ 10^{-33} eV and the natural-ALP-mass range m_a ∈ [10^{-22}, 10^{-15}] eV) is parameterized in the paper not as a ρ_θ-vs-ρ_Λ density gap but as an *overshoot* in ρ_θ from the natural ALP mass range, of "∼22 orders of magnitude (at m_θ ∼ 10^{-22} eV) to ∼36 orders of magnitude (at m_θ ∼ 10^{-15} eV)" (line 691-694).

So the 35-order ratio in the prompt is *the mass-squared overshoot*, not a ρ_θ ≲ 10^{-46} eV⁴ upper bound on the parity-odd density. The paper has the right physics (overshoot in `(m_θ/H_0)^2 ρ_Λ`) but the prose around line 691-694 is hard to parse: the parenthetical "(because m_θ=H_0≈1.5×10^{-33} eV is the only point where ρ_θ=ρ_Λ, and the natural ALP range lies entirely above that point, so the overshoot is monotonic in m_θ)" is *correct* but stuck inside a 60-word parenthetical that breaks the reader's parse twice. An adversarial referee reading this paragraph for the first time will probably miss the load-bearing direction of the inequality (`m_θ > H_0` ⇒ ρ_θ > ρ_Λ) and ask whether the paper has just produced a free-parameter ALP that matches ρ_Λ at the order-of-unity level — exactly the opposite of the no-go conclusion.

**Fix:** Break line 691-694 into two short paragraphs. (a) State first that `ρ_θ = (m_θ/H_0)^2 ρ_Λ_at_m_θ=H_0`, so for `m_θ > H_0` ρ_θ exceeds ρ_Λ monotonically. (b) Then quote the overshoot at the two endpoints of the natural-ALP-mass window: 10^{22} at 10^{-22} eV, 10^{36} at 10^{-15} eV. (c) Then state the conclusion: the *only* mass that closes the gap is m_θ = H_0 itself, which is the CC fine-tuning relabelled. Three short sentences, no nested parentheticals. The physics is fine; the prose buries it.

### MINOR-6: R3 mass-dimension-lock argument (§IV.R3, line 633-646) is overstated. The argument concludes with `(Δγ/γ)·(H/M_Pl) ∼ 10^{-63}` suppression.
