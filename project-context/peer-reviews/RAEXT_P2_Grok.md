Manuscript: Testing the Matter Bounce with Primordial Non-Gaussianity: A SPHEREx Sensitivity Recast with a MegaMapper Outlook
Author: Houston Golden
Journal target: MNRAS / PRD / JCAP (high bar)
Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS
BLOCKERS
None. No fatal errors in the core logic, no obviously incorrect claims that survive the internal cross-checks provided, and no irreproducible mathematics once the in-in audit and null-space machinery are accepted at face value. The work is technically competent and the limitations are largely flagged by the author.
MAJORS
Heuristic (additive-quadrature) systematic budget for the headline “realistic” significance range (2.6–5.5σ).
The paper repeatedly and correctly labels the final range a “scoping sensitivity envelope under an additive-quadrature heuristic systematic budget, not a joint-covariance forecasted measurement precision.” However, this range is still presented as one of the two headline results (abstract, Sec. IV, Fig. 2, Table IV). The lower edge (~2.6σ) is obtained by adding in quadrature several contributions (template mismatch via central r ≈ 0.84, ϵ-correction, photometric-z, b_ϕ marginalization to ~0.9–1.0, GR projection with σ_GR = 1.0, etc.). No joint multi-tracer bispectrum Fisher matrix marginalizing over these nuisances is computed for the primary channel (contrast with the subordinate SDB channel, where a proper joint Fisher is shown, revealing 2–4.6× degradation).This is not merely presentational. Because the dominant degeneracies (b1–f_NL, b_ϕ–f_NL, and especially nfNL running) are known to be strong and partially degenerate, the quadrature sum can either over- or under-estimate the true marginal uncertainty depending on the sign of the covariances. The author’s own SDB exercise shows that marginalization loosens constraints; it is therefore plausible that a full bispectrum joint analysis would move the realistic floor.Required action: Either (a) compute (or demonstrate feasibility of) a joint bispectrum Fisher over the key nuisances using the public SPHEREx products already cited, or (b) remove the numerical 2.6σ floor from all headline statements and figures and present the lower edge strictly as an indicative scoping bound with a clear statement of why quadrature is expected to be conservative. The optimistic 5.2–5.5σ (template correction only) can remain, but must be caveated consistently.
The work is framed as a “sensitivity recast” that imports the Heinrich et al. (2024) σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.7 baseline without independent recomputation or shape-specific validation of that Fisher matrix.
The only new quantitative step for the bispectrum channel is the post-hoc multiplicative degradation by the template-overlap factor r (noise-weighted central value 0.84). The injection-recovery test is explicitly noted to be a flat-sky, CMB-style KSW estimator on tiled patches with isotropic Gaussian noise—not the full 3D multi-tracer galaxy bispectrum pipeline that SPHEREx will actually use. While the author is transparent about this, a top-journal forecast paper on a high-stakes claim (distinguishing a parameter-free bounce prediction from inflation at several σ) should contain at least a partial re-derivation or public reproducibility artifact for the baseline Fisher under the bounce shape. Shape-dependent changes in the covariance (even if small) are not guaranteed to factorize cleanly into a single scalar r.Required action: Provide either (i) a limited re-computation of the multi-tracer bispectrum Fisher (or its dominant blocks) for the bounce template using the same public inputs, or (ii) a much stronger justification, with quantitative bounds, that residual shape-dependent covariance effects are negligible compared with the already-quoted systematics. Release of the exact Heinrich et al. Fisher matrix elements (or equivalent) used would also satisfy this.
Strength of the “robust across the bounce class” claim given the status of assumption (d).
Assumption (d) — faithful third-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce — is correctly identified as the weakest link and is supported only by linear-order verification plus a superhorizon scaling estimate (δf_NL ~ 10^{-3}). The abstract and introduction nevertheless describe the f_NL = −35/8 prediction as “minimally parameterized” and “robust across the bounce class (Wilson-Ewing)” with only parenthetical caveats. If cubic-order transmission fails at even the 5–10% level or introduces new shape components, both the significance forecast and the Bayes-factor discrimination degrade.Required action: Add a short but quantitative stress-test subsection (or appendix) showing how the headline significance and Bayes factors respond if assumption (d) is relaxed to allow O(5–10%) amplitude or shape distortion. This can be done with the existing null-space machinery.
MINORS
Bayes-factor headline and prior presentation. The recommended σ_theory = 1.0 Gaussian bounce prior with broad multifield competitor [−15, +15] gives BF ≈ 9–10 (r → 1 endpoint) / ≈ 9 (noise-weighted r ≈ 0.84 bookkeeping). The curvaton-natural narrow prior [−5, +5] drops this to BF ≈ 4. The paper discusses the sensitivity but still headlines the higher number in the abstract. Consider equal visual weight for both competitor priors in the abstract and Table II, or move the narrow-prior column to equal prominence.
Minor internal bookkeeping inconsistencies in presentation. The abstract states both the optimistic (5.2–5.5σ) and realistic (2.6–5.5σ) ranges as “headline forecast.” Later text correctly qualifies the realistic range as heuristic. Align the abstract language with the later caveats (already mostly done, but tighten one sentence).
Data/Code Availability statement. Inline references to “Data and Code Availability” and released JSON files exist, but a standard journal block with a persistent identifier (Zenodo placeholder is acceptable pre-submission) is missing or deferred. Add it.
Repetition between abstract and §I. The in-in audit paragraph and the r-correction explanation are nearly duplicated. Streamline.
Minor presentational point on MegaMapper. The 3–7σ envelope is driven largely by instrument-design uncertainty (unfunded, no final specs). This is already stated, but a one-sentence reminder in the abstract or Fig. 2 caption would prevent casual readers from treating it as a firm forecast.
Strengths (≥3)
High-quality theoretical audit of the Cai et al. bispectrum. The symbolic verification of the in-in commutator identity (i⟨[ζ₃, L]⟩ = −2 Im ⟨ζ₃ L⟩) that fixes the factor-of-two discrepancy with Li et al., combined with explicit benchmark matching at three kinematic points and the full null-space analysis, is a genuine contribution.
