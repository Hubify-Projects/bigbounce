## Referee Report: Technical Verification Companion to the ECH Spin-Torsion Program

**Paper ID:** P1B (Companion to Paper I(a))
**Journal:** Physical Review D (Target)
**Date of Review:** June 13, 2026

### Executive Summary
This manuscript presents a "technical verification" layer for the Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) spin-torsion no-go program. It documents three numerical analyses: a stock-CAMB $\Lambda$CDM+$\Delta N_{\text{eff}}$ proxy, a NaMaster pseudo-$C_\ell$ pipeline validation, and a Spectator-ALP consistency check.

While the authors are explicit about the *scope limitations* of these analyses (e.g., stating the proxy is not a test of the spin-torsion theory itself), the paper suffers from **fundamental structural flaws** that render it unsuitable for publication in *Physical Review D* at this time. The primary issues are:
1.  **Citation Forensics Failures:** Multiple references contain hallucinated or incorrect bibliographic data (journal names, years, arXiv IDs), including a reference to a "Phys. Rev. Lett." for a Planck paper that was published in *Phys. Rev. D* (or *Astron. Astrophys.*), and references to papers dated "2025" or "2026" that appear to be non-existent or self-referential placeholders.
2.  **Internal Consistency & "Future-Dated" Metadata:** The paper is dated June 13, 2026, and cites results from "2025" (e.g., Ref [4], , ) and "2026" (e.g., Ref [1], [6], [7], [8]). In a rigorous physics journal, citing future-dated or non-existent papers as established results is a major violation of scientific integrity unless they are explicitly marked as "in preparation" with a clear distinction from published data. The text treats these as established facts.
3.  **Load-Bearing Scalar Discrepancies:** The abstract claims a "3.6$\sigma$" tension, but the body clarifies this is from a specific joint analysis (Eskilt & Komatsu) while the stock-CAMB proxy *fails* to resolve it. The paper oscillates between claiming the proxy is a "null-consistency test" and implying it contextualizes the ECH program, creating a confusing narrative that undermines the "no-go" conclusion.
4.  **Reproducibility & Versioning:** The manuscript relies heavily on repository version stamps (v1B.0.66) and "pending" DOI assignments. While open science is encouraged, a paper submitted to PRD must have *frozen*, citable data. The reliance on "pending" identifiers and the admission that "fresh re-verification chains are NOT bundled" makes the results unverifiable by the reviewer at the time of submission.

**Recommendation:** **REJECT**. The paper requires a complete overhaul of its bibliography, a retraction of future-dated citations, and a rigorous separation of "in preparation" claims from established results before it can be reconsidered.

## PASS 2 — self-critique findings (what initial review missed)

[P1B-E4] **Equation dimensional consistency issue in Eq. (1): the normalization is not dimensionally explicit.** The estimator is written as \(\chi^2(\beta)=\sum_b\left[C_b^{EB,\mathrm{decoupled}}-\tfrac12\sin(4\beta)\,C_b^{EE,\mathrm{tmpl}}\right]^2\), but the paper never states whether the bandpowers are in the same units and whether the implicit weighting by a covariance or variance factor has been absorbed into the definition of \(\chi^2\). As written, the expression is a sum of squared bandpower differences, not a dimensionless chi-square, so the “\(\chi^2\)” label is only justified if an omitted normalization is built into the code or the bandpower units are already dimensionless.

[P1B-M3] **The reported “\(\sim12\%\) multiplicative under-recovery” is numerically inconsistent with the stated injections.** The paper says \(0.238/0.27=0.302/0.342=0.88\), but \(0.238/0.27\approx0.8815\) and \(0.302/0.342\approx0.8830\), so the actual under-recovery is about \(11.7\%\), not exactly \(12\%\). This is small, but it is an arithmetic rounding claim that should be stated as approximate rather than exact.

[P1B-M4] **The stated “\(\sim25\times\)” fine-tuning of the misalignment initial condition is not consistently derived from the numbers given.** In the abstract the spectator condition is phrased as \(\theta_i\sim0.1\) versus a natural-prior midpoint \(\theta_i\sim0.5\), which is a factor of 5 in \(\theta_i\), not 25. The paper later converts this into an \(\Omega_a\propto \theta_i^2\) statement, which does support an order-\(25\times\) reduction in energy density, but the text oscillates between “misalignment initial condition” and “backreaction fraction” without making the square-law dependence explicit at the point where the \(25\times\) claim is first made.

[P1B-M5] **The “0.01–0.48°” span is not the same object as the observed \(0.342^\circ\), but the prose risks reading it that way.** The paper states that the committed EOM grid gives \(\beta \approx 0.01\text{–}0.48^\circ\) across a parameter box and that this “brackets the observed value,” but the interval is a *model-output range* over many trajectories, not a posterior interval. Because the text uses that range in the same paragraph as the posterior-supported \(0.336^\circ\pm0.10^\circ\) and \(0.344^\circ\pm0.10^\circ\) fits, it should more clearly distinguish *parameter-space coverage* from *statistical support*.

[P1B-M6] **The “3.9σ upper bound” in Eq. (5) is not directly comparable to the published 3.6σ headline, but the paper does not keep that distinction consistently tight.** The text does say the 3.9σ value assumes zero Planck–ACT correlation and is therefore an upper bound, yet it is then used in nearby narrative as a cross-check against the correlated 3.6σ result. This is a null-procedure comparability problem: the 3.9σ and 3.6σ values come from different correlation assumptions and should not be juxtaposed without a stronger reminder that one is deliberately optimistic and not a measurement-equivalent significance.

[P1B-F1] **The abstract’s “spectator-ALP consistency check” claim is only partially supported by the body.** The body shows consistency only in a very restricted region: \(fa\sim M_{\mathrm{Pl}}\), \(m\gg H_0\) for the fixed-\(C_{a\gamma}=8\) fit, or tuned \(\theta_i\sim0.1\) spectator-safe subsets. The abstract compresses this into a generic “consistency check,” but the main text repeatedly emphasizes that the signal is accommodated only near the edge of the prior box and is not a natural prediction. The abstract should state that qualification explicitly.

[P1B-F2] **The abstract’s wording that the paper “documents technical verification material for the ECH spin-torsion no-go program” overstates what the body actually verifies.** The body repeatedly says the CAMB proxy is *not* a spin-torsion theory-module test, the NaMaster work is a *pipeline validation*, and the ALP section is a *consistency check* in standard GR+ALP. That means the paper verifies supporting computations and consistency relations, not the ECH no-go theorem itself. The abstract should say “supporting computational checks” rather than “verification” of the program.

[P1B-G1] **The manuscript makes a novelty-style claim by implication: “technical verification layer” and “structural closure no-go result” are framed as program-defining outputs, but no explicit comparison benchmark is shown for this combination of analyses.** The paper shows individual components are standard—CAMB proxy, NaMaster validation, ALP consistency check—but does not show that this triad is itself a first, largest, or otherwise unique analysis strategy in the literature. If the intended claim is novelty of the *integrated companion-paper structure*, it needs an explicit comparison to prior companion/verification papers.

[P1B-H1] **The phrase “consistent with zero” hides materially different quantitative distances across the table.** The paper reports \(\Delta N_{\mathrm{eff}}=-0.020\pm0.169\) and \(+0.058\pm0.179\), but the text only summarizes both as “consistent with zero.” For a referee report, the actual normalized distances should be stated explicitly: about \(0.12\sigma\) and \(0.32\sigma\), respectively. Without that, the reader cannot see that the two frozen chains differ in sign but not in significance.

[P1B-H2] **The phrase “not competitive sky detection” hides a direct comparison that is actually quantified and should be stated more cleanly.** The body gives the pipeline-recovered \(\hat\beta=0.238^\circ\) for an injected \(0.27^\circ\) signal and a worst-case bias of \(0.040^\circ\), but the comparison to the observational \(0.342^\circ\pm0.094^\circ\) result is qualitative. The paper should explicitly state that the recovered angle differs from the observed central value by about \(0.104^\circ\), which is roughly \(1.1\sigma\) using the observational uncertainty alone, before invoking the different meaning of the pipeline statistic.

[P1B-I1] **The appendix and main text are not fully aligned on what is “committed” versus what is “recomputed.”** The main text says the frozen \(\Lambda\)CDM+\(\Delta N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) chains are fully frozen and that fresh re-verification chains are not bundled, while Appendix A says the exact table entries were recomputed directly from the committed raw chains and the corrected summary files. That is not a contradiction, but it is a versioning asymmetry: the paper mixes “raw chains are committed” with “fresh re-verification is not bundled,” which makes it unclear what a reader can independently reproduce from the paper alone without using the repository.
