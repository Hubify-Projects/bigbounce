VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

1. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS

The manuscript contains a potentially publishable negative/clarifying result: in minimal Einstein–Cartan–Holst gravity with canonical scalar matter, torsion is not sourced, the Holst dual contraction vanishes on the torsion-free branch by the algebraic Bianchi identity, and scalar/tensor perturbation observables are therefore transparent to the Holst sector. That result is clean, useful, and largely self-contained.

However, the paper's broader "channel-level closure" framing remains too strong for a top-journal acceptance in its present form. The manuscript itself repeatedly acknowledges that the four routes are not an operator-level basis; R2/R3 are ansatz-level estimates rather than derivations; R4 is not amplitude-closed but only naturalness-disfavored; and many of the "14 barriers" are heuristic or classificatory rather than independent no-go results. These caveats are commendably explicit, but they undercut the headline claim. The paper should be reframed around the rigorous perturbation-transparency theorem plus a clearly labelled phenomenological audit, not as a near-final closure of minimal-ECH dark-energy routes.

2. BLOCKERS

B1. The title/abstract/conclusions overstate "closure" relative to what is proven.
The paper repeatedly states that it is not proving operator-basis closure and that omitted operators include Jackiw–Pi gravitational Chern–Simons and a parity-odd four-fermion partner. That is scientifically honest, but the title and repeated "channel-level closure" language still read stronger than the demonstrated result. For MNRAS/PRD/JCAP level, either the operator-level basis must be provided, or the claims must be narrowed to "a phenomenological audit of four selected minimal-ECH-inspired channels."

B2. R2 and R3 cannot carry a no-go conclusion without a derived EFT coefficient or a more formal bound.
The manuscript labels the R2 one-loop operator and R3 Immirzi-running equation as upper-bound EFT ansätze, not calculations extracted from the cited literature. That is acceptable for a scoping study, but not for a central "route closed" claim. These should be moved into a "phenomenological estimates" section unless the authors derive the operators and coefficients from a controlled EFT.

B3. R4 is not closed in the same sense as R1–R3.
The manuscript itself says a free-coupling spectator-ALP fit can reproduce both βobs and ρΛ, with the objection being naturalness/mθ ∼ H0 tuning. That is not a no-go; it is an explanatory-deficit argument. The route summary should not group R4 under the same "closed" umbrella without a very clear separate category: "not explained by minimal ECH."

B4. The "14 barriers / 13 mechanism-class constraints" framing is too heterogeneous.
Some barriers are rigorous, some are order-of-magnitude, some are heuristic, and some are philosophical/naturalness statements. Table III helps, but the main text still treats the catalog as collective closure. This should be reorganized into tiers: theorem, standard derivation, dimensional estimates, naturalness objections, and heuristic observations.

3. MAJORS

M1. The perturbation-transparency theorem should become the central result.
This is the strongest part of the paper. The scalar-matter proof is simple but important: canonical scalar matter has zero spin density, hence torsion vanishes, the connection reduces to Levi-Civita, and the Holst dual contraction vanishes by the algebraic Bianchi identity. This should be elevated and presented independently of the speculative dark-energy ansatz.

M2. The dimensional status of the parity-odd operator remains a major weakness.
Appendix B admits that Eq. (6) has off-shell mass dimension +1 and that the ρΛ mapping requires an on-shell scaling ansatz. That is a serious limitation. The paper should not present Ntot ≈ 92 or the "fine-tuning reduction" as more than bookkeeping. A top-journal version should either construct a dimension-4 operator basis or demote the dark-energy mapping to an illustrative parameterization.

M3. The companion-paper dependence is acceptable only if the imported numbers are non-load-bearing everywhere.
The manuscript repeatedly says the MCMC, SPHEREx forecast, NaMaster validation, ALP fit, galaxy chirality pipeline, and anomaly catalog are in companion papers. That is acceptable in a coordinated submission, but every such result must be clearly excluded from the logical proof. Some figures and tables still visually imply broader observational support than the present paper establishes.

M4. The "surviving predictions" are not predictions of ECH.
The paper acknowledges that fNL = −35/8 is matter-bounce-class and β ≈ 0.27° is GR+ALP-class. This should be made even sharper in the abstract, introduction, and conclusion. Otherwise readers may infer that ECH predicts observables that the paper explicitly says are ECH-independent.

M5. The structural tension between Ntot ≈ 92 and observable matter-bounce fNL is interesting but overdeveloped relative to its role.
The scale-history argument is plausible, but the manuscript also says the full transfer-function calculation is deferred. The result should be framed as a consistency warning, not as an independent closure mechanism.

M6. The galaxy-spin material is not central to this paper.
The DESI chirality null appears repeatedly, but the actual pipeline is in Paper IV. In this manuscript it mostly serves as context. Consider compressing it to one paragraph unless the paper is meant to be a synthesis article rather than a theory paper.

M7. The R1 amplitude estimate should use cosmological mean density as the main benchmark, with dense-ISM as an intentionally conservative side bound.
The use of nψ ∼ 10² cm⁻³ is defensible as a conservative upper bound, but it is not the relevant late-time cosmic mean. The manuscript notes this, but the presentation should put the physical cosmological value first.

4. MINORS

m1. Clarify notation β.
The manuscript distinguishes β(γ) from birefringence β, but the repeated use still risks confusion. Consider renaming the RG function to βγ or Bγ throughout.

m2. Reduce duplicate caveats.
The paper is admirably transparent, but the same caveats recur many times: "not operator-level," "ansatz not derivation," "companion non-load-bearing," "ECH-independent." Condensing these into a single scope box plus targeted reminders would improve readability.

m3. Figure ordering appears odd.
Figure 3 appears after Figures 4–7 in the parsed PDF. If this is also true in the rendered PDF, reorder figures or renumber to match first citation order.

m4. "Fine-tuning reduction from 10¹²² to 10⁵" should be avoided or visually de-emphasized.
Even with caveats, this phrase invites misreading. "Reparameterization of sensitivity into Ntot" is safer.

m5. Avoid calling LiteBIRD a discriminator of β = 0.27° versus current βobs.
The conclusion correctly notes the separation is only ~0.7σ when current uncertainty is included. Keep only the nonzero-β detection forecast unless a joint forecast is actually performed.

m6. Some language is too advocacy-like for a journal article.
Phrases such as "surviving tests," "closure map," and "all four routes closed" should be toned down unless paired immediately with the evidentiary tier.

m7. Define "minimal ECH" operationally earlier.
The paper excludes propagating torsion, dynamical Immirzi fields, non-minimal matter, fermion-loop sectors in the transparency theorem, and excludes some parity-odd operators from the four-route enumeration. A precise definition near the beginning would prevent scope drift.

5. Strengths

S1. The perturbation-transparency result is clean and valuable.
The torsion-free scalar-matter branch and Bianchi-identity argument provide a useful clarification for cosmological perturbation claims involving the Holst sector.

S2. The manuscript is unusually honest about evidentiary status.
Table III and the repeated caveats make clear which pieces are rigorous, structural, or ansatz-level. This transparency is a major strength, even though the headline framing should be narrowed.

S3. The R4 correction is scientifically mature.
Rather than falsely claiming an amplitude no-go, the paper correctly reframes the ALP/birefringence route as a naturalness/explanatory-deficit issue. That is a better and more defensible conclusion.

S4. The distinction between Holst dual contraction and Pontryagin density is handled carefully.
The manuscript explicitly prevents a common confusion: the one-curvature Holst dual contraction vanishes on the torsionless branch, while the two-curvature Pontryagin density is a separate invariant.

S5. The paper could serve as a useful negative-results map if retitled and reorganized.
A top-journal version focused on "what minimal ECH cannot do under stated assumptions" would be valuable, especially if speculative estimates are cleanly separated from theorem-level claims.
