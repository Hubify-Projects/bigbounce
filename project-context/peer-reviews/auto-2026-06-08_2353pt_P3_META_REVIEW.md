### P3-META-E2 — The "Fisher-positivity respecting form" 1/σ² = F₀ + cα² is *not* the function used to generate Table VII; c=0.0747 is a single-point match, not a fit
**Section**: §V.b (p. 11), §VI D caveat (i) (p. 13), Appendix C Table VII (p. 16)
**Why missed**: P3-E16 (Brutal) and P3-E1 (Gemini) noticed the 7.9% vs 9.4% arithmetic mismatch but did not audit the c=0.0747 coefficient itself; Perplexity P3-E3 flagged the Fisher derivation as un-cited but did not check internal consistency.
**Problem**: Appendix C states Table VII is constructed "by **linear scaling** of the fiducial 7-bin Fisher result at α = 0.15 (Section V)", giving σ(α) = 8.98 × (1 − (6.1%/0.15)α). §VI D caveat (i) then quotes a quadratic positivity form 1/σ² = F₀ + cα² with F₀ = 1/8.98², c = 0.0747, claiming "verified positive via 5-α refit". But a quadratic in α² cannot reproduce data generated by a linear function in α. Extracting c at each Table VII row:
- α = 0.05: c = (1/8.80² − 1/8.98²) / 0.0025 ≈ **0.204**
- α = 0.15: c ≈ **0.0747** ← the quoted value
- α = 0.25: c ≈ **0.0473**
- α = 0.50: c ≈ **0.0286**

The "refit" is therefore a one-point match at the fiducial α = 0.15, not a multi-point fit, and the form does not respect the table it claims to derive from. Crucially, the 1σ envelope [3.92, 8.98] reported in the abstract was computed by plugging α = α_jk ± σ_α into this self-inconsistent form. The headline cosmological forecast rests on a curve fit that contradicts its own training data.
**Required fix**: Either (a) derive F₀ and c from an actual Fisher matrix computation at multiple α (not from linear extrapolation), or (b) discard the quadratic form and report σ(f_NL) using the linear-scaling Table VII directly with a transparently linear error envelope.

## ADDITIONAL MAJOR findings

### P3-META-M1 — Recommended catalog-grade subset of "∼265,000" is arithmetically incoherent
**Section**: Abstract (p. 1)
**Why missed**: Gemini P3-m4 noted that DESI+SDSS+eROSITA+Gaia+NEOWISE sums to 274,951 and asked for clarification; no reviewer worked out the implied dedup arithmetic.
**Problem**: Abstract: "the recommended catalog-grade subset is ∼265,000 unique objects (DESI + SDSS + eROSITA + Gaia + NEOWISE), which excludes the LAMOST exploratory tier (∼113,000 objects)". The implied derivation is 378,080 − 113,342 = 264,738. But that subtraction assumes **zero positional overlap** between LAMOST and the other five point-source surveys — i.e., all 113,342 LAMOST native-retrain anomalies are unique to LAMOST and contribute zero to the 9,576 intra-survey duplicate count. This is implausible: LAMOST shares its footprint with SDSS and DESI in the northern hemisphere, and the 7-way dedup at 5″ would necessarily collapse some LAMOST↔SDSS and LAMOST↔DESI pairs. The correct "catalog-grade subset" requires re-running the dedup without LAMOST as input, not subtracting the LAMOST native count from the dedup'd total. The 265,000 figure is therefore a lower bound, not the catalog count of the recommended subset.
**Required fix**: Re-run the 6-way (LAMOST-excluded) positional dedup and quote the actual unique count; or remove the "265,000 catalog-grade subset" framing from the abstract.

### P3-META-M2 — 5-fold cross-validation Jaccard test is structurally optimistic
**Section**: §II.B (p. 2), §VI D caveat (i) (p. 13)
**Why missed**: All five reviewers accepted the J̄ = 0.862 PASS at face value or focused on the 0.874 ↔ 0.732 mismatch (P3-E17). None questioned what the test is actually measuring.
**Problem**: "each fold trains a fresh BigAE on 80% (37,600) and scores the full 47,000". Any two folds share 60% of their training data (overlap of 80% × 80% = 64%, more precisely 80% common = 30,000 of 37,600). With ~75% training overlap between any two folds, top-1% reconstruction-error sets must be highly correlated by construction. A J̄ = 0.862 in this regime is plausibly compatible with overfitting to common training spectra, not generalization stability. The proper test would be **disjoint** train/score splits (e.g., 5×20% fold-out testing), which the paper does not run. The gate ≥ 0.70 is therefore set against an artificially easy baseline.
**Required fix**: Add a disjoint-fold test where each model scores *only* its own held-out 20%, and report the Jaccard over the union of held-out sets. The current ≥ 0.70 gate is not informative as a stability claim.

### P3-META-M3 — The 47,000-spectrum DESI training pool composition is undefined; µ_val and σ_val for Eq. (2) depend on it
**Section**: §II.B (p. 2), §III.A (p. 4)
**Why missed**: P3-E1 (Brutal), P3-E5 (Perplexity) flagged the Eq. (2) score definition as internally inconsistent; none asked how the training pool itself was selected.
**Problem**: "the model is trained on a representative subset of the data (47,000 spectra for DESI…)" — no selection criterion is given. If the 47,000 are drawn only from the ~6.5M validated-TARGETTYPE subset (the well-classified BGS/LRG/ELG/QSO/MWS), then µ_val and σ_val reflect *clean* spectra, while the score is applied to the full 22.5M catalog including 16M filler/sky/calibration spectra whose typical MSE distribution is unrelated to the training pool. The 0.87% anomaly rate then mixes (i) genuinely unusual sources within the 6.5M validated set and (ii) the bulk of the 16M unclassified pool registering anomalously simply because the training pool excludes their physics. The OOD validation footnote already concedes that an uncurated SPARCL sweep flags >50% of spectra; this implies the training pool is far from "representative". The 0.87% headline anomaly rate is therefore a function of the **unstated training-pool curation**, not of the survey.
**Required fix**: Document the TARGETTYPE / Z_WARN / coadded-SNR cuts applied to construct the 47,000-spectrum training pool, and re-state the 0.87% rate as conditional on those cuts.

### P3-META-M5 — No null/sanity test on shuffled or synthetic data
**Section**: §II.D / Path-C protocol (entire validation block)
**Why missed**: All five reviewers critiqued the failed gates; none asked for a null-data control.
**Problem**: The Path-C protocol's six steps include injection-recovery (which 3 of 6 surveys fail) and Jaccard stability (already addressed above), but it does not include the elementary control of training BigAE on random/shuffled spectra to verify that the autoencoder produces a *non-anomaly* signal on null data. Without this, the 0.87% DESI rate cannot be distinguished from the rate expected from a trivially over-parameterized 660K-parameter network reconstructing 496-dim spectra. PRD methodology referees routinely demand such a test for novel pipeline claims.
**Required fix**: Add a null-shuffle test (train on flux-randomized DESI spectra; score original) and quote the resulting "anomaly rate"; expect ≤ 0.1% for a working pipeline.

### P3-META-M6 — The "anomalies peak at z ∼ 0.75 vs. z ∼ 0.93 for normal spectra" comparison is misleadingly juxtaposed with the high-z Gunn–Peterson claim
**Section**: §III.A (p. 4), repeated on p. 5
**Why missed**: Brutal P3-E11 caught the duplication; none parsed the substantive misjoinder.
**Problem**: The paper writes: "anomalies peak at z ∼ 0.75 vs. z ∼ 0.93 for normal spectra. The three highest-scored anomalies (S = 25.2, 24.6, 24.5) are Z-dominant, consistent with high-z Gunn–Peterson absorption." But Gunn–Peterson signal lives at z ≳ 6, not 0.75; and the anomaly population peaking at *lower* z than the normal population is *opposite* to what a high-z Gunn-Peterson population would produce. It does not.
**Required fix**: Separate the two findings with explicit framing: (a) the bulk anomaly population is galaxy-dominated and lower-z because galaxies outnumber QSOs in the anomaly tier; (b) three individual outliers are consistent with z ≈ 6 Gunn–Peterson independently.

### P3-META-M7 — NEOWISE input of 43,518 sources is an undocumented sub-sample of a >10⁹-source catalog
**Section**: §III.H (p. 8), Table I
**Why missed**: Brutal P3-M1 listed Gaia and NEOWISE input sizes as "not justified" but did not quantify the slice fraction.

### P3-META-M8 — Planck native CMB injection-recovery at "5σ Gaussian-bump amplitude" — amplitude relative to what noise, and at what angular scale?
**Section**: §III.F (p. 7), Fig. 7 caption (p. 13)
**Why missed**: All reviewers either accepted the 100% PASS or focused on the criterion-(a) val-loss failure; none asked about the test definition.
**Problem**: A Gaussian bump at "5σ amplitude" on a 64×64 SMICA patch is amplitude-relative to the per-pixel temperature noise. The CMB power spectrum is far from white; a 5σ-per-pixel bump at sub-arcminute scales is many orders of magnitude weaker than a 5σ-per-pixel bump at degree scales (the SMICA Wiener-filtered output has a particular signal-to-noise structure). Without specifying the angular scale and noise reference, the 100% recovery is unconstrained — recovery of a sufficiently large feature at any scale will pass. This matters because the Planck-native autoencoder is one of only 3 PASS gates and the criterion-(b) injection result is the entire justification for retaining the CMB tier after criterion (a) val_loss = 0.4437 (vs. ≤ 0.30 gate) fails by ~50%.
**Required fix**: Specify the bump FWHM (in arcminutes), the noise reference (per-pixel SMICA noise estimate or theoretical CMB-only variance), and the temperature amplitude in µK. Report recovery as a function of (FWHM, amplitude) rather than a single number.
