1. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS

The paper is unusually transparent about its own assumptions and limitations, and the perturbation-transparency result is potentially publishable. However, at top-journal bar, the manuscript still over-claims relative to what is actually proven. The central "closure" is a mixture of one rigorous theorem, one standard torsion-elimination estimate, several ansatz-level dimensional arguments, and several naturalness/heuristic objections. That is valuable, but not yet strong enough for the current no-go framing without major restructuring.

2. BLOCKERS

B1 — "Four-route no-go" is not a no-go theorem.
The paper repeatedly says "all four routes closed," but also admits the route list is not an operator basis and omits gravitational Chern–Simons and parity-odd four-fermion operators. This is not just scope wording; it changes the theorem status. The title/abstract/conclusion must be recast as a conditional channel audit, not a no-go.

B2 — Dark-energy mapping rests on an uncontrolled dimensional ansatz.
Appendix B states Eq. (6) is dimension +1, not a controlled dimension-4 EFT operator, and that the mapping to ρΛ is phenomenological. Any headline involving dark-energy "closure" must be subordinate to this. The paper cannot both rely on this mapping for route bookkeeping and present the result as robust EFT exclusion.

B3 — R2/R3 closures use unpublished or non-derived EFT ansätze.
The manuscript explicitly says the displayed one-loop/RG forms are not literal derivations from the cited literature. These can be upper-bound estimates, but they cannot support a strong no-go unless the allowed operator space and coefficient priors are defined.

B4 — Companion dependence remains too high for observational claims.
Although the paper says companions are non-load-bearing, many headline numbers—SPHEREx 2.6–5σ, MCMC values, NaMaster validation, ALP fitting, galaxy chirality null—are imported from in-preparation work. The present paper must either remove those from the primary claims or provide enough standalone derivation/validation.

3. MAJORS

M1 — The 13/14-barrier catalog is over-counted rhetorically.
Several barriers are explicitly heuristic, naturalness-based, or broad classification arguments. Calling them "13 distinct mechanism-class constraints" is acceptable only if the abstract and conclusion stop implying comparable evidentiary weight.

M2 — R4 is not "closed" in the same sense as R1–R3.
The paper correctly admits R4 can match βobs and ρΛ with mθ ∼ H0, and is rejected on explanatory/naturalness grounds. That is not a closure at amplitude level. It should be separated as "not explained by minimal ECH," not "closed."

M3 — The Ntot ≈ 92 tension with fNL needs a transfer-function calculation or softer wording.
The scale-history argument is plausible, but "definitively erased" is too strong without the transfer-function calculation the paper says is deferred.

M4 — Figures 3–5 risk misleading readers.
Figure 3 uses an illustrative high H0 different from the adopted value and shows a 2–3% residual dominated by H0 offset. Figure 5 compares fine-tuning scores, including literature-level illustrative entries, while the paper itself says the 10^5 reduction is only a reparameterization. These should be demoted or redesigned.

M5 — Perturbation transparency is the strongest result but buried.
The paper should be reorganized around the theorem: assumptions, proof, scope, breaking cases, implications. The route audit should follow as conditional phenomenology.

4. MINORS

- The abstract is too long and contains too many caveats, making the actual contribution hard to parse.
- Use one consistent convention for α/M, gaγ, θ, and ϕ in the main text rather than relying on long footnotes.
- "June 29, 2026" is current and fine.
- Companion "in preparation / posted concurrently" citations are acceptable, but all such imported numbers should be marked as non-peer-reviewable inputs.
- The use of "confirmed null" for galaxy chirality should be softened unless the companion catalog is available to the referee.

5. Strengths

- The paper is unusually self-auditing and explicitly flags many limitations that most manuscripts would hide.
- The perturbation-transparency theorem for canonical scalar matter is clear, useful, and likely publishable if isolated and sharpened.
- The correction that R4 is a naturalness objection rather than an amplitude no-go is scientifically honest and improves the manuscript.
- The distinction between ECH-specific claims and ECH-independent bounce/ALP tests is important and mostly handled well.
- The torsion-elimination/NJL amplitude estimate is convincing and appropriately conservative.
