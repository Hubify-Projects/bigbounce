## Per-finding detail

### M1 (MAJOR) §sec:limitations line 583 — "17.8% as provisional upper bound" contradicts the very same sentence's "single-sample point estimate" reframe

The R46/M1 fix was supposed to retire the asymmetric "provisional upper
bound" framing of the 17.8% novelty rate and replace it with the
neutral "single-sample point estimate". The abstract (line 54) does this
correctly. But §sec:limitations line 583 still reads:

> We label $17.8\%$ a ``provisional upper bound'' on the full-catalog
> novelty rate under the working hypothesis that lower-scored anomalies
> are more frequently associated with the margins of known-source
> populations than the highest-scored anomalies are; this hypothesis is
> empirically untested in the present analysis (the converse hypothesis
> ... is at least equally plausible a priori). [...] until that
> measurement is in hand, $17.8\%$ should be read as a single-sample
> point estimate rather than a robust bound on the full catalog.

So the same sentence both **labels** 17.8% a "provisional upper bound"
AND tells the reader it should be **read as a single-sample point
estimate**. The two are mutually exclusive framings: an "upper bound"
asserts directionality (full-catalog ≤ 17.8%), a "point estimate"
asserts no directionality. The R46/M1 closure should have **deleted**
the "provisional upper bound" label outright, not glossed it as
"actually means single-sample point estimate". Compounding this:
Conclusions item 2 (line 627) reuses a third label — **"genuine
novelty floor of approximately 17.8%"** — which asserts the *opposite*
directionality (full-catalog ≥ 17.8%) from "upper bound".

Three contradictory labels for the same number across three sections
of the same paper is a real consistency hit a referee will catch.

**Fix:** Strike the "provisional upper bound" sentence in §sec:limitations
entirely; replace Conclusions item 2 "genuine novelty floor" with
"single-sample point estimate of genuine novelty"; harmonize on the
abstract's framing across all three sites.

### M2 (MAJOR) §Data availability line 651 — Acknowledgments paragraph directs consumers to the WITH-ACT (sensitivity-check) parquet, not the canonical no-ACT file

The data availability paragraph defines two parquets in its second
sentence:
- `pathc_unique_objects_no_act.parquet` (378,280 rows, the canonical
  ACT-quarantined headline file)
- `pathc_unique_objects.parquet` (378,480 rows, the with-ACT sensitivity
  check)

Then the same paragraph closes:

> Consumers are directed to \texttt{pathc\_unique\_objects.parquet} and
> the \texttt{\_pathc\_native} suffixed files for all headline numbers.

This points readers at the **with-ACT** variant for "all headline
numbers", which directly contradicts the rest of the paper's
position that ACT is quarantined and excluded from the 378,280
headline. Anyone following this directive downloads the wrong file
and reproduces an off-by-200 (the ACT cross-transfer patches) headline.

**Fix:** Change the closing sentence to "Consumers are directed to
\texttt{pathc\_unique\_objects\_no\_act.parquet} (the canonical
ACT-quarantined headline) and the \texttt{\_pathc\_native} suffixed
files for all headline numbers; \texttt{pathc\_unique\_objects.parquet}
is the with-ACT sensitivity-check artifact only."

### m2 (MINOR) §sec:fnl line 550 — body conflates "b_GS/b_full = 3.17" (signal-bin geomean) with "b_GS/b_full = 2.83 ± 2.03" (jackknife geomean) without a single-sentence rule

The body of §sec:fnl line 550 says, regarding the Wave 14-KKKK Path-B
re-measurement:

> ... yields $b_{\rm GS}/b_{\rm full,\,jk} = 2.83 \pm 2.03$ at the
> geomean over jackknife realizations [...], with the central-value
> geomean over the three signal bins giving $b_{\rm GS}/b_{\rm
> full,\,geo} = 3.17$ ...

Earlier in the same paragraph:

> the $1{,}122$-object Gold+Silver subset of Pipeline-1 (the prior
> "preliminary $1.58\times$" benchmark) gives $b_{\rm GS}/b_{\rm
> full} = 3.17$ (geomean) at the same signal scales

The first "3.17" is *defined* as the per-bin geomean (signal-bin
weighted). The second "3.17" — used as the cross-check anchor against
Pipeline-1's 1.58× — is **the same number**, but the body never tells
the reader that the cross-check uses the per-bin geomean rather than
the headline jackknife geomean (2.83). A skeptical referee will flag
this as cherry-picking the larger of two equally-defensible central
values for the Pipeline-1 consistency narrative.

**Fix:** Add one sentence before the Pipeline-1 sentence: "We use the
per-bin geomean ($b_{\rm GS}/b_{\rm full,\,geo} = 3.17$) for this
cross-check rather than the headline jackknife geomean ($2.83$)
because the per-bin geomean is the estimator most directly comparable
to the Pipeline-1 random-baseline analysis, which is itself a per-bin
ratio. The two estimators agree within their respective uncertainties."

### m3 (MINOR) §sec:fnl line 550 — Wave 14-II Fisher computation reports σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.07 internally and Münchmeyer consensus σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.4–0.9; the abstract still says σ(f_NL) = 0.7, leaving Münchmeyer-vs-internal ambiguity

The body §sec:fnl reports the Wave 14-II marginalized Fisher floor at
σ(f_NL) ∈ 0.067–0.116 across six configurations and immediately notes
this is "a factor of ~3–10 tighter than the Münchmeyer et al. consensus
σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.4–0.9 for SPHEREx-class surveys", and asserts "absolute
σ(f_NL) figures from this computation are reported as a relative
ranking deliverable, not a replacement for the literature-consensus
forecast."

The abstract (line 54) does NOT cite an absolute σ(f_NL) figure, only
"$3$–$5\sigma$ detection significance" inserted via the Heinrich+2023
methodology. The Conclusions item 5 (line 633) inherits the same
narrative. So far no inconsistency at the headline level.

But the abstract's "preliminary forecast yields $3$–$5\sigma$
detection significance for $\fnl = -35/8$" + body's "Fisher floors to
0.067–0.116" + body's "literature consensus is 0.4–0.9 (Münchmeyer)"
creates a numerical question a referee will ask: at σ(f_NL) = 0.07,
$|f_{\rm NL}|/\sigma = 4.375/0.07 \approx 62\sigma$ (absurd), while at
σ(f_NL) = 0.7, $4.375/0.7 \approx 6.3\sigma$ (also above the quoted
range), and at σ(f_NL) = 0.9, $4.375/0.9 \approx 4.9\sigma$ (just
inside). The "3–5σ" range in the abstract is therefore implicitly
anchored to the **Münchmeyer-conservative end** of the σ(f_NL) range,
not to the internal Fisher result. The paper never states this
explicitly.

**Fix:** Add one sentence to the abstract right after "$3$–$5\sigma$
detection significance": "(this range is anchored to the Münchmeyer
\etal~\cite{Munchmeyer2019} σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.4–0.9 SPHEREx-class consensus;
our internal Wave 14-II Fisher floor of 0.07–0.12 is reported as a
relative ranking deliverable rather than a literature-replacement
forecast, see §sec:fnl)."

### n2 (NIT) §sec:nanograv line 557 + §appendix line 949 — three-significant-figure rounding policy is asserted as "throughout the paper" but the abstract uses 2 sig figs

§sec:nanograv line 557:

> ... synthetic-from-power-law summary-statistic fit ($\gamma = 3.20
> \pm 0.42$; raw fit $3.193 \pm 0.423$, reported to three significant
> figures throughout) ...

The abstract does not cite the synthetic fit at all (since the
real-KDE supersedes it), so technically no abstract collision. But
the appendix line 949:

> The synthetic-from-power-law summary-statistic fit used in earlier
> internal drafts ($\gamma = 3.20 \pm 0.42$, three-significant-figure
> rounding of the raw fit $3.193 \pm 0.423$)

Both sites round 3.193 → 3.20 (3 sig figs) and 0.423 → 0.42 (2 sig
figs). The "three significant figures throughout" claim is therefore
narrowly applied to the central value but not to the uncertainty.
Strict 3-sig-fig rounding would give "3.19 ± 0.423" or "3.20 ± 0.420".

**Fix:** Either change the rounding-policy claim to "rounded to three
significant figures on the central value, two significant figures on
the uncertainty (standard PRD practice)", or actually round both
quantities to 3 sig figs.
