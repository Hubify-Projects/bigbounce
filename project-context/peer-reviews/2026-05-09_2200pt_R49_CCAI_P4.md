## Findings

### M1 — `fn:mc_count` (footnote at line 1073-1087) is contradicted by the body it footnotes

**Location:** §IV.B Dipole Analysis, footnote attached to line 1072 (production-run `N_MC=1,000`).

**Reproduction:** Footnote asserts uniform `N_MC=1,000` across:
1. Bootstrap dipole significance — but main text line 1092 says `N_MC=10,000` ("canonical $N_{\rm MC}=10{,}000$ result on the Catalog~C equivariant spiral subsample"), and footnote 31 (line 1099-1104) explicitly supersedes the 1,000 with 10,000.
2. ℓ=1-5 multipole-null significances in Table 4 — body line 1226 says `N_MC=500` for the MASTER post-deconvolution null at ℓ=1, and line 1234 says `N_MC=1,000` for ℓ≥2 raw pseudo-Cℓ. Asymmetric within Table 4.
3. MC envelopes in Figs. sky_map and multipoles — Fig. multipoles caption (line 1317) does say `1,000`, consistent.

So three distinct MC counts (500, 1,000, 10,000) coexist in §IV.B and `fn:mc_count` claims uniform 1,000 across all of them. The footnote then continues: *"the 2.75σ claim (p ≈ 0.006) is therefore resolved by the simulation. A 10,000-realization run confirms p = 0.005 ± 0.001"* — this is now narratively obsolete because (a) the canonical primary is `−0.12σ MASTER-deconvolved`, not 2.75σ, and (b) the body now treats 10,000 MC as the canonical (not 1,000) for the simple-dipole bootstrap.

**Severity:** MAJOR. A hostile referee reads `fn:mc_count`, then reads three contradictory MC counts in the body it footnotes, and writes "the paper does not consistently report Monte Carlo sample sizes."

**Suggested fix:** Rewrite `fn:mc_count` to acknowledge the three regimes:
- Simple-dipole bootstrap: `N_MC = 10,000` (canonical, JJJJJ closure).
- MASTER post-deconvolution null at ℓ=1: `N_MC = 500` (full M_ℓℓ' inversion is ~2× more expensive per draw).
- Raw pseudo-Cℓ null at ℓ≥2 (Table 4 rows 2-5) and the figures: `N_MC = 1,000`.

Drop the now-orphan "2.75σ p≈0.006 resolved by simulation" sentence; that comparator no longer applies after the canonical-primary policy.

### m1 — Abstract conflates 3.86× (NS-pool) with the within-spiral 3.04× monopole reduction

**Location:** Abstract line 80; cross-reference §III.B line 416-418.

**Reproduction:** Abstract states "post-processing stage with a measured suppression factor of `3.86×` on the full catalog." Three lines later the abstract cites "global clockwise fraction `0.4974` ... `9.5σ` from `0.5000`" — the within-spiral monopole. The within-spiral monopole reduction sequence is `+0.79% → +0.4% → −0.26%` (Table 1, factor `3.04×`), explicitly distinct from the NS-pool `+2.05% → −0.53%` factor `3.86×` (line 2061-2063). §III.B line 416-418 explicitly says: *"the two factors should not be conflated — the headline 3.86× throughout this paper is the NS-pool factor, not the within-spiral monopole factor."*

The abstract uses `3.86×` as the global headline but pairs it (within four lines) with the within-spiral monopole numbers, which were reduced by `3.04×`. A reader who applies `3.86×` to the within-spiral monopole gets the wrong arithmetic.

**Severity:** minor (the body resolves it; abstract is the worst place to leave it).

**Suggested fix:** Add a single phrase to the abstract `3.86×` sentence: *"...with a measured suppression factor of `3.86×` on the full-catalog NS-pool transition (the within-spiral monopole reduction is the smaller `3.04×`; see Sec. III.B)."*

### m2 — Table 1 caption denominator claim is broader than the displayed σ-deviations support

**Location:** Table `tab:cw_frac` caption, line 1039-1046.

**Reproduction:** Caption says "Uncertainties are 1σ binomial: σ = √(p(1-p)/N_spiral) with N_spiral = 3,201,160 (canonical equivariant total)." Then the table:
- Row A (raw): 0.5079 ± 0.0003, deviation 28.8σ. Math: 0.0079/√(0.25/3,201,160) = 28.3σ ≠ 28.8σ. Under the snapshot 3,321,795: 0.0079/√(0.25/3,321,795) = 28.79σ → 28.8σ ✓.
- Row B (calibrated): 0.504 ± 0.0003, deviation 14.6σ. Under 3,201,160: 0.004/√(0.25/3,201,160) = 14.32σ. Under 3,321,795: 14.59σ → 14.6σ ✓.
- Row C (equivariant): 0.4974 ± 0.0003, deviation 9.5σ. Under 3,201,160: 0.00265/√(0.4974·0.5026/3,201,160) = 9.48σ → 9.5σ ✓.

So rows A and B are computed against the **snapshot** 3,321,795 (Cat A spiral total = 1,687,069 + 1,634,726 = 3,321,795 — which is correct because Cat A counts BEFORE the equivariant recount that produced 3,201,160). Only row C uses the canonical 3,201,160. The caption's blanket "with N_spiral = 3,201,160" is wrong for rows A and B.

**Severity:** minor. The deviations are physically correct (each tier uses its own N), but the caption asserts a uniform denominator that is not the per-row reality.

**Suggested fix:** Caption should read: *"Uncertainties are 1σ binomial: σ = √(p(1-p)/N) with N taken from each tier's own spiral count (Cat A: 3,321,795 spirals; Cat B: 3,321,795 calibrated; Cat C: 3,201,160 equivariant — see Sec. IV.A footnote on the recount)."*

### m3 — Bonferroni-650 vs healpix-768 reconciliation under-explains why the two are not comparable

**Location:** §IV.D Hemisphere Asymmetry, line 1340-1347.

**Reproduction:** The reconciliation now reads "the ~650 count is the number of distinct hemisphere axes sampled on the 10° -stride Galactic-l/b grid used in this section; the independent direct Monte Carlo calibration in the footnote below pixelizes the sky at HEALPix NSIDE=8, N_pix=12·NSIDE²=768, so the two numbers refer to different sampling schemes." Good — the schemes are correctly distinguished. But the prose does not say *why* both are reported.

A hostile referee will ask: if the analytic Bonferroni penalty uses 650 trials and the direct-MC null uses 768 directions, why aren't these in tension? The implicit (correct) answer is that 650 directions on a great-circle grid are *more* correlated than 768 healpix pixels (great-circle hemispheres at neighboring 10° axes share 90%+ of galaxies), so Bonferroni-650 is the *conservative* analytic bound, and the 768-direction direct MC is the empirical tightening. The paper does not say this.

**Severity:** minor. One sentence closes it.

**Suggested fix:** After "different sampling schemes" add: *"The 650-axis Bonferroni penalty is conservative because neighboring 10° hemisphere axes share most of their galaxies (correlated test statistics); the 768-direction direct MC at NSIDE=8 samples weakly correlated patches and produces the tighter empirical p_LEE ≤ 10⁻⁴ bound below."*
