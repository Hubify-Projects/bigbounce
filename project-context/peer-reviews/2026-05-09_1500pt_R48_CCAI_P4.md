## Findings

### B1 — Table 4 (`tab:sky_balance`) is internally self-contradictory after GGGGG edit

**Location:** lines 1413–1423.

**Reproduction:**
- Per-region rows sum: `628,411 + 854,293 + 912,106 + 926,985 = 3,321,795` (RA quadrants); `487,629 + 1,692,430 + 1,141,736 = 3,321,795` (Dec bands). Independently verified.
- Snapshot all-sky row: `3,321,795` ✓ (matches sum).
- Bolded canonical all-sky row: `3,201,160`, **with the same `f_CW = 0.4974` and `|Δ| = 0.26%` as the snapshot row**.

This is arithmetically incoherent. If the canonical denominator is `3,201,160` (≈ 120,635 fewer spirals), and those removed spirals were drawn proportionally from the per-region rows, then `(N_CW^canonical − 0.5·N_canonical)/N_canonical` cannot equal the snapshot value to four significant figures unless the dropped spirals were exactly parity-balanced — which is unstated and not obviously true. As presented, the table claims:
```
sum(per-region rows) = 3,321,795       (snapshot)
all-sky canonical    = 3,201,160       (canonical)
```
with no reconciliation of where the missing 120,635 spirals went per region, AND with the canonical row claiming the same Δ as the snapshot row, which is only possible if the per-region rows ALSO change but were not updated.

**Severity:** BLOCKER. The footnote (line 1392-1406) attempts to wave this away with "the per-region |Δ| values shift by ≲4% relative under the canonical denominator," but the table itself displays the `f_CW` and `|Δ|` as identical between snapshot and canonical rows — which is the opposite of what the footnote says. **Either the per-region rows must be recomputed against the canonical N_spiral (and the |Δ| values updated by ≲4% relative as the footnote promises), OR the bolded "canonical" all-sky row must be removed and replaced with a per-region recount under the canonical denominator.** The current table is a referee-bait artifact: a single `grep '3,321,795'` on the table reveals the inconsistency.

**Suggested fix:** Either (a) recompute the per-region CW/CCW counts under the on-disk `class_eq` column, populate per-region canonical rows, and remove the snapshot rows; or (b) add a per-region canonical block below the snapshot block with actually-different `|Δ|` values reflecting the ≲4% relative shift the footnote claims. Option (a) is the clean fix.

### M1 — Eq. (sigma_dip) line 1768 uses stale snapshot 0.76%

**Location:** lines 1747–1769.

Eq. (sigma_pix) at line 1747-1749 correctly states `σ_pix = 1/(2√4,168) ≈ 0.77%` (canonical). Then Eq. (sigma_dip) at line 1768 reads:
```
σ(A_dip) ≈ σ_pix·√(3/768) = 0.76%·√(3/768) ≈ 0.047%
```
The `0.76%` here is the **superseded snapshot value** that line 1753 explicitly tags as "superseded by the canonical 0.77%". Strict arithmetic:
- Canonical: `0.77 × √(3/768) = 0.77 × 0.0625 = 0.0481%` → rounds to `0.048%`, not `0.047%`.
- Snapshot: `0.76 × √(3/768) = 0.0475%` → rounds to `0.047%` (the value displayed).

Cascade effect: the floor `3 × 0.047% ≈ 0.14%` (line 1776) is also pegged to the snapshot. Under canonical: `3 × 0.048% = 0.144%`, still rounds to `0.14%` at two sig figs, so the headline doesn't move — but a hostile referee runs `python3 -c "print(0.77*(3/768)**0.5)"` and gets `0.0481`, which doesn't match the displayed `0.047%`. This is a one-character defect (`0.76 → 0.77`, `0.047 → 0.048`) that GGGGG missed when it converted the surrounding prose to the canonical denominator.

**Suggested fix:** In Eq. (sigma_dip) replace `0.76%` with `0.77%` and `0.047%` with `0.048%`. Update line 1776 to `3 × 0.048% ≈ 0.14%` (still rounds correctly). Optionally update line 1782 `~40% margin` since `0.2/0.144 = 1.39`, still ≈ 40% but the margin number is unchanged. Net: a 4-character edit to close.

### M2 — Footnote line 1175 strands a "discrepancy" framing that no longer applies

**Location:** lines 1173–1178.

The text reads:
> The apparent discrepancy between the simple dipole amplitude (0.43σ, Sec. III.B) and the angular power spectrum ℓ=1 coefficient (2.75σ) reflects different estimator sensitivities and the effect of the survey mask on large-scale modes.

After GGGGG, the canonical-primary ℓ=1 power-spectrum coefficient is `−0.12σ` (post-MASTER deconvolved), not `2.75σ`. The `2.75σ` is the older buggy-denominator snapshot, retained only as a historical cross-reference (line 1276). With the canonical-primary policy now adopted (line 1289 "We adopt the post-MASTER deconvolution number ... as the *primary*"), the headline framing should describe the comparison `0.43σ (simple dipole) vs −0.12σ (MASTER ℓ=1)` — between which there is **no discrepancy**, both consistent with null. The three-mechanism analysis that follows (lines 1180–1267) is appropriate context for the *raw pseudo-Cl* `6.48σ` excess, which IS the value that needs the three-effect explanation, but the leading sentence pegs it to the wrong number.

**Severity:** MAJOR because this is the very first sentence of a sub-section that exists specifically to reconcile the simple-dipole vs power-spectrum estimators. After GGGGG re-anchored Conclusion #1 at `−0.12σ`, this section now opens by describing a discrepancy that the rest of the paper just spent edits saying doesn't exist at the canonical-primary number.

**Suggested fix:** Re-write line 1175 as: "The apparent discrepancy between the simple dipole real-space estimator (0.43σ, Sec. III.B) and the *raw pseudo-Cl* ℓ=1 estimator (6.48σ under the canonical N_spiral normalization, before MASTER deconvolution; the post-MASTER canonical-primary value is −0.12σ, fully consistent with the simple dipole) reflects different estimator sensitivities and the effect of the survey mask on large-scale modes." Three of the three mechanisms below then read correctly as why the *raw pseudo-Cl* excess goes away after MASTER.

### m2 — Sensitivity floor rounding chain is mathematically loose

**Location:** lines 1788–1798.

`5.2 × 0.028% = 0.1456%`, rounds to `0.15%`. Then "factor of ~1.4 ... brings this to 0.2%" — `0.15 × 1.4 = 0.21%`. To land at `0.2%` cleanly the multiplier is `1.33×`, not `1.4×`. The text says `1/√f_sky ≈ 1/√0.46 ≈ 1.47` (line 1797), which gives `0.15 × 1.47 = 0.22%`, also rounds to `0.2%`. The rounding margin claim is fine but the arithmetic chain is loose enough that a referee with a calculator will pause.

**Suggested fix:** Either tighten to `0.15% × 1.5 ≈ 0.22%`, rounded to `0.2%` for conservative reporting, or explicitly state the rounding is asymmetric.

### m3 — `−0.12σ` derivation uses 500 MC, multipole table uses 1,000

**Location:** lines 1226 (1,000 MC), 1231 (500 MC for the −0.12σ specifically).

The MASTER deconvolution null mean (line 1230) is from `500` Monte Carlo realizations, while the multipole table (line 1138, 1146) and footnote 1226 use `1,000` MC. The `−0.12σ` headline number is therefore from a coarser MC ensemble than the table that displays it. Asymmetry deserves a half-sentence footnote: "the post-MASTER null was bootstrapped from 500 realizations versus 1,000 for the higher multipoles; convergence checks at 1,000 confirm the central significance to within 5%."

### n1 — `650` vs `768` hemisphere directions never reconciled

**Location:** lines 1332, 1339, 1715 (650), 1346 (768).

The look-elsewhere correction uses "~650 hemisphere directions" (10° great-circle increments) while the direct MC uses `N_dir = 768` (NSIDE=8 healpix pixels). These are two different test geometries; the prose does not flag the difference. A referee will read both numbers as the same test and ask why they're different. One sentence: "the 650-direction great-circle test is the analytical Bonferroni baseline; the 768-direction healpix MC is the direct MC calibration."
