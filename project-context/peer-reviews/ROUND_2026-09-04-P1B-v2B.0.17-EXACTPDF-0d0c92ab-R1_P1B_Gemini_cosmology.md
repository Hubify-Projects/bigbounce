Here is a referee report for the manuscript "namaster-proof: Content-bound execution receipts as a shortcut detector for pseudo-$C_\ell$ computations," submitted to the Journal of Open Research Software.

### General Comments
The manuscript presents a Python verification layer designed to ensure the reproducibility and integrity of pseudo-$C_\ell$ computations, specifically targeting operations related to the NaMaster code. The author introduces a cryptographic receipt mechanism and tests it via a blind shortcut-detection protocol. The mathematical decomposition of the rotated spectra (Equations 1–5) is sound, and the software engineering practices (atomic replacement, strict JSON schema) are rigorous. 

However, the manuscript contains two critical methodological reporting flaws that must be addressed before publication. First, the abstract juxtaposes the package's capability to evaluate spin-2 NaMaster operators with a 100% detection rate in a blind test, strongly implying the test validated the NaMaster pipeline. In reality, the test was performed on a surrogate spin-0 Python implementation. Second, the claim of a "0% false-positive rate" under a "pre-declared" protocol obscures the fact that the pre-declared rules actually failed this criterion and required post-hoc modification to achieve the 0% rate. 

These issues, along with missing statistical uncertainties on the recovered angles, require major revisions.

### Findings

**ESSENTIAL: Abstract-Last Drift / Surrogate Estimator**
*   **ID:** P1B-E1
*   **Location:** Abstract (page 1) and Section 6 (page 4)
*   **Problem:** The abstract states: "First, it evaluates a uniformly rotated EE, EB, BE, BB spectrum through the complete NaMaster bandpower-window operator... The verifier detected all 12 runs (100%) drawn from four shortcut classes... exactly as predicted by the threat model." This strongly implies the blind test was executed on the spin-2 NaMaster pipeline. However, Section 6 ("Scope limits") reveals: "The estimator exercised here is this repository’s own spin-0 MASTER implementation, not NaMaster itself". 
*   **Required Fix:** The abstract must explicitly state that the blind shortcut-detection test was performed on a surrogate spin-0 Python implementation, not the NaMaster C/C++ library. The juxtaposition in the abstract is currently misleading.

**ESSENTIAL: Post-Hoc Modification of a "Blind" Test**
*   **ID:** P1B-E2
*   **Location:** Abstract (page 1) and Section 6 (page 4)
*   **Problem:** The abstract claims a "0% false-positive rate on 3 honest runs" under a "pre-declared, sealed blind protocol". However, Section 6 states: "Two corrections, both discovered by running the test. First, wall clock is not a usable rule... the pre-declared wall-time floor... would have fired on 3 of 3 honest runs; it is recorded per verdict as `wall_rule_would_fire` and excluded from the decision rule set." If a pre-declared rule fires on honest runs, the pre-declared protocol *failed* its 0% false-positive criterion. Achieving 0% by dropping rules after unblinding the data invalidates the claim that the success was strictly "pre-declared."
*   **Required Fix:** The abstract must explicitly acknowledge that the 0% false-positive rate was achieved only after post-hoc removal of two pre-declared rules (wall-time and M-hash). Section 6 must clearly state that the strict pre-declared protocol failed the false-positive criterion prior to these manual corrections.

**MAJOR: Uncomputed Quantitative Claims / Missing Uncertainties**
*   **ID:** P1B-M1
*   **Location:** Section 8 (page 6)
*   **Problem:** The text claims: "a 500-realization, foreground-free synthetic run recovered the injection from the mean bandpowers on the declared 0.001° grid: 0.270° $\mapsto$ 0.270° and 0.342° $\mapsto$ 0.342°. The null injection, also run with 500 realizations, recovered 0.000°." No standard error or uncertainty is provided for these recoveries. 
*   **Required Fix:** If this is an average over noisy realizations (e.g., cosmic variance from CAMB spectra), the standard error of the mean must be reported to justify the exact 3-decimal-place match. If the recovery is exact due to algebraic cancellation (e.g., using identical random seeds for the unrotated and rotated maps), explicitly state this and explain why 500 realizations were necessary for a purely algebraic check.

**MINOR: Scale-Dependent Absolute Differences**
*   **ID:** P1B-M2
*   **Location:** Section 7 (page 5)
*   **Problem:** The text states: "a maximum absolute difference of $1.41 \times 10^{-18}$ between direct window contraction and the couple–decouple operator." An absolute difference is meaningless without knowing the scale or units of the multiplicands. (For typical dimensionless CMB temperature variances $\sim 10^{-10}$, machine epsilon yields $\sim 10^{-26}$; for $\mu K^2 \sim 10^3$, it yields $\sim 10^{-13}$).
*   **Required Fix:** State the typical magnitude (or the units) of the bandpowers being compared so the reader can assess this value as a relative error or understand its relation to IEEE-754 double-precision epsilon.

**NIT: Equation 4 Notation**
*   **ID:** P1B-N1
*   **Location:** Section 4 (page 2)
*   **Problem:** Equation 4 introduces $C_\ell^{j,k}$ and $R_b^{i,k}$, but $k$ is not explicitly defined in the surrounding text, though it can be inferred from Equation 5.
*   **Required Fix:** Explicitly state that $k \in \{0, c, s\}$ indexes the angle-independent, $\cos(4\beta)$, and $\sin(4\beta)$ components defined in Equations 1–3 immediately following Equation 4.

## Summary recommendation
MAJOR REVISIONS

The software engineering and mathematical foundations of the package are solid, and the concept of content-bound execution receipts is highly relevant to modern cosmological data analysis. However, the manuscript currently misrepresents the scope and success of its primary validation test in the abstract. The blind test was performed on a spin-0 surrogate rather than the spin-2 NaMaster pipeline, and the "pre-declared" 0% false-positive rate was only achieved via post-hoc rule deletion. Correcting these reporting flaws to accurately reflect the actual methodology is essential before the paper can be accepted.
