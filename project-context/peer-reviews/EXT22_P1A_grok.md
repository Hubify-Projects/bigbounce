Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONS

The paper presents a careful, scoped channel-level analysis of whether minimal Einstein–Cartan–Holst (ECH) spin-torsion gravity can source late-time dark energy through four enumerated routes. It closes those routes under explicitly stated assumptions and scaling ansätze, establishes a clean "perturbation transparency" result for canonical scalar matter, and catalogs 13 logically independent structural barriers (14 historical entries). Two ECH-independent class-level predictions of the broader bounce/ALP program survive and are identified as testable by SPHEREx and LiteBIRD. The work is technically solid, transparently scoped, and makes a useful contribution to the bounce-cosmology literature by clarifying what minimal ECH can and cannot do at the amplitude and perturbation level.
No blockers or major scientific rework are required. The core arguments rest on standard derivations (Hehl–Datta torsion elimination, algebraic Bianchi identity), explicit phenomenological ansätze (flagged repeatedly), and a systematic barrier taxonomy. The perturbation-transparency proof is correct and well explained; the four-route closures are conditional on the stated assumptions and do not overclaim operator-basis exhaustiveness. Minor revisions are requested only for emphasis, companion-paper handling, and polish to ensure the scoping language cannot be misread.

STRENGTHS (≥ 3; actually six substantive ones)

Exemplary scoping and intellectual honesty: The repeated, explicit labeling of the dark-energy mapping as a phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz (not a derivation), the channel-level (vs. operator-basis) enumeration, the canonical-scalar limitation, and the deliberate omission of Jackiw–Pi and parity-odd four-fermion partner terms set a high standard for theoretical cosmology papers. Readers are never left uncertain what has and has not been proven.

Clean, positive structural result — perturbation transparency: The demonstration that, for canonical scalar matter, torsion vanishes at all perturbative orders and the Holst dual contraction vanishes identically on the Levi-Civita connection by the algebraic Bianchi identity (distinct from the Pontryagin density) is a genuine advance. The five-step proof, differential-form decomposition, explicit verification to second order, and clear statement of what would break transparency (fermions, propagating torsion, non-minimal couplings) are model-independent within the stated domain and will be useful to the broader ECH/LQC community.

Systematic 14-barrier catalog with clear taxonomy: The separation into 7 foundation mechanism classes (A–G) and 6 observational branches (H, J, L, M, N, O), the explicit mapping of which barriers close which routes, and the distinction between novel ECH-specific calculations and known results provide a reusable framework for future model-building. Barrier 14 (perturbation transparency) correctly subsumes the earlier parity-even interaction barrier (B8) as its observational consequence.

Insightful structural-tension analysis: The incompatibility between the N_tot ≈ 92 requirement of the dark-energy dilution ansatz and the survival of the matter-bounce f_NL = −35/8 signature at SPHEREx-accessible scales is a valuable consistency check. It is presented correctly as an independent robustness argument (not a co-equal closure mechanism) once the four amplitude routes are already closed.

Clear identification of surviving ECH-independent class tests: The paper correctly labels f_NL = −35/8 and spectator-ALP β ≈ 0.27° as class-level predictions of the broader bounce/ALP landscape, not distinctive ECH outputs. The LiteBIRD and SPHEREx forecasts, with proper caveats on significance interpretation (differential vs. naïve 0.27°/0.03°), are responsibly stated.

Appendices and reproducibility infrastructure: App. B gives the necessary dimensional accounting with explicit acknowledgment of phenomenological status; App. C derives the rotation-angle factor 1/2 from first principles for the adopted operator normalization. The public GitHub repository with Cobaya YAMLs, frozen chains, and pipeline code (even if companions supply additional validation) is exactly the transparency expected in 2026 cosmology.

Overall verdict
This is high-quality specialized theoretical work that meets MNRAS/PRD/JCAP standards for rigor, scoping, and clarity. It does not require substantial scientific rework. With the minor revisions above (primarily emphasis and companion-note handling), the manuscript will be suitable for publication. The perturbation-transparency result and the systematic barrier catalog are lasting contributions; the honest channel-level closure prevents over-claiming while still advancing the program by showing what minimal ECH cannot do and what class-level tests remain viable.

I recommend acceptance after minor revisions.
