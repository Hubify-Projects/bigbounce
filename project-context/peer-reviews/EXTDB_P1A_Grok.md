Referee Report: Channel-Level Closure of Four Minimal Einstein–Cartan–Holst Dark-Energy Routes and Perturbation Transparency for Scalar Matter

1. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS

The paper presents a scoped but ambitious channel-level analysis arguing that four enumerated minimal ECH routes to late-time dark energy are closed (or naturalness-obstructed) under explicitly stated assumptions, while establishing a perturbation-transparency result for canonical scalar matter and identifying a structural tension with matter-bounce observables. The work is technically interesting, unusually transparent about its ansätze and scope, and contains at least one clean technical result (the Bianchi-identity decoupling). However, the framing as a "closure" of minimal-ECH dark-energy routes, the representativeness of the four enumerated channels, and the weighting of the 14-barrier catalog require substantial strengthening before the paper meets the acceptance threshold of these journals. The core claims are not obviously incorrect within their stated boundaries, but the boundaries themselves and the evidential support for the broader narrative need tightening.

2. BLOCKERS

None. No mathematical contradictions, sign errors, or misuse of external data were identified in the provided sections. All major assumptions (phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz, enumerated routes only, canonical scalar matter only, exclusion of propagating torsion/dynamical Immirzi/fermion loops/non-minimal couplings) are explicitly flagged. The perturbation-transparency claim rests on standard algebraic properties of torsion and the first Bianchi identity and does not appear internally inconsistent.

3. MAJORS

M1. Insufficient justification that the four enumerated routes constitute the relevant "minimal" or standard channels (core framing issue).
The paper repeatedly and correctly states that it performs a channel-level closure of four enumerated routes and explicitly disclaims a complete operator-basis theorem (Jackiw–Pi gravitational Chern–Simons term and the parity-odd four-fermion partner of R1 are named as omitted). However, the title, abstract, and introduction present the work as closing "Four Minimal Einstein–Cartan–Holst Dark-Energy Routes." No systematic derivation or table is provided showing how these four exhaust (or even representatively sample) the parity-odd/torsion-sourced operators that could arise from the minimal ECH action plus standard matter couplings and that could source a late-time vacuum energy or observable parity violation. Required revision: Add a short section or appendix that (a) starts from the ECH + Holst + matter action, (b) enumerates the leading parity-odd operators after torsion integration, and (c) maps the four routes onto that list with a clear statement of what is omitted. If the four are simply the routes most commonly discussed in the prior literature, say so explicitly and adjust the title/abstract language accordingly.

M2. Central reliance on the phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz for the dark-energy mapping, with insufficient exploration of its uniqueness or alternatives.
All R4 and dark-energy-route closures are conditional on the phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz. If other mappings or mechanisms exist by which the same (or related) parity-odd ECH structures could source an effective vacuum energy without invoking this specific ansatz, the closure is narrower than the title and abstract suggest. Required revision: (i) Move dimensional analysis and motivation for the ansatz to main text. (ii) Explicitly discuss whether alternative mappings could evade the amplitude/naturalness closures. (iii) Adjust abstract/intro language so that "closure of dark-energy routes" is always qualified by "under the stated phenomenological on-shell scaling ansatz."

M3. Uneven rigor and ECH-specificity across the 14-barrier catalog; overstatement of collective constraining power.
Several barriers (B5 scale separation, B6 attractor-sensitivity dilemma, B7 parameter immunity, B9 Liouville conservation, B10 UV–IR specificity, B13 gravitational democracy) read as general no-go considerations applicable to broad classes of bounce or modified-gravity models rather than sharp, ECH-specific calculations. Required revision: Provide a compact table classifying each barrier by (a) ECH-specific vs. general, (b) quantitative amplitude bound vs. naturalness argument vs. qualitative observation, and (c) which of the four routes it primarily constrains. Downgrade or re-label more general barriers so the "13 logically independent" claim accurately reflects their individual force.

4. MINORS

m1. Over-reliance on "in preparation" companions for narrative elements that affect perceived strength of the result. The 2.6–5σ SPHEREx forecast range, the "confirmed null" galaxy-spin result, and specific MCMC parameter values are all relegated to companion works. Required: either include self-contained summary of key companion results or rephrase to emphasize class-level statements without quoting specific σ values.

m2. Minor presentational and scoping clarifications: abstract states both "the central result is perturbation-transparency" and details the 13-barrier closure — readers may be unsure which is primary. Some wording in conclusions about simultaneously being "a dark-energy generator and a matter-bounce host" is slightly loose given the dilution–fNL tension.

m3. Small technical clarifications: R2/R3 ansatz uncertainty reminder in Sec. IV E; scheme-dependence qualifier on ρcrit/ρPl window; dimensional vs. partition-function justification for dilution factor Eq. (11) already labeled but could use consistent qualifier.

5. Strengths (≥3)

S1. Exemplary transparency on scope and assumptions — repeatedly flags channel-level vs. operator-level scope, phenomenological ansatz status, and named omitted operators. Rare in ambitious no-go-style papers.

S2. Clean and well-scoped perturbation-transparency result (Sec. X) — the Bianchi-identity decoupling for canonical scalar matter is a sharp, first-principles technical result that stands independently of the dark-energy closure and is likely to be cited on its own.

S3. Identification of structural tension between dark-energy dilution and matter-bounce fNL survival — the Ntot≈92 requirement quantitatively separates the DE mechanism from the fNL=-35/8 signature. Genuine conceptual contribution.

S4. Careful treatment of Route 4 as a naturalness/explanatory-deficit objection rather than pure amplitude no-go — mθ∼H0 diagnosis is accurate and methodologically honest.

S5. Useful organizing framework — the Foundations (A–G) / observational Branches taxonomy provides coherent structure that future ECH/bounce cosmology work can build upon.

Overall: Serious, carefully scoped theoretical contribution. Main technical results (perturbation transparency, channel-level closures, dilution–fNL tension) are sound. Principal obstacles: (M1) representativeness of four routes, (M2) centrality of scaling ansatz, (M3) uneven barrier rigor. Referee happy to re-review revised manuscript.
