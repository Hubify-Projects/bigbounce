## 3. Claim Maps

### P1 Claim Map

**Primary claim:** Minimal ECH spin-torsion gravity cannot produce late-time dark energy or distinctive scalar/tensor perturbation observables; matter-bounce fNL survives as a compatible but non-ECH-derived observable.

**Secondary claims:**

* LQC provides a nonsingular bounce at ρcrit≈0.27–0.41ρPl.
* ECH/Holst structure produces four-fermion interactions and parity-odd motivation.
* Fourteen structural barriers close minimal bounce-to-dark-energy routes.
* For canonical scalar matter, torsion vanishes at all perturbation orders and γ is invisible.
* MCMC with ΔNeff recovers ΛCDM-like values.
* Spectator ALP birefringence can accommodate β≈0.27° but is not a distinctive ECH prediction.
* Galaxy chirality channel is null.

**Key assumptions:** canonical scalar matter for perturbation transparency; LQC effective Friedmann equation as bounce mechanism; phenomenological α/M; assumed late-time w = −1; spectator ALP as independent mechanism; stock CAMB ΔNeff as proxy for bounce particle production.

**Strongest claims:** perturbation transparency under spinless/canonical scalar matter; MCMC null result for ΔNeff; recognition that ECH dark energy is not derived.

**Weakest claims:** dark-energy motivation, parity-odd vacuum energy ansatz, ALP/ECH connection, model-comparison claims, “unified framework” language.

**Needs strengthening:** formal theorem statement for transparency; clear split between ECH, LQC, ALP, and matter-bounce mechanisms; remove or relegate failed dark-energy scaling ansatz.

---

### P2 Claim Map

**Primary claim:** SPHEREx can test the matter-bounce fNL≈−35/8 prediction at realistic 3–5σ after systematic degradation.

**Secondary claims:**

* Matter-bounce bispectrum is close enough to local template that local estimators recover r≈0.84 of the signal.
* The Cai et al. −35/8 normalization is the correct Planck-convention value.
* MegaMapper could improve the test but is speculative.
* Bayes factors favor bounce over tuned inflationary competitors if fNL≈−4.375 is detected.

**Key assumptions:** exact/quasi matter contraction; GR perturbations during contraction; Bunch-Davies initial state; bispectrum transmission through bounce; no prolonged post-bounce inflation.

**Strongest claims:** focused observational decision framework; explicit systematics discussion; useful template-overlap question.

**Weakest claims:** convention audit not decisive; Bayes factors prior-dominated; no independent survey Fisher matrix; anomaly-tracer improvements imported from P3.

**Needs strengthening:** independent derivation or authoritative resolution of fNL normalization; realistic SPHEREx mock pipeline; clearer statement that this is a forecast recast.

---

### P3 Claim Map

**Primary claim:** BigAE applied across eight astronomical archives yields 378,480 unique anomalous objects/sources after Path-C validation and deduplication.

**Secondary claims:**

* DESI anomalies are robust with k-fold stability.
* SDSS and LAMOST cross-transfer artifacts were corrected by native retraining.
* eROSITA/Planck/NEOWISE/Gaia add multi-wavelength components.
* Novelty fraction is ∼17.8% for DESI top-1,000.
* Anomaly-selected tracers could improve fNL constraints.

**Key assumptions:** reconstruction error is meaningful across heterogeneous surveys; per-survey thresholds define comparable “anomalies”; 5″ dedup is appropriate across archives; failed-with-diagnostic components can still be included; CMB patches can be treated alongside object catalogs.

**Strongest claims:** scale of engineering; candid Path-C artifact disclosure; DESI/SDSS cross-match examples; recognition of LAMOST training-bias problem.

**Weakest claims:** headline count, inclusion criteria, threshold consistency, ACT/CMB treatment, novelty framing, cosmological utility.

**Needs strengthening:** validated-only primary catalog; separate catalogs by data type and validation status; independent anomaly-detection baselines for DESI/SDSS/LAMOST; public release with exact reproducible manifests.

### P4 Claim Map

**Primary claim:** A large chirality catalog finds no large-scale galaxy morphology parity violation and refutes ∼3% chirality-asymmetry claims.

**Secondary claims:**

* ViT classifier plus flip-TTA mitigates orientation bias.
* Raw classifier produces a 94.6σ spurious dipole, demonstrating need for equivariance.
* Equivariant catalog has fCW≈0.4974 and simple dipole 0.43σ.
* Hemisphere and angular-power signals do not survive correction.
* Catalog sensitivity reaches ∼0.2% dipole at 3σ.

**Key assumptions:** CE-ResNet-derived labels are acceptable training labels; horizontal flip-TTA sufficiently removes chirality bias; residual monopole is spatially uniform; binomial uncertainties approximate effective errors; edge-on contamination dilutes but does not bias.

**Strongest claims:** null dipole after bias correction; demonstration that raw classifiers can produce spurious high-significance dipoles; large sample size.

**Weakest claims:** count consistency; “equivariant” wording; systematic floor versus 0.2% sensitivity; rotation reasoning; circular validation against CE-ResNet.

**Needs strengthening:** corrected catalog statistics; D4/rotation handling or justification; magnitude/PSF/axis-ratio bias tests; independent manually labeled validation set.
