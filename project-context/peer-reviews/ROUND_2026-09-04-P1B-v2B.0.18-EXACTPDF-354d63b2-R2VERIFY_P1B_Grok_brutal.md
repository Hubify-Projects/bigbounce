**Referee Report – P1B (JORS-SOFTWARE)**

**P1B-E1**  
Section: Abstract (p. 1)  
Problem: “the first pre-registered, sealed blind measurement of shortcut-detection sensitivity for pseudo-C_ℓ execution receipts” is stated as a knowledge-qualified claim of primacy. No literature search, prior-art table, or citation to any earlier sealed-blind provenance experiment in astronomy or scientific computing is supplied.  
Required fix: Remove the primacy claim or supply a systematic comparison showing no prior sealed, pre-registered shortcut-detection study exists.

**P1B-E2**  
Section: Abstract + §6 (pp. 1, 4–5)  
Problem: Abstract reports one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson bounds (0.861, 0.451) derived from 20/20 + 0/5 counts. These numbers are internally consistent with Table 3, but the abstract presents them without the explicit caveat given in §6 that the five replicates per arm are seed-varied executions of a deterministic variant and therefore measure reproducibility, not sampling variance. The abstract therefore overstates statistical strength.  
Required fix: Either delete the numerical bounds from the abstract or append the reproducibility caveat in the same sentence.

**P1B-E3**  
Section: Entire manuscript (12 pp.)  
Problem: JORS-SOFTWARE papers are expected to describe reusable research software with demonstrated or plausible adoption beyond a single narrow workflow. The package implements a verification layer usable only with the authors’ own spin-0 MASTER estimator under a fixed NaMaster protocol; it explicitly disclaims being a pipeline, foreground model, or inference engine. No usage metrics, dependent packages, or external users are reported.  
Required fix: Either broaden the software to a general receipt/provenance layer or submit to a methods journal.

**P1B-M1**  
Section: §2, §6 (pp. 2, 4)  
Problem: The motivating “shortcut” (effective-multipole rescaling) produces a median 0.17 fractional deviation, yet the detection rules R0–R6 contain no value-level tolerance on bandpowers. S6 therefore evades every rule by construction. The paper presents this as a finding rather than a design limitation.  
Required fix: Either add a value-level rule (with pre-registered tolerance) or explicitly state that the current receipt system cannot detect downstream value shortcuts.

**P1B-M2**  
Section: §12 (p. 9)  
Problem: The reproducibility section supplies commit hashes and Zenodo DOIs but no frozen release hash of the exact NaMaster workspace tensor or the precise Wigner-3j count used in the sealed runs. An external reader cannot recompute the reference contract without reverse-engineering the exact mask and ℓ-grid.  
Required fix: Publish the exact workspace tensor (or deterministic generator) and the integer 3j count as separate archived artifacts with their own DOIs.

**P1B-M3**  
Section: §1, §7 (pp. 1, 6)  
Problem: The text repeatedly asserts that the receipt “establishes the primitive as a detector of structural shortcuts in instrumented steps, not of forged metadata or of value-level shortcuts.” This scope limitation is never quantified against realistic analyst behaviour (e.g., an adversary who also edits the harness). The threat model is therefore incomplete.  
Required fix: Either expand the threat model to include harness modification or remove all language implying the method detects “structural” shortcuts under an unmodified-instrumented-harness model.

**P1B-N1**

Required fix: Replace with conventional submission or acceptance date.

**P1B-N2**  
Section: Table 2 caption (p. 5)

Required fix: Move to text or caption footnote.

**Summary recommendation**  
REJECT

The manuscript describes a narrowly scoped verification shim whose only demonstrated user is its own author, packaged in a 12-page article whose length and methodological framing exceed JORS norms. The core empirical claim (blind detection of structural shortcuts) is valid only inside an artificially restricted threat model that the authors themselves flag as unrealistic. No evidence of broader utility, adoption, or comparison against existing provenance frameworks is provided. These defects are structural, not cosmetic; the paper is therefore unsuitable for JORS-SOFTWARE.
