### M1 (MAJOR) — §X Conclusion #1 still quotes "marginal $2.75\sigma$" for $\ell=1$ as a current/canonical value

**Location:** L2255-2256 (§X Conclusions, item 1)

**Problem:** The conclusion reads:
> "The simple dipole is $0.43\sigmaunit$; the power-spectrum $\ell = 1$ mode is a marginal $2.75\sigmaunit$."

This is the **superseded raw pseudo-$C_\ell$ value computed with the buggy $N_{\rm total}$ shot-noise denominator**, explicitly disowned earlier in the paper:

- L1280-1283: "the raw pseudo-$C_\ell$ value at $\ell=1$ ($6.48\sigmaunit$ with the corrected $N_{\rm spiral}$ normalization; $2.75\sigmaunit$ in older drafts that used the buggy $N_{\rm total}$ denominator)".
- L1149: Table~II $\ell=1$ row Significance = $-0.12\sigma$ (MASTER-deconvolved canonical).
- L1273-1278: "We adopt the post-MASTER deconvolution number ($-0.12\sigmaunit$) as the *primary* angular-power-spectrum dipole significance for this paper."

The paper's own §VII.B (L1473-1475) explicitly claims:
> "This high-confidence-primary policy is consistent with the methodological recommendation of external peer review and is implemented uniformly throughout this paper: the abstract, introduction, and conclusions all quote the high-confidence dipole upper bound."

Conclusion #1 violates this self-stated invariant by quoting $2.75\sigma$ (which is BOTH a stale-normalization number AND the unstratified value, not the high-confidence stratum number $0.3\sigma$). A hostile referee will weaponize this: "the authors claim uniformity, fail to deliver in §X."

**Severity:** MAJOR. This is the primary one-sentence summary of the result a referee will read; it cannot be wrong.

**Fix:** Replace L2255-2256 with the canonical line, e.g.:
> "The simple dipole is $0.43\sigmaunit$ ($p=0.30$ at $N_{\rm MC}=10{,}000$); the post-MASTER-deconvolved $\ell=1$ power is $-0.12\sigmaunit$, fully consistent with null. The high-confidence-stratum dipole significance is $0.3\sigmaunit$."

### M3 (MAJOR) — §III.B 3.86× suppression-factor claim is arithmetically inconsistent with $+0.79\% \to -0.26\%$

**Location:** L416-418 (§III.B), cross-referenced against L2010-2012 (§IX.B)

**Problem:** Two paragraphs claim the same 3.86× suppression factor applies to two different number pairs:

- §III.B L416-418: "the $3.86\times$ asymmetry-suppression factor of Sec.~\ref{sec:cw_frac} reduces from raw $+0.79\%$ to equivariant $-0.26\%$." But $|0.79| / |0.26| = 3.04$, not $3.86$.
- §IX.B L2010-2012: "the catalog-wide handedness asymmetry drops from $+2.05\%$ in the raw model to $-0.53\%$ after equivariance—a suppression factor of $3.86\times$." Here $|2.05| / |0.53| = 3.87 \approx 3.86$. ✓

The 3.86× factor was measured on the §IX.B raw$\to$equiv-NS pool ($+2.05\% \to -0.53\%$, edge-on contamination probe), NOT the §III.B Catalog-A$\to$Catalog-C global CW-fraction pair ($0.79\% \to 0.26\%$, factor 3.04). §III.B is wrong to attribute the same factor to a different pair of numbers.

This is also flagged in the hostile-checklist item 11 ("Equivariance suppression factor 3.86× — raw +2.05% → equivariant -0.53%. Is the −0.53% sign maintained?"). The −0.53% sign IS maintained in §IX.B; the bug is that §III.B is grafting the §IX.B factor onto §IV.A's number pair without checking arithmetic.

**Severity:** MAJOR. A hostile referee running a calculator over the abstract+§III.B+§IX.B will catch this in the first pass. It directly questions whether the authors checked their own arithmetic.

**Fix options:**

1. **Recommended:** Compute the §III.B-pair factor: $0.79/0.26 = 3.04$. Replace L416-418 with: "the post-processing pipeline reduces the catalog-wide CW excess from raw $+0.79\%$ to equivariant $-0.26\%$, a factor-of-$3.04$ collapse on the global asymmetry; the §IX.B edge-on raw-to-eq-NS pool shows a parallel factor-of-$3.86$ collapse from $+2.05\%$ to $-0.53\%$ on a different (within-pool) metric."

2. Drop the "3.86×" reference from §III.B entirely and say only "the equivariant procedure reduces the residual offset substantially."

Either way, both sections must NOT claim the same numeric factor for arithmetically inconsistent pairs.

### m3 (MINOR) — Per-pixel spirals: 4,168 derived but $\sqrt{4{,}326}$ used in $\sigma_{\rm pix}$

**Location:** L1722-1727 (§IX.D Eq. \ref{eq:sigma_pix})

**Problem:** L1722-1723:
> "${\sim}4{,}170$ spirals per pixel from $N_{\rm spiral} / N_{\rm pix} = 3{,}201{,}160 / 768 \approx 4{,}168$"

Then L1727:
> "$\sigma_{\rm pix} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{N_{\rm spiral}/N_{\rm pix}}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{4{,}326}} \approx 0.76\%$"

The 0.76% headline rounds either way, but the algebra in Eq. \ref{eq:sigma_pix} is *demonstrably not* what produces the 0.76% from the stated $N_{\rm spiral}=3{,}201{,}160$ — and a sharp referee will catch it.

The "4,326" value appears to be a stale residual from the old $N_{\rm spiral} = 3{,}321{,}795$ snapshot: $3{,}321{,}795 / 768 = 4{,}325.5 \approx 4{,}326$. So this is another instance of the canonical-recount not propagating uniformly.

**Severity:** MINOR. Doesn't change the headline, but is the kind of arithmetic detail R47 is supposed to catch.

**Fix:** L1727 → "$\frac{1}{2\sqrt{4{,}168}} \approx 0.78\%$" and update the downstream $\sigma(A_{\rm dip}) = 0.78\%\sqrt{3/768} = 0.0488\% \to 3\sigma$ threshold $\approx 0.146\%$ (still rounds to ~0.2% under the f_sky cushion).
