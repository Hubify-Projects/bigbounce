**Referee Report on "Spectrally Unusual Sources at Scale..."**

This paper presents a large catalog of anomalous astronomical sources, generated by applying an autoencoder framework to 37.3 million sources across seven major surveys. The authors present the catalog itself, detail the validation procedures, and demonstrate two potential cosmological applications: constraining primordial non-Gaussianity (f_NL) and testing bouncing cosmology models with NANOGrav data.

The work is substantial, and the resulting catalog is a potentially valuable resource for the community. The authors are commendably transparent about the methodological challenges, validation failures, and limitations of their analysis. The cosmological applications are presented with appropriate caveats and rigor, correctly framed as forecasts and consistency checks rather than new constraints.

However, the paper requires substantial revision before it can be considered for publication in Physical Review D. The current presentation is confusing, mixing the final results with a narrative of the methodological development in a way that obscures the primary scientific contribution. Several key components of the catalog have significant, admitted validation and reproducibility issues that must be more prominently addressed.

Below is a detailed list of required revisions.

### ESSENTIAL

**P3-E1: Main text and figures must present final results, not superseded baselines.**
*   **Section/Page:** Throughout, but especially Fig 2 (p. 7), Table I (p. 8), and associated text.
*   **Problem:** The paper is structured around a "Path-C rebuild," and constantly refers to a "superseded" cross-transfer baseline. Figures of and numbers from this superseded baseline appear in the main body of the paper (e.g., Fig 2 caption: "Cross-transfer baseline map... (superseded by the Path-C native catalog)"). This is not standard for a research article, which should present the final, canonical results. Mixing the methodological history with the final results is deeply confusing for the reader.
*   **Fix:** The paper must be restructured. The final "Path-C" catalog and its properties should be the sole subject of the main results sections. All discussion, figures, and tables related to the initial "cross-transfer" scan must be moved to a dedicated methods section or an appendix, where they can serve their proper purpose: motivating the native-retrain methodology. Specifically, Figure 2 must be re-plotted using the final 378,280 unique anomalies. Table I must be stripped of all references to cross-transfer counts in its main body and simplified to present only the final numbers. The term "superseded" should be removed from the main text.

### MAJOR

**P3-M1: The catalog tiering structure is unclear and needs simplification.**
*   **Section/Page:** Abstract (p. 1), Introduction (p. 1-2), Section III (p. 6).
*   **Problem:** The abstract and introduction are dense with multiple, slightly different numbers for the catalog size ("≥ 268,519", "≥ 268,319", "269,317", "269,117", "378,280", "378,080"). The distinction between "validated catalog-grade", "recommended tier", and "total catalog" is not made clear until page 6. A reader cannot be expected to parse these numbers without a clear, upfront definition.
*   **Fix:** The abstract must be rewritten for clarity. Start with the total unique count (378,280), then immediately state how it is stratified into a primary "validated" tier and a secondary "exploratory" tier, giving the counts for each. The introduction should contain a single, clear paragraph defining these tiers and their components before any results are discussed. The current three-tier definition on page 6 is good but should be moved earlier or summarized clearly in the introduction.

**P3-M2: Significant reproducibility and validation failures for Gaia and eROSITA components.**
*   **Section/Page:** Abstract (p. 1), Sec IIB (p. 4), Sec IIIE (p. 12), Sec IIG (p. 15).
*   **Problem:** The paper admits to major issues with two components of its "recommended" tier:
    1.  **Gaia:** "the exact 20-feature production preprocessing script for this run was not recovered from pod backups" (p. 15). This is a critical reproducibility failure.
    2.  **eROSITA:** "the production SBigAE score values are not printed: that score axis is irreproducible" (p. 14, Table IV caption). This means the primary ranking metric cannot be reproduced.
    Both surveys also fail the injection-recovery validation gate. While the authors are transparent, these issues are severe enough that these components cannot be considered "catalog-grade".
*   **Fix:** The status of the Gaia and eROSITA tiers must be downgraded more strongly throughout the paper. They should be explicitly excluded from the primary "validated" catalog count in the abstract's headline statement. The abstract should state clearly that two of the seven surveys have irreproducible or non-validated pipelines. The term "recommended tier" is too weak; "validated tier" and "exploratory addendum" should be used consistently. The limitations section must re-emphasize these specific failures as primary caveats.

**P3-M3: The paper is too long for its primary contribution.**
*   **Section/Page:** Entire manuscript (32 pages).
*   **Problem:** While the work is comprehensive, the paper's length is excessive. The narrative style, which details the methodological evolution, contributes significantly to the length. The cosmological sections, while well-done, are secondary and add considerable length to a paper whose main contribution is the catalog.
*   **Fix:** After addressing P3-E1 (moving superseded results to an appendix), the authors should aggressively edit the entire manuscript for conciseness. The core paper (main text + main figures/tables) should focus on presenting the final catalog and its validation. The cosmological applications could be shortened or moved to appendices to streamline the main text. A target length of ~20-24 pages for the entire manuscript seems more appropriate.

## Summary recommendation

**MAJOR REVISIONS**

This paper introduces a large and potentially impactful catalog of anomalous sources. The authors have demonstrated a high level of rigor in their cosmological analyses and have been laudably transparent about the many caveats and limitations of their data product. However, the paper in its current form is not acceptable for publication. The presentation is fundamentally confusing, failing to clearly separate the final scientific results from the methodological development process. This structural issue, combined with the significant validation and reproducibility failures for the Gaia and eROSITA components, requires a major overhaul of the manuscript. The paper is also unnecessarily long. I recommend that the authors restructure the paper to focus squarely on the final catalog, move all developmental and superseded material to appendices, and more prominently feature the significant limitations of their exploratory data tiers. If these revisions are carried out effectively, the paper will be a valuable contribution.
