This is a referee report for "The exact local non-Gaussianity of a matter-dominated contraction: $f_{NL} = -35/16$ and its orientation dependence" submitted to Physical Review D Letters.

### Overall Assessment
The author presents a rigorous, from-scratch in-in computation of the exact local non-Gaussianity for a matter-dominated contraction, resolving a long-standing factor-of-two discrepancy in the literature. The identification of the orientation dependence sourced by the non-attractor shear is physically sound, and the demonstration that the factor of two perfectly maps across the local, equilateral, and folded configurations is highly convincing. The core theoretical physics in Sections I–III is excellent. 

However, the manuscript suffers from several critical presentation and self-containment issues. It relies on mutable GitHub links for load-bearing physics proofs, contains a blatant algebraic contradiction in the $\delta N$ cross-check, and presents inconsistent arithmetic in the observational forecasts. The paper requires major revisions to meet the rigorous standards of PRD Letters.

### Findings

**ESSENTIAL: paper cannot be accepted without this fix**

*   **P2L-E1** (Section III, page 2): Algebraic contradiction in the $\delta N$ cross-check. The text states: "gives, for general $\epsilon$, $f_{NL}^\rho = (5\epsilon - 7) \cdot 5/8$ reduced at $\epsilon = 3/2$ to $(5\epsilon - 35)/8 = -55/16$". These two expressions are algebraically inequivalent. Evaluating $(5\epsilon - 7) \cdot 5/8$ at $\epsilon = 3/2$ yields $5/16$, not $-55/16$. The expression $(5\epsilon - 35)/8$ evaluated at $\epsilon = 3/2$ does yield $-55/16$. The correct general formula must be provided and mathematically consistent with its evaluated limit.
*   **P2L-E2** (Abstract, page 1): Abstract-last drift sweep failure. The abstract claims "SPHEREx reaches 2.6–3.7$\sigma$ between them." However, Table II shows the SPHEREx bispectrum separation is 2.6–3.1$\sigma$ and the SPHEREx target (P+B) separation is 3.7–4.4$\sigma$. The abstract erroneously mixes the lower bound of the first channel (2.6) with the lower bound of the second channel (3.7). It must state the full range (2.6–4.4$\sigma$) or explicitly specify the channel.
*   **P2L-E3** (Data Availability, page 3 & Refs [13-15], page 4): Provenance surface violation. The text points to GitHub repositories for derivations and multi-channel reach calculations, but References [13-15] use `blob/main/...` URLs. These are mutable branch pointers, not frozen commit hashes or permanent DOIs. This violates reproducibility standards; all code/data links must point to immutable, frozen releases.
*   **P2L-E4** (Section IV, page 2): Standalone-reader test and uncomputed claims. Section IV claims a universal bound $0 < T_{fNL} \le 1/2$ and specific transmission coefficients, but explicitly states this "depends on physics this Letter does not compute in full." The evidence relies entirely on a citation to a GitHub markdown file (Ref [15]). Load-bearing physics results cannot be imported via unpublished GitHub links. This material must either be derived in an appendix, cited to a published/arXiv companion paper, or removed if it exceeds the scope of the Letter.

**MAJOR: significant revision required**

*   **P2L-M1** (Section V, Table II, page 3): Internal arithmetic consistency. Table II lists a single scalar for $\sigma_{fNL}$ (e.g., 0.7 for SPHEREx bispectrum) but provides a range for the separation ($2.6-3.1\sigma$). The separation between $-35/16$ and $-35/8$ is $\Delta f_{NL} = 2.1875$. Using the provided scalar, $2.1875 / 0.7 = 3.125\sigma$, which only matches the upper end of the quoted range. The source of the $2.6\sigma$ lower bound (which implies $\sigma_{fNL} \approx 0.84$) is unexplained. The same issue applies to the P+B row ($2.1875 / 0.5 = 4.375\sigma$, leaving the $3.7\sigma$ bound unexplained).
*   **P2L-M2** (Section III, page 2): Uncomputed quantitative claim. The text states "reconciled by $\zeta_\rho = 2\zeta_c$ at linear order in a non-attractor phase". This is a specific, quantitative gauge-transformation claim presented without a derivation or a literature citation.
*   **P2L-M3** (Section IV, page 2): Version-history language. The sentence "This supersedes an earlier, narrower statement about the transfer of the sub-dominant constant branch alone" is internal review-log/drafting prose and must be removed from a published manuscript.
*   **P2L-M4** (Section IV, page 2): Mathematical clarity. The text states "factors in the range 0.165-0.409 (a 1.64x spread between two mode-function conventions on the same background)". The ratio of the range bounds is $0.409 / 0.165 \approx 2.48$, not $1.64$. If the $1.64\times$ spread refers to a specific subset of this range for a single background, the phrasing is highly misleading and must be rewritten for clarity.

**MINOR: address but paper can proceed**

*   **P2L-N1** (Section V, page 2): Citation format anomaly. "quoted from Ref. [9] (§ 2311.13082, abstract)" appears to contain a typo where the section symbol "§" was used instead of "arXiv:".
*   **P2L-N2** (Throughout): Spelling error. The word "isosceles" is misspelled as "isoceles" four times (Abstract, Section I, Section III, Table I caption).
*   **P2L-N3** (References [13-15], page 4): Internal bookkeeping tags. The reference titles include internal project management tags such as "Track a3:", "Track a2:", and "Adjudication:". These should be cleaned up for the final published bibliography.

## Summary recommendation
MAJOR REVISIONS

The core in-in calculation of the exact local non-Gaussianity and the identification of the orientation dependence are theoretically excellent and well-executed. However, the manuscript currently fails the standalone-reader test by relying on mutable GitHub links for load-bearing physics claims regarding bounce transmission. Furthermore, there are explicit algebraic contradictions in the $\delta N$ cross-check and inconsistent arithmetic in the observational forecasts. Once the external dependencies are properly formalized (or scoped out) and the mathematical inconsistencies are corrected, this paper will be a strong contribution to PRD Letters.
