This is a referee report for the manuscript "namaster-proof: Content-bound execution receipts as a shortcut detector for pseudo-$C_\ell$ computations" submitted to the Journal of Open Research Software.

The manuscript presents a Python verification layer designed to detect structural shortcuts in pseudo-$C_\ell$ computations (specifically spin-2 bandpower window evaluations). The paper is exceptionally rigorous in its methodology, employing a pre-registered, sealed blind protocol to evaluate the sensitivity of its execution receipts. The mathematical formalism for the uniform rotation of polarization spectra (Eqs. 1-3) is correct, and the statistical reporting of the blind test is generally excellent, with explicit Clopper-Pearson bounds provided for the detection rates. 

However, there is a definitive statistical error in the reporting of one of the confidence intervals, a missing quantitative bound for a performance claim, and leftover internal audit tags in the text that must be resolved before publication. 

Below are the specific findings:

### ESSENTIAL
*   **P1B-E1**: Section 11, page 9. Internal audit tags. The text contains the phrases "(S3, open)" and "(S2, in progress)". These appear to be internal issue-tracker placeholders or review-log tags that were accidentally left in the manuscript. 
    *   *Required fix*: Remove these internal bookkeeping placeholders and integrate the status of these items directly into the prose.
*   **P1B-E2**: Section 6, page 5. Statistical error in confidence interval. The text states: "across all six shortcut arms, 20/30 (point estimate 0.667, 95% interval [0.501, 0.807])." The interval [0.501, 0.807] is mathematically the **90%** two-sided Clopper-Pearson interval for $k=20, n=30$ (i.e., $\alpha=0.10$). The actual 95% two-sided interval ($\alpha=0.05$) is [0.472, 0.827]. 
    *   *Required fix*: Either change the label from "95% interval" to "90% interval", or update the numerical bounds to the correct 95% values.

### MAJOR
*   **P1B-M1**: Section 12, page 9. Uncomputed quantitative claim. The text claims the test suite completes with a "negligible memory footprint". In software metapapers, qualitative robustness assertions regarding performance must be backed by reproducible numbers.
    *   *Required fix*: Replace "negligible" with a quantitative upper bound (e.g., "< 50 MB") or an explicit measurement from the CI environment.

### MINOR
*   **P1B-M2**: Section 2, page 2. The text notes that a fractional deviation of 1.66 is "large enough to bias a fit". While a fractional error of order unity is self-evidently problematic, the paper would be strengthened by briefly stating the approximate magnitude of the bias on a standard cosmological parameter (e.g., tensor-to-scalar ratio $r$ or cosmic birefringence angle $\beta$) to ground the claim in physical units, though this is not strictly required given the scope of the software.

### NIT
*   **P1B-N1**: Section 6, page 4. Typography. The phrase "pilot / rule-development round" uses spaces around the slash, which is non-standard typography. 
    *   *Required fix*: Remove the spaces or rephrase to "pilot or rule-development round".

## Summary recommendation
MINOR REVISIONS

The manuscript is a highly rigorous, well-tested contribution to cosmological software provenance. The blind-testing protocol is commendable and sets a high standard for reproducibility claims. The required revisions are strictly localized to correcting a mislabeled statistical confidence interval, providing a hard number for a memory claim, and cleaning up leftover internal notes. Once these minor corrections are made, the paper will be fully suitable for publication.
