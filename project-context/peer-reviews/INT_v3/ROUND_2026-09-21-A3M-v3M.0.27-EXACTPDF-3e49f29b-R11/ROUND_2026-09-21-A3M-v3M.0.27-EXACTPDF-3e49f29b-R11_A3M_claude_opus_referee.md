## 2. Summary

The paper re-derives, from scratch and vertex by vertex, the squeezed-limit local non-Gaussianity of a dust-dominated contraction in the in-in formalism, obtaining f_NL^local = −35/16 = −2.1875 with a per-vertex attribution table (Table I) and a general-orientation result f^sq(µ) = −35/16 + (15/16)µ². It localizes the long-standing factor-of-two disagreement with Cai *et al.* (2009) to the step between that reference's own printed shape function and its own quoted amplitudes, and cross-checks the in-in result against a classical super-Hubble (separate-universe/threading-map) route whose residual offsets are reconciled at the equation level in Appendix A. Section III then asks whether the amplitude survives a nonsingular bounce: it computes a linear mode-mixing transfer and the bounce's own cubic term on three explicit bounce backgrounds in scheme S1 (z = a), and — the principal new content of this revision — argues from the Bardeen potential Φ, which contains no choice of z, that the continuation ambiguity at H = 0 is resolved in favour of the effective-fluid scheme S2 on the Quintin-type background, yielding a single transmitted value f_NL^after = −1.25 and, because the tensor transfer is unaffected, an amplification of r from 24 to ≈ 9.4 × 10². Sections IV–VI take the amplitude through pulsar timing, PBH abundance (compaction-function criterion), SMBH seeds, and the LSS bispectrum, returning three honest nulls and one channel that cannot discriminate. Section VIII establishes a joint (r, f_NL) no-go: the tensor-viable and Planck-viable constant-sound-speed windows are disjoint by a factor ≈ 350 in c_s, exactly λ-independently, leaving a curvaton-type spectator as the only surviving route.

This is a careful, unusually honest manuscript. My substantive concerns are concentrated in the newest material (the Bardeen/S2 scheme selection and its propagation through the rest of the paper), plus a set of internal inconsistencies where superseded scheme-S1 numbers are still carried as the paper's own.

## 4. ESSENTIAL findings

**ESSENTIAL 1 — The Bardeen-potential scheme-selection argument, which is this revision's headline new result, is asserted rather than derived, and the specific regularity claim it rests on is incorrect as printed.**

*Location:* Sec. III A, "Scheme S2" subsection, p. 6, the paragraph beginning "S1 and S2 are not two regularizations of one answer…".

*The defect, in three parts.*

(a) **The claimed regularity at the NEC crossing is not correct for either form the paper writes down.** The paper states the Bardeen equation "is regular at the bounce (H = 0 …) and equally regular at the NEC crossing ρ + p = 0, where z_S2² vanishes and ζ is logarithmically divergent." But in the printed second-order form, Φ″ + 2(H − φ″/φ′)Φ′ + (k² + 2H′ − 2Hφ″/φ′)Φ = 0, the coefficients contain φ″/φ′, and ρ + p ∝ φ′² vanishes at precisely the NEC crossing — so φ′ → 0 there and φ″/φ′ **diverges**. In the first-order (Φ, Ξ) form, which I verified is exactly equivalent (see §3.2(n)), the singularity reappears as µ = −1/Ḣ, and ρ + p = −2M_Pl²Ḣ, so µ has a **simple pole at exactly the NEC crossing**. The term (a² − µk²)Φ in Ξ′ therefore has a pole there, and Ξ acquires an O(k²) logarithmic divergence at the crossing — which is the expected physical behaviour (Ξ ∝ the velocity perturbation v ∝ δφ/φ̇). The statement "it is free of δ-functions, so Φ and Ξ are continuous across the NEC boundaries and no junction prescription need be imposed" is therefore **not established** by anything in the manuscript. It is very likely *true at the working accuracy* (at kη_B ≤ 10⁻² the µk² term is utterly negligible), but that is a quantitative statement the paper must make, not assume.

(b) **The claim that (Φ, Ξ) continuity "is identically S2's [ζ] = 0, [z²ζ′] = 0" is stated without proof.** This single sentence is what converts a regular Φ-propagation into a *selection between schemes*, and it is the load-bearing step of the entire new result — it is what changes the paper's transmitted amplitude by a factor 2.5 and its tensor-to-scalar ratio by a factor 39. For PRD a reader must be able to reproduce it from the manuscript. At present the only support offered inside the paper is a one-clause appeal to an archived script and to an "independent blind adjudication".

(c) **The regularity claim is internally inconsistent with the paper's own treatment of the other two backgrounds.** Sec. VII states "S2 is not defined on the LQC/poly backgrounds' Ḣ = 0 crossings", and Next-steps (i) says those crossings "require a separate indicial analysis not yet performed". But Ḣ = 0 *is* ρ + p = 0 — the same condition the paper asserts is unproblematic on the Quintin-type background. Either the crossing is benign generically (in which case the LQC/poly exclusion needs a different justification) or it is not (in which case the Quintin-type case needs the same indicial analysis). As written the two statements cannot both stand.

*Concrete fix.* Add a short appendix (two to three pages) that: (i) states the background class explicitly, including whether a scalar field φ with φ′ ≠ 0 exists (my reading is that the real distinction from LQC/poly is that those are specified as effective fluids with no φ, not that their Ḣ = 0 crossing differs — say so); (ii) performs the indicial analysis of the (Φ, Ξ) system at Ḣ = 0, exhibiting Φ as analytic and Ξ as finite at O(k⁰) with the O(k²µ) piece bounded and shown to be ≲ (kη_B)² × log, i.e. ≲ 10⁻⁴ at the k used; (iii) *derives* — not asserts — that propagating the regular Φ and converting to ζ at the two asymptotic ends (where the Φ ↔ ζ map is non-degenerate) is equivalent to imposing [ζ] = 0, [z_S2²ζ′] = 0, and *inequivalent* to S1's matching; and (iv) states what φ″(η_B) is at the bounce (see MINOR 5). 3. **Abstract pairs c_s ≃ 0.08 with r ≃ 1.8.** r = 24c_s gives 1.92 at c_s = 0.08 and 1.8 at c_s = 0.075; the committed floors are 0.0734–0.0788 with r = 1.76–1.89. Quote a single consistent pair.
4. **"9.6 decades below".** With T_B ≈ 2.3 GeV and the condition T_B ≳ 6 × 10⁹ GeV the gap is 9.4 decades (10.4 against 6 × 10¹⁰). 
5. **s = 39/16 is the c_s → 0 limit.** From Li Eq. (A.19) with Λ = 7/(16c_s²) I get s = (3/2)(13/8 + c_s²)/(1 + c_s²), which is 39/16 only as c_s → 0 (63/32 at c_s = 1). Say "in the small-c_s limit".

7. **"on both physical lines Λ ∝ 1/c_s²".** Both lines have Λ ∝ (1/c_s² − 1), not 1/c_s². The statement is only asymptotically true.
8. **Figure 2 plots f_PBH up to ∼10¹⁰** without the "uncapped, not a physical probability" caveat that Table V's caption carries. Add it to the caption.
9. **Table III caption grammar.** "The first three rows are scheme S1 …, superseded on the Quintin-type background …" — only the first row is Quintin-type. Rephrase.
10. **Appendix A §4 ordering.** §4 presents f^ρ_NL = 5(2ϵ − 15)/24 = −5/2 as the direct second-order continuation and then says the gap is closed by a weight correction, whereas Sec. II D presents 5(ϵ − 7)/8 = −55/16 as the (corrected) direct continuation. Both are consistent once read carefully, but a reader meets the superseded value first in the appendix. Present the corrected result first and the −5/2 as the superseded intermediate.
11. **Numerical collision of two distinct "−1.25"s.** The selected transmitted amplitude f_NL^after = −1.25 and the curvaton f_NL^curv at r_dec = 1 (Sec. VIII, Eq. (18)) are both exactly −1.25. 12. **"Two independent failures against the borrowed γ = 3" (Sec. IV D).** The slope and amplitude both derive from the same single committed power-law spectrum (A_s, n_s); they are not statistically independent. "Two distinct failures" would be accurate.

## 8. Overall assessment

The core of this paper is strong and, where I could check it, right. I independently reproduced the factor-of-two localization from Cai *et al.*'s own published Eq. (37) in all three configurations; I verified every equation-number citation to Li *et al.* that I could retrieve; I re-derived Appendix A's f_δN = −5 identity and the newly added general-n_s cancellation and found both exact; I confirmed the claim that no number in Sec. II or Appendix A changed under the new n_s = 1 scope caveat; I verified the Bardeen first-order system is exactly equivalent to the second-order Bardeen equation; and I recomputed roughly forty numerical claims — Tables III, IV, V, VI, VII, the c_s window boundaries, the perturbativity floor, the DBI best case, the curvaton formulas, the PTA z-distances, and the induced-GW normalization — against either closed-form algebra or the committed JSONs, finding agreement in every case I completed. The reproducibility statement is unusually good: all thirty-four cited paths resolve, and the JSONs contain the quoted digits. The paper's epistemic discipline (labelling reproduced / new / cited, publishing nulls as nulls, flagging the non-monotonicity and the ΩDM footnote) is well above the norm.

What stands between this manuscript and acceptance is that the **newest and most consequential material is the least finished**. The Bardeen-potential scheme selection changes the paper's transmitted amplitude by a factor 2.5 and its tensor-to-scalar ratio by a factor 39, yet it occupies one paragraph, its central equivalence claim ([Φ, Ξ] continuity ⇔ S2 matching) is asserted rather than derived, its regularity claim at the NEC crossing is incorrect as printed for both forms of the equation given, and it is internally inconsistent with the paper's own reason for not extending S2 to the LQC and poly backgrounds. The selected headline number is quoted to three figures while the paper discloses an O(1) convention dependence whose convention it never states — and the committed artifact shows a 23% drift of the window integral over the η* range explored. Beyond those two essentials, the propagation of the new selection through the rest of the paper is incomplete: the abstract still carries a superseded S1 band as the retained one, Sec. III still contains a blanket claim the selection contradicts, and four further passages (Secs. IV D, V C, VII, VIII) quote S1-derived numbers as the model's own. None of these change a physical conclusion — every verdict in the paper survives the corrections — but for PRD they must be fixed before the paper reads as a single coherent statement.

I expect all of this to be tractable in one revision: one new appendix, one convergence table, and a careful scheme-labelling sweep. I would be glad to see the revised manuscript.
