# Referee Report — ApJS (catalogue + method paper)

**Manuscript:** "A provenance-sealed DESI DR1 anomaly-score candidate catalogue: selection, validation, and a null known-object recovery benchmark"
**Author:** Houston Golden (Hubify Research)
**PDF audited:** `pipelines/p1_highz_tracers/anomaly_flagship_draft/main.pdf`, 18 pages, dated "September 22, 2026 (vAF.0.7)"
**Review profile:** ApJS-CATALOG. **Referee remit:** theoretical physics + observational rigor; scope-vs-dataset match; abstract-vs-body drift; figure/table internal consistency; unsupported cosmological/statistical claims.
**Reviewer note:** I have not seen any other referee's report. This is a cold independent read of the rendered PDF.

---

## 0. Overall assessment before the itemised list

This manuscript is doing something genuinely unusual and genuinely valuable: it publishes a machine-flagged candidate list *together with* the machinery that would allow a third party to falsify it, and it declines — repeatedly, explicitly, and at cost to its own headline — to claim a discovery. The `iron`-release provenance gate result (Sec. III B / Table II / Fig. 1) is a real and useful service to the community: it demonstrates that any reconstruction-error outlier search on public DESI coadds that omits an `OBJTYPE`/`COADD_FIBERSTATUS` gate returns >99.5% sky fibres in every bin above S = 3. That is a publishable negative infrastructure result on its own. The validation contract (Table VI) with two requirements that *fail by design* is a model other catalogue papers should copy.

I recomputed every arithmetic quantity in the paper that is recomputable from displayed numbers. **The arithmetic is, with two exceptions, exceptionally clean.** Specifically verified as correct: the deduplication chain (28,425,963 − 878,740 = 27,547,223); the selection fraction (1,244/21,793,550 = 5.71 × 10⁻⁵); every cell of Table II against Table III (the six bin populations sum to 320,418 all-fibre / 1,244 science, and every cumulative survival count and science fraction reproduces); the threshold rule's own logic against Table III; the stability check (5 × 1.3605/√20000 = 0.0481); all five Wilson intervals and the 3.3× enrichment in Table VII; the Spearman p-values in Figs. 5 and 6 and Sec. V B/V C against n = 1,244; all Table VIII family sizes (sum 675) and cluster counts (sum 23); every Table IX dominant-share product (all integers) and the 71.9% weighted mean; Fig. 7's three panels against Table VIII; the redshift splits (424 + 73 + 5 = 502; 1,153 + 86 + 5 = 1,244); the morphology census (838 typed + 406 empty = 1,244); the Lyman-α break wavelengths at 1215.67 Å for all four FT-A redshifts; every flux ratio and AB magnitude in Sec. VII B against Table XI; the 94.7th-percentile claim (66/1,244); the `worst_band` census (1,180 + 60 + 4 = 1,244, 94.9%); the 16-row contract tally against its four outcome classes; the 33-row/30-distinct-object count in Table X; and −35/16 = −2.1875 with the −6%…−16% → 1.06…1.19 inversion.

That said, the paper **cannot be accepted in its present form**, for reasons that cluster into four groups:

1. **The manuscript still contains its own internal review-process record.** A "2026-09-22 confirmation board" is named twice — once in the body of Sec. V A, once in the Acknowledgments — and an unpublished "earlier construction" of the taxonomy is narrated in the abstract, in Sec. VI A (at length), and again in Sec. VI D. A published paper has no earlier drafts; a reader has no access to them and cannot evaluate a comparison against one.
2. **The taxonomy of Sec. VI, which the round's own framing makes load-bearing, is under-specified in ways that make it unreproducible and, at one point, self-contradictory.** The feature scaling is never stated; the `pca_components=10` hyperparameter is incompatible with the stated 4-feature input; the hyperparameter provenance sentence contradicts itself two paragraphs later; the family roll-up rule ("by descriptor identity") is violated by Table VIII's own rows 3 and 8; and Table VIII leaves four of its eleven columns undefined.
3. **The sealed provenance chain does not cover the reported taxonomy**, and the title word "provenance-sealed" therefore overclaims. The landing receipt that V2 re-hashes against is dated 2026-09-03; the taxonomy reported here comes from `reclustering_2026_09_22`, which has no row in Table I and no SHA-256 anywhere in the paper.
4. **The abstract drifts harder than the body in several load-bearing places**, most seriously in "almost entirely explained by", in "anomaly-selected … quasar candidates", in "refutes", and in "eleven pass outright" (which the body's own Sec. V A contradicts by calling V1 a *partial* pass).

None of these is fatal to the underlying work. All are fixable. I am recommending **MAJOR REVISIONS**.

## 1. ESSENTIAL findings

### AF-E1 — Internal review-process prose in the body of Sec. V A (p. 7)
**Location:** Sec. V A, p. 7, right column.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "…the univariate regression of `mean_mse` on r_B alone gives R² = 0.198 (against 0.001 for r_R and 0.004 for r_Z) — **the 2026-09-22 confirmation board flagged the summary locations for conflating this univariate figure with the full-model collapse-on-removal figure above**; both numbers point the same direction (the blue arm dominates), but are not interchangeable, and both are now stated explicitly wherever either is quoted."
**Problem:** "The 2026-09-22 confirmation board" is an internal review artifact. No such body is defined in the paper, no reader can consult it, and the clause "both are now stated explicitly wherever either is quoted" is a changelog entry, not a scientific statement. This is a version-history record printed in a manuscript section.
**Required fix:** Delete everything from "— the 2026-09-22 confirmation board" through "wherever either is quoted." Replace with the scientific content only: *"This is distinct from what r_B explains in isolation: the univariate regression of `mean_mse` on r_B alone gives R² = 0.198 (against 0.001 for r_R and 0.004 for r_Z). The two readings are not interchangeable — one is a drop-one variance decomposition, the other a marginal fit — and both are reported wherever either is used."*

### AF-E2 — Internal review-process prose in the Acknowledgments (p. 17)
**Location:** Acknowledgments, p. 17, right column, bibliography-verification paragraph.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "…and [2–4] (added 2026-09-22, **in response to the confirmation board's ESSENTIAL finding** that these three works were named in prose without a bibliography entry)."
**Problem:** Same defect as AF-E1, now with an explicit referee-severity tag ("ESSENTIAL") printed in the manuscript. Additionally, the entire paragraph — "added 2026-09-21", "not independently re-checked against live ADS in this appendix", "a live ADS check of these five specifically remains open and is not claimed as done" — is a bibliography-audit work log. It tells the reader that five of the paper's references have not been verified, which is an argument against publishing the paper, not a disclosure that belongs in it.
**Required fix:** Verify Refs. [1]–[5] against ADS (they are all correct as printed — I checked them: Labbé 2023 Nature 616, 266; LoVerde et al. 2008 JCAP 04, 014; Eisenstein & Hu 1998 ApJ 496, 605; Planck 2018 VI A&A 641, A6; Boylan-Kolchin 2023 Nat. Astron. 7, 731), then **delete the entire verification paragraph**. A bibliography is either correct or it is not; a paper does not publish its own QA trail. Retain only the substantive note that Ref. [9] is in preparation and Refs. [16, 36] are living documentation, and move that to the reference entries themselves.

### AF-E3 — Version-history narration of an unpublished earlier draft, in the abstract
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1, sentence 2.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "One downstream, non-selection analysis — the descriptive taxonomy of Sec. VI — has its clustering now fully specified and reproducible (spherical sky embedding, stated hyperparameters and seed), though **its specific cluster and family boundaries differ moderately from an earlier construction** (Sec. VI)."
**Problem:** The abstract of a published paper compares its result to an unpublished earlier version of itself. A reader cannot access "an earlier construction", cannot evaluate "moderately", and has no reason to care. Worse, the word "now" ("has its clustering *now* fully specified") presupposes a previous state the reader has never seen. This is mistake-narration promoted to the most-read 250 words of the paper.
**Required fix:** Delete the clause. State the method positively and once: *"The descriptive taxonomy of Sec. VI is a downstream, non-selection analysis; its clustering is fully specified (spherical sky embedding, stated hyperparameters and seed) and reproducible from the released files."* The sky-wrap issue is a legitimate *methodological* point and should survive as a one-sentence methods statement in Sec. VI A (see AF-E4), stripped of any reference to a prior draft.

### AF-E4 — Sec. VI A contradicts itself on the provenance of the clustering hyperparameters
**Location:** Sec. VI A, p. 10, right column, consecutive paragraphs.
**Offending text (verbatim), paragraph 1:** "Every hyperparameter and the random seed (`pca_components=10, umap_neighbors=15, umap_min_dist=0.05, min_cluster_size=15, min_samples=3, random_state=42`) are stated and **reused verbatim from an earlier construction's own run manifest**, so this result is reproducible from the released files and this manuscript alone."
**Offending text (verbatim), paragraph 2:** "…that earlier construction's largest family had a naive RA span of 350.4° … and **its hyperparameters and seed were not recorded in the manuscript or its manifest**."
**Problem:** These two sentences are in direct logical contradiction, twelve lines apart. Either the earlier run's manifest recorded the hyperparameters (in which case they could be reused verbatim) or it did not (in which case they could not). The contradiction is not cosmetic: it goes to whether the "isolating the sky-embedding fix as the only changed variable" claim in the next sentence is supportable at all. If the earlier hyperparameters are unknown, the corrected clustering differs from the earlier one in an unknown number of variables, and the ARI 0.60 / AMI 0.63 comparison has no controlled interpretation.
**Required fix:** Resolve the contradiction and state which is true. Then — per AF-E3 — remove the framing entirely and simply state the method as adopted: the four-feature spherical embedding, the six hyperparameters, the seed, and the determinism check. If the ARI/AMI comparison against a prior partition is retained at all, it must be against a *released, hash-bound* partition (see AF-E7); otherwise delete it.

### AF-E5 — The family roll-up rule is violated by Table VIII's own rows
**Location:** Sec. VI A, p. 10 ("roll up **by descriptor identity** into 9 families"); Table VIII, p. 12, rows 3 and 8.
**Problem:** Families are said to be formed by merging clusters that share a family descriptor. Table VIII prints, for each family, a Tier and a Survey/prog. — the only descriptor-like quantities shown. Family 3 is (`low`, `sv1/other`). Family 8 is (`low`, `sv1/other`). **They are identical.** Under the stated rule they must have merged into one family; they did not. Either the descriptor carries a third component that the paper never names, or the roll-up is not by descriptor identity, or Table VIII is wrong. As printed, the taxonomy's construction rule is falsified by the taxonomy's own evidence table.
**Required fix:** Print the actual `family_descriptor` string (it is a released column, per Table IV) as a column of Table VIII, and state the exact roll-up rule in Sec. VI A, including every field the descriptor is built from. If families 3 and 8 genuinely share a descriptor, explain what separates them.

### AF-E6 — Table VIII leaves four of its eleven columns undefined
**Location:** Table VIII caption, p. 12.
**Caption (verbatim):** "Per-family evidence over the 675 SIMBAD/NED-unmatched objects. N_cl is the number of source clusters, r_B the median b-camera residual, N_Z0 the number with ZWARN = 0, z̃_Z0 their median redshift, and N_W the number with an AllWISE counterpart."
**Problem:** The table has eleven data columns. The caption defines five. Undefined: **Tier**, **Survey/prog.**, **Score med. (5–95%)**, **N_{z>2}**, **N_QSO**.
- **"Tier"** is the worst of these: the values `low`/`elevated`/`high`/`extreme` are *never defined anywhere in the manuscript*, yet the text relies on them ("Family 2, **the same score tier's** worst redshift reliability", p. 11; "Family 6 … is **the extreme-score family**", p. 11). A categorical label with no stated cut points is not a machine-checkable quantity and cannot appear in a catalogue paper's evidence table.
- **N_QSO** is ambiguous between "SPECTYPE = QSO over all family members" and "over the ZWARN = 0 members only". The distinction matters: the paper elsewhere states flatly that ZWARN = 4 redshifts "must not be used as measurements" (p. 5), and 386 of the 675 unmatched objects have ZWARN = 4. If N_QSO includes ZWARN = 4 rows then "3 spectroscopically confirmed quasars" in family 2 (p. 11) is not supported.
- **N_{z>2}** has the same ambiguity and additionally uses a strict inequality where Sec. IV B uses "z > 2" and Sec. VII A uses "z ≥ 4"; the convention is never fixed.
- **"Score med. (5–95%)"** does not state the percentile estimator. This matters: I attempted to reconcile family 6's 95th percentile (6.52) against the FT-C scores the text places in family 6 ("three of the five are in family 6", p. 13) and could not close the check without assuming unlisted ZWARN = 4 members between 6.39 and 7.15 — a reader should not have to make that assumption.
**Required fix:** Define every column in the caption. Define the score tiers quantitatively (give the cut points) in Sec. VI A or drop the Tier column. State whether N_QSO and N_{z>2} are computed over all members or over ZWARN = 0 members only, and make it the latter if the paper's own redshift-reliability rule is to be honoured. State the percentile method.

### AF-E7 — The reported taxonomy is not inside the sealed provenance chain, and the title overclaims
**Location:** Title; Table I, p. 3; Table VI check V2, p. 9; Sec. IX, p. 16.
**Problem:** Table I binds thirteen artifacts, among them `flagship_taxonomy_v2.json`. Check V2 (PASS) states that "every released artifact's SHA-256 equals the value in the landing receipt (`PHASE3_V2_LANDING_2026-09-03.md`)". That receipt is dated **2026-09-03**. The taxonomy reported in this paper — 23 clusters, 9 families, Tables VIII and IX, Figs. 7 and 8, the latent-space test — is produced by `reclustering_2026_09_22` and `taxonomy_adoption_2026_09_22` (Sec. IX, p. 16), i.e. **nineteen days after the receipt was sealed**. Neither script nor its output appears in Table I; neither carries a SHA-256 anywhere in the manuscript. Check V6 (PASS) asserts "23 clusters roll up into 9 families" — a statement about the *new* partition — while V2 re-hashes the *old* one. V2 and V6 cannot both be PASS against the same artifact set.
Consequently the title adjective "**provenance-sealed**" is not true of the paper as a whole. It is true of the *selection* chain (score → gate → threshold), which is the paper's real and defensible contribution.
**Required fix:** Either (a) re-issue the landing receipt to cover the corrected taxonomy, add `reclustering_2026_09_22` and `taxonomy_adoption_2026_09_22` and their outputs to Table I with real SHA-256 values, update the receipt name and date in V2, and state the re-issue date; or (b) state explicitly in Table I's caption, in V2's requirement text, and in Sec. VI A that the taxonomy is **outside** the sealed chain and carries its own separate provenance. In either case, narrow the title: e.g. "*A DESI DR1 anomaly-score candidate catalogue with a sealed selection chain: …*".

### AF-E8 — The clustering feature scaling is never stated, and the stated PCA hyperparameter cannot run on the stated input
**Location:** Sec. VI A, p. 10.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "The 675 unmatched objects are grouped by PCA → UMAP [18] → HDBSCAN [19] on the sky position embedded on the unit sphere, (cos α cos δ, sin α cos δ, sin δ), together with the anomaly score S … (`pca_components=10`, …)"
**Problem, part (a) — scaling.** The four input features are three direction cosines, each bounded on [−1, 1], and the anomaly score S, which on this sample ranges from 3.0002 to 11.3316. **S therefore carries roughly five times the dynamic range of the entire sky embedding combined**, and in an unscaled Euclidean metric — which is what PCA and UMAP's default metric use — the sky position is very nearly inert. The paper never states whether the features were standardised, whitened, or left raw. This single unstated choice determines the entire taxonomy: unscaled, the partition is essentially a score-only stratification and Table IX's large RA/Dec spans are a mathematical inevitability rather than a finding; scaled, it is a genuine score × sky partition. The paper's own headline conclusion in Sec. VI B — "no family is a compact sky region" — is uninterpretable until this is stated.
**Problem, part (b) — the PCA hyperparameter.** `pca_components=10` is requested on a four-column input. The paper acknowledges this ("non-dimension-reducing by construction (more components requested than features)") but does not say how it was handled. A standard `sklearn.decomposition.PCA(n_components=10)` call on a (675, 4) array raises `ValueError`. The manuscript therefore does not describe a runnable pipeline.
**Required fix:** State the feature scaling explicitly (and if none was applied, report the effective per-feature variance contribution to the PCA input so a reader can judge how much sky information entered at all). State how `n_components=10` was reconciled with a 4-feature input (clipped? padded? a different implementation?). If the answer is that PCA was effectively an identity rotation, say so plainly and drop the component count from the hyperparameter list, where it currently reads as a meaningful setting.

### AF-E9 — The two R² readings are juxtaposed without the "not interchangeable" qualifier in three of the four places they appear
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; Contribution 4, p. 2; Conclusions, p. 16. Qualifier present only at Sec. V A, p. 7.
**Problem:** The paper quotes two R² statistics derived from two different procedures — a drop-one variance decomposition of the three-camera model (0.777 → 0.006 without r_B) and a univariate marginal regression (0.198) — and correctly warns at one location that "both numbers point the same direction … but are not interchangeable". A text search of the PDF confirms the phrase "not interchangeable" occurs **exactly once**, in Sec. V A. The abstract, the contributions list, and the conclusions all place "R² = 0.78 … collapsing to 0.01 … while it alone, univariately, explains R² = 0.20" side by side with no qualification whatsoever. A reader of the abstract alone will read these as commensurable and will conclude the blue arm explains ~78% of the score's variance.
**Required fix:** Carry the qualifier to every juxtaposition. In the abstract, the minimum acceptable form is: *"…the full three-camera drop-one decomposition attributes R² = 0.78 to the three residuals jointly and collapses to R² = 0.01 when the blue arm is removed, while the blue arm's own marginal regression explains R² = 0.20; the two are different decompositions and are not directly comparable."*

### AF-E10 — Abstract: "almost entirely explained by" is stronger than any computation in the paper supports
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; repeated verbatim at Contribution 4, p. 2, and in the Conclusions, p. 16.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "Second, the score is not merely correlated with but **almost entirely explained by** the blue spectrograph arm's own reconstruction residual…"
**Problem:** Three independent in-paper measurements bound how much r_B explains the score: ρ_s(S, r_B) = +0.527 (ρ² = 0.28), the univariate R² = 0.198, and the fraction of objects for which b is the worst-fit camera, 94.9%. The only number consistent with "almost entirely" is the drop-one figure, which measures the *joint* model's dependence on r_B, not r_B's own explanatory power. The strongest defensible statement is "dominated by", which is in fact the phrase the body uses ("the blue arm dominates the loss arithmetically", p. 7). The abstract, contributions list, and conclusions all use the stronger phrase.
**Required fix:** Replace "almost entirely explained by" with "dominated by" in all three locations, and state the univariate R² adjacent to it in each.

### AF-E11 — Abstract calls the FT-A objects "anomaly-selected" when three of the four are below the catalogue's median anomaly score
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; Sec. VII A, p. 13; Table X, p. 14; Sec. VIII, p. 15.
**Offending text (verbatim, abstract):** "of four **anomaly-selected** DESI-pipeline z ≥ 4 quasar candidates with no cataloged counterpart…"
**Problem:** The four FT-A objects carry S = 3.496, 3.232, 3.061, 3.033 (Table X). The catalogue's median score is 3.28 and its 90th percentile is 4.08 (Sec. IV A, p. 4). **Three of the four FT-A candidates score below the catalogue median, and all four sit in the bottom half-decade of a distribution that extends to 11.33.** The FT-A rule as stated (Sec. VII A: "`ZWARN` = 0 and z ≥ 4") contains no score criterion beyond bare catalogue membership. These objects are therefore selected by redshift and cross-match status, not by anomaly. Calling them "anomaly-selected" in the abstract, and describing them in Sec. VIII as "the interesting subset", attributes to the score a role it demonstrably did not play. This is the single most consequential piece of abstract drift in the paper, because the FT-A tier is the only part of the work that touches a cosmological question.
**Required fix:** Remove "anomaly-selected". State plainly, in Sec. VII A and again in Sec. VIII, that the FT-A tier is a redshift-and-counterpart selection applied *within* the catalogue and that its members' anomaly scores are unremarkable (give the four values against the catalogue median in the text). Additionally, state how many of the 675 unmatched objects satisfy "`ZWARN` = 0 and z ≥ 4" — if the answer is exactly four, say so; if the `SPECTYPE = QSO` condition is also part of the rule (all four rows are QSO), it must be written into the rule.

### AF-E12 — Abstract says "refutes"; the body says "disfavoured"
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; Sec. VII B, p. 13; Conclusions, p. 17.
**Offending text (abstract):** "the released Legacy Survey photometry already **refutes** one on public data alone"
**Offending text (Sec. VII B, same object):** "We classify it as ***disfavoured by public imaging*** — either the photometry is blended or the redshift solution is wrong — and retain it in the table so that the tier rule can be audited."
**Problem:** The paragraph is headed "**Refuted:**" but concludes "disfavoured". The body's own calibrated verdict offers two alternatives ("blended photometry" *or* "wrong redshift") — under the first of which the candidate is not refuted at all, merely unmeasurable. The abstract and Conclusions ("refutes one of four high-redshift candidates") both take the stronger word. Sec. X takes a third, weaker form ("one of four has already failed a public-data check"). Three different strengths for one result.
**Required fix:** Pick the body's calibrated word and use it everywhere: *disfavoured*. Change the paragraph heading from "Refuted:" to "Disfavoured:", and change the abstract and Conclusions to "disfavours one on public data alone". If the author believes "refuted" is correct, then the blended-photometry alternative must be closed in-paper, which the paper states it cannot do without inverse variances.

### AF-E13 — Abstract says "eleven pass outright"; the body says V1 is a partial pass
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; Table VI, p. 9; Sec. V A, p. 7.
**Offending text (abstract):** "(eleven **pass outright**; two are reported quantities rather than thresholds; one returns a weak positive signal; two fail by design…)"
**Offending text (Sec. V A):** "**V1 is a partial pass**: it verifies `TARGETID` > 0, the one of its three provenance-gate conditions that survives in the published file, because `OBJTYPE` and `COADD_FIBERSTATUS` are not carried in the released tables."
**Problem:** Only ten of the eleven "outright" passes are outright. Worse, Table VI's V1 *requirement text* has been written to accommodate the gap — "(`TARGETID` > 0; `OBJTYPE`/`FIBERSTATUS` **where carried**)" — so the requirement passes by construction. A pre-declared validation contract whose requirement text is softened after the fact to match what the release happens to contain is not a pre-declared contract. This is the one place where the paper's own integrity mechanism has been bent, and it is exactly the mechanism the paper asks the reader to trust.
**Required fix:** (i) Add a `PARTIAL` outcome to Table VI and assign it to V1. (ii) Restore V1's original pre-declared requirement text (all three gate conditions, unqualified) and let it read PARTIAL against that text. (iii) Change the abstract to "ten pass outright; one passes partially because two of its three gate columns are absent from the release; two are reported quantities…". (iv) Correspondingly correct "Eleven requirements pass outright (V1–V10, V3b)" in Sec. V A.

### AF-E14 — The wavelength-to-bin mapping is declared undocumented, yet the paper's central instrumental argument depends on it
**Location:** Sec. III A, p. 3; Sec. V C, pp. 7–8; Conclusions, p. 16.
**Offending text (Sec. III A, verbatim):** "…its training corpus …, **its wavelength-to-bin mapping across the three cameras**, and its architecture beyond 'convolutional autoencoder, 496 → 128' are **not documented in any artifact this release controls**."
**Problem:** The `peak_residual_wavelength` column is derived from the model's residual vector, which lives in the 496-bin space. If the bin→wavelength mapping is undocumented, then the numerical values 4057 Å, 3632 Å, 5930 Å, 4211 Å and 2098 Å (Sec. V C) are not traceable to any documented calibration, and the paper's *single most consequential interpretive result* — "concentrates more tightly in the observed frame than the rest frame … evidence favouring a fixed-wavelength calibration origin" — rests on an uncheckable mapping. The Conclusions restate this as a load-bearing result ("concentrates more tightly in the observed wavelength frame than the rest frame, evidence that leans instrumental"). The paper cannot simultaneously declare the mapping undocumented and use the wavelengths it produces as evidence.
**Required fix:** Either recover and publish the bin→wavelength mapping as a hash-bound artifact (it is a single 496-element array), or explicitly downgrade every wavelength-based claim in Sec. V C and the Conclusions to a labelled assumption ("under the assumption that the released `peak_residual_wavelength` values are correctly calibrated, which this release cannot verify"). The former is trivially cheap and is the right answer for a catalogue paper.

### AF-E15 — Abstract claims a "complete provenance chain" that the body says is incomplete
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; Sec. III A, p. 3.
**Offending text (abstract):** "…together with **the complete provenance chain needed to reproduce the catalogue's selection** (score, provenance gate, threshold)…"
**Offending text (Sec. III A):** "Two consequences follow directly from this [missing documentation]: if the training corpus is drawn from the same imaging/spectroscopic population scored here, objects inside it would carry an artificially low reconstruction error, **a selection effect operating on the exact quantity that defines the catalogue** … **Neither can be assessed** without the missing documentation, which we did not reconstruct or guess at for this release; **closing it is a named prerequisite for any use of this catalogue** beyond the data-release-with-caveats framing adopted here."
**Problem:** A chain that cannot rule out a training/scoring overlap acting on the defining quantity is not a *complete* chain for reproducing the selection; it is a complete chain for *replaying* the selection given a frozen black box. Those are different guarantees, and the abstract claims the stronger one while the body names the weaker one as a blocking prerequisite. The abstract nowhere mentions that the scoring model is undocumented.
**Required fix:** Change the abstract to the accurate claim — e.g. "*…together with the chain needed to replay the catalogue's selection byte-for-byte from a frozen, hash-bound model (score, provenance gate, threshold); the model's training corpus is not documented by this release, and a possible training/scoring overlap is recorded as an open selection effect.*" The caveat must be in the abstract; it is the largest single limitation on the product.

### AF-E16 — `[PLACEHOLDER: Zenodo DOI for the public deposit]` printed in the Data Availability section
**Location:** Sec. IX, p. 16, left column (rendered in red).
**Problem:** For an ApJS catalogue paper the deposited data product *is* the contribution, and its DOI is the single most-used string in the paper. A red placeholder in the Data Availability section means the release does not yet exist. The paper cannot be reviewed as a data release against a deposit that has not been made.
**Required fix:** Mint the Zenodo deposit, insert the DOI, and confirm that the deposited files' SHA-256 values match Table I. A reserved (pre-mint) DOI is acceptable provided the record is created and the reservation is stated as such; a bracketed placeholder is not.

### AF-E17 — f_NL = −35/16 is load-bearing and uncited
**Location:** Sec. VIII, p. 15.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "For the amplitude **this programme's** matter-dominated-contraction bounce predicts (f_NL = −35/16 = −2.1875), applying the LoVerde, Miller, Shandera & Verde (2008) [2] non-Gaussian correction…"
**Problem:** This is the paper's only cosmological input parameter, and it drives the paper's only cosmological conclusion. It is attributed to "this programme" with **no citation, no derivation, and no companion-paper reference**. A standalone reader of ApJS has no way to obtain, check, or contest the value. (Note that −35/16 is not the textbook matter-bounce value; a reader who reaches for the literature will find competing conventions and will not be able to tell which one is intended.) Instruction: a load-bearing number in a published paper must be either derived in-paper, cited to a published or preprinted source with an identifier, or dropped.
**Required fix:** Cite the derivation (journal reference or arXiv ID) or reproduce it in an appendix — the derivation of a single second-order coefficient is a paragraph. If neither is possible, remove the specific value and state the null in terms of a general |f_NL| scaling: "for any |f_NL| ≲ 3 the correction is a few-to-fifteen-percent suppression", which is the only part the paper's own computation supports.

## 2. MAJOR findings

### AF-M1 — The latent-space test is circular and the paper claims otherwise
**Location:** Sec. VI C, p. 11.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "So we tested the partition in the space the model actually encodes spectra into, and **which the clustering never saw**: its silhouette over the 128 latent dimensions is −0.0183…"
**Problem:** The clustering *did* see a deterministic function of that space. By Eq. (1), S is an affine function of `mean_mse`; `mean_mse` is the reconstruction error of the decoder applied to the same 128 latents. The 128 latents therefore determine S exactly. A partition built on S is a partition built on a function of the latents, and it must carry latent-space information by construction. The permutation test asks whether the partition carries *any* latent structure — a question whose answer was fixed before the test was run. The paper's conclusion (weak structure, negative silhouette) is honest, but the test as framed cannot distinguish "the families encode spectral structure" from "the families encode S, which is a scalar summary of the latents".
**Required fix:** State the circularity explicitly. Then run the test that is actually informative and is free: compute the silhouette over the 128 latents **after residualising out S** (or over the latents of objects within a single narrow score bin). If the structure survives at fixed S, that is a real result; if it does not, the correct statement is that the families carry no latent information beyond the score, which is a stronger and cleaner null than the one currently reported.

### AF-M2 — 50 permutation draws is an unjustifiably weak null for a test that costs nothing
**Location:** Sec. VI C, p. 11; Fig. 8, p. 12; Table VI check V12, p. 9.
**Problem:** The null distribution has 50 draws, which floors the achievable empirical p-value at ~1/50 = 0.02 and gives the ±0.0059 spread an sampling uncertainty of ~10%. The paper states in Sec. IX that the entire taxonomy stage is "CPU only, < 1 minute, $0 cost". There is no defensible reason for 50 draws when 10⁴ costs minutes. The reported effect (observed −0.0183 vs null −0.0309 ± 0.0059, i.e. ~2.1σ) is precisely in the regime where the draw count matters.
**Required fix:** Re-run with ≥10⁴ draws, report the exact empirical p (or the Gaussian-equivalent z with its Monte-Carlo error), and redraw Fig. 8. Update V12's outcome text with the new number.

### AF-M3 — The recovery benchmark uses a 1.5″ match radius while the match/unmatch split uses 3″
**Location:** Sec. IV D, p. 6 ("SIMBAD [17] and NED [6] cross-matching at 3″"); Sec. V D, p. 9 and Table VII caption, p. 11 ("1.5″ positional match").
**Problem:** The benchmark whose job is to *find* recovered objects uses a radius half that of the cross-match whose job is to *remove* already-catalogued objects. The stricter radius systematically suppresses recovery and therefore biases the paper's headline negative result toward null. No justification for the asymmetry is given anywhere.
**Required fix:** Justify the choice, and report the benchmark at 3″ as a sensitivity row (a second column of Table VII, or a sentence). If the recovery at 3″ is materially different — in particular if the BAL enrichment moves — that must be stated. This costs one re-run of an already-committed script.

### AF-M4 — The observed-vs-rest-frame concentration test has no null and no effect size
**Location:** Sec. V C, p. 8.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "…`peak_residual_wavelength` is more tightly concentrated in the *observed* frame (median 4211 Å, interquartile range 39% of the median) than in the rest frame after de-redshifting (median 2098 Å, interquartile range 68% of the median)."
**Problem:** This is the paper's key discriminator between an instrumental and an astrophysical origin, and it is reported as a bare comparison of two fractional IQRs with no null. The comparison is confounded by the redshift distribution itself: de-redshifting by a quantity whose own spread is large will inflate the rest-frame fractional IQR *even if the feature has a genuine rest-frame origin plus noise*. The reader needs the number that closes this: given the actual (1 + z) distribution of the 502-object ZWARN = 0 subset (median z = 1.14), what fractional IQR would an exactly-rest-frame-fixed feature produce in the observed frame, and what would an exactly-observed-frame-fixed feature produce in the rest frame? 39% vs 68% is meaningless without those two reference values.
**Required fix:** Compute and quote both null expectations from the sample's own redshift distribution, and state the result as a ratio against them. State the effect size (how much more concentrated, in units of the expected spread) rather than only the direction.

### AF-M5 — Fig. 6's caption contradicts the paper's own p-values
**Location:** Fig. 6 caption, p. 10.
**Caption (verbatim):** "The score tracks the b-camera residual and **is uncorrelated with the other two**."
**Problem:** The panel headers of the very same figure print ρ_s = −0.142 for r_Z, and the body (Sec. V C, p. 7) gives p ~ 5 × 10⁻⁷ for that coefficient — a correlation significant at better than 5σ. ### AF-M6 — Abstract: "five independent measures" — they are not independent
**Location:** Abstract, p. 1; Sec. V B, p. 7.
**Offending text (abstract):** "(Spearman |ρ_s| ≤ 0.10 against **five independent measures**)"
**Problem:** The five are median coadd S/N, coadd exposure time, TSNR2_LRG, TSNR2_QSO and r-band flux. TSNR2 is, by construction, a template-weighted signal-to-noise integral; TSNR2_LRG and TSNR2_QSO are near-duplicates of one another and both are strong monotone functions of exposure time and coadd S/N. Calling them five *independent* measures inflates the apparent strength of the null by a factor of roughly the effective number of degrees of freedom, which is closer to two. The body does not claim independence; the abstract adds it.
**Required fix:** Replace with "five exposure-quality and brightness columns" and, if the claim is to be quantitative, report the correlation matrix among the five (or the effective number of independent tests) so a reader can judge the multiplicity.

### AF-M7 — The "≳ 0.3 dex stellar-mass systematic floor" is uncited, and the comparison is a category error
**Location:** Sec. VIII, p. 15.
**Offending text (verbatim):** "That shift is a factor of roughly 1.06 to 1.19, well inside the **≳ 0.3 dex (≈ 2×) stellar-mass systematic floor of current high-redshift photometric censuses**, not more than an order of magnitude below it…"
**Problem, part (a):** The 0.3 dex figure is an assertion about the observational literature with no citation and no in-paper computation.
**Problem, part (b):** A 0.3 dex *stellar-mass* systematic is compared directly against a *halo-abundance* factor. The comparison as written makes the non-Gaussian correction look safely subdominant by mismatching the units of the two effects.
**Required fix:** Cite the 0.3 dex figure. Then either propagate it through the same mass function the paper already uses (the script `ledger6_png_highz_abundance.py` is committed and could emit this in one line) so that both sides of the comparison are abundance factors, or delete the comparison.

## 4. Scope, length, and venue fit

**Is the paper appropriately scoped for its dataset?** Mostly yes, and this is the manuscript's principal virtue. The author has resisted every available temptation to over-read 1,244 objects. Sec. X ("What this catalogue is not") is the right instinct and should be kept verbatim. The one place where scope slips is Sec. VIII's f_NL null, which imports a cosmological parameter from outside the paper (AF-E17) to bound a contribution the catalogue explicitly cannot make. That section is defensible *only* if the f_NL value is cited; as an uncited import it is out of scope for an ApJS catalogue paper and should otherwise be cut.

**Does the abstract's scope match the analysis?** No — see AF-E9 through AF-E15. The abstract is both too long (AF-M16) and, in four load-bearing places, stronger than the body.

**Is the paper too long for the claimed contribution?** Marginally, yes. ApJS legitimately hosts long catalogue papers, and eleven tables is not excessive for a data release. But the contribution as honestly stated is: a 1,244-object candidate list with a sealed selection chain; a provenance-gate negative result; a 16-item validation contract; a null recovery benchmark; a descriptive stratification the paper itself declines to call classes; and a 30-object follow-up list of which one item has been tested. Sec. VI currently spends **two and a half pages, two tables, and two figures** on a result the paper characterises as "structured but not separated … descriptive strata, not classes" — i.e. a null. That is disproportionate.

**Recommended maximum: 14 pages** (excluding bibliography). Concretely: merge Tables VIII and IX into one per-family table (they share the Family and N columns and describe the same nine objects); drop Fig. 7's third panel (the AllWISE count is six objects across nine families — it is a table cell, not a figure); compress Sec. VI A to a single methods paragraph once the earlier-construction narration is removed per AF-E3/AF-E4; and cut the abstract to 250 words per AF-M16. That recovers roughly three to four pages with no loss of content.

**Figures that earn their place:** Fig. 1 (the paper's best figure — it carries the provenance-gate result unaided), Fig. 2, Fig. 6, Fig. 9. **Figures that are marginal:** Fig. 3 (the "high score and high redshift are not the same population" point is a single Spearman coefficient, ρ_s = 0.009, and needs no two-panel figure), Fig. 4 (a sky map that the caption concedes "traces DR1 tiling, not any property of the score" — it demonstrates a null and could be supplementary), Fig. 7 panel 3, Fig. 8 (one number against a 50-draw null).

**Effect-size compliance:** good on the whole. The Mann–Whitney results in Sec. IV D correctly carry medians alongside p-values; the Spearman results carry ρ; Table VII carries Wilson intervals and enrichment. The failures are AF-M4 (no effect size for the frame-concentration test) and the χ²-adjacent Δχ² values in Table XI, which are quoted (23.4, 135.5, 22.0, 4205.1) with no degrees of freedom and no statement of what Δχ² threshold constitutes a secure redshift beyond the FT-C rule's "> 25".

VI C), the Spearman p-values (Secs. IV B, V B, V C), the Wilson intervals (Table VII), and the 5σ/√n stability bound (Sec.
