## Summary

The manuscript (i) re-derives the matter-contraction squeezed bispectrum vertex-by-vertex, obtaining f_NL^local = −35/16 + (15/16)μ² and locating a factor of two in Cai et al. (2009) at their amplitude-normalization step; (ii) claims a method-independent classical super-Hubble confirmation and a linear identity δN_c = (1−ε/3)ζ reconciling separate-universe values; (iii) bounds linear transmission through a nonsingular bounce (0.165–0.409) and adds a computed bounce cubic term, giving f_NL^after ∈ [−0.65, −0.50] in one scheme; (iv) runs three observational channels — NANOGrav slope (γ = 3 consistent), PBH compaction-function ratio A(−35/16)/A(−35/8) ≈ 1.73 plus a 7-decade null for the lab's own spectrum, and DESI/SPHEREx bare significances. The in-in table is internally consistent (vertex sums, angular averages, equilateral/folded values, all PTA/LSS tension arithmetic check). The paper is unusually candid about scope, but three internal contradictions and two physics misstatements prevent acceptance as is.

## Verdict

**major-revisions**

## MAJOR findings

1. **Pre-bounce vs post-bounce amplitude is applied inconsistently across channels, and the stated kη_B validity window contradicts itself (§III A p.4, §V C p.7, §VI B/Table IV p.8, §VII A p.8–9).** §III A derives f_NL^after ∈ [−0.65, −0.50] valid for kη_B ≲ 10⁻². §V C then states this condition "is satisfied at every PBH mass scale considered here," i.e. the transfer applies at *smaller* kη_B. But §VII A asserts the CMB/LSS pivot (k = 0.05 Mpc⁻¹, the smallest kη_B of any scale in the paper) "lies far outside that validated kη_B window," and on that basis Table IV and the abstract headline SPHEREx at 3.13–4.38σ using the *pre-bounce* −35/16. The window kη_B ≲ 10⁻² is an upper bound on k; the pivot scale is deeper inside it than any PBH scale. If §III A's result holds, the observable LSS amplitude is |f_NL| ≈ 0.5–0.65 and the SPHEREx bare significance is 0.7–1.3σ, not 3.1–4.4σ, and the claim that galaxy surveys "discriminate −35/16 from −35/8" collapses (both map to ≈ −0.5 to −0.65 and ≈ −1.0 to −1.3 after transmission; the *transmitted* pair is what a survey sees). **Resolve by** either (a) propagating the S1 transmission to the pivot scale and headlining f_NL^after in Table IV/abstract with the pre-bounce value as a secondary row, or (b) giving a concrete physical reason (an IR cutoff on the S1 calculation, not "outside the window") why the transmission does not apply at k = 0.05 Mpc⁻¹, and then removing the §V C sentence that extends it to all PBH scales.

2. **The "method-independent confirmation" and "derived identity" claims (abstract; §II D pp.2–3) are asserted, not shown.** The paper states that a "classical O(k⁰) super-Hubble solution, organized by the [L]/[K]/[X]/[S] shift decomposition, reproduces Eq. (4) exactly" and that "a final-time re-threading boundary term … corrects it to the in-in coefficient exactly," but no equation, intermediate result, or appendix appears — only script paths. Three specific problems: (a) the abstract calls this a "gradient-expansion route," while §II D itself says the gradient-expansion assumption N_i = O(∇) *fails* (shift is O(1/k_L)); the terminology is self-contradictory. (b) Eq. (5) is a *linear* identity; the paper concedes that at second order "no local f_NL relation between δN_c and ζ_Mald exists." A linear rescaling by 1/2 would map f_NL by a factor 2 if the map were local; the actual gap (−5 vs −15/8, ratio 8/3) is therefore *not* accounted for by Eq. (5) — Eq. (5) only shows the two calculations compute different variables. The abstract's "the gap to every separate-universe δN value is now a derived identity" overstates what is derived. (c) The general-ε results f_NL^ρ = 5(ε−7)/8 and f_NL^c = −5 "for all ε", and the Bianchi-I zero-monopole statement, are also given without derivation. **Resolve by** adding an appendix with the classical super-Hubble solution, the per-vertex [X]/[K]/[L] bookkeeping showing the 1/k_L pole cancellation and the boundary term, the derivation of Eq. (5) (including its sign convention), and the δN^ρ/δN^c formulas; and by rewording the abstract to "the difference is traced to a change of variable" rather than "derived identity."

3. **Infrared slope physics is misstated (§IV A p.4, §IV D p.5).** §IV D says Ω_GW ∝ f² (γ = 3) "is the causality-limited infrared slope common to scalar-induced backgrounds of essentially any origin." The causality-limited universal IR scaling for a source of finite duration is Ω_GW ∝ f³ (Cai, Pi & Sasaki 2020, PRD 102, 083528; γ = 2), with f² ln²f for narrow-peak induced GWs. An f² tail without logarithm is not generic; it requires a specific broad/scale-invariant source (which a matter bounce may supply, per Ref. [8]). As written, the paper both claims γ = 3 as the bounce's signature (§IV A, abstract) and disclaims it as generic (§IV D), and the generic statement is wrong. This matters for the channel's interpretation: if the causal floor is γ = 2, then γ = 3 is more distinctive than the paper admits, but the derivation (why a bounce evades the f³ floor) must be shown or precisely cited. **Resolve by** correcting §IV D, citing the f³ result, and giving the specific mechanism/equation in Ref. [8] that yields f² for the matter bounce; state whether the slope holds across the full NANOGrav band or only asymptotically.

4. **The bounce-energy validity statement in §V C (p.7) is off by many orders of magnitude.** "kη_B ≲ 10⁻² — satisfied at every PBH mass scale considered here for any bounce energy above the BBN scale." With η_B ~ (a_B H_B)⁻¹ and radiation after the bounce, a_B H_B ∝ T_B; at T_B ~ 1 MeV, a_B H_B ~ 10⁴ Mpc⁻¹, so kη_B ≲ 10⁻² allows only k ≲ 10² Mpc⁻¹ — excluding *every* PBH scale (k ~ 10⁴–10¹⁵ Mpc⁻¹ for 10⁴ M_⊙ down to 10¹⁵ g). Covering 10¹⁵ g requires T_B ≳ 10¹⁰ GeV. **Resolve by** stating the actual condition (T_B or H_B as a function of the smallest PBH mass considered) and restricting the null claim accordingly, or by showing the null is insensitive to the transfer assumption (plausible given 7 decades, but must be shown).

## minor findings

1. **§II A/B (p.2), Eqs. (2)–(4):** Eq. (2) defines the isosceles limit k₂ = k₃; but with k₁ = −(k₂+k₃) and |k₂| = |k₃|, the squeezed limit forces μ = k̂₁·k̂ = −k₁/(2k) → 0. A general-μ squeezed limit (Eq. 4) requires relaxing k₂ = k₃ at O(k₁). State the configuration used for Eq. (4) explicitly, and state that Eq. (3) is the μ = 0 value (it is *not* the angular average −15/8).
2. **§III (p.3), Eq. (6) and abstract:** with ρ ∈ (0, 1], T = (1−ρ)/2 ∈ [0, 1/2), not "0 < T ≤ 1/2". Either the ρ interval or the T interval is misstated; fix both places.
3. **§III A (p.4), Eq. (7):** ρ_B is never defined. From the table it equals ρ(η_B) = 1 − 2T (verified: 0.672, 0.499, 0.609), which makes f_NL^after = −(85/48)T − 5/24 a one-parameter function of T. Define ρ_B and state this relation — it is more informative than three numbers.
4. **§III A (p.4):** "at 28–39% of the transmitted contraction term" is arithmetically wrong. From the table: 0.140/0.361 = 39%, 0.127/0.429 = 30%, 0.104/0.547 = **19%**. Verified against `research/cubic_bounce_transmission/A2_TRANSMISSION_BRIEF_2026-09-02.md` §8.2 (0.10431/0.546875 = 0.191), which carries the same error. Should read 19–39%.
5. **§V C (p.7):** n_s − 1 = 12w/(1+w) is incorrect; for a contracting phase with a ∝ η^{2/(1+3w)} the standard result (Wands 1999; Cai et al. 2009) is n_s − 1 = 12w/(1+3w). Numerically negligible at w ≈ −0.003 but a formula error in a paper about exactness. The same formula appears in `research/track_a3_multichannel/inlab_delta2_zeta_2026-09-03.py` line 24 (checked for this item only).
6. **§V B (p.7), Eq. (12) and footnote 1:** the footnote admits the printed Ω_DM = 0.674 is actually h. Print the correct Ω_DM h² or Ω_DM in Eq. (12); the claim that "no number here changes" is not exact for the Gaussian calibration A_* = 0.131446 (f_PBH ∝ β/Ω_DM, so A_* shifts). Quote the recomputed A_* or the size of the shift.
7. **§V B (p.6–7):** γ_cr ≡ σ_cr²/(σ_cσ_r) — subscripts c, r are undefined; define the compaction and curvature variances and windows.
8. **Table III (p.7):** f_PBH = 3.5×10³ and 2.2×10⁸ are unphysical as abundances. Label the column as the nominal (uncapped) value at the Gaussian-calibrated amplitude or replace by β.
9. **Abstract and §V B:** "shape-robust required-amplitude ratio ≈ 1.7–1.9" is headlined without the perturbativity caveat that the paper itself gives (1.2|f_NL|σ_r up to 1.0 at −35/16 and 2.0 at −35/8; f_PBH non-monotonic by 55 decades along the continuity path). Robustness to lognormal shape is not robustness to truncation of the local ansatz; carry the caveat into the abstract and §VII A.
10. **§IV C (p.5):** five realizations with mean pulls +0.016σ and +0.033σ do not establish "unbiased at well under 0.1σ" without the scatter of the pulls; report the standard error of the mean pull.
11. **§IV B (p.4):** state the k-range over which |r| ≫ 1 holds (r = −9iA²I_∞/k³ is k-dependent) and give the parameters of the three bounce backgrounds ("poly (analytic non-LQC)" is undefined).
12. **Manuscript-history language** unsuitable for a submitted article: §II D title "…: closed" and "left open in earlier drafts of this work"; §IV C "This also supersedes an earlier, misdescribed claim in this section…"; §V A "why it had to be redone." Present results, not revision history; the first-pass Press–Schechter discussion can be reduced to one paragraph explaining why the truncated quadratic map fails.
13. **§II C (p.2):** the central bookkeeping claim — that Cai et al.'s printed shape function evaluated in the squeezed limit gives −35/16 while their Eqs. (38)–(40) print −35/8 — should be displayed (their polynomial, the limit, and the prefactor) rather than described. Also give equation numbers in Li et al. [4] and Quintin et al. [3] where −35/16 appears, and state whether Li et al. describe the factor as an error or a convention difference.
14. **Table I footnote:** "O(k²S²)" — S is undefined.
15. **Abstract:** ~380 words and dense with paper-internal labels ("scheme S1", "(A4)", "zero-shift-threading") that mean nothing to a reader before §III. Simplify.

## Questions to authors

1. Is Eq. (4) evaluated with k₂ ≠ k₃ at O(k₁)? What is Li et al.'s Eq. 4.19 configuration?
2. What is the sign convention behind δH/H = −(ε/3)ζ̇_L/H, and does δN_c = +½ζ or −½ζ at ε = 3/2? A direct integration ∫δH dt on the growing mode ζ ∝ t⁻¹ gives magnitude ½ with sign depending on this convention.
3. Why does the S1 transmission computation not apply at k = 0.05 Mpc⁻¹ if it applies at every PBH scale (MAJOR 1)?
4. What is the bounce temperature/energy implied by "kη_B ≲ 10⁻² at 10¹⁵ g" (MAJOR 4)?
5. For the Bianchi-I check: which shear response was used (long-mode induced anisotropy of the short-mode power spectrum via the ∂_i∂_jχ̃ vertex?) and why is a traceless response expected to have no monopole at second order in the short mode?
6. Does the "|f_NL| ≲ 60 perturbativity bound" attributed to Choudhury et al. refer to the same expansion parameter 1.2|f_NL|σ_r used here?

## Integrity note

All in-paper arithmetic that can be checked from printed inputs (vertex sums, angular averages, PTA z-scores, ESS, Savage–Dickey ratio, ζ_max values, amplitude ratios, DESI/SPHEREx significances, FIRAS fraction) reproduces, with one exception: the "28–39%" figure in §III A is wrong (19–39%). No number appears fabricated; several central results (§II D classical route, Bianchi-I, general-ε δN formulas, Eq. 7) rest solely on archived scripts and are unverifiable from the manuscript.
