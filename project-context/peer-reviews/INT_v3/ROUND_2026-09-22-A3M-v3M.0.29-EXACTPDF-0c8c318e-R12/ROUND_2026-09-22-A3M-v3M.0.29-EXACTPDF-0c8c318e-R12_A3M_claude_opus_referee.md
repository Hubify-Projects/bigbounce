## Summary

The paper makes one theoretical claim and then spends most of its length testing that claim's observational consequences. The theoretical claim is that the squeezed local non-Gaussianity of a dust-dominated contraction preceding a nonsingular bounce is $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local}=-35/16$, not the $-35/8$ printed by Cai *et al.* (2009). Sec. II supports this with a from-scratch, vertex-by-vertex in-in computation (Table I), a general-orientation extension $f^{\rm sq}(\mu)=-35/16+(15/16)\mu^2$ (Eq. 4), a localization of the factor-of-two discrepancy to the normalization step between Cai *et al.*'s printed shape function and their quoted amplitudes, and a second, classical $\delta N$/shift-decomposition route (Sec. II D, Appendix A) that reproduces Eq. (4) vertex by vertex.

Sec. III asks whether the amplitude survives the bounce. It introduces two continuation schemes across $\mathcal H=0$ — S1 ($z=a$, "dressed-metric") and S2 ("effective-fluid", raw-ADM) — and argues from the Bardeen potential, which carries no choice of $z$, that S2 is the physical one. On the Quintin-type background this gives the single number $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm after}=-1.25$; on two smooth-crossing backgrounds (LQC effective dust, "poly") the cubic-order term is not computed, and the result is reported only as a bracket, $[-1.20,-0.09]$ and $[-1.27,-0.13]$ (Table III). Sec. III A also computes the bounce's own cubic contribution in S1, $\Delta f_{\rm NL}^{\rm bounce}=-(5/24)\rho_B$ (Eq. 7).

Secs. IV–VI carry the transmitted amplitude through three observational channels — NANOGrav pulsar timing, primordial black hole abundance on the compaction-function criterion, and the DESI/SPHEREx LSS bispectrum. The verdict in three of four is a null, stated as such. Secs. VII–VIII then turn the argument against the background itself: the tensor transfer is identical to the S1 scalar transfer, so $r_{\rm after}=24$ (S1) or $10^2$–$10^3$ (S2), and reducing $c_s$ to suppress $r$ drives $f_{\rm NL}$ out of Planck's window, producing a joint $(r,f_{\rm NL})$ exclusion of the full $P(X)$ class at constant $c_s$, $\lambda$-independently. A curvaton spectator survives but dilutes the intrinsic amplitude below detectability.

The manuscript is unusually disciplined about evidential grading: it distinguishes reproduced from new from cited, it labels superseded rows as superseded, it declines to quote $f_{\rm PBH}$ as a constraint, it declines to report a Bayes factor at $\gamma=5$ because the KDE would be extrapolating, it prints a footnote conceding that a quantity it inherits from Choudhury *et al.* is numerically $h$ rather than $\Omega_{\rm DM}$, and it states four limitations in Sec. IX B that it does not answer. My criticisms below should be read against that background: the failures I found are internal-consistency and notation failures concentrated in Sec. III A and Sec. V B, not fabrications, and my recomputation rate on this manuscript was higher than I typically achieve.

### Findings

**1. [ESSENTIAL] — Sec. V B / Eq. (12) / Table V / abstract, pp. 11–13.** The abstract's Channel-II headline, $A(-35/16)/A(-35/8)=1.84\pm0.03$, is measured on 144 grid points restricted to $\gamma_{\rm cr}\in[0.267,0.630]$. That window lies entirely inside the region the manuscript itself declares uncontrolled, in three separate places:

- Table V's caption (p.12) calls "the $\gamma_{\rm cr}\lesssim0.8$ rows" the "**non-perturbative branch**; $1.2|f_{\rm NL}|\sigma_r\approx0.5$–$2$, i.e. $O(\epsilon)\sim1$ where the linearized criterion above is not controlled, IR-cutoff-dependent per the text."
- The main text (p.12) states that below $\gamma_{\rm cr}\approx0.85$ the calculation's "apparent enhancement ... is an IR-divergent $O(\epsilon^2)$ artefact of the quadratic compaction map's saddle expansion ... **not a physical effect**", and that in $0.408\lesssim\gamma_{\rm cr}\lesssim0.85$ "higher orders in $\epsilon$ are not controlled."
- The *mechanism* offered for the sign of the result (p.11) explicitly requires the opposite regime: "because the correlation $\gamma_{\rm cr}$ ... is **large and positive (0.766–0.968)** at these spectrum shapes: the Gaussian-case formation channel rides a correlated ridge that negative $f_{\rm NL}$ destroys." At $\gamma_{\rm cr}\in[0.267,0.630]$ that justification does not apply, and the manuscript offers no substitute.

Compounding this, **Table V displays no row from the headline window**: its five printed rows have $\gamma_{\rm cr}=0.897,0.968,0.888,0.766,0.857$, and its own summary line is $1.732\pm0.050$, $n=27$ — which differs from the headline by $3.6$ times the headline's quoted standard deviation. Fig. 2 is likewise at $(\Delta,r_pk_p,C_{\rm th})=(0.5,1.0,0.5)$, i.e. $\gamma_{\rm cr}=0.888$, again out of coverage. The perturbativity diagnostic $1.2|f_{\rm NL}|\sigma_r\approx0.54$–$1.01$ is reported "across the grid", i.e. the 27-point out-of-coverage grid; no perturbativity number is reported for the 144 in-coverage points, and the caption's own trend implies it would be worse there.

The net effect is that the one number the abstract advertises for this channel cannot be checked against any displayed datum, and every statement the manuscript makes about the validity of the calculation points away from the regime in which that number was measured. Either (a) demonstrate that Eq. (12)'s 144 points are computed after the $O(\epsilon^2)$ artefact is removed and that perturbativity holds there — with the in-coverage perturbativity numbers printed — and add in-coverage rows to Table V; or (b) withdraw $1.84\pm0.03$ from the abstract in favour of a number that is in a stated controlled regime. As it stands, the sentence "This ratio is the channel's most reproducible output" is not supported by anything the referee can inspect.

**2. [ESSENTIAL] — Sec. III p.5 vs Table III p.6.** On the LQC background the manuscript reports two different, mutually inconsistent non-S1 linear transfers and never reconciles them:

- p.5: "The upper value, 0.409, is the LQC background under the effective-fluid convention (**scheme S2**) ... (the linear S2 transfer on LQC exists, $T=0.409$)."
- Table III, row 5: "LQC effective dust (**Bardeen**) $T_{f_{\rm NL}}=0.500$", from which the abstract's $-1.094$ and the bracket anchor $[-1.20,-0.09]$ are computed ($-2.1875\times0.500=-1.09375$).

The whole point of Sec. III A is that "a scheme-independent Bardeen-potential argument ... **selects S2**." If the Bardeen argument selects S2, the LQC Bardeen transfer should *be* the LQC S2 transfer, i.e. $0.409$, not $0.500$; if it selects something else on LQC, the manuscript must say what. Three further internal signals show this is not a typographical slip:

- Table III's caption excludes $0.409$ because "S2 has been carried through cubic order only on the Quintin-type background" — but row 5 has an empty $\Delta f^{\rm bounce}_{\rm NL}$ column, so the stated exclusion criterion applies equally to the row that *was* kept.
- p.7 says S1 "understates $|f^{\rm after}_{\rm NL}|$ ... by ... $2.00\times$ (LQC)", i.e. $1/R=1/(1/2)$, confirming that $0.500$ (not $0.409$) is what the Bardeen construction returns.
- Consequently the manuscript's own quoted measure of scheme systematic on LQC — "a $1.64\times$ spread between two mode-function conventions (geometric vs. effective-fluid) on the same LQC background ($0.409/0.250$)" — is *smaller* than the $2.00\times$ spread between the two LQC rows it actually tabulates.

Using $T=0.409$ instead would give $f^{\rm after,lin}_{\rm NL}=-0.895$ rather than $-1.094$, changing an abstract number. This must be resolved explicitly.

**3. [MAJOR] — Sec. III A, pp. 6–7: "$\rho+p$" denotes two different quantities, one of which vanishes at the crossing and one of which is explicitly stated not to.** The selection argument runs:

- p.6: "at a simple zero of $\dot H$ (**the NEC crossing $\rho+p=0$**, where $z^2_{S2}$ vanishes and $\zeta$ is logarithmically divergent)".
- p.7: "unlike the Quintin-type background – where $\dot H$ jumps and no $\rho+p=0$ crossing exists – **both cross $\rho+p=0$ smoothly**: at the crossing $\Phi$ and $\Phi'$ stay continuous..."
- p.7, two columns later: "the crossing is where the quantum-geometry factor $(1-2x)$ vanishes, **not** where the matter kinetic term does (**the dust source has $\rho+p=x\neq0$ there**)".

The third statement contradicts the literal content of the first two on exactly the backgrounds they are about. It is recoverable — the first two use $\rho+p$ as a synonym for $-2\dot H$ (the GR identity), the third uses the matter $\rho+p$, and in LQC $-2\dot H=(\rho+p)(1-2x)$ so the two part company — but the entire LQC "prescription-free anchor," and hence the abstract's $-1.094$, turns precisely on which of those two weights carries the zero. A reader cannot audit the argument while the same symbol string carries both meanings within one subsection.

Two aggravating factors in the same subsection:

- **$x$ is never defined.** It appears at p.7 in "$\rho+p=x$", "$(1-2x)$", "$-2\dot H=x(1-2x)$", and "the quantum-geometry factor $(1-2x)$ vanishes". One can back it out of "$H^2=(\rho/3)(1-\rho/\rho_c)$" as $x=\rho/\rho_c$ in units of $\rho_c$, and the structural relation $-2\dot H=x(1-2x)$ then checks out — but the reader should not have to reverse-engineer the symbol on which the continuation argument rests.
- **$\rho$ itself is overloaded inside Sec. III A.** Eq. (6) defines $\rho(\eta_h)\equiv|J(-\eta_h)|/I_\infty\in(0,1]$, the mode-mixing fraction, and Eq. (7) uses $\rho_B\equiv\rho(\eta_B)=1-2T_{f_{\rm NL}}$. The same subsection then writes $H^2=(\rho/3)(1-\rho/\rho_c)$, $\rho+p$, $\rho/\rho_c$ for the energy density. Because $\rho_c$ is standard LQC notation for the critical density and the bounce occurs at $\rho=\rho_c$, a reader meeting "$\rho_B$" and "$\rho_c$" in adjacent paragraphs will read $\rho_B$ as the bounce density. $\rho_B$ appears in Eqs. (7) and (17) — the manuscript's two new closed-form results — and in Table VII's construction. Please rename one of the two.

**4. [MAJOR] — Sec. V B / abstract, p.12 caption vs Eq. (12).** Related to Finding 1 but separately actionable: the Table V caption asserts "The abstract's $1.84\pm0.03$ is therefore a shape-resolved scan result, not a union of a scan and an out-of-coverage point." That defends the number against one objection while leaving the load-bearing one unaddressed — that the *displayed* scan and the *quoted* scan occupy disjoint $\gamma_{\rm cr}$ intervals ($[0.766,0.968]$ vs $[0.267,0.630]$) and give $1.732\pm0.050$ vs $1.84\pm0.03$. The $-0.20$/unit-$\gamma_{\rm cr}$ OLS slope quoted on p.12 does reconcile them to within $\sim0.5$ std ($1.84-0.20\times0.418=1.756$ vs $1.732$), which is reassuring and should be stated as the reconciliation — but the paper currently leaves the reader to do that arithmetic.

**5. [MINOR] — Sec. III, p.4, and Table III.** The bound "$0\le T_{f_{\rm NL}}<1/2$" is disclaimed for exactly one background: "on the Quintin-type background scheme S1 is replaced by the scheme-independent Bardeen-potential argument ... it should therefore not be read as this paper's transfer bound on **that background**." But under the selected Bardeen continuation the transfers are $0.500$ (LQC, at the excluded endpoint) and $0.521$ (poly, outside it) as well as $1.03$ (Quintin-type). The disclaimer should cover all three, or the section should say that $[0,1/2)$ is a statement about a scheme that is superseded everywhere.

**6. [MINOR] — Sec. III A, p.8.** "Quintin *et al.*'s reported bispectrum growth ... is not contradicted: their growth is the additive bounce-generated term, the counterpart of Eq. (7), **sharing its sign (both enhance $|f_{\rm NL}|$)**." This is true only in scheme S1. Under the selected S2 continuation on the same (Quintin-type) background, Table III's net cubic contribution is $+1.00$, which takes $|f^{\rm after}|$ from $2.25$ down to $1.25$ — it *suppresses* $|f_{\rm NL}|$ by 44%. The paragraph carries no scheme qualifier, and it is placed *after* the S2 result is established (the very next sentence announces $f^{\rm after}_{\rm NL}=-1.25$). Note that the analogous sentence on p.6 *is* correctly qualified ("(S1, all three backgrounds; the Quintin-type entry replaced by the S2 result below)"), so the omission here looks accidental. The structural part of the reconciliation (additive vs multiplicative terms) survives; the sign clause does not.

**7. [MINOR] — Table II, p.5, and Sec. III, p.5.** Table II tabulates $|f^{\rm bounce}_{\rm NL}|$ but not the "ABS literature plateau" against which the deficits are quoted. Consequently none of the caption's dex figures can be checked: the implied plateau is $\approx5.7\times10^3$ at $k\eta_B=0.1$ (to give $4.41$ dex from $0.22$), $\approx4.1\times10^3$ at $0.3$, and $\approx1.1\times10^3$ at $1$ — i.e. the comparator is itself $k$-dependent and undisclosed, while the text describes it as "the literature's order-$10^3$ plateau". **10. [MINOR] — Sec. III A, p.5; Sec. VIII, Eq. (17); Sec. IX B(a).** "$\epsilon_{\rm eff}=1/2$" is used four times and never defined. It is presumably fixed by $z=a\sqrt{2\epsilon_{\rm eff}}/c_s=a$, but the reader must guess, and the guess has to survive the fact that $\epsilon\equiv1-\mathcal H'/\mathcal H^2=3/2$ is the background value used everywhere else in the paper. More substantively: Eqs. ### Overall assessment

This is a substantially stronger manuscript than its length and breadth would normally predict. I attempted roughly forty independent checks and the great majority reproduced exactly — including several that are not easy to fake: the full vertex sum of Table I, the $\epsilon$-general identity in Appendix A 2 under both worldline labels, the closure of Eq. (7) against all three S1 rows of Table III through $\rho_B=1-2T_{f_{\rm NL}}$, the correctly non-multiplicative propagation of the $-35/8$ candidate through Table VI, the derivation of $\Lambda$ for both the $P\propto X^n$ and DBI lines from the stated definition of $\lambda$, and three separate boundary reproductions of Table VII from Eqs. (16) and (17) to the printed precision. The treatment of NANOGrav's $\pm0.6$ as a 90% half-width, the refusal to quote a Bayes factor from an unsampled tail, the footnote conceding that an inherited $\Omega_{\rm DM}$ is numerically $h$, and the explicit statement that the predicted local amplitude is *positive* across the entire Planck-viable sound-speed window — contradicting the paper's own flagship sign — are all marks of a careful author who is not steering the result.

Against that, two problems are serious enough to block acceptance in the present form.

The first is Channel II. The abstract's headline number for that channel is measured entirely inside a $\gamma_{\rm cr}$ window that the paper's own table caption labels the non-perturbative branch, that its own text says is dominated by an IR-divergent artefact, and for which the physical mechanism it offers (large, positive $\gamma_{\rm cr}$) explicitly does not hold — while every displayed row, and the figure, come from a disjoint window giving a different answer. That is not a presentational quibble: it is an unresolved question about whether the number means anything, and no reader can settle it from the manuscript.

The second is Sec. III A. The section carries the paper's most consequential new result — the selection of a continuation and hence the single value $-1.25$ and the two brackets — and it is the section whose notation most obstructs audit: $\rho+p$ used for two quantities, one of which vanishes at the crossing and one of which the paper says does not; $\rho$ used for both a mode-mixing fraction and an energy density; $x$ undefined; and two irreconcilable LQC linear transfers ($0.409$ and $0.500$), one of which feeds an abstract number. Finding 2 in particular has to be answered with a number, not a clarification.

None of these findings suggest fabrication or a broken calculation, and none of them look expensive to fix: Finding 1 needs the in-coverage perturbativity diagnostics printed and in-coverage rows added to Table V (or the abstract number withdrawn); Finding 2 needs one paragraph saying which LQC transfer is the Bardeen continuation's and why; Finding 3 and the notation findings need a symbol pass. On the physics proper, I found no error that survived checking. The $(r,f_{\rm NL})$ no-go of Sec. VIII in particular is, so far as I can verify it, correct, quantitatively reproducible, and honestly bounded — including the manuscript's own observation that the tree-level calculation expires long before the tensor-viable sound speed is reached, which it reports against itself rather than burying.

I recommend major revisions, and I expect to recommend acceptance on a resubmission that resolves Findings 1–3 and cleans up the notation.
