## CENTRAL CLAIM

The paper makes three linked claims: (i) a from-scratch in-in computation confirms the matter-contraction squeezed amplitude $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local}=-35/16$ and localizes the factor-of-two disagreement with Cai *et al.*; (ii) transmission through three explicit nonsingular bounces gives a post-bounce amplitude, $-1.25$ on the Quintin-type background under a Bardeen-selected continuation and brackets on the other two; (iii) the single-field matter bounce at constant sound speed for the full $P(X)$ class is jointly excluded by $r$ and $f_{\rm NL}$, with three observational channels (PTA, PBH, LSS) returning honest nulls. Claim (i) is well supported and, so far as the PDF permits checking, internally airtight: Table I's five vertex entries sum exactly to $-35/16+\tfrac{15}{16}\mu^2$; the angular average $\langle\mu^2\rangle=1/3$ gives the stated monopole $-15/8$ and Legendre $\ell=2$ coefficient $5/8$; Eq. (15) at $\Lambda=0,c_s\to1$ and Eq. (16) at $c_s=1$ both return $-35/16$; and substituting $\Lambda=(1-c_s^2)/(6c_s^2)$ into Eq. (15) reproduces Eq. (16) identically. Claim (iii)'s arithmetic also survives independent recomputation -- I reproduced the Planck 95% and 68% $c_s$ edges (0.525, 0.624) to three digits from Eqs. (16)+(17) with $T_{f_{\rm NL}}=0.165$, $\rho_B=0.670$, obtaining 9.311 against the stated 9.3 and 4.194 against 4.2 -- so the exclusion is real as computed. **But the paper's framing of claim (iii) overstates what it demonstrates.** By the manuscript's own Sec. VIII, "the tensor channel alone closes the window over the entire range in which the non-Gaussian calculation is meaningful," and in the only region where $r$ is viable ($c_s<1.5\times10^{-3}$) the manuscript explicitly disowns its own $f_{\rm NL}$ number as "reported here only as the formal continuation ... not as a prediction," 6.9 decades past its own perturbativity criterion. A no-go whose $f_{\rm NL}$ leg is redundant where it is controlled and uncontrolled where it would be decisive is not a *joint* $(r,f_{\rm NL})$ no-go in the sense the title and abstract assert. Separately, the $f_{\rm NL}$ leg is compared against Planck's **local-template** bound with an implicit shape overlap $r_{\rm sh}=1$, while the paper's own Sec. VI insists that $r_{\rm sh}<1$ and that an experiment quoting $\sigma_{\rm local}$ constrains this model at $\sigma_{\rm local}/r_{\rm sh}$ -- the correct treatment is stated in one section and not applied in another. Claim (ii) is the weakest: on two of three backgrounds the post-bounce amplitude is not a number but a bracket spanning a factor of 10-13, which contradicts the sentence introducing Table III. None of these is fatal -- my spot-checks suggest the exclusion survives a factor-2 shape penalty ($350\times\to\approx300\times$) and survives dropping Eq. (17) in direction if not in margin -- but each is a claim the manuscript asserts more strongly than it proves, and several are internal contradictions rather than disclosed limitations.

**Covariance, likelihood, and null construction.** Channel I's likelihood is a re-use of NANOGrav's published per-bin KDE grids with a two-parameter power law over 30 bins; the marginal is explicitly non-Gaussian (skewness $-1.10$, standard deviation 0.382 exceeding the 68% half-width 0.289) and prior-bounded, yet Table IV's refit column reports Gaussian $z$-distances to three significant figures. The paper flags this but keeps the numbers. The injection test is, by the paper's own account, a bias test only: four of five realizations per injection are bootstrap bin-resamples of one re-centred dataset, so the bin-to-bin scatter of a real observation is never regenerated, and the recovered widths (0.16-0.17) are $2.4\times$ narrower than the real-data 0.382. No coverage test exists (Minor 9). Channel III uses EZmock covariance re-used across configurations with an ad hoc $\sqrt2$ inflation; **the number of mocks is never stated and no Hartlap or Percival correction is mentioned** for a 46-dof QSO fit or the three-bin LRG fit, and the three redshift bins are combined as independent because no cross-bin covariance product exists. The manuscript labels these errors "optimistic" -- correctly, and the direction matters: optimistic errors only strengthen the "DESI DR1 cannot distinguish the candidates" conclusion, so this gap does not threaten the claim, and the report should say so. **Multiplicity is nowhere addressed**: the 15-row systematics table, the 255/144/62-point PBH scans, the 27-point fiducial grid, the 12-point evaluation-time scan, and the three-background $\times$ two-scheme transmission grid carry no trials treatment. Because no detection is claimed anywhere, this is mostly a presentation matter -- with one exception: the Channel II headline (Major 5) is a *data-dependent selection* on a variable correlated with the observable at $+0.83$, which is a genuine multiplicity/selection-bias problem, not a cosmetic one. Nulls are, in each channel, correctly constructed and honestly labelled: Channel I is a null in both amplitude ($10^{15.2}$) and slope ($5.1\sigma$); Channel II returns $f_{\rm PBH}=0$ in double precision against a 6.7-7.0 decade amplitude shortfall; Channel III is a non-discriminating consistency ($0.24\sigma$ discriminating power). The manuscript deserves credit for refusing to convert any of these into a "supporting consistency."
