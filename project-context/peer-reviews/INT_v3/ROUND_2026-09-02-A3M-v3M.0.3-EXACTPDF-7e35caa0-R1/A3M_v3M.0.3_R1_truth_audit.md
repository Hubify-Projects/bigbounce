## 6. Science / scope decisions required, with my recommendation

### Decision 1 — PTA channel: what is the paper's γ estimate?

**Recommendation: adopt the official NANOGrav posterior as the primary comparison, demote the lab refit to a secondary analysis, and delete the γ = 5 Bayes factor.**

Reasons. (i) The official γ_HD = 3.2⁺⁰·⁶₋₀.₆ on the lowest 14 frequencies is the published, refereed, collaboration-calibrated result on this dataset; a 30-bin free-spectrum refit by a third party is a legitimate *cross-check* but cannot silently replace it — and the omission is what makes the current text indefensible, more than the number itself. (ii) The refit is **not** discredited: the lab's own synthetic injection recovers γ = 3.19 ± 0.42 with the identical 30-bin pipeline (V6), so the pipeline is approximately unbiased and the refit is worth keeping; it sits only 1.20σ from official. Reporting both, with the difference stated, is stronger than either alone. (iii) The γ = 5 row must go from the abstract regardless: with **zero** samples in the region, no amount of framing makes a KDE value there a measurement. An upper bound plus a Gaussian-approximate z, both labelled, is honest and loses nothing — the primordial-tensor shape was never the paper's target. (iv) A 14-bin refit is a ~25 s run and would let the paper show the two selections side by side; I recommend it as a cheap strengthener, but the decision above closes the finding without it.

Consequence for the claim: "rejecting the two competing spectral shapes" becomes *"the matter-bounce slope γ = 3 is consistent with both the official NANOGrav posterior (0.55σ) and our free-spectrum refit (1.14σ); the SMBHB value 13/3 is in moderate tension with the official posterior (≈3.1σ; NANOGrav place it at the 99 % credible boundary) and more strongly disfavoured by the refit; the primordial-tensor value γ = 5 has no posterior support."*

### Decision 2 — Transmission: is T = (1−ρ)/2 a bound?

**Recommendation: relabel it a handoff-scheme bound with the freezing assumption explicit; drop the word "universal"; keep the calculation and the three-background spread.**

Reasons. (i) The arithmetic is right — Fable re-derived Eq. (5) from the stated premise and I found no error; there is nothing to retract. (ii) But "universal" is doing work the derivation cannot support in two independent ways: the bound is universal only *over ρ within a fixed scheme*, while the computed T spans 0.165–0.409 across schemes and handoffs (a 2.48× spread the paper itself prints two lines later); and the freezing premise is in tension with the end-time independence §II proves. A reader meeting "universal suppression" in the abstract will take away something the paper does not have. (iii) Deleting one adjective and adding two sentences (assumption A4, and the reconciliation with §II) costs nothing scientifically and removes the strongest overclaim objection any referee raised. (iv) I explicitly **reject** the alternative of computing the cubic term now: that is item A3-2-class new physics, weeks of work, and directive R2 says the round should not be held hostage to it — the paper's job is to state its scope correctly, which it can do today.

Consequence: the channel's honest claim is *"linear transfer through the bounce, in a scheme that freezes the contraction-phase bispectrum at the NEC boundary, rescales f_NL by (1−ρ)/2 ≤ 1/2 (scheme-dependent, 0.165–0.409 across three backgrounds and two conventions). The bounce's own cubic self-interaction is not computed and can dominate; no bound on the physical post-bounce f_NL follows."*

### Decision 3 — PBH: is the 1.73 ratio a result or an illustration?

**Recommendation: keep the ratio as a result, but state its regime of validity and disclose the non-monotonicity — do not demote it to an illustration.**

Reasons. (i) The ratio is genuinely the channel's most stable output: it reproduces exactly on my re-integration path, holds at every one of 27 grid points, and is robust to spectrum shape (std 0.050 on 1.732). Demoting it to "illustrative only", as Grok asks, would discard a real, reproducible finding to satisfy a framing objection — that is watering down science, which directives L and M forbid. (ii) But Grok and Fable are both right that it is currently oversold: it is a ratio inside a quadratic local map evaluated at excursions where the paper's own diagnostic (1.2\|f_NL\|σ_r = 0.54–1.01 at −35/16, 1.09–2.02 at −35/8) says the map is uncontrolled, and the ordering it reports is the *opposite* of the perturbative literature for a reason (anti-correlated-branch dominance) the paper never gives. A referee who works out the mechanism unaided will conclude it was hidden. (iii) The non-monotonic scan is already committed; publishing it is one sentence or one table row and converts the channel's weakest-looking claim into its most transparent one. (iv) The abstract's "the one robust output" must inherit the shape-only scope it already carries in §V B.

Consequence: *"Within the quadratic local map — at curvature excursions where that truncation is not perturbatively controlled — reaching a fixed f_PBH requires 1.732 [1.610, 1.809] times more curvature amplitude at −35/16 than at −35/8, at every point of a 27-point grid. f_PBH(f_NL) at fixed amplitude is strongly non-monotonic (a ~55-decade minimum near f_NL ≈ −0.35); both candidates lie on the rising branch, where formation is dominated by the anti-correlated ζ_G < 0 region. The ratio is robust to spectrum shape; it is not robust to the truncation, and f_PBH itself is not quotable."*

### What the joint statement can honestly claim after all three decisions

*"The matter-bounce prediction f_NL^local = −35/16 (independently re-derived here within the in-in formalism, confirming Li et al. 2017 and localising the factor-of-two slip in Cai et al. 2009; a method-independent confirmation remains open) is consistent with every channel currently able to speak. Pulsar timing is consistent with the associated induced-GW slope γ = 3 under both the official NANOGrav posterior and our free-spectrum refit, but is insensitive to the value of f_NL and therefore does not test it. Primordial black holes yield a reproducible amplitude ratio between the two candidate values but no quotable abundance, and only within a truncated map at excursions where that map is uncontrolled. Transmission through the bounce is bounded only within a handoff scheme that freezes the contraction-phase bispectrum; the bounce's own cubic term is uncomputed and may dominate. Galaxy surveys are the only channel that both discriminates between −35/16 and −35/8 and depends on the value: DESI DR1 is consistent at 0.16σ, and SPHEREx reaches 2.6–3.7σ once a shape-overlap projection is applied — a projection this paper must either derive or drop."*

That statement is weaker than the current abstract in exactly three places, and every one of the three is a place where the current text asserts more than the committed artifacts support. It remains a publishable multi-channel consistency paper: the theory result is the contribution, and the honesty of the channel-by-channel grading is a strength, not a concession.
