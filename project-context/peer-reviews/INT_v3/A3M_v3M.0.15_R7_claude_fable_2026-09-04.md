## Summary (5 lines)
1. The paper re-derives the matter-contraction squeezed amplitude f_NL = -35/16 vertex-by-vertex, locates a factor of two in Cai et al. 2009, reports a scheme-qualified transmitted amplitude (S1: [-0.65,-0.50]; S2: ~ -1.25), derives r = 16 eps = 24 for the same background, builds a joint (r, f_NL) no-go in c_s, and sketches a curvaton escape; PTA/PBH/SMBH-seed channels are nulls and DESI DR1 is reproduced.
2. Arithmetic I could check independently is sound: Table I sums, Eq. (4) angular average, Eqs. (A2)-(A3) at eps = 3/2, Eq. (7)/(6) -> Table III, Table VI/VII numbers, Eq. (11) values, the sigma-distances in Table IV, the c_s window (cs = 1.5e-3, 0.444, ratio 296) from row14_cs_window.py, and the curvaton r and f_NL^curv formulas (Eq. 16) all reproduce.
3. My own sympy check of Cai et al. 2009 (arXiv:0903.0631, fetched) confirms the factor of two but NOT the paper's description of it: Cai's own Eqs. (34)-(36) summed with their own Eq. (21) [f_NL = (10/3) A / sum k^3, not (20/3) as the paper states] give -35/16, -255/128, -9/8, exactly half their quoted (38)-(41); their printed Eq. (37) is NOT identical to Li et al. Eq. (4.19) (it differs by -(99/128) sum k_i^3 under the permutation reading of the triple sums), contradicting the paper's "difference zero, checked symbolically".
4. Two scope problems: r = 24 "exactly, bounce-invariant" and "tensor transfer = scalar transfer" are scheme-S1 (z = a) statements by construction; in the paper's own S2 (|lambda_zeta| = 0.97 vs tensor 6.06) r_after would be ~ 24 x (6.06/0.97)^2 ~ 9e2, so the "bounce-invariant" wording and the dismissal of Quintin et al.'s scalar-only amplification are not scheme-independent as presented (the no-go itself survives, a fortiori).
5. The PBH section's headline "sign resolved: negative f_NL suppresses" coexists with a Table V row where f_PBH(-35/16) = 3.5e3 and f_PBH(-35/8) = 2.2e8 at the "Gaussian-calibrated" amplitude (Gaussian = 1), and with a ratio whose direction (more negative f_NL forms PBHs more easily) lives on the branch the paper itself flags as perturbatively uncontrolled (1.2|f_NL| sigma_r ~ 0.5-2). Reference [25] is wrong (arXiv:0912.2951 is an unrelated X-ray-binary paper; the curvaton paper is Cai, Brandenberger & Zhang, JCAP 1103:003, arXiv:1101.0822).

## MAJOR findings
**M1 (p.2-3, Sec. II C; abstract) - The Cai et al. factor-of-two localisation is mis-stated and partly not reproducible.**
Issue: (a) The paper writes Cai's conversion as f_NL = (20/3) A / sum k^3; Cai et al.'s printed Eq. (21) is |B|_NL = (10/3) A / sum k_i^3, identical to this paper's Eq. (2). (b) The paper asserts Cai's Eq. (37) "agrees with Li et al.'s Eq. 4.19 identically (difference zero, checked symbolically)". Transcribing Cai's Eq. (37) as printed (3/(256 prod k^2) {3 sum k^9 + sum k_i^7 k_j^2 - 9 sum k_i^6 k_j^3 + 5 sum k_i^5 k_j^4 - 66 sum_{i!=j!=k} k_i^5 k_j^2 k_k^2 + 9 sum k_i^4 k_j^3 k_k^2}) and Li's (4.19) at c_s = 1 (transcribed from the paper's own row14_cs_window.py) gives a difference of -(99/128) sum k_i^3 with full-permutation triple sums, and a non-symmetric remainder with the j<k reading; Cai's (37) squeezed limit is then -305/64, not -35/16. What DOES hold (verified by me with sympy): Cai's own per-order Eqs. (34)+(35)+(36) at eps = 3/2 equal Li's (4.19) at c_s = 1 exactly, and with Cai's own Eq. (21) give -35/16, -255/128, -9/8 - exactly half of Cai's quoted (38)-(41). So the factor of two sits between Cai's own (34)-(36)+(21) and their quoted numbers, with their printed (37) an additional (separate) transcription inconsistency.
Resolves: state the conversion formula as Cai actually print it; replace the "(37) = (4.19) identically" claim by the (34)-(36) sum identity (or state the triple-sum multiplicity convention under which (37) closes, if one exists, and show it); display both papers' definitions of A relative to <zeta zeta zeta> (Cai Eq. 19 uses (2pi)^7 P_zeta^2 with P_zeta defined via their Eq. 29's (2pi)^4 k^-3 convention) so a reader can see the factor is not a P_zeta normalisation convention. Commit the sympy check.

**M2 (Sec. VII, abstract) - "r = 16 eps = 24 exactly, bounce-invariant" and "tensor transfer = scalar transfer to 8e-5" are scheme-S1 statements presented as scheme-independent.**
Issue: In S1 the scalar MS variable uses z = a, so the scalar and tensor equations coincide by construction and the 8e-5 agreement is a numerical tautology, not a check (row14_cs_window.py integrates the scalar with gradient (c_s k)^2 and the same z). The paper's own S2 (Sec. III A) transmits the scalar with |lambda_zeta| = 0.97 while tensors (no scheme ambiguity, z_T = a) get lambda_T = 6.06 on Quintin-type, implying r_after ~ 24 (6.06/0.97)^2 ~ 9 x 10^2, not 24. The dismissal of Quintin et al.'s scalar-only amplification route (Sec. VIII: "lambda = 4.0-6.1 with no net r suppression") is likewise evaluated only in the scheme where scalar-only amplification is impossible by construction.
Resolves: qualify r = 24 as the pre-bounce value and give r_after per scheme (S1: 24; S2: quote the computed number); state explicitly that in S1 the equality is structural; re-evaluate the Quintin Eq. (31) test in S2 or say it cannot be done there. The no-go conclusion survives (S2 makes r worse) but the wording "exactly", "bounce-invariant", "no bounce in the class relieves it" must be scoped.

**M3 (Sec. V B, Table V, abstract "PBH with the sign resolved", ratio 1.84 +/- 0.03) - The PBH sign statement, the table, and the regime of validity are mutually inconsistent as written.**
Issue: (a) Text: "negative f_NL suppresses the compaction abundance throughout, and Choudhury et al. are right"; caption: f_PBH columns "evaluated at the Gaussian-calibrated amplitude A_* with no upper cutoff", where Gaussian gives f_PBH = 1 by construction. Row (0.80, 0.75, 0.6, gamma_cr = 0.766) reports f_PBH(-35/16) = 3.5e3 and f_PBH(-35/8) = 2.2e8, i.e. ENHANCEMENT by 3.5-8.3 decades relative to Gaussian (and unphysical f_PBH > 1). Either A_* is a single value from the representative point (then the columns are not Gaussian-calibrated per point and the "suppression" comparison is unavailable for that row), or the suppression claim is false at that point. The paper must say which. (b) The direction of the headline ratio - more negative f_NL forms PBHs more easily (f_PBH(-35/16) < f_PBH(-35/8) everywhere) - is the opposite of "negative f_NL suppresses" as a trend, and the paper attributes it to the anti-correlated (zeta_G < 0, J > 1) branch of the quadratic map where it concedes 1.2|f_NL| sigma_r ~ 0.5-2, i.e. exactly the truncated-quadratic-map regime Sec. V A dismisses as an artefact for the Press-Schechter map. An abstract-level number (1.84 +/- 0.03) resting on that branch needs either a non-perturbative check (full non-Gaussian PDF or a cubic-order map) or demotion to a stated-regime result outside the abstract.
Resolves: per-point Gaussian calibration in Table V (or a Gaussian column), cap f_PBH <= 1 or label the uncapped values as ratios only, reword "suppresses throughout" to "suppresses relative to the Gaussian case at fixed amplitude in the covered gamma_cr range, but grows with |f_NL| between the two candidates", and add one controlled-regime check of the ratio (e.g. at sigma_r small enough that 1.2|f_NL| sigma_r < 0.3) or remove the ratio from the abstract.

**M4 (Sec. VIII, Ref. [25], "-320/pi^4", "Eqs. (62)-(64)") - The curvaton literature reference is wrong.**
Issue: [25] is listed as Cai, Xue & Brandenberger, "Non-Gaussianity in a Matter Bounce", JCAP 05, 011 (2011), arXiv:0912.2951. arXiv:0912.2951 (fetched) is McBride et al., "The Magellanic Bridge: evidence for a population of X-ray binaries" (MNRAS). The matter-bounce curvaton paper is Cai, Brandenberger & Zhang, "The Matter Bounce Curvaton Scenario", JCAP 1103:003 (2011), arXiv:1101.0822 (fetched; authors and title confirmed). The body also calls it "Cai-Xue-Brandenberger's 2011 matter-bounce curvaton". The -320/pi^4 figure and the "Eqs. (62)-(64)" pointer therefore cannot be checked against the cited source; the paper itself says the equations "remain un-re-derived by this lab".
Resolves: correct the reference (authors, title, journal, arXiv id); verify the equation numbers and the -320/pi^4 quote against 1101.0822, or drop the quote. Also add bibliography entries for Lyth-Ungarelli-Wands and Sasaki-Valiviita-Wands (cited by name only) and for Planck 2018 f_NL (the 5.1 sigma used in Table VII).

**M5 (Sec. VIII, Table VII, abstract) - The c_s window silently drops the bounce term and hides a sign flip of the flagship prediction.**
Issue: Table VII's "|f_NL^after| <= 5.1 requires c_s >= 0.444" is computed (row14_cs_window.py, cs_for_fnl_after) as T x f_NL^pre(c_s) with NO Delta f_NL^bounce, while every other transmitted number in the paper is T f_pre + Delta; the Sec. III A cubic bounce term Eq. (7) was derived at c_s = 1 and is applied nowhere in the window. Numerically small at c_s ~ 0.44 (Delta = -0.14 vs 5.1), but the text asserts full c_s-independence of "the transmission", which was verified only for the LINEAR transfer. More importantly, the script records that f_NL^pre(c_s) = -165/16 + 65/(8 c_s^2) changes sign at c_s = 0.888: on the whole "Planck-viable" window c_s in [0.444, 0.888) the k-essence prediction is POSITIVE (up to +5.1). The paper's framing ("the matter-bounce prediction f_NL = -35/16", Sec. IX) never states that the constant-c_s k-essence branch reverses the sign for c_s < 0.89. Also "strengthened 3.8x" (abstract) is (0.0029/0.0015)^2, a ratio of implied f_NL^pre values, not a factor in c_s (1.9x) or r (1.9x); the metric must be named.
Resolves: state the window formula used (with or without Delta and why), add the sign-change c_s = 0.888 to Table VII/text, and define what "3.8x" measures.

## Questions for the authors
Q1. In S2, what is the tensor transfer through the Quintin-type bounce, and hence r_after? If tensors use z_T = a in both schemes, r_after(S2) ~ 9e2; please quote it.
Q2. Which triple-sum multiplicity convention makes Cai's printed Eq. (37) equal the sum of their (34)-(36)? If none, please say the printed (37) is itself inconsistent and base the localisation on (34)-(36)+(21).
Q3. Table V: is A_* a single number (0.131446, representative point) applied at all 27 grid points, or re-calibrated per point? The row with f_PBH = 3.5e3 / 2.2e8 needs the answer.
Q4. Does Delta f_NL^bounce[S1] (Eq. 7) have any c_s dependence? The cubic k-essence action carries (1/c_s^2 - 1) operators that dominate at c_s = 1.5e-3; the window at that end (6-9e5) would move by O(1/c_s^2) if the bounce term scales like the contraction term.
Q5. Why is the Planck bound applied as a 1 sigma half-width (5.1) rather than the 95% interval (~ +/-10.2, which the script also computes: c_s >= 0.336, r >= 8.1, gap 224x)? The no-go holds either way, but the abstract's 296x should be the 2 sigma number or both.
