**Reviewer persona:** Gemini-3.1-Pro, theoretical-physics framing (gauge issues, GR projection, model-class boundaries, EFT counting)
**Round:** R4 (prior round closed 7 BLOCKERs + 19 MAJORs)
**Scope:** what the previous reviewers missed on cosmology/theory.

## Summary

The convention chain (Cai vs. Li-Brandenberger), template-overlap, Bayes-factor accounting, and shot-noise caveats are now in good shape — that work is done. What remains under-resolved is the **theoretical framing layer**: gauge-frame vs. physical-frame f_NL is named but its observational consequence is finessed; the "Wilson-Ewing class" scope is asserted in prose but never sharply bounded against quintom / Cuscuton / ekpyrotic; the Mercuri/Freidel ECH decoupling claim hand-waves a non-trivial fermion sector; the QSFI boundary is mis-attributed to a "principal vs complementary series" cut that is dimension-dependent; and the ε-EFT counting (w = −0.003, but κ₁ ∈ [5.6, 80]) is internally inconsistent at the order it's quoted. The b_φ universality flag is now honest but the bispectrum-vs-SDB relativistic-projection cliff is **only flagged for MegaMapper**, not for the SPHEREx headline.

## Findings

### P2-GEM-B1 — BLOCKER — Gauge-frame ratio is featured; physical-frame consequence is sidelined

**Lines:** 29 (abstract), 38 (intro), 181 (§Bayesian), 385 (conclusion)
**Issue:** The paper repeatedly headlines `|f_NL^bounce|/|f_NL^inf,gauge| ≈ 290` and uses 0.015 as the inflation benchmark. The Pajer / Tanaka–Urakawa result is that the **physical** local f_NL in conformal-Fermi coordinates is zero (or O(slow-roll squared)) for single-field inflation. The abstract acknowledges this in a parenthetical but still leads with the 290× number. For a Bayes-factor paper, this matters: if the inflation prediction is **exactly zero** in the observer frame, the bounce-vs-inflation BF is qualitatively different (any detection at all rules out single-field inflation, not a 290× amplitude contrast). The current framing understates the discriminating power on one hand and inflates the apparent gauge-frame contrast on the other.
**Fix:** In the abstract and conclusion, lead with the physical-observer-frame statement ("single-field inflation predicts 0, bounce predicts −4.375") and demote the 290× gauge-frame ratio to a parenthetical compatibility note. Update §Bayesian competitor list: single-field slow-roll inflation (SSFSR) should be entered at f_NL = 0, not 0.015. The BF-vs-SSFSR column of Tables 2 and 3 quantitatively depends on this — recompute or justify why the gauge-frame value is the right input to the likelihood.

### P2-GEM-B2 — BLOCKER — "Mechanism-independent within ECH-compatible class" is over-stated; fermion sector is hand-waved

**Lines:** 42 (intro, last paragraph)
**Issue:** Line 42 asserts that in minimal Einstein-Cartan-Holst gravity "scalar perturbations reduce exactly to the standard Mukhanov-Sasaki sector" because "the Holst term becomes a topological invariant when torsion vanishes for canonical scalar field matter (Mercuri 2006; Freidel et al. 2005)." This is true **only for the pure-scalar sector with no fermion matter**. The Hehl-Datta–Mercuri torsion contact term is sourced by the fermion bilinear ⟨ψ̄γ⁵γᵃψ⟩²; the moment fermion matter is reinstated (which any realistic bounce must do post-bounce to populate the radiation era), torsion does not vanish, the Holst term is not topological, and the Barbero-Immirzi parameter γ_BI re-enters scalar observables through the four-fermion contact ΔN_eff contribution. The paper presents the decoupling as if it underwrites the "mechanism-independent" claim across the full ECH class. It does not — it underwrites it only in a fermion-free contracting phase.
**Fix:** Add a one-paragraph caveat in §II.D ("Mechanism Independence") explicitly stating: (i) the Mercuri/Freidel decoupling holds in the scalar-only sector; (ii) fermion-sourced torsion contributes at the bounce transition and through ΔN_eff; (iii) the f_NL = −35/8 prediction is robust because the **contracting-phase mode functions** are scalar-dominated, but the broader "ECH-compatible class" claim should be narrowed to "scalar-sector ECH-compatible." Cite the Hehl-Datta-Mercuri four-fermion structure explicitly.

### P2-GEM-M1 — MAJOR — Higuchi-bound attribution at μ/H = 3/2 is geometrically wrong

**Lines:** 369 (§Discussion, Joint forecast paragraph)
**Issue:** The paper writes: "at μ/H = 3/2 (the QSFI principal-vs-complementary-series boundary m² = 9H²/4, distinct from the Higuchi bound which applies to spin-2 fields with m² ≥ 2H²) Δ = 3/2 and the exponent vanishes." Two problems: (1) The principal-vs-complementary series cut in dS for **scalar** fields (spin-0) is at m² = (d−1)²H²/4 = 9H²/4 in d = 4 — that part is right. But the "distinct from the Higuchi bound" parenthetical is **wrong in direction**: the Higuchi bound for spin-2 in dS is m² ≥ 2H²(j(j+1) − ...) which evaluates to m² ≥ 2H² for spin-1 partially-massless modes and 0 for spin-2 graviton; for **massive spin-2** the Higuchi bound is m² ≥ 2H², not 2H². The paper conflates spin labels. (2) The bispectrum scaling exponent in QSFI is Δ_+ = 3/2 − √(9/4 − μ²/H²) for the complementary series (μ² < 9H²/4) and analytic continuation through μ² = 9H²/4 gives the oscillating principal series — at the boundary the shape is logarithmic, not flat-local, so the claim that "the exponent vanishes" and "reproduces the local-template flat scaling" is the asymptotic μ/H → 3/2⁻ limit, not the boundary value itself.
**Fix:** Rewrite the parenthetical: drop the Higuchi-bound comparison entirely (it's irrelevant for scalar QSFI) and replace with "the scalar dS principal-vs-complementary series boundary m² = 9H²/4 (Chen & Wang 2009; not to be confused with the spin-2 Higuchi bound m² ≥ 2H²)." Replace "Δ = 3/2 and the exponent vanishes" with "in the limit μ/H → 3/2⁻, Δ → 3/2 and the squeezed-limit scaling approaches the local-template flat behavior up to logarithmic corrections."

### P2-GEM-M2 — MAJOR — ε-EFT counting is internally inconsistent: w = −0.003 quoted with κ₁ ∈ [5.6, 80] is not a small correction

**Lines:** 100 (§Assumptions), 326–333 (§Consistency relation)
**Issue:** The paper takes w = −0.003 as the quasi-dust deviation (giving Δε = (3/2)|Δw| ≈ 4.5 × 10⁻³) and reports f_NL(ε) = −35/8 + κ₁(ε − 3/2) + O(...)². Line 327 says κ₁ ∈ [5.6, 80] (more than an order of magnitude). The "central" 1–8% correction this maps to ([−4.35, −4.02]) is computed assuming a moderate κ₁; the **upper-bound κ₁ = 80** would give |Δf_NL| ≈ 80 × 4.5×10⁻³ ≈ 0.36, which is half of σ(f_NL) = 0.7 and dominates the systematic budget. More fundamentally: if κ₁ can be as large as 80 because the mode-function Hankel index diverges near ε = 3/2, then the EFT expansion in (ε − 3/2) **is not under control** at the precision being claimed. You cannot quote σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.7 sensitivity against a prediction whose first-order EFT coefficient is uncertain by a factor of ~14.
**Fix:** Either (a) tighten κ₁: explain why the mode-function-amplitude contribution to κ₁ = 80 is **not** the dominant one for observable squeezed modes (i.e., show the kη_bounce ≪ 1 suppression argument from §Assumptions actually bounds κ₁ at ~10), or (b) widen the f_NL prediction uncertainty band: f_NL = −4.375 ± Δ with Δ ≈ κ₁ × |Δε| ≈ 0.05 (κ₁ = 12) to 0.36 (κ₁ = 80). The "1–8% correction" framing in the abstract should be replaced with the honest range that includes the κ₁ upper bound, and the Bayes-factor σ_theory = 0.5 row of Table 2 should be revisited — at κ₁ = 80, σ_theory = 0.5 is too narrow.

### P2-GEM-M3 — MAJOR — b_φ universality flagged for SPHEREx but the SDB-vs-bispectrum cross-coupling at z > 1 is not modeled

**Lines:** 265 (§Systematics, b_φ subsection), 369 (§Discussion, joint Fisher)
**Issue:** The b_φ universality discussion now correctly says the bispectrum has residual b_φ dependence through Δb(k) cross-terms (good — that closed an earlier round's finding). What's still missing: at z > 1 the **relativistic projection cliff** (Doppler + grav redshift + integrated Sachs-Wolfe + lensing) couples to the b_φ-dependent SDB term in a way that **does not factor out of the bispectrum estimator** the way it does for SDB-only. Jolicoeur et al. 2025 (cited at line 278) compute this for SDB; the bispectrum-channel propagation has not been done in the literature for SPHEREx. The paper claims the bispectrum channel is "less sensitive to b_φ than SDB" — which is true at z ≈ 0.5 — but at the high-z tail of the SPHEREx sample (z ≈ 2), the cross-coupling can be 10–20% of the bispectrum amplitude itself, on top of the 20–50% b_φ universality degradation.
**Fix:** Add a sentence to §Systematics.b_φ explicitly noting that the bispectrum-residual b_φ dependence is **z-dependent** and that at z > 1 it couples to relativistic projection at O(10–20%) of the bispectrum amplitude. Either degrade the post-systematic headline 3–5σ to 2.5–4.5σ to absorb this, or restrict the headline forecast to the z < 1 SPHEREx sub-sample (which loses survey volume but is theoretically cleaner).

### P2-GEM-M4 — MAJOR — Maldacena consistency relation cited but the bounce derivation isn't shown to satisfy it

**Lines:** 38 (intro), 181 (§Bayesian)
**Issue:** The paper invokes Maldacena's single-field consistency relation to set f_NL^inf,gauge ≈ 0.015 and uses this as the inflation baseline. Maldacena's theorem is **specifically a statement about single-field inflation**: in the squeezed limit, the bispectrum is determined by the power spectrum tilt regardless of inflaton potential. The bounce **does not** satisfy this consistency relation — and that's the whole point — but the paper never explicitly states this. A reader who knows Maldacena 2003 will wonder: does the matter bounce **violate** the consistency relation, or does it not apply? The answer is the latter (the consistency relation requires an attractor, slow-roll, and a single-field inflationary background; the bounce has none of these), but the paper should say so.
**Fix:** Add one sentence in §II.A or §V.A: "The Maldacena consistency relation assumes a single-field slow-roll inflationary attractor, which the matter bounce does not realize; the bounce's f_NL = −35/8 prediction is not in tension with the theorem because the theorem does not apply." This closes the obvious theoretical-physics-reviewer question.

### P2-GEM-M5 — MAJOR — "Wilson-Ewing class" scope is asserted but never bounded against quintom / Cuscuton / ekpyrotic

**Lines:** 42 (intro), 100 (§Assumptions, assumption e), 102–104 (§Viable Model)
**Issue:** The paper restricts the f_NL = −35/8 prediction to "the Wilson-Ewing class" (no prolonged post-bounce inflation) and acknowledges that bounce models with prolonged inflation erase the signal. Good. But the paper never bounds the class on the **other side**: which bounce models that **don't** have prolonged inflation also fail to give −35/8? Quintom-B bounces have a contracting phase that is **not** matter-dominated (the equation-of-state crosses w = −1) — so f_NL is not −35/8 there. Cuscuton bounces have w varying through the bounce. Ekpyrotic contracting phases have w ≫ 1 (stiff), not w ≈ 0 — they give a completely different f_NL spectrum. The "Wilson-Ewing class" is therefore narrower than just "no prolonged post-bounce inflation": it's specifically **matter-dominated contracting bounces without prolonged post-bounce inflation**. Without this clarification, a null result at SPHEREx falsifies far less than the paper implies.
**Fix:** In §II.D (Mechanism Independence) or §II.E (Assumptions), add a bounded model-class table or paragraph: "The f_NL = −35/8 prediction applies to matter-dominated contracting bounces (w ≈ 0 during the relevant mode-exit epoch) with no prolonged post-bounce inflation. Models with stiff contracting phases (ekpyrotic, w ≫ 1), quintom-class bounces with w crossing −1 during mode exit, and Cuscuton bounces with rapidly-varying w give different f_NL predictions and are not constrained by a SPHEREx null on −35/8." Update assumption (e) to explicitly require **matter-dominated mode exit**, not just "no prolonged post-bounce inflation."

### P2-GEM-N1 — minor — "Wilson-Ewing class" is never glossed for the non-bounce-specialist reader

**Lines:** 42, 100, 104, 385
**Issue:** The phrase "Wilson-Ewing class" appears at least four times but is defined only obliquely (via citation to WilsonEwing:2012 and the parenthetical "Λ-CDM quasi-dust model"). A theoretical-physics referee from a non-bounce specialty will not know what makes the Wilson-Ewing model a class rather than a single example. Given how load-bearing this phrase is for the falsification claim, it deserves one explicit sentence.
**Fix:** In §II.A or the intro, add: "We call any matter-dominated contracting bounce that connects directly to radiation domination (with at most N ≪ 55 e-folds of post-bounce inflationary transient) a 'Wilson-Ewing class' model, after the loop-quantum-cosmology realization of Wilson-Ewing 2012; the f_NL prediction is robust within this class." Eight words pays off four sections of "class" language.
