1. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS

The manuscript is ambitious and unusually self-aware, but it is not yet at MNRAS/PRD/JCAP acceptance level. The central "channel-level closure" is plausible in spirit, yet several closures rely on acknowledged ansätze, heuristic naturalness arguments, or companion-paper numbers, while the paper's rhetoric sometimes exceeds what those ingredients can support.

2. BLOCKERS

The headline no-go is not yet a theorem. The paper repeatedly states "four-route closure," but also admits the route set is not an operator-level basis and omits Jackiw–Pi gravitational Chern–Simons and parity-odd four-fermion partners. This is acceptable only if the title/abstract/conclusions stop implying a broad minimal-ECH no-go.

Dark-energy mapping rests on an uncontrolled dimensional ansatz. Appendix B correctly says Eq. (6) has off-shell dimension +1, not +4, and that ρΛ mapping is phenomenological. That makes the dark-energy "closure" partly a closure of the authors' own ansatz rather than of ECH dynamics.

R2/R3 are not derived from cited literature. The paper admits the displayed one-loop/RG formulas are EFT upper-bound ansätze, not literal results from Mercuri, Date–Kaul–Sengupta, etc. These routes cannot be presented as rigorously closed without a controlled derivation or reframing as order-of-magnitude plausibility bounds.

R4 is not closed in the same sense as R1–R3. The manuscript now says R4 is a naturalness/explanatory-deficit objection, not an amplitude no-go. That is scientifically honest, but the global "all four routes closed" language must distinguish "excluded," "suppressed," and "not explanatory."

3. MAJORS

Overcounting/heterogeneity of the "13 mechanism-class constraints." The catalog mixes sharp results, naturalness arguments, qualitative observations, and explicitly heuristic barriers. The paper acknowledges this, but still uses the count rhetorically. Reduce count-based claims or grade barriers by rigor.

Thermal washout is conditional but rhetorically strong. Γwash > H is not computed. The text should not use thermal reset as closure until a Boltzmann calculation is provided.

Companion dependency remains heavy. The paper says companions are non-load-bearing, yet many observational claims, forecasts, MCMC numbers, and pipeline validations are imported. A referee can audit the analytic core, but not the observational program.

The fNL vs Ntot erasure argument needs a transfer-function treatment. The scale-history reasoning is plausible, but "definitively erased" is too strong without the promised transfer calculation.

Figures 3–5 risk misleading readers. Fig. 3's H(z) deviation is dominated by an H0 mismatch; Fig. 5 compares tuning scores of unequal status; both need demotion or redesign.

4. MINORS

Tighten terminology: "parity-odd operator" vs "parity-violating background" is confusing in R2.
Avoid "confirmed null" for galaxy spin unless the exact statistic and confidence are in this paper.
Replace "early 2030s"/survey timing with cautious phrasing.
Move long convention footnotes into appendices.
Standardize γ notation: Barbero–Immirzi γ vs PTA γ vs β-function β.
Clarify that β≈0.27° is fitted/benchmark, not predicted.
The title should include "under stated ansätze" or "channel-level assessment."

5. Strengths

The paper is unusually transparent about limitations, omitted operators, and ansatz status.
The perturbation-transparency argument for canonical scalar matter is clear and potentially useful.
The R1 Planck-suppression estimate is solid and well contextualized.
The R4 revision from amplitude no-go to naturalness objection is scientifically mature.
The manuscript gives a coherent map of where minimal ECH fails versus where broader bounce/ALP tests remain viable.
