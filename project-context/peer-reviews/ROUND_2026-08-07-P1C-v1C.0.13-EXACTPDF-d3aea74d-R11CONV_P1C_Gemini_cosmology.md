Here is a referee report for the manuscript "A Structural No-Go Survey of Minimal Spin-Torsion Routes to Dark Energy and Bounce Phenomenology," evaluated for *Classical and Quantum Gravity* / *Physical Review D*.

### General Comments
The manuscript presents a comprehensive, channel-level structural no-go survey of minimal Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) gravity as a source for late-time dark energy and bounce phenomenology. The author catalogs 14 mechanism-class constraints and evaluates four specific routes, concluding that minimal ECH cannot source the observed dark energy density without introducing new light scales or non-minimal couplings. 

While the physical arguments are generally well-constructed and the explicit separation of evidentiary tiers (rigorous theorems vs. structural arguments vs. ansatz-level estimates) is highly commendable, the manuscript suffers from several structural and formatting issues that preclude acceptance in its current form. Chief among these are the explicit reliance on an unpublished companion paper for a load-bearing section of the analysis, the deliberate overloading of fundamental constants, and the inclusion of internal bookkeeping/local file paths in the main text. 

Below is an itemized list of findings that must be addressed.

### Itemized Findings

**ID: P1C-E1**
*   **Classification:** ESSENTIAL
*   **Section:** Data and Code Availability, Page 16
*   **Specific Problem:** The text states: "An updated archival deposit containing this survey's own verification scripts is planned prior to publication, so that every computational artifact cited here is backed by a frozen-release DOI."
*   **Required Fix:** A manuscript under review must have its computational artifacts frozen and available to the referee *at the time of submission*, not as a future promise. You must mint the DOI for this paper's specific verification scripts and provide the frozen-release hash/DOI in the text. 

**ID: P1C-M1**
*   **Classification:** MAJOR
*   **Section:** II. Conventions and Setup, Page 2
*   **Specific Problem:** The text states: "...in terms of the reduced Planck mass $M_{Pl} \equiv (8\pi G)^{-1/2} \simeq 2.44 \times 10^{18}$ GeV this is $\kappa = M_{Pl}^{-2}$, while order-of-magnitude prose throughout writes $\kappa \sim M_{Pl}^{-2}$ with the full Planck mass $M_{Pl} \equiv G^{-1/2}$ — a deliberate factor-$8\pi$ abuse (exactly, $\kappa = 8\pi M_{Pl}^{-2}$), never a definition."
*   **Required Fix:** Overloading the exact same symbol ($M_{Pl}$) to mean two different values differing by a factor of $\sqrt{8\pi}$ depending on whether the surrounding text is "exact" or "order-of-magnitude prose" is unacceptable in a theoretical physics paper and guarantees reader confusion. You must use distinct symbols for the reduced and full Planck masses (e.g., $\bar{M}_{Pl}$ vs $M_{Pl}$, or $M_P$ vs $M_{Pl}$) and apply them consistently.

**ID: P1C-M2**
*   **Classification:** MAJOR
*   **Section:** IV C. The fourth channel and the combined closure, Page 10
*   **Specific Problem:** The text states: "What is not reproduced here is the full spectator-ALP derivation and the companion's consistency check against a birefringence benchmark [1, 14]; the R4 leg of this survey's accounting is scoped accordingly, and rests on those external, not-yet-peer-reviewed derivations..."
*   **Required Fix:** A standalone paper cannot rest a load-bearing conclusion (the closure of Route 4, which represents 25% of the enumerated routes) on a non-peer-reviewed companion paper without providing sufficient derivation to be refereed here. You must either summarize the R4 derivation sufficiently so that it can be independently evaluated within this manuscript, or explicitly remove R4 from the definitive claims of this paper.

### Summary recommendation
MAJOR REVISIONS

The manuscript provides a highly detailed and physically rigorous bounding of the ECH parameter space, and the explicit separation of evidentiary tiers is excellent. However, the paper fails the standalone-reader test by resting the Route 4 closure on an unpublished companion paper, violates provenance requirements by promising a future DOI for its code artifacts rather than providing it for review, and introduces severe notational confusion by overloading the Planck mass symbol. Once these structural and formatting issues are corrected, the manuscript will be a strong candidate for publication.
