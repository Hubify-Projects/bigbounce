Referee Report (MNRAS / PRD / JCAP)

Title: Channel-Level Closure of Four Minimal Einstein–Cartan–Holst Dark-Energy Routes and Perturbation Transparency for Scalar Matter

1. Recommendation
MINOR REVISIONS

This is a careful, unusually self-critical theoretical paper that has substantially improved its scientific precision through explicit scoping. The manuscript repeatedly distinguishes between proven results, phenomenological ansätze, amplitude-budget arguments, and naturalness arguments. In particular, it now avoids claiming an operator-level no-go and instead consistently frames its conclusion as a channel-level closure under stated assumptions.

The central perturbation-transparency theorem appears internally consistent and is the strongest original contribution. Most potential overclaims have been explicitly downgraded or caveated.

I do not identify any remaining scientific flaw that invalidates the principal conclusion as stated.

2. BLOCKERS
None.
I do not find a remaining issue requiring rejection or major revision.

3. MAJORS
Major 1 — Route 2 and Route 3 remain explicitly ansatz-based rather than derived
The manuscript now openly states that the R2 and R3 amplitudes are conservative EFT scaling ansätze rather than literal derivations from the cited literature.
This is acceptable because:
- the text repeatedly labels them as such,
- the closure no longer depends on exact numerical coefficients,
- the suppression margins are extremely large.
However, readers may still mentally interpret Eqs. (14–16) as derived RG equations.
Recommendation: Consider adding a one-sentence reminder immediately before the Route 2 calculations that "The following coefficient is adopted solely as a conservative EFT upper-bound ansatz rather than a unique one-loop prediction." This is primarily a readability improvement rather than a scientific correction.

4. MINORS
Minor 1 — "13 mechanism-class constraints" vs "14 historical entries"
The paper consistently explains this distinction. However, the reader repeatedly encounters 14 barriers / 13 mechanism-class constraints throughout Abstract, Sections IX, XV, figures, and tables. Although internally consistent, it is cognitively taxing. A brief parenthetical reminder in the abstract (e.g., "13 distinct mechanism classes; 14 historical catalog entries") would help.

Minor 2 — Companion-paper dependence
The manuscript repeatedly states that companion papers are not load-bearing, which is good. Nevertheless, some numerical benchmark values (forecast significances, MCMC outputs, γPTA values, etc.) appear throughout the text. A brief sentence near the beginning of Section XIII reminding readers that these numbers are illustrative context rather than ingredients in the proof would improve clarity.

Minor 3 — Length and repetition
Several important caveats appear three or four separate times. Examples include: operator-level vs channel-level closure, perturbation transparency, phenomenological status of α/M, Ntot bookkeeping. These repetitions improve referee confidence but reduce readability. A modest reduction would improve presentation without changing scientific content.

Minor 4 — Figure density
Figures 4 and 7 communicate closely related forecast information. Readers may benefit from either explicitly distinguishing their purposes in the captions, or merging them in a future revision. This is presentation only.

Minor 5 — Appendix B wording
Appendix B is admirably transparent regarding the dimensional status of the parity-odd operator. One sentence could emphasize that the phenomenological on-shell scaling is not intended as an EFT completion. Although this is already implied, stating it once more at the beginning of Appendix B would prevent misreading.

5. Strengths
1. Excellent scientific self-scoping — manuscript unusually careful about separating theorem, ansatz, phenomenology, illustrative benchmark, companion result.
2. Strong perturbation-transparency theorem — clear assumptions (canonical scalar matter, torsion-free branch, no propagating torsion, no dynamical Immirzi field, no nonminimal couplings), logic appears internally consistent.
3. Honest treatment of limitations — repeatedly states what it does not prove: no operator-basis closure, no first-principles derivation of α/M, no EFT derivation of the dimensional ansatz, no unique ECH prediction for surviving observational signatures.
4. Careful distinction between amplitude closure and naturalness closure — Route 4 treatment ("closed by explanatory deficit rather than amplitude mismatch") is much more defensible than amplitude exclusion alone.
5. Internal consistency — numerous previous consistency issues addressed: channel-level vs operator-level scope, parity bookkeeping, perturbation assumptions, dimensional accounting, companion-paper dependence, surviving predictions, explicit theorem domains.

Overall assessment: mature theoretical paper whose principal contribution is not a proof that ECH gravity cannot generate dark energy in every conceivable extension, but rather a carefully delimited demonstration that four explicitly enumerated minimal-ECH channels fail under stated assumptions, together with a perturbation-transparency theorem for canonical scalar matter. No remaining scientific deficiencies requiring major revision.

Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONS.
