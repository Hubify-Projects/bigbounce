This is a referee report for "namaster-proof: Content-bound execution receipts as a shortcut detector for pseudo-$C_\ell$ computations" submitted to the Journal of Open Research Software.

The paper presents a Python verification layer for pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimators, utilizing content-bound execution receipts to detect structural shortcuts in instrumented steps. The core methodology—using a sealed, pre-registered blind test to validate the receipt mechanism—is highly rigorous, and the primary result (Batch 2) correctly accounts for the statistical dependence of seed-varied replicates. However, the manuscript contains a critical statistical contradiction in how it treats subsequent batches, a broken cross-reference to non-existent text, and several internal bookkeeping placeholders that must be removed. 

Below is an itemized list of findings that must be addressed.

### ESSENTIAL

**P1B-E1: Statistical Contradiction and Math Error in Probability Bounds**
*   **Section 6, Page 6:** *"batch 1's own confusion counts (12/12 on S1–S4, one-sided 95% lower bound 0.779; 0/3 honest false positives, upper bound 0.632)"*
*   **Section 6, Page 7:** *"Structural arms S1–S4b pool to 24/24 (lower bound 0.905)."*
*   **Problem:** On Page 5, the author correctly establishes a rigorous "Independence caveat": because the rules compare seed-independent quantities against a fixed contract, replicates within an arm are not independent draws. The effective $n$ per class is 1, yielding a lower bound of 0.473 for $n=4$ classes in Batch 2. However, the author completely abandons this caveat for Batch 1 and Batch 3, treating the runs as independent to claim artificially tighter bounds ($0.05^{1/12} \approx 0.779$ for Batch 1, and $0.05^{1/30} \approx 0.905$ for Batch 3). Furthermore, there is a mathematical typo in Batch 3: S1, S2, S3, S4, and S4b constitute 5 arms of 6 runs each (30 runs total), which matches the 0.905 calculation, but the text erroneously says "24/24".
*   **Required Fix:** Apply the independence caveat consistently across all batches. For Batch 1, the structural classes have $n=4$, so the lower bound is 0.473. For Batch 3, the structural classes have $n=5$, so the lower bound is $0.05^{1/5} \approx 0.549$. Correct the "24/24" text to "30/30", but do not use the run-level $n=30$ to compute the probability bound.

## Summary recommendation
MAJOR REVISIONS

The software verification framework and the blind-test protocol are exceptionally well-designed, and the primary results (Batch 2) are statistically sound. However, the manuscript requires major revisions to correct a severe methodological contradiction where the author abandons their own statistical independence caveat to claim artificially tight probability bounds for Batches 1 and 3. Additionally, the broken cross-references, uncomputed quantitative claims, and internal audit tags must be resolved before the paper can be accepted.
