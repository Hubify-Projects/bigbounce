ISSUES

[MAJOR] Sections IV & VII: Heuristic Systematic Budget and Lack of Joint Fisher Matrix The manuscript relies on a sensitivity recast that scales a single imported baseline. The author explicitly states a full bispectrum joint Fisher over systematic nuisances is not performed. The imported covariance assumes a purely local template, meaning additional variance from non-local tails is absorbed into a heuristic additive-quadrature systematic budget. A heuristic quadrature budget is insufficient for a rigorous observational forecast; a full multi-tracer Fisher matrix evaluated at the specific bounce fiducial is required to validate the statistical claims.

[MAJOR] Section II.C & Appendix A: Factor of Two Discrepancy Resolution The manuscript attempts to resolve a literature discrepancy between the Cai et al. value of -35/8 and the Li et al. value of -35/16 using a symbolic operator-algebra identity. However, the author relies on indirect cross-checks rather than a complete numerical re-derivation of the four-vertex in-in integrals. For a foundational theoretical prediction that anchors the target survey significance, this discrepancy should be definitively closed via an explicit physical calculation rather than carried as an explicit stress branch.

[MAJOR] Abstract & Section II.C: Unverified Cubic-Order Transmission The entire forecast is conditional on faithful third-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce. The author acknowledges this is verified only at linear order and is supported at cubic order solely by an order-of-magnitude superhorizon-scaling estimate. Without a rigorous full cubic in-in computation across an explicit bounce, the paper forecasts a conditional theoretical hypothesis rather than a robustly derived prediction.

[MINOR] Section III.B: Projection Noise Quantification The manuscript claims projection noise is subdominant by citing a shape cosine suppression where 1−r cos^2 ≈0.03. This suppression is calculated as a heuristic shape-similarity indicator in an unweighted shape metric rather than the specific survey Fisher metric of the estimator. The degradation impact must be quantified directly within the appropriate estimator covariance framework.

CONCLUSION

The central claim that upcoming surveys like SPHEREx can meaningfully constrain the matter bounce scenario is conditionally supported by the sensitivity recasts, but it ultimately rests on heuristic systematic error budgets and unverified third-order theoretical assumptions that require full derivation before meeting the standards of an independent physical forecast.
