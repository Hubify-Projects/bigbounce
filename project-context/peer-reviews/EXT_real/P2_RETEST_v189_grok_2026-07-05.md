This is a strong, rigorous, and honest sensitivity recast paper. It makes a clear, falsifiable prediction for the matter bounce (f_NL = −35/16 at leading order, with quantified 0.6–8% ϵ-correction uncertainty) and maps it onto near-term observables in a transparent way.

Core Contribution

The headline result is that SPHEREx can test the corrected central value at a realistic post-systematic-budget level of ∼1.3–2.75σ (bispectrum channel, template-overlap corrected with r ≈ 0.84 noise-weighted) through the multi-tracer galaxy bispectrum, with MegaMapper offering a more powerful but design-uncertain follow-up. The Bayesian model comparison (closed-form + 3×10^5 MC validation) shows BF ≈ 9–14 (r ≈ 0.84 bookkeeping) favoring the bounce over tuned multifield competitors for a detection at the central value.

The paper correctly positions itself as a sensitivity recast (not an independent Fisher forecast), which is the right framing.

Major Strengths
Resolution of the Cai–Li discrepancy (Appendix A): This is a genuine, valuable contribution. The explicit re-summation of Cai et al.’s own four cubic vertices at ϵ = 3/2, the identification of the spurious +(99/128) ∑_i k_i^3 term in their final polynomial (Eq. 37), and the cross-check against Li et al.’s general-c_s result are clean and convincing. Adopting −35/16 as central (and retaining −35/8 only as erroneous literature reference) is the correct choice; it halves the headline amplitude relative to the prior erroneous value.
Template mismatch quantification: The null-space scan (10k samples), shape-cosine stability (r_cos > 0.97), and Fisher-weighted r = 0.84 ± 0.02 (noise-weighted central) across physically motivated schemes are thorough. The injection-recovery cross-check (r_meas ≈ 0.90) adds credibility.
Systematic budget transparency: The additive-quadrature scoping envelope (Table IV), explicit treatment of b_ϕ marginalization, GR projection stress-testing (Table III + continuous marginalization), and photo-z outlier estimate are well done. Labeling the 1.3–2.75σ range as a “scoping sensitivity envelope” rather than a joint-covariance precision is accurate and responsible.
Honest scoping and caveats: The repeated emphasis on assumption (d) (faithful cubic-order transmission) as the single weakest link, the conditional nature of the UV-completion independence, and the single-source limitation of the recast are exemplary. The load-bearing caveat (⋆) in the abstract/scope is exactly right.
Bayesian comparison: Closed-form derivation + MC validation across frameworks, explicit prior-sensitivity mapping (delta vs. σ_theory = 1.0 Gaussian bounce priors; narrow vs. broad competitor priors), and the template-mismatch bookkeeping paragraph are high-quality.
Code/data availability: The detailed artifact list and GitHub/Zenodo commitment are excellent.
Areas for Clarification or Minor Improvement
Length and redundancy — At 32 pages this is long for a sensitivity-recast paper. Some material (e.g., the detailed SDB Fisher in §IX.D, parts of the GR parameterization discussion, and repeated scoping statements) could be tightened or moved to appendices without loss of rigor. The “response to recurring referee concerns” signpost in §VII.E is useful but could be shorter.
SDB vs. bispectrum channel hierarchy — The paper correctly labels the joint (f_NL, n_fNL) SDB Fisher as subordinate. Consider making this even clearer in the abstract and §IV (e.g., one sentence: “The headline forecast uses the Heinrich et al. multi-tracer bispectrum channel; a separate SDB running analysis is reported in §IX.D as a scale-dependence cross-check.”). The r = 0.84 factor does not apply to SDB, which is already noted but worth repeating once more explicitly.
GR projection stress-test calibration — The σ_GR ∈ {0, 0.5, 1.0} grid is a reasonable internal stress test, but the mapping to Addis et al. (∼3σ bias for Euclid-like, ∼20σ for MegaMapper-like) is acknowledged as not a direct Fisher translation. A short parenthetical quantifying the implied σ(f_NL) inflation (∼23% at σ_GR = 0.5) is already present and helpful; it could be cross-referenced once in §V (MegaMapper) for readers who jump there.
b_ϕ marginalization — The 20%/30%/50% prior-widening scenarios are well motivated by Barreira and Heinrich et al. The statement that the bispectrum channel is “less sensitive but not independent” of b_ϕ (via tree-level cross-terms) is correct and important. Consider adding one sentence noting that anomaly-selected tracers (autoencoder QSO candidates, etc.) could partially mitigate this via improved multi-tracer conditioning.
Minor presentational points
Table I and Fig. 1 are excellent; ensure the final published versions have crisp axis labels and that the “corrected” column is visually prominent.
The phrase “sensitivity recast rather than an independent forecast” appears multiple times — good, but the very first sentence of the abstract/scope already says it clearly, so later repetitions can be slightly condensed.
In §VI the four-corner Bayes-factor grid is clear; the continuous marginalization over σ_theory (uniform hyperprior) giving BF ≈ 8.8 (broad) / ≈ 3.5 (narrow) is a nice robustness check.
