1. Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS
The paper has the ingredients of a useful community anomaly-candidate catalogue, and I appreciate the unusually explicit disclosure of failed tiers, reproducibility caveats, and validation limitations. However, in its present form it should not be accepted. The main obstacles are not stylistic: the headline volume/count accounting, the “validated catalog-grade” definition, the DESI science/non-science target reconciliation, and the survival of Gaia-derived synthetic/historical quantities in figures and novelty summaries all need correction before publication.
The manuscript is potentially publishable after a substantial revision that rebases the headline claims on a single clean provenance table, removes or quarantines all synthetic/historical quantities from science figures, and uses strictly tiered validation language.
2. BLOCKERS — must fix before publication
B1. Headline scan volume and denominator accounting are internally inconsistent
Section/line: Title/Abstract, p. 1, anchor “37.3 million sources and CMB map patches”; Table I, p. 9, anchors “Total (cross-transfer, ACT-incl.) 37,292,042” and “Path-C unique 37,272,042”; §III/Table I notes, pp. 8–10. The abstract presents the 268,519 subset and the 377,482 inclusive catalogue as clean post-Path-C products, while Table I’s denominators mix native-rescored samples, full catalogue denominators, excised eROSITA/Gaia history, and Planck cross-transfer versus native patch banks. The manuscript itself states that the primary 268,519 count is built from DESI+SDSS+Planck+NEOWISE detections and that eROSITA and Gaia are excluded from all counts cv_P3, while Table I explains that SDSS and LAMOST headline counts are slices of the native-rescored pools and that SDSS has only 1,925,279 rescored spectra cv_P3. The Planck section also states that the released native pipeline uses a 2×10^5 patch bank, while Table I’s 20,000-patch denominator is the historical cross-transfer input cv_P3.
Problem: A reader cannot determine what was actually scanned for the title/abstract “37.3 million” claim without reverse-engineering historical, failed, excised, and unscored components. This is a publication-level accounting problem because the title, abstract, rates, and “largest scan” framing depend on it.
Proposed fix: Add a single provenance table, early in §II or §III, with columns: public archive size; retrieved/read successfully; quality-cut/scored; native-training pool; thresholded detections; count-retained detections; validated detections; exploratory detections; excised detections. Recompute the title, abstract, Table I total rows, and all rates from that table. Do not include synthetic Gaia, unretrieved spectra, or historical ACT/Planck cross-transfer denominators in the headline “scan volume” unless they are explicitly labelled as historical non-catalog processing.
B2. The SDSS contribution to the 268,519 “validated” headline is a fixed-size continuity slice, not a defensible anomaly threshold
Section/line: §II B/Table I footnotes, p. 5 and p. 10, anchors “SDSS headline of 77,905 is instead a fixed-size continuity slice” and “only 12 sources at the field-defining S > 5”. The manuscript states that SDSS’s 77,905 objects are a fixed-size continuity slice equal to the cross-transfer count, not a top-1% cut; the native top-1% gives 19,253 objects, and strict S>5 gives only 12 cv_P3. The same 77,905 objects are nevertheless included in the 274,353 detections that deduplicate to the 268,519 validated headline cv_P3.
Problem: This is not merely a presentation issue. The SDSS tier is about 29% of the validated headline count. If the included count is selected to preserve a historical cross-transfer size rather than by a native score threshold or validation-calibrated operating point, the “validated catalogue-grade” number is arbitrary.
Proposed fix: Either (i) remove the SDSS 77,905 continuity slice from the validated headline and recompute the headline using a justified native threshold, e.g. top-1% or a validated recovery/purity operating point; or (ii) provide a rigorous, pre-specified justification for 77,905 as a catalogue threshold, including recovery, contamination, and stability as a function of threshold. In either case, Table I must clearly distinguish “continuity slice” from “catalogue threshold.”
B3. The DESI majority is simultaneously described as non-primary/sky-filler dominated and as “not sky/fiber/calibration artifacts”
Section/line: §III A, pp. 10–11, anchors “dominated by sky-fiber and filler spectra” and “∼98.7% of DESI anomaly clusters coincide with spectra carrying no primary science-class target bit”; §III C, p. 13, anchor “98.8% galaxy … not sky/fiber/calibration artifacts.” The manuscript states that only 2,468 of 190,015 deduplicated DESI clusters match primary science-class target bits and that 86% have DESI TARGET=0, described as sky fibers and secondary/ToO programs cv_P3. Later it states that all DESI anomalies join to Redrock and are “overwhelmingly real Redrock-classified spectroscopic objects … not sky/fiber/calibration artifacts” cv_P3.
Problem: DESI supplies 195,829 of the 274,353 validated-input detections, so this reconciliation is central. A Redrock SPECTYPE assignment alone does not establish that a spectrum is an astrophysical target rather than a sky/filler/calibration spectrum misfit by a template pipeline. The two descriptions currently point in opposite directions.
Proposed fix: Provide a DESI anomaly accounting table by fibermap class and quality flags: TARGETID uniqueness, DESI_TARGET/MWS_TARGET/BGS_TARGET/CMX_TARGET bits, OBJTYPE, FIBERSTATUS, ZWARN, SPECTYPE, DELTACHI2, science-primary bits, sky fibers, standards, calibration, secondary, ToO, and filler categories. The abstract and conclusions should separately report: full-stream reconstruction outliers, primary-science-target outliers, and non-primary/filler/sky/calibration outliers. Claims such as “not sky/fiber/calibration artifacts” should be withdrawn unless supported by this classification plus a representative visual/QA audit of the non-primary majority.
B4. Synthetic Gaia-derived quantities remain in science-facing figures and novelty summaries despite the Gaia tier being excised
Section/line: §III G, p. 17, anchor “not real Gaia DR3 data but the synthetic-placeholder fallback”; Fig. 6/§IV A, p. 19, anchor “Gaia DR3 27%” and “235/400 … historical four-survey pool including the now-removed Gaia DR3 tier”; Fig. 10, p. 28, anchor “Gaia DR3 … historical methodological record only.” The paper correctly discloses that the Gaia anomaly table was synthetic and has zero real science content cv_P3. Yet Fig. 6 still plots “Gaia DR3 27%” and the 58.8% SIMBAD aggregate is described with an inconsistent three-survey/400-object denominator involving historical Gaia cv_P3. Fig. 10 also retains a Gaia DR3 injection-recovery curve, albeit labelled historical cv_P3.
Problem: Once a tier is identified as synthetic-placeholder output, it must not appear in science figures, pooled novelty fractions, validation summaries, or aggregate denominators. Labelling it historical is insufficient in the main results.
Proposed fix: Remove Gaia from all main-text figures, pooled fractions, and conclusions except a short provenance-audit subsection. Recompute Fig. 6 and the 58.8% aggregate using only real tiers, with one radius and one denominator. If a Gaia failure-mode plot is retained, move it to an appendix explicitly titled “synthetic placeholder audit — not data” and exclude it from all validation wording.
B5. The reproducibility claim is not yet publication-ready because key artifacts are future-release or unavailable, and some raw production material was lost
Section/line: Data Availability, pp. 29–30, anchors “will be made public with the arXiv posting” and “Zenodo DOI will be minted”; §VI D(i), p. 26, anchor “raw production spectra were lost when the compute pods were wiped.” The manuscript says the data are staged and will be public with arXiv posting, and that a DOI will be inserted at submission cv_P3. It also states that raw production DESI spectra were lost and had to be re-pulled from SPARCL for injection-recovery testing cv_P3.
Problem: This paper lives or dies on reproducibility. A referee cannot verify the central 268,519 and 377,482 products, threshold choices, eROSITA membership list, and validation artifacts if the immutable release is not public and versioned at review/publication time. The lost raw-production-material statement is acceptable only if the clean-room rerun is fully reproducible and documented.
Proposed fix: Before acceptance, provide a public Zenodo/HuggingFace/GitHub release with immutable version tags, checksums, environment file, and a clean-run log demonstrating reproduction of: the 268,519 validated headline; the 377,482 inclusive count; all per-survey thresholds; the SDSS alternate-threshold dedup results; eROSITA top-298 membership; Fig. 6 after Gaia removal; and all injection-recovery curves. Replace “will be” language with DOI-backed present-tense availability.
3. MAJORS — should fix
M1. NEOWISE should not be called “validated catalog-grade” without a detector-sensitivity test
Section/line: Abstract, p. 1; §III H, p. 17; Fig. 10, p. 28. The manuscript explicitly states that NEOWISE passes only a masking-geometry QA gate by construction and not a detector-sensitivity test cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Either remove NEOWISE from the “validated” count or rename the headline tier to “detector-validated + geometry-QA” and give two headline numbers: detector-sensitivity-validated only, and detector+geometry-QA. The latter should not be abbreviated as simply “validated.”
M2. The Planck CMB tier needs stronger separation from the point-source catalogue
Section/line: §III F, p. 16, anchor “200 sky-region patches, NOT point-source objects”; Table I/Fig. 10. The paper is clear that Planck contributes 200 CMB map-patch sky regions, selected as a fixed top-200 from a native bank, scored in full including training patches cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Treat Planck as a separate CMB-patch catalogue, not part of the same “source” catalogue. Report the main point-source count independently everywhere, including title/abstract, and put CMB patch validation in a separate subsection/table. The title should be “sources and CMB map patches,” not “sources” alone.
M3. eROSITA remains too prominent for an excluded, score-irreproducible tier
Section/line: §III E, pp. 14–16; Table I; Data Availability. The eROSITA score axis is described as irreproducible, with the tier released only as a membership list and excluded from all counts cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Move eROSITA’s detailed score-axis forensics to an appendix and keep only a short main-text statement. In Table I, either remove eROSITA from the main per-survey block or put it in a visibly separate “excluded addendum” block with no rate column.
M4. The cross-survey coincidence significance requires footprint-preserving mocks
Section/line: §IV A–C, pp. 18–21. The manuscript itself acknowledges that the RA-shifted control is heuristic and not geometry-preserving cv_P3, while also concluding that 637 multi-survey clusters are >60× the expected random coincidence rate.
Proposed fix: Replace the uniform-density/RA-shift argument with survey-footprint-preserving random catalogues or rotations for each pair, including local sky density, masks, and the exact dedup algorithm. Quote pairwise expected coincidences and uncertainties. Until then, avoid “>60×” language.
M5. The cosmological applications are overextended relative to the catalogue result
Section/line: §V–§V A, pp. 23–25; Conclusions, p. 28. The paper states that the empirical fNL improvement is consistent with no improvement and that NANOGrav is a secondary illustrative application, not a detection cv_P3.
Proposed fix: For an MNRAS catalogue paper, move most cosmology material to appendices or a companion paper. Keep only a short “possible applications” paragraph. If retained in the main text, remove “decisive” language from the conclusions and ensure all cosmological claims are explicitly secondary and non-detections.
M6. The methods are too dependent on the companion repository
Section/line: §II B–D, pp. 3–7; Data Availability, pp. 29–30. Many load-bearing details are deferred to pipeline paths, logs, or JSON artifacts rather than described in the paper.
Proposed fix: The paper must be understandable without reading the repository. Add a compact methods table for each survey: input selection, preprocessing, training sample definition, random seeds, train/validation split, threshold rule, validation test, and released score axis.
M7. DESI artifact/purity validation is insufficient for a 195,829-object headline tier
Section/line: §III A, p. 11; §VI D(i), p. 26. The top-200 visual inspection and broad-template injection-recovery are useful, but the paper also acknowledges a B-dominant component and no independent architecture for DESI cv_P3 cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Add a stratified visual/QA sample across score, TARGETTYPE/fibermap class, and band-dominance; publish the labels. Include a second detector or ablation for DESI, even if only on a representative subset. At minimum, report purity estimates separately for science targets and non-primary/filler/sky spectra.
M8. SDSS cross-transfer and native-retrained results remain muddled in narrative and figures
Section/line: §III C, Fig. 3/Fig. 4, §VI B. The paper states that the released SDSS tier is native-rescored, while §VI B still describes “the transfer-learning approach used for SDSS” and quotes a 3.38% cross-transfer rate as if it characterizes the catalogue cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Move all cross-transfer SDSS material into a historical-baseline subsection or appendix. Main SDSS text should discuss only the native-retrained catalogue tier and its selected threshold. Figures 3 and 4 should be labelled “historical cross-transfer diagnostic, not released SDSS catalogue.”
M9. The “total anomaly rate” should be removed or demoted
Section/line: Table I, p. 9. The manuscript states that Planck and NEOWISE are fixed top-1% selections and that total-row rates are bookkeeping ratios, not anomaly frequencies cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Do not put “1.01%” or “0.86%” total rates in the main table unless the table visually marks them as non-physical bookkeeping quantities. Better: remove total-rate cells entirely.
M10. Novelty metrics need a clean single-radius, single-denominator treatment
Section/line: §IV A/Fig. 6, pp. 17–19. The paper appropriately distinguishes SIMBAD absence from genuine novelty, but it mixes 3″ and 5″ radii, top-100 and top-1000 samples, historical Gaia denominators, eROSITA addenda, and DESI-only genuine novelty cv_P3.
Proposed fix: Provide a novelty table with one row per survey and columns for radius, sample definition, catalogues searched, matched fraction, and “not found in all searched catalogues.” Keep 17.8% as a DESI-top-1000 point estimate only; do not call it the catalogue discovery rate unless all tiers receive comparable treatment.

5. Strengths
The paper tackles a genuinely large and useful problem: cross-archive anomaly-candidate generation at tens-of-millions scale.
The authors are unusually transparent about failure modes: LAMOST training bias, eROSITA score-axis irreproducibility, synthetic Gaia fallback, ACT quarantine, and NEOWISE’s geometry-only validation are all explicitly disclosed.
The DESI validation package is much stronger than in early drafts of many ML-catalogue papers: k-fold stability, OOD stability, injection-recovery on re-pulled spectra, and a visual top-rank audit are all valuable ingredients.
The manuscript correctly distinguishes SIMBAD absence from genuine novelty and makes a serious attempt at multi-catalog archival cross-matching.
The deduplication audit and threshold-sensitivity checks are the right kind of reproducibility work for a community catalogue.
The LAMOST failure is scientifically useful as a methodological warning: anomaly detection measures rarity relative to the training set, not rarity in the Universe.
