Gemini said Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONSGeneral OverviewThis paper presents a remarkably rigorous, large-scale measurement of the large-scale chirality dipole of spiral galaxies using the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR8 footprint. By leveraging an equivariant deep learning framework (a flip-equivariant Vision Transformer) applied to 8.47 million galaxies, the author constructs a high-confidence sample of approximately 9.5×105 spirals to test cosmological isotropy. The primary finding is a robust real-space null result (z=+0.41σ,p=0.31), which places a sub-percent sensitivity constraint (A50​≈0.75%) on late-universe axial-vector anisotropy, standing in severe tension with previous high-amplitude dipole claims in the literature (e.g., Shamir).The manuscript stands out for its exceptional transparency, methodological defensive driving (such as pre-registering cuts via immutable git history and conducting extensive bias-hardening suites), and the thoroughness with which it tracks down and quantifies harmonic-channel systematics (e.g., monopole-mask leakage).The paper is nearly ready for publication and represents a textbook example of how deep learning should be audited for precision observational cosmology. I recommend acceptance after a few minor points are polished.StrengthsStatistical Power at Scale: Analyzing 8.47 million galaxies to isolate over 3.2 million spirals (and nearly 1 million high-confidence objects) provides an unprecedented sample size that is roughly 1.6$\times$ the scale of previous equivariant attempts and orders of magnitude larger than early photographic or human-voted samples.
