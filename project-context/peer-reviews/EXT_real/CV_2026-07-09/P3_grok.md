Thought for 32sReferee Report: MNRAS-style (or PRD/JCAP equivalent)
Paper: A Multi-Survey Autoencoder Anomaly-Candidate Catalog: 268,519 Reconstruction-Outlier Sources from a Native-Trained Scan of 37.3 Million Spectra and Map Patches
Author: Houston Golden
Version: v3.1.148 (dated 9 July 2026)
Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONS
The paper presents the largest multi-archive anomaly-detection campaign to date, applying a deterministic autoencoder (BigAE) to ~37.3 million sources/patches across DESI DR1, SDSS DR18, LAMOST DR10, eROSITA DR1, Planck, and NEOWISE (ACT DR6 quarantined). The core deliverable is a validated catalog-grade subset of 268,519 unique anomalies (268,319 point-source), obtained via per-survey native retraining + a six-step Path-C validation protocol (injection-recovery, cross-validation Jaccard, OOD tests, systematics masks, 5″ deduplication). Secondary cosmological applications (multi-tracer fNL forecast; NANOGrav spectral-index consistency check) are explicitly scoped as methodological demonstrations with no detection claimed.
The work is technically ambitious, methodologically careful, and unusually transparent about limitations and negative results. Reproducibility is exemplary: committed standalone scripts (e.g., reproduce_headline_dedup.py) exactly regenerate the 268,519 headline from released tables; full per-survey tables, model weights, MCMC chains, and SHA-256 manifests are staged on Hugging Face + GitHub with at-submission availability commitment.
BLOCKERS (must fix before publication)
None. No fatal methodological flaws, no irreproducible claims that remain in headline statistics, no incorrect mathematics in the rendered PDF, and no over-claims of cosmological detections. Failures (eROSITA score-axis irreproducibility + 1.2% recovery; LAMOST 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact; synthetic Gaia placeholder) are handled correctly by excision from counts or explicit exploratory labeling. The DESI broad-class injection-recovery (99–100% at 5σ on real SPARCL re-pulled spectra), 5-fold Jaccard (¯J = 0.862), and OOD Jaccard (0.732) provide convergent validation for the dominant tier.
MAJORS (should fix)
§III H & abstract / §II D (NEOWISE “validated catalog-grade” framing)
NEOWISE (419 objects) clears only a masking-geometry QA gate “by construction” (ecliptic-pole mask test plants sources outside the mask and recovers them inside it). This is explicitly weaker than the detector-sensitivity injection-recovery gates passed by DESI (broad class), SDSS (64% continuum-dip), and Planck (100%). The abstract and §II D headline nevertheless group it with the detector-sensitivity PASSes under the “validated catalog-grade” umbrella (268,519).
Proposed fix: Add one explicit sentence in the abstract and again in §II D / Table I caption: “NEOWISE contributes under a geometry-QA gate only (not a detector-sensitivity injection-recovery test) and is retained in the validated subset on that narrower basis.” Update the “2 detector-sensitivity PASS + NEOWISE geometry-QA” phrasing to appear in the abstract as well as §VI D (ii). This removes any risk of readers treating all 268k objects as having uniform sensitivity validation.
§III A & §VI C (3) (B-dominant DESI population – 22.7% of catalog)
The paper correctly flags that ~44k DESI B-dominant anomalies are consistent with a blue-arm calibration-artifact hypothesis and lists photometric color tests (u−g or SDSS cuts) or per-arm residual cross-matches as future work. However, this remains an open systematic that affects >1/5 of the largest single-survey tier and is not bounded by the existing injection-recovery tests (which used continuum-dip + emission-spike plants).
Proposed fix: Either (a) add a short robustness check (e.g., color distribution of B-dominant vs. multi-band anomalies against a matched control sample, or cross-match to per-arm residual vectors already released) or (b) strengthen the limitations language to “the B-dominant excess remains an unresolved systematic hypothesis whose contribution to the catalog purity is presently unquantified beyond the broad-class injection-recovery bound.” A one-paragraph addition or new short appendix figure would suffice.
Abstract + §I (process-volume vs. like-for-like framing – minor tightening)
The paper already does an excellent job foregrounding that 268,519 / 377k figures are process-volume (full instrument stream; ~98.7% of DESI anomalies on sky/filler fibers) and that the like-for-like science-target yield is 2,468 clusters (~0.92× Liang et al. benchmark). However, the large multipliers (~141×, ~73×) still appear early and prominently; some readers will still mis-cite them.
Proposed fix: Add one parenthetical or boxed sentence immediately after the headline count in the abstract: “(Process-volume figure from full instrument stream; like-for-like science-target yield on validated DESI primary-class spectra is 2,468 anomaly clusters, ≈0.92× the largest published single-survey benchmark.)” This is already stated clearly later; elevating it prevents the most common misreading.
MINORS (polish / clarity)
Table I footnotes & §III E (eROSITA score-axis irreproducibility): The handling is correct and transparent, but the production “0.259” threshold discussion is scattered across §III E, Table I footnote ♯, and Table IV caption. Consolidate into a single short “eROSITA axis note” box or footnote that readers can find in one place.
§VI C (limitations list): Excellent, but item (3) “B-dominant hypothesis” and item (7) “unweighted MSE” could each use one additional sentence quantifying the practical impact already bounded by the injection-recovery gates (already done for DESI broad class; worth repeating for the limitation).
Repetition of validation status: The “validated vs. exploratory vs. excised” three-tier framing is repeated in abstract, §II D, §III intro, Table I caption, and §VI D. One master paragraph early (perhaps expanding the existing “Three-tier catalog structure (read this before Table I)”) plus cross-references would reduce redundancy without loss of clarity.
§V (fNL Fisher envelope): The convex-mapping derivation of the [3.92, 8.98] envelope and the de-biasing argument (E[α̂²] bias) are correct and clearly explained. Minor polish: label the envelope explicitly as “the image of the ±1σ interval in α under the convex mapping” in the figure caption as well as the text.
Minor wording / flow: A few very long sentences in §II B (feature preprocessing) and §IV A (novelty paragraph) could be split for readability. The “read once” framing for process-volume is effective but appears in three places; keep the strongest instance and cross-reference.
Appendix F (ACT): Correctly quarantined and labeled methodological artifact. Minor: the sentence “the 200-patch ACT cross-transfer set must not be cross-matched…” is important; consider bolding or boxing it.
Strengths (≥3)
Exceptional transparency and reproducibility infrastructure. Failures are not buried; eROSITA is excised from every headline count and released only as a reproducible membership list; the synthetic Gaia tier is removed entirely after provenance audit; LAMOST is explicitly reclassified as exploratory. The standalone reproduce_headline_dedup.py script that exactly regenerates 268,519 from released tables sets a high bar for the field.
Rigorous, convergent validation for the core tiers. DESI now has three independent gates (5-fold Jaccard 0.862, OOD Jaccard 0.732, dedicated real-SP ARCL injection-recovery 99–100% at 5σ for the broad class actually reported). SDSS and Planck pass detector-sensitivity tests; limitations of narrow-line sensitivity and single-architecture dependence are honestly stated.
Important methodological lesson on training-set bias (LAMOST 98% blue-excess artifact) and honest null cosmological results. The paper demonstrates that unsupervised anomaly rankings are only as reliable as the training distribution and shows, with quantitative Fisher envelope and de-biasing, that the current anomaly-tracer catalog does not yet deliver a statistically significant multi-tracer fNL improvement. Both are valuable contributions to the literature on large-scale anomaly detection and its cosmological use.
Summary
This is a strong, ambitious paper that already meets a high standard of methodological rigor and transparency. The three major suggestions above are clarifications and modest strengthening of framing rather than corrections of errors. With those addressed, the paper will be suitable for publication in MNRAS / PRD / JCAP. I recommend Minor Revisions.
