Paper: A Null Chirality Dipole in 8.5 Million DESI Galaxies from Equivariant Deep Learning, v1.0.227, dated 9 July 2026. cv_P4
Recommendation: MAJOR REVISIONS
The manuscript presents an ambitious and potentially valuable null test of large-scale galaxy handedness using a very large DESI Legacy imaging sample and a flip-equivariant classifier. The central result — a high-confidence real-space chirality dipole consistent with zero — is plausible and scientifically interesting. However, the paper currently overstates several inferences, mixes estimator-specific significances in ways that remain confusing despite repeated caveats, and relies on unfrozen computational artifacts for a number of load-bearing claims. I would not recommend publication in its current form, but I do think the paper could become publishable after substantial revision.
The most serious concerns are not that the null result is obviously wrong; rather, they are that the manuscript sometimes treats “not detected,” “below a recovery threshold,” and “therefore non-cosmological” as equivalent, and that it gives primary cosmological weight to a template-fit exclusion whose handling of classifier dilution and sample selection is not yet adequate.
I use page/section references because the submitted PDF has no printed line numbers; where useful, I also refer to approximate PDF text-extraction line locations.
BLOCKERS — must fix before publication
B1. The paper makes an invalid inference that an unexplained residual is “non-cosmological” because it is below the real-space recovery threshold.
Location: Abstract, p. 1; Sec. IV D, p. 14–15, especially the paragraph beginning “Bottom line, stated first”; p. 15, lines approximately 38–53 in text extraction.
The manuscript states that the imaging+morphology model explains only about 53% of the post-MASTER residual, leaving about 47% open, but then argues that this remainder is “bounded a fortiori as non-cosmological” because the corresponding amplitude is below the real-space A50/A95 recovery thresholds. The text explicitly says the remainder “therefore cannot be a coherent cosmological dipole” and is attributed to survey systematics. cv_P4 cv_P4
This is not a valid statistical inference. A signal below a 50% or 95% recovery threshold is not proven non-cosmological; it is merely not reliably detectable or not excluded by that estimator. The current argument can support “this residual is too small to affect the claimed exclusion of Shamir-scale dipoles,” but not “this residual is non-cosmological.”
Proposed fix: Rewrite all such claims in the abstract, Sec. IV D, Discussion, and Conclusions. State instead: “The unexplained residual is below the real-space estimator’s current recovery threshold and therefore does not affect our exclusion/sensitivity statements for dipoles above A95; its physical origin remains unresolved.” Remove “bounded as non-cosmological,” “not a cosmological loophole,” and “cannot be a coherent cosmological dipole” unless a proper likelihood model or independent systematic closure demonstrates that.
B2. The hemisphere/look-elsewhere result is internally contradictory.
Location: Table I, p. 5; Table II, p. 6; Appendix C, p. 26; Discussion, p. 16.
Table I says “No directional hemispheric asymmetry survives look-elsewhere control,” yet Table II and Appendix C report a direct-MC look-elsewhere statistic of pLEE ≤ 10⁻⁴, explicitly described as the principled look-elsewhere correction. cv_P4 cv_P4 cv_P4
If the direct max-statistic null incorporates the 648 tested directions, then pLEE ≤ 10⁻⁴ is a highly significant rejection of the random-label null after look-elsewhere correction. The paper may reasonably attribute this to systematics, but it cannot simultaneously claim that no hemispheric asymmetry survives look-elsewhere control.
Proposed fix: Replace the Table I claim with something like: “The hemisphere max-statistic rejects the random-label null after look-elsewhere correction, but the excess is attributed to survey/classifier systematics and is not interpreted cosmologically.” Remove or demote the Bonferroni “<1σ” statement unless it is clearly labelled as non-primary and not used to support the scientific claim.
B3. The z ≈ −18 “clean 1.7% dipole” exclusion is not forward-modelled through classifier dilution and label uncertainty.
Location: Abstract; Sec. III B, p. 4; Appendix D, p. 27–29; Table XIV; Fig. 10.
The manuscript gives primary cosmological weight to a block-bootstrap WLS template fit excluding a clean 1.7% dipole at z ≈ −18. It also states that the block-bootstrap uncertainty does not separately include per-galaxy classifier-label uncertainty, and that classifier dilution is handled elsewhere through injection-recovery. cv_P4 cv_P4
This is not a harmless bookkeeping issue. The paper reports an external GZ1 chirality accuracy of 69.91%, giving a rough symmetric dilution factor g = 2a − 1 ≈ 0.398. cv_P4 A true-sky Ap = 0.034 dipole would not necessarily appear as Ap = 0.034 in the measured classifier field. Comparing the fitted measured-field amplitude directly to Aref = 0.034 overstates the significance of excluding a true cosmological dipole unless the classifier response to that injected signal is explicitly forward-modelled in the same WLS/template pipeline.
Proposed fix: Recompute the template exclusion using injected true-sky dipoles passed through the full classifier/selection/noise model, or explicitly relabel z ≈ −18 as an observed-label-field template-disfavor statistic, not a cosmological exclusion. If retaining a comparison to Shamir-scale amplitudes, quote the diluted/forward-modelled significance and uncertainty, not only the observed-space z ≈ −18.
B4. The reproducibility package is not yet publication-grade.
Location: Data Availability, p. 31.
Many load-bearing claims depend on local or repository artifact paths. The Data Availability section states that artifacts currently resolve against a live main branch, that the immutable Zenodo snapshot will be deposited later, and that DOI/commit hashes will be inserted at submission time. cv_P4 It also says the catalog DOI has not yet been minted. cv_P4
For a paper whose evidential structure depends on many artifact-level checks, a live branch and future DOI placeholders are not acceptable at publication or final acceptance.
Proposed fix: Before acceptance, deposit the exact PDF, source, code, trained model, catalog, all null distributions, and all “canonical_provenance” artifacts in an immutable archive with DOI and SHA256 checksums. Replace all local paths with resolvable archived links. Provide a single reproducibility manifest mapping each numerical claim to an archived file and script.
B5. The primary sample definition and sample mismatch between estimators are not yet controlled well enough.
Location: Sec. III B, p. 4–6; Sec. IV C, p. 10–11; Table I; Table II.
The paper’s main real-space null uses the high-confidence peq > 0.6 subsample, while the WLS template exclusion uses the full Catalog C field. Table I makes both of these “primary” cosmological estimators, but on different samples. cv_P4 The paper also states explicitly that the peq > 0.6 selection function, based on uncalibrated network confidence scores, is not propagated into the block-bootstrap covariance model of the WLS fit. cv_P4
This matters because the unthresholded/full-sample real-space estimator has a z ≈ 4.2–4.4 excess that disappears only after the confidence cut. The cut may indeed be justified, but at present the distinction between “systematic diagnostic,” “primary estimator,” and “selection-defined science sample” is not clean enough for a high-precision null claim.
Proposed fix: Use one declared primary science sample for all primary cosmological statements, or present separate primary claims with separate, fully propagated selection functions. At minimum, rerun the WLS/template exclusion on the HC peq > 0.6 sample and report the full-sample WLS result only as diagnostic. The final paper should include a compact pre-analysis rationale for peq > 0.6 based on external validation or blinded criteria, not only the observed collapse of the signal after the cut.
MAJORS — should fix
M1. The pixel-permutation null is less appropriate than the label-shuffle/binomial null for the primary real-space estimator.
Location: Sec. IV C, p. 9–11.
The primary p-value is based on permuting per-pixel Ap values across the mask. This preserves the one-point distribution but not the relationship between pixel noise variance and Nspiral(p). The paper reports that per-galaxy label-shuffle tests give similar results, which is reassuring, but the label-shuffle or binomial null is the more physically appropriate primary null because it preserves per-pixel counts and heteroscedasticity. cv_P4
Proposed fix: Promote the per-galaxy label-shuffle/binomial null to primary, or present both as co-primary with a clear justification for any difference.
M2. The GZ1-human-only test is over-described as “decisive.”
Location: Sec. II, p. 3; Sec. VI A, p. 17–18.
The paper repeatedly calls the GZ1-human-only null a decisive rebuttal of pseudo-label inheritance. But the manuscript itself states that this test has only N ≈ 4.6 × 10⁴ and is therefore insensitive to the sub-percent amplitudes constrained by the headline sample; it estimates A50 ≈ 3.4% and A95 ≈ 4.5–6.8% for this human-only test. cv_P4
This is a useful cross-check against large inherited dipoles, but it cannot validate the sub-percent null or the pseudo-label independence of the full 9.5 × 10⁵ HC sample.
Proposed fix: Replace “decisive rebuttal” language with “important independent large-amplitude cross-check.” State explicitly that it does not test sub-percent inherited structure.
M3. The claim that overconfidence “cannot bias the null” is too strong.
Location: Sec. IV A, p. 7; Appendix B, p. 25–26.
The paper correctly warns that peq is not calibrated and that mean confidence 0.951 is far above the GZ1 accuracies. cv_P4 However, it then argues that miscalibration cannot bias the dipole because the estimator uses hard argmax labels. That is only true for a fixed selected sample. Since peq defines the sample, and peq can correlate with depth, morphology, seeing, redshift, or footprint, miscalibration can still bias the selected spatial field.
Proposed fix: Replace the absolute statement with a conditional one: “For a fixed selected sample, monotone recalibration does not alter hard argmax labels; however, the spatial selection induced by peq must be validated.” Add validation of confusion matrices versus depth, morphology, imaging leg, magnitude, and sky region.
M4. The harmonic-channel discussion remains confusing and should be simplified.
Location: Sec. III A–B; Sec. IV C–D; Table V; Conclusions.
The paper reports +3.64σ, +7.28σ, +7.93σ, pMC = 0.030, p = 6 × 10⁻⁴, and Gaussian-equivalent ≈1.9σ for related harmonic-channel diagnostics, while repeatedly stating they are not comparable. cv_P4 The caveats are technically helpful, but the presentation is still hard to audit.
Proposed fix: Create a single “estimator dictionary” table with exact field, mask, weighting, monopole subtraction, null, NMC, data value, null mean/std, rank-p, and role. Move most harmonic variants to an appendix. The main text should carry only the result needed for the scientific argument.
M5. The comparison to Shamir should be more carefully separated into observed-space and true-sky comparisons.
Location: Sec. V A, p. 16; Sec. VI C, p. 20; Conclusions, p. 21.
The manuscript says it does not claim a frequentist exclusion of Shamir’s Ganalyzer estimator, which is good. But it also states that a Shamir-scale real-space dipole would have been detected and that the absence is jointly expressed by +0.41σ and z ≈ −18. cv_P4 Because classifier dilution and sample selection are central, this comparison needs a matched forward model.
Proposed fix: Present three quantities separately: Shamir’s reported estimator amplitude; the expected measured amplitude under this classifier and selection; and the observed measured amplitude. Do not use the observed-field z ≈ −18 as a direct proxy for excluding Shamir’s estimator.
M6. Several theory citations in Sec. VI C appear mismatched.
Location: Sec. VI C, p. 20; References p. 32.
The text cites [20,21] for primordial magnetic fields with net helicity, but the listed references are Eskilt & Komatsu and Cosmoglobe cosmic-birefringence papers, not primordial magnetic helicity papers. The arXiv records for 2205.13962 and 2305.02268 describe cosmic birefringence constraints from WMAP/Planck/Cosmoglobe, not primordial magnetic helicity. arXiv+1 The Lue, Wang & Kamionkowski reference [17] is a CMB parity-violation/cosmic-birefringence paper; it is not accurately described as “chiral inflation models with an axial gauge coupling” without further qualification. arXiv
Proposed fix: Replace or supplement these references with appropriate papers on helical primordial magnetic fields and axial-gauge-field chiral inflation, or rewrite the paragraph to match the cited literature.
M7. The “future falsification” criterion is not a frequentist upper limit.
Location: Abstract, p. 1; Sec. VI B, p. 19–20; Conclusions, p. 22.
A95 is a 95%-recovery-at-3σ injection threshold on a coarse grid with NMC,inj = 100 per amplitude, not a confidence upper limit on the dipole amplitude. The manuscript mostly acknowledges this, but then uses A95 as a falsification boundary. cv_P4
Proposed fix: Rename this as a “recovery threshold” or “detection-efficiency threshold,” not an exclusion/falsification threshold, unless a proper upper-limit construction is added.
M8. The flip-identity QC issue needs a clearer placement in the main analysis.
Location: Data Availability, p. 31; Appendix B, p. 25.
The catalog contains a non-negligible population of rows whose reconstructed flip probabilities fall outside [0,1] by up to 0.09; 59,515 HC rows are flagged, and the authors report that excluding them does not change the dipole. cv_P4 This is likely not fatal, but it cuts against the repeated “strictly flip-equivariant” presentation.
Proposed fix: Move a concise version of this QC issue into the Methods or Results, not only Data Availability/Appendix B. State exactly which columns are affected, which are safe for downstream use, and whether Catalog C probabilities themselves or only reconstructed raw/flip quantities are implicated.
MINORS — polish and presentation
Tone down adversarial rhetoric. Phrases such as “decisive rebuttal,” “so it cannot be missed,” “single cleanest rebuttal,” “do not fabricate,” and “pod-bound” are not appropriate for MNRAS/PRD/JCAP style. Replace with neutral scientific language.
Shorten the abstract. It is too long and contains too many estimator-specific caveats. Keep the primary result, key sensitivity, and main systematic limitation; move the rest to the main text.
Reduce repetition. The paper repeats the “distinct null procedures are not comparable” caveat many times. A single estimator dictionary plus one main-text reminder would be clearer.
Use one amplitude convention table early. The Ap, fCW deviation, “full amplitude A,” and Shamir fCW-asymmetry conventions are easy to confuse. A boxed conversion table near Eq. (3) would help.
Avoid live local artifact paths in prose. Long pipelines/p2_chirality/... paths interrupt readability. Put them in a reproducibility manifest table or appendix.
Add printed line numbers in the submitted manuscript. This would make peer review much easier.
Clarify which figures are diagnostic vs. load-bearing. Fig. 8 and Fig. 9 are visually prominent and show significant harmonic excesses; captions should explicitly say whether each figure supports a primary claim or only a systematic diagnosis.
Reformat Table XIV. The rank-deficient nuisance design and enormous leg-template uncertainties are hard to read. Consider moving the full coefficient table to an appendix and keeping only the dipole-amplitude posterior in the main text.
Use “not spiral” consistently. The manuscript alternates among “NS,” “not spiral,” “non-spiral,” “edge-on,” and “morphologically indeterminate.” Define the class once and use consistent notation.
Check final references carefully. Beyond the theory-citation mismatch above, the final bibliography should be audited for exact titles, DOIs, arXiv IDs, and journal formatting before submission.
Strengths
Large and valuable data product. A chirality-labelled catalog of 8.47 million galaxies, with 3.2 million classified spirals, is a substantial community resource if archived and documented properly. cv_P4
Bias-hardening is taken seriously. The raw-to-equivariant comparison is compelling: the manuscript shows that a small classifier handedness bias can create spurious large-scale signals, and the flip-equivariant TTA design is a sensible mitigation.
The authors are unusually transparent about systematics. The paper openly reports classifier overconfidence, a significant global CW monopole, harmonic residuals, low-confidence-bin excesses, and artifact-level provenance. That transparency is valuable, even where I disagree with the interpretation.
Injection-recovery analysis is a good practice. The A50/A95 recovery curves give readers a concrete sense of the estimator’s detection efficiency, even though they should not be interpreted as formal upper limits. cv_P4
Independent human-label cross-check is useful. The GZ1-only test does not validate sub-percent amplitudes, but it is still a helpful model-independent cross-check against large-amplitude pseudo-label inheritance. cv_P4
The paper identifies an important methodological lesson. The demonstration that monopole-mask leakage and classifier bias can masquerade as dipole power is likely useful for future galaxy-chirality analyses, regardless of the final cosmological interpretation.
Bottom-line recommendation
MAJOR REVISIONS. The core high-confidence real-space null may well be publishable, and the dataset/methodology are potentially important. However, the current manuscript must substantially revise its central inference about the unexplained harmonic residual, correct the hemisphere/look-elsewhere inconsistency, archive all artifacts immutably, and reframe the z ≈ −18 template result so that classifier dilution and sample selection are handled consistently.
