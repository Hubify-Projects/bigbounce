## RAW VERBATIM

VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS
ISSUES:
[MAJOR] Abstract and Sec. IV (Scope paragraph): The central claim is framed as a "channel-level amplitude closure" of four enumerated minimal-ECH routes, yet the text repeatedly qualifies that the four routes "are not proven to be a complete diffeomorphism-invariant operator basis," that the result is "not an operator-level theorem," and that only M_Pl-power-counting completeness is asserted via the Fierz-by-Fierz lemma (App. C). This creates a material mismatch between the headline claim and the actual logical scope; the abstract and introduction must state the precise limitation up front with the same explicitness used in Sec. IV.
[MAJOR] Sec. X (Perturbation-Transparency Result): The headline statement that "torsion vanishes at all classical metric/scalar perturbation orders" and that "the Holst sector therefore decouples from all scalar/tensor perturbation equations of motion" is asserted for canonical scalar matter. The provided argument rests on the algebraic Bianchi identity R_μ[νρσ] = 0 making the Holst dual contraction vanish on the Levi-Civita connection (T = 0). While the background-level identity is standard, the text supplies only a sketch for the scalar sector and an "extension to tensor sector"; no explicit linearized Einstein equations, no verification that the Holst contribution drops from the perturbed action at quadratic order, and no treatment of metric back-reaction on the algebraic torsion constraint appear. The claim therefore lacks the explicit derivation required for a Tier-I result.
[MAJOR] Sec. IV D–E (Routes 2 and 3) and App. B: Route-2 and Route-3 closures rely on phenomenological one-loop operators (Eq. 15) and a chiral-count ansatz (Eq. 17) whose coefficients are described as "bounded EFT inputs" or "conservative upper bounds," even while citing Shapiro–Teixeira and Benedetti–Speziale. The subsequent 10^{-58}–10^{-62} suppressions are robust to O(1) rescalings, but the paper mixes derived β-function results with ad-hoc normalizations without a clean separation of what is proven versus assumed. The single-scale NDA no-go in App. B is standard dimensional analysis; its presentation as a novel "structural" closure requires explicit demonstration that the two readings (on-shell M_Pl^5 vs. off-shell coupling rescaling) do not open additional loopholes inside minimal ECH.
[MINOR] Sec. IX (14-barrier catalog): The enumeration of 13 "distinct mechanism-class constraints" (14 historical entries with B8 subsumed by B14) consolidates well-known issues (Planck suppression, thermal washout, operator decoupling, naturalness, attractor sensitivity). Several barriers overlap in physical content or are direct consequences of the perturbation-transparency result itself. The catalog would be strengthened by an explicit table or paragraph demonstrating that each probes a logically independent failure mode rather than re-labeling the same suppression or naturalness obstruction.
[MINOR] Sec. II C 1 and Sec. XIV D (Inflationary dilution and f_NL tension): The N_tot ≈ 92 bookkeeping that converts the M_Pl^4 → (meV)^4 hierarchy into a ΔN_tot ≈ 4 residual is presented as quantitative. The derivation mixes an exponential dilution factor with a (T_reh/M_GUT)^{3/2} phase-space prefactor whose justification is acknowledged as "dimensional-analysis aesthetic." The subsequent claim that this N_tot definitively erases the matter-bounce f_NL = −35/16 signature at SPHEREx scales is logically sound but rests on the same approximate dilution factor; a one-paragraph sensitivity check varying the prefactor by an order of magnitude would remove any ambiguity.
[MINOR] Overall presentation: The manuscript is self-contained for its primary logical claims (as stated), yet the density of footnotes, cross-references to six companion papers, and repeated "not load-bearing" disclaimers for numerical inputs from those companions make the reading unnecessarily laborious. Condensation of the barrier catalog (Secs. IX–XI) and clearer demarcation between novel results (perturbation transparency, explicit Fierz lemma) and standard route closures would improve accessibility without altering technical content.

One sentence on the central claim: The channel-level amplitude closures for the four enumerated routes under the stated single-scale NDA and scaling assumptions are supported by the order-of-magnitude estimates and naturalness arguments given in Sec. IV (with large suppression margins for R2/R3), while the perturbation-transparency result and power-counting completeness rest on sketches and a lemma whose full derivations are asserted but not fully displayed in the supplied sections.
