CENTRAL CLAIM: The paper’s headline result is the construction of a validated, reproducible catalog-grade subset of 268,519 unique reconstruction-outlier anomalies (268,319 point-source) from a native-trained BigAE autoencoder scan of 37.3 million spectra and map patches across six archives, supported by per-survey stability (Jaccard) and sensitivity (injection-recovery) gates for the dominant broad/continuum-dominated class; this is presented as a process-volume resource rather than a list of confirmed physical detections, with the like-for-like science-target benchmark being only 2,468 DESI clusters. The claim is supported by the detailed methodology (§II), explicit tier definitions and exclusions, survey-by-survey validation results (§III), cross-survey analysis (§IV), and repeated emphasis on committed reproducibility scripts and artifacts.

ISSUES:

Three-tier structure and scope of “validated catalog-grade” introduced too late for a data-release paper. The critical distinction among (i) the 268,519 validated subset (DESI + SDSS native + Planck native + NEOWISE), (ii) the separately-released eROSITA 298-member list (excised for irreproducible score axis + 1.2% recovery), and (iii) the LAMOST exploratory tier (~113k objects, 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact, 5.8% injection-recovery FAIL) is only fully defined in §III. While the abstract correctly flags the exclusions and the “process-volume” framing, readers can still mis-cite the headline number as including LAMOST or eROSITA. Move a concise, self-contained paragraph defining the three tiers, which surveys contribute to the 268,519, and the exact gate each passed/failed to the end of the abstract or a new early subsection in §I (before any numbers are quoted). This is a presentation issue, not a scientific flaw—the paper already does the hard work of excision and disclosure.
Consolidation of validation-gate status per survey is scattered. The text correctly states that only DESI (broad class), SDSS (continuum-dip), and Planck pass detector-sensitivity injection-recovery, while NEOWISE passes a geometry-QA test by construction and eROSITA/LAMOST fail. However, there is no single table or paragraph that maps every survey contributing to the 268,519 headline to (a) its exact gate type, (b) quantitative result (e.g., DESI 99–100% at 5σ for broad class; narrow lines only ≥15σ), and (c) whether selection was in-sample or held-out. Adding this as a compact summary table (or expanded Table I footnote block) in §III or §VI would make the meaning of “validated catalog-grade” unambiguous at a glance without forcing the reader to cross-reference multiple subsections and footnotes. The underlying tests themselves appear sound and are adequately caveated where limitations exist (e.g., Planck held-out over-representation test, spatial-correlation caveat on the p-value).
Selection of the 5,384 QSO-candidate subsample used for the multi-tracer fNL demonstration (§V) is not stated in the main text. The abstract reports a Landy–Szalay bias measurement yielding α_jk = 0.19 ± 0.65 on this sample and correctly concludes there is no multi-tracer improvement at current S/N. However, the precise definition of the sample (which anomaly tier(s), redshift cuts, confidence threshold, or additional filters) is not given in the visible sections; it appears only as a number. Even if full details live in the companion pipeline, the main text must contain a one-paragraph description sufficient for a reader to understand the tracer selection without external artifacts. This is especially important because the fNL result is presented as a methodological demonstration feeding into the broader Big Bounce program. (The paper’s framing that neither cosmological application constitutes a detection is already appropriate and does not require change.)

These are all addressable with targeted revisions to structure, consolidation, and explicitness; none rise to unsupported claims, statistical errors, or undisclosed fatal limitations. The manuscript already demonstrates unusual rigor for a catalog paper through self-audit (removal of synthetic Gaia tier, excision of eROSITA on provenance grounds), committed reproducibility artifacts, and consistent qualification of what “validated” and “process-volume” actually mean. With the three clarifications above, the work would be suitable for PRD.
