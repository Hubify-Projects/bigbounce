## Full verbatim response

I understand the immense technical effort and pipeline engineering required to process 37.3 million sources across multiple archives. However, evaluating this manuscript against the rigorous standards of Physical Review D requires a candid assessment of its cosmological impact and methodological readiness.

VERDICT
REJECT

ISSUES
[MAJOR] Scope and Journal Fit (Sections I and V): The authors state that the "primary, headline deliverable of this work is a validated catalog-grade subset" of astronomical anomalies. The cosmological applications regarding primordial non-Gaussianity constraints (fNL) and the NANOGrav background spectral index are explicitly presented as "secondary demonstrations" that do not yield statistically significant improvements or detections. Because the primary contribution is a multi-wavelength observational catalog rather than a fundamental physics result, this manuscript is distinctly out of scope for Physical Review D and should be submitted to an astrophysical data journal (e.g., ApJS or A&A).
[MAJOR] Methodological Irreproducibility (Section III E): The manuscript admits that the eROSITA anomaly score axis threshold is entirely irreproducible under any of 16 monotone rescalings due to an undocumented post-hoc production step. A peer-reviewed catalog cannot rely on a hardcoded, mathematically opaque "membership list"; the selection criteria must be rigorously reproducible.
[MAJOR] Cosmological Constraint Rigor (Section V A): The use of a decoupled KDE likelihood for a free-spectrum refit to test the matter-bounce prediction (γ=3.0) ignores inter-bin covariance. Furthermore, the authors acknowledge that environmental effects on supermassive black hole binaries can produce spectral indices consistent with their recovered γ=2.567, which critically undermines the physical significance of the derived Bayes factor.
[MINOR] Manuscript Clutter and Formatting (Section III G): The inclusion of extensive discussion regarding a removed "synthetic-placeholder fallback" for Gaia clutters the manuscript. Because it contributes zero objects to the final catalog, this pipeline failure should be relegated to a brief footnote.
[MINOR] Narrative Tone: The text reads heavily like an internal pipeline debugging log, repeatedly citing specific .py and .json script paths in the prose, which must be formalized and moved to an appendix, footnotes, or a data availability statement for publication.

CENTRAL CLAIM
The central claim—that a multi-survey autoencoder pipeline can systematically assemble a validated anomaly catalog—is supported by the rigorous cross-validation and injection-recovery testing on the primary DESI and SDSS subsets, but the cosmological claims presented to justify its physics relevance lack the statistical power to provide meaningful new constraints.
