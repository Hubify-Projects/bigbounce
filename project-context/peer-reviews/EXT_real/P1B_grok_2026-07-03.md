## RAW RESPONSE (verbatim)

This is a clean, well-scoped technical companion paper (P1B) to your concurrent P1(a) structural-closure no-go result. It does exactly what a reproducibility/consistency-check companion should: it documents three limited numerical cross-checks with explicit scope limitations stated upfront and repeatedly, provides frozen chains + full reproducibility artifacts, and avoids overstating what the analyses can claim.

Overall Assessment

Strengths (these are load-bearing and well-executed):

Scope discipline is excellent. The abstract, introduction, Sec. III scope statement, Sec. IV scope note, Sec. VI framing, and conclusions all hammer the same point: none of the three analyses implements or tests a torsion-modified Boltzmann module or verifies the ECH spin-torsion sector. They are adjacent cross-checks (stock-CAMB generic-radiation proxy, foreground-free synthetic pipeline validation, and literature-data ALP accommodation). This framing is honest and protects the main P1(a) result.
First-principles ECH ΔN_eff derivation (Sec. III A). Integrating out the algebraic torsion to the Planck-suppressed four-fermion interaction and showing ρ_tor/ρ_rad ~ (T/M_Pl)^2 → ΔN_eff^(ECH) ~ 10^{-44} at BBN (and even smaller at recombination) is exactly the right move. It correctly reframes the stock-CAMB run as a ~40-order-of-magnitude conservative observational envelope rather than a proxy for an unknown ECH prediction. This is one of the strongest parts of the paper.
NaMaster pipeline validation (Sec. IV). 500 MC realizations, robustness battery (apodization scale, sky fraction, purification, BB shape, estimator weighting), explicit attribution of the ~12% multiplicative under-recovery + worst-case |bias| = 0.040° to the unweighted χ² template fit (dominant) + injected-BB shape (secondary), and clear statement that this is a pipeline-validation figure on synthetic CMB-only skies, not a sky-detection significance or real-sky systematics bound. The sign-symmetric recovery check and f_sky-independence are nice. Carrying the worst-case bias forward transparently is the right conservative choice.
Spectator-ALP consistency check (Sec. VI). The prior-predictive Monte Carlo (11.6% of draws within 1σ of β_obs at fixed C_aγ = 8; drops to 6.1% under the broader continuous prior) is a good way to quantify “accommodation cost” and push back against both “tautological” and “knife-edge fine-tuning” framings. The disclosure of the two independent tunings required for a spectator-safe interpretation (C_aγ ≳ 8–10 even in the posterior-preferred saturated-displacement regime; θ_i ~ 0.1 or ~25× relative to the natural-prior midpoint for Ω_a < 0.01) is appropriately prominent (including in the abstract and main text, not buried). Table IV subset readout is clear and useful. The conclusion that “the model accommodates but does not predict” and that the same β arises in standard GR (not a distinctive ECH signature) is correctly stated.
Reproducibility practices. Committed frozen chains (two dataset combinations, 309k raw samples), Cobaya YAMLs, NaMaster driver + robustness scripts, ALP ODE + MCMC configs, HuggingFace datasets, IMPLEMENTATION_MAP.md, KNOWN_GAPS.md, claims-classification table (Table V), and transparent handling of the column-permutation bug in the legacy JSON export are all best-practice. The version-stamp commit + CHANGELOG discipline is exactly what makes this auditable.
Model-comparison and nested-sampling deferral. Correctly left for follow-up work; no over-claiming of preference statistics here.
Suggestions for Polish / Strengthening (constructive)

These are mostly framing/readability or minor robustness points, not fundamental issues:

Title precision. The current title (“Technical Reproducibility and Consistency-Check Companion to the ECH Spin-Torsion Program”) is slightly broader than the content supports. Since every section stresses that none of the analyses tests the torsion sector itself, a tighter phrasing such as “Technical Reproducibility Companion to the ECH Spin-Torsion Structural Closure No-Go” or “... to Paper I(a)” would align even more closely with the repeated scope statements.
ALP prior-predictive paragraph (abstract + Sec. VI). The 11.6%/23.9% (fixed C_aγ = 8) and 6.1%/12.6% (continuous) numbers are valuable. A single additional sentence interpreting the scale—“a genuinely tautological model would yield order-unity fractions; a knife-edge tuning would yield ≪1%; the observed one-in-eight to one-in-twenty range places the signal in a reachable but non-generic corner”—already appears and is good; you could make it even punchier.
NaMaster bias attribution. The robustness battery cleanly isolates the dominant contribution (unweighted estimator equal-weighting noise-dominated high-ℓ bins) and secondary (injected-BB shape). You might add one short clause noting that the ~12% multiplicative factor is tied to the specific unweighted χ² template fit chosen for direct comparability with published NaMaster birefringence driver scripts; the inverse-variance-weighted variant removes ~80% of it. This keeps the “not a real-sky systematics bound” disclaimer front-and-center while giving readers context.
ΔN_eff one-sided vs. two-sided limits. The explanation and post-processing (renormalizing the posterior on ΔN_eff ≥ 0 and reading the 95th percentile) is clear and physically motivated. The full-tension one-sided 95% upper limit < 0.31 and Planck+BAO+SN < 0.40 are useful; keep both conventions as you do.
Cross-paper linkage to P1(a). The “Imported theory results” summary (13 barriers, perturbation-transparency theorem, surviving f_NL = −35/8 for the minimal w=0 matter-bounce class) is helpful for standalone reading. A tiny one-row table or explicit sentence mapping “which of the three checks here most directly contextualizes which barrier class” would make the companion relationship even tighter (even if the mapping is intentionally loose, since these are null-consistency checks).
LiteBIRD forecast sentence. The ~9σ illustrative detection significance against β = 0 is fine, but the immediate clarification that it is not a model-discrimination forecast between spectator-ALP and other parity-violating mechanisms (and that LiteBIRD alone cannot separate the spectator-ALP fiducial 0.27° from the current central value at high significance) is already present and should stay prominent.
Minor readability. A few long sentences in the abstract and Sec. VI could be split without losing precision. The repeated scope reminders are intentional and effective, but in the final journal version you can trim one or two for flow while keeping the core disclaimers.
Scientific Takeaways (at the level of the scoped claims)
The stock-CAMB ΛCDM+ΔN_eff proxy is consistent with zero and recovers Planck-like H_0; the bespoke ECH four-fermion contribution is negligible at the epochs probed, so there is no tension.
The synthetic-pipeline recovery bias is characterized at the level needed for a methods validation; the published Planck/ACT DR6 sky significances remain the primary observational reference.
The spectator-ALP model can accommodate the Eskilt–Komatsu β_obs within the scan-prior envelope, but only near the upper edge in coupling/displacement and with the misalignment tuning required for true spectator status (Ω_a < 0.01 subset = 13% posterior mass). This is correctly presented as a genuine (if not parameter-free) consistency check rather than a prediction or distinctive ECH signature.

Nothing in the three analyses constitutes evidence for a bounce cosmology; they are compatibility/null-consistency checks that sit comfortably alongside the structural no-go of P1(a). The distinctive surviving target remains the matter-bounce f_NL = −35/8 (Paper II territory).

Bottom line: This is a rigorous, transparent, and appropriately modest companion paper. The reproducibility artifacts and honest scope statements make it easy to audit and build on. The ALP section in particular does a good job of quantifying both the accommodation and the tunings required.
