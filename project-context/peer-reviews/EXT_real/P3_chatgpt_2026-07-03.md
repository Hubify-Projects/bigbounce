## Full verbatim response

(1) VERDICT: REJECT.
(2) ISSUES:
[MAJOR] Abstract/§III/VII "validated catalog-grade 268,519" claim: the validation standard is internally inconsistent, since the count includes NEOWISE on a mask-geometry QA that is explicitly not a detector-sensitivity test, Planck on an in-sample top-200 selection, SDSS on a fixed-size continuity slice rather than a physically motivated threshold, and DESI only for broad/extended anomalies, so the manuscript does not justify calling the aggregate a validated catalog-grade anomaly set.
[MAJOR] Abstract/§III A/§VI E/§VII size-comparison claim: the advertised ∼73×, ∼100×, and ∼141× increases are not like-for-like scientific catalog comparisons, and the paper itself states that the science-target-restricted DESI recount is 2,468 objects, ≈0.92× the cited Liang et al. benchmark; the headline scale claim is therefore a process-throughput claim, not a validated anomaly-catalog advance.
[MAJOR] §II B/§III E/§III G reproducibility: the eROSITA score axis is admitted to be irreproducible, Gaia was a synthetic placeholder only removed after audit, several results depend on private committed artifacts and pod paths, and the data/model release is described as future rather than available at review; this is inadequate for a submission whose central deliverable is a recomputable catalog.
[MAJOR] §II D/§VI D validation gates: the validation thresholds are heuristic and heterogeneous, mixing validation loss, injection recovery, Jaccard overlap, and geometry checks across incomparable data types; the manuscript admits these were engineering thresholds rather than statistically powered criteria, so "PASS" labels cannot support the scientific strength claimed.
[MAJOR] §III C/§VI B SDSS treatment: the manuscript alternates between presenting SDSS cross-transfer anomalies as astrophysically organized populations and replacing them with a native re-score in which strict S > 5 yields only 12 objects; Table III and Fig. 4 characterize a cross-transfer failure mode, not the released native catalog, making the SDSS catalog content scientifically ambiguous.
[MAJOR] §III D/§VII LAMOST contribution: the inclusive 377,780 total includes a ∼113,000-object LAMOST tier that is explicitly a 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact and fails injection recovery, yet the total is repeatedly used to frame the scale and importance of the work; a known-failed systematic artifact should not be part of a headline scientific catalog.
[MAJOR] §III A DESI anomaly population: ∼98.7% of DESI anomaly clusters are stated to fall on sky-fiber/filler/non-primary-class spectra, but the manuscript still treats the full-stream DESI anomaly count as a major catalog result without demonstrating that these spectra correspond to scientifically meaningful astrophysical objects rather than fiber, sky, calibration, or targeting artifacts.
[MAJOR] §IV A novelty assessment: the paper first reports large SIMBAD-unmatched fractions, then acknowledges that extended catalog matching reduces DESI top-1,000 genuine novelty to 17.8% and that SDSS/eROSITA/NEOWISE SIMBAD-unmatched samples were 100% identifiable in broader archival searches; the term "genuine novelty" remains overclaimed without full-catalog, local-density, and survey-depth-controlled cross-matching.
[MAJOR] §V fNL application: the empirical bias measurement α = 0.19 ± 0.65 is consistent with zero, the de-biased estimate gives no improvement over the single-tracer baseline, and the tracer selection lacks a demonstrated selection function; the cosmological forecast is therefore not a result suitable for PRD except possibly as a speculative appendix.
[MAJOR] §V A NANOGrav application: the Bayes factor compares against an idealized circular SMBHB spectral index using a simplified free-spectrum KDE refit rather than a full PTA timing-data likelihood or realistic SMBHB population model; it is not connected to the anomaly catalog and should not be presented as supporting bounce cosmology.
[MAJOR] §III B high-z QSO candidates: the z ≈ 6 candidates rely on Redrock template redshifts at low continuum S/N and selection cuts, with no independent spectral confirmation, no robust contamination estimate, and no quantitative comparison to known high-z QSO selection methods; the claim that these are the most scientifically compelling DESI anomalies is not established.
[MAJOR] §II B scoring and preprocessing: per-survey score definitions, thresholds, and preprocessing are not uniform; tabular scalers were fit on full samples, Planck uses raw MSE, eROSITA uses a membership-only score, SDSS uses multiple incompatible thresholds, and S values are not cross-survey comparable, undermining the premise of a coherent multi-survey catalog.
[MINOR] Figures 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and Appendix F: several figures retain quarantined, removed, or cross-transfer historical artifacts, including ACT and Gaia, which makes the visual narrative misleading for the actual released catalog.
[MINOR] §IV B spatial analysis: the HEALPix χ² test is correctly caveated as dominated by survey footprint geometry, so it should either be removed or replaced by a selection-function-aware test; in its current form it adds significance-looking numbers without interpretable astrophysical content.
[MINOR] Presentation throughout: the manuscript is excessively burdened with caveats, parenthetical reconciliations, path names, and self-corrections, which makes the scientific claims hard to audit and is not acceptable as a PRD article without substantial restructuring.
(3) The central claim is not supported by the evidence presented, because the stated validated catalog is assembled from heterogeneous, partly non-detection-based, partly failed, and partly non-reproducible components rather than from a uniform, independently validated anomaly-detection pipeline.
