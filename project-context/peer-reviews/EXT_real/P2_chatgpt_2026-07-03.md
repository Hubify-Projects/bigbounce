[MAJOR] Abstract/Scope and Secs. III–IV — the headline SPHEREx significances are not independently forecasted but are obtained by rescaling a single local-template Fisher forecast, with the manuscript itself stating that no independent bispectrum Fisher matrix is constructed and that cross-parameter correlations are neglected; this is insufficient to support a PRD-level quantitative forecast for a non-local bounce bispectrum.

[MAJOR] Secs. II–III, template-overlap factor r=0.84 — the central degradation factor is built from a reconstructed polynomial basis constrained by only three benchmark configurations while six coefficients remain underdetermined; fitting all intermediate triangle dependence from a null-space scan is not a derivation of the Cai et al. bispectrum shape, so the quoted r, the 5.2–5.5σ significance, and the later Bayes-factor bookkeeping rest on an uncontrolled shape model.

[MAJOR] Appendix A and Sec. II C, Cai/Li factor-of-two claim — the generic in-in identity i⟨[ζ^3,L]⟩=−2Im⟨ζ^3 L⟩ does not demonstrate that Li et al. omitted a physical time-ordering or that their result is merely a single-ordering intermediate; the manuscript does not map Li et al.'s full conventions, Hamiltonian sign, permutation factors, field normalization, and local-template normalization into Cai et al.'s calculation, so the claimed closure of the factor-of-two ambiguity is not established.

[MAJOR] Sec. II C and Conclusion, assumption of faithful cubic transfer through the bounce — the prediction f_NL=−35/8 is explicitly conditional on third-order bispectrum transmission through the nonsingular bounce, but only linear transfer is cited and the cubic statement is supported only by an order-of-magnitude superhorizon scaling estimate; this is a load-bearing theoretical gap, not a small caveat.

[MAJOR] Secs. IV and VII, systematic budget — the lower endpoint of the 2.6–5.5σ range is produced by additive quadrature of template mismatch, b_ϕ, GR projection, photo-z, and other effects without a joint covariance or nuisance Fisher analysis; because these effects are correlated with f_NL, b_1, b_ϕ, redshift selection, and relativistic projection terms, the quoted "realistic" envelope is not a calibrated confidence forecast.

[MAJOR] Secs. III–IV, use of a scalar template projection for a galaxy-bispectrum survey — replacing the full bounce-template estimator by σ(f_NL^bounce)=σ(f_NL^local)/r ignores the dependence of the galaxy-bispectrum covariance on triangle configuration, redshift binning, nonlinear bias, RSD, survey window functions, shot noise, and nuisance marginalization; a shape cosine or amplitude overlap is not a substitute for a bounce-template Fisher matrix.

[MAJOR] Sec. VI and Tables II–III, Bayesian comparison — the Bayes factors compare a point or Gaussian prior centered at the bounce value to arbitrary uniform priors in f_NL, using mock detections centered on the bounce prediction; this is a prior-volume exercise, not a model evidence calculation over actual inflationary or bounce parameter spaces, and the Monte Carlo validation only samples the assumptions already built into the analytic formula.

[MAJOR] Sec. IX.D, joint (f_NL,n_fNL) SDB Fisher forecast — this is presented as a newly computed Fisher result but the manuscript does not give the full covariance, tracer definitions, number densities, redshift kernels, bias priors, k-cuts, window treatment, or derivatives needed to reproduce or referee the calculation; referring to external code artifacts is not enough for a PRD submission.

[MAJOR] Secs.
