Gemini said Here is a formal, rigorous peer review of the submitted manuscript for Physical Review D.Referee ReportManuscript: "A Null Chirality Dipole in 8.5 Million DESI Galaxies from Equivariant Deep Learning"
Author: Houston Golden
Journal: Physical Review DSummaryThe author presents a large-scale analysis of spiral galaxy chirality using a dataset of 8.47 million galaxies from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys. By applying a Vision Transformer with a test-time augmentation (TTA) protocol to enforce flip-equivariance, the author finds a chirality dipole consistent with a null result (z = +0.41, p = 0.31). The manuscript effectively demonstrates how spatial variations in unmitigated classifier bias, coupled with survey mask geometry, can masquerade as highly significant cosmological dipoles. The paper directly challenges previous literature claims of parity-violating dipoles at the 1.7% to 4.0% scale.  PDF+ 4While the methodology is extensive and the identification of the monopole-mask leakage channel is a valuable contribution to the field, the manuscript contains several critical vulnerabilities regarding unmodeled systematics, pseudo-label dependence, and sample selection that must be addressed before it meets the stringent standards of Physical Review D. The ~47% Unmodeled Harmonic Residual Must Be ClosedContext: The author reports highly significant post-MASTER harmonic residuals at l=1 (+3.64 sigma on the canonical mask, +7.28 sigma on the apodized footprint). An imaging/morphology forward model only accounts for ~53% of this residual amplitude, explicitly leaving ~47% as an "open item".  PDF+ 2Critique: For a paper whose central thesis is the stringent control of systematics to establish a null cosmological measurement, leaving nearly half of a highly significant low-l residual unexplained is unacceptable. While the author bounds this unmodeled remainder below the real-space recovery threshold of A50 = 0.75% , arguing that it would not trigger a real-space detection regardless of origin, this is a phenomenological band-aid. The author hypothesizes that this ~47% is driven by a "confidence-vs-depth response" but explicitly defers the GPU compute required to prove this to future work.  PDF+ 2Action Required: The author must execute the deferred per-pixel classifier confidence-vs-depth forward modeling. A definitive systematic attribution requires a closed budget. If the remainder cannot be modeled away, the physical origin of the residual must be treated with far more statistical caution.2. Circularity and Dependence on CE-ResNet Pseudo-LabelsContext: 66.5% of the classifier's training labels are derived from CE-ResNet predictions rather than independent human truth. The author attempts to prove independence via a Galaxy Zoo 1 (GZ1) human-label-only cross-check.  PDF+ 3Critique: The GZ1 cross-check yields a null result, but as the author admits, its statistical sensitivity is poor due to sample size, yielding a recovery floor of A50 ~ 3.4% and A95 between 4.5% and 6.8%. Therefore, the sub-percent sensitivity constraints (A95 between 1.0% and 1.5%)  rely entirely on a model heavily trained by CE-ResNet. The argument that the TTA procedure  washes out inherited bias is well-taken, but if CE-ResNet contains an underlying spatially correlated bias that survives TTA, this pipeline would inherit it.  PDF+ 3Action Required: The author must train a strict "GZ1-only" baseline model (0% CE-ResNet labels) and evaluate the dipole on the resulting full DESI footprint. Even if overall accuracy drops, establishing that a purely independent model achieves a sub-percent null is required to break the circularity.3. Spatial Selection Bias via Uncalibrated Confidence ThresholdsContext: The primary real-space dipole measurement applies a high-confidence cut of peq​>0.6. However, the classifier is explicitly noted to be "strongly overconfident," with a mean confidence of 0.951 versus a true three-class accuracy of 58.7%.  PDF+ 2Critique: The author defends this by noting that a monotone recalibration does not alter the hard argmax labels. This is true for the labels, but false for the selection function. Because the raw scores are highly miscalibrated, a static cut at 0.6 across the sky will inevitably interact with local seeing, depth, and PSF variations, creating a spatially varying selection function. The author confirms that the discarded low-confidence tail (peq​≤0.5) contains a massive systematic excess (z ~ 4.0 to 4.3). By cutting on an uncalibrated metric, the author risks leaking this depth-correlated systematic unevenly into the primary sample.
