3. MAJOR — The manuscript manufactures an unphysical “null-space uncertainty” after claiming to know the full vertex-derived polynomial
Sections II.A and III.B describe six coefficients constrained by three benchmark values and consequently introduce a three-dimensional null space. Ten thousand arbitrary coefficient sets are then sampled within basis-dependent Euclidean balls, producing r=0.85±0.13r=0.85\pm0.13r=0.85±0.13.
This procedure is not a physical uncertainty model. The coefficients of a bispectrum generated by a specified cubic action are determined by that action, not by fitting three benchmark points. The manuscript itself provides a purported exact vertex-derived polynomial in Eq. (A4), which fixes all coefficients. Alternative coefficient vectors that merely reproduce three values generally do not correspond to any interaction Hamiltonian or in-in calculation.
The manuscript correctly admits that the uniform measure and radius are basis-dependent. That admission removes the statistical meaning of the quoted distribution, percentiles, and “distributional floor.” The claim that the samples are “physical” because they reproduce three benchmark configurations and have high shape cosine is unjustified.
Required fix: Delete the null-space distribution as a theoretical uncertainty unless a genuine family of cubic actions or physically allowed parameter variations is constructed. Numerical uncertainty should instead arise from identified model parameters, numerical integration errors, or controlled approximations. A fixed action gives a fixed shape.
