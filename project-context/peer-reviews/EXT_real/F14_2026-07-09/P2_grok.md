Manuscript: "Testing the Matter Bounce with Primordial Non-Gaussianity: A SPHEREx Sensitivity Recast with a MegaMapper Outlook" by Houston Golden
Summary of assessment
The manuscript resolves a long-standing factor-of-two discrepancy in the matter-bounce local bispectrum amplitude (Cai et al. vs. Li et al.), adopts the corrected central value fNLlocal=−35/16  f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local} = -35/16  fNLlocal​=−35/16, and recasts published SPHEREx/MegaMapper sensitivity forecasts onto this benchmark. It includes an explicit vertex-level re-summation (Appendix A), an independent tree-level multi-tracer bispectrum Fisher validation, a null-space analysis of the underdetermined polynomial representation, a template-overlap factor r≈0.84  r \approx 0.84  r≈0.84, a systematic budget, and a Bayesian model comparison. The work is technically ambitious, transparent about assumptions and limitations, and provides committed code for reproducibility.
The central theoretical claim (the corrected amplitude) is supported by the algebra presented. The forecast is a sensitivity recast, not an independent end-to-end calculation, and is appropriately caveated. Statistical methodology is careful and honest about heuristic elements. No fabricated or overstated problems are identified below.
Specific concerns (classified MAJOR/MINOR)
MINOR — Appendix A, Table VI and the vertex re-summation (central claim of discrepancy resolution)
The re-summation of Cai et al.’s four cubic vertices at ϵ=3/2  \epsilon = 3/2  ϵ=3/2 yielding exactly −35/16  -35/16  −35/16 in the squeezed limit (Eqs. A1, A3), the identification of the spurious +(99/128)∑iki3  +(99/128)\sum_i k_i^3  +(99/128)∑i​ki3​ term in the printed polynomial (Eq. A2), and the cross-check against Li et al.’s general-cs  c_s  cs​ formula and Cai’s own ϵ  \epsilon  ϵ-ordered intermediates (Eq. A5) constitute the load-bearing theoretical result. The algebra is presented with exact fractions, per-vertex tables (VI, VII), and intermediate polynomials (A4). However, Table VI is transcribed from the arXiv .tex source without line-by-line equation references for each monomial coefficient in the four vertices. While the committed script p2_vertex_check.py allows verification, a referee (or reader) cannot audit the transcription step without running external code or re-deriving the mode-function contractions from scratch. This is a clarity/completeness issue for a claim that settles an 8-year literature discrepancy; it does not undermine the result itself but should be addressed.
MINOR — Sec. II C (assumption (d): faithful cubic-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce)
This is correctly identified as the largest model-dependence (starred caveat in the abstract). The argument upgrades a dimensional estimate δfNL≲10−3  \delta f_{\rm NL} \lesssim 10^{-3}  δfNL​≲10−3 to a bounded O((kηbounce)2)≈10−4  O((k\eta_{\rm bounce})^2) \approx 10^{-4}  O((kηbounce​)2)≈10−4 correction via nonlinear superhorizon ζ  \zeta  ζ-conservation for a single-clock adiabatic mode (Lyth–Malik–Sasaki / Maldacena–δN  \delta N  δN), noting that effective LQC adds no new scalar degree of freedom. The logic is sound and consistent with the Wilson–Ewing framework (linear-order verification cited in [4]). However, the step from linear conservation to the cubic correlator is presented as a direct extension of the separate-universe argument without an explicit intermediate expression showing how the cubic interaction Hamiltonian transforms under the bounce (or why gradient corrections remain parametrically O((kη)2)  O((k\eta)^2)  O((kη)2) at third order). A short clarifying paragraph or one additional equation would make the bound fully self-contained at the level expected for a PRD theory+forecast paper.
MINOR — Sec. IV and Sec. VII (statistical methodology: template mismatch recast + additive-quadrature systematic budget)
The recast of Heinrich et al.’s σ(fNL)≈0.7  \sigma(f_{\rm NL}) \approx 0.7  σ(fNL​)≈0.7 via the noise-weighted overlap r=0.84±0.02  r = 0.84 \pm 0.02  r=0.84±0.02 (Eq. 6), validated by the independent Fisher yielding reff≈0.99  r_{\rm eff} \approx 0.99  reff​≈0.99, is correctly implemented and the distinction between flat-shape cosine and survey-optimal recovery is well explained. The systematic budget (template mismatch, ϵ  \epsilon  ϵ-correction, photo-z  z  z, bϕ  b_\phi  bϕ​ marginalization, GR projection, null-space scatter) is combined in additive quadrature and explicitly labeled a “scoping sensitivity envelope” rather than a joint-covariance forecast. This is honest and appropriate for the scope. The only residual issue is that the text does not explicitly state why a full joint marginalization over the nuisance parameters was not performed (computational cost of re-deriving the Heinrich covariance with the bounce template vs. the recast scope). A single sentence would remove any appearance of over-precision in the 1.3  1.3  1.3–2.75σ  2.75\sigma  2.75σ range.
MINOR — Sec. II A (null-space analysis of the degree-9 polynomial)
The 10,000-sample scan over the 3-dimensional null space of the underdetermined monomial basis, yielding r=0.85±0.13  r = 0.85 \pm 0.13  r=0.85±0.13 and rcos>0.97  r_{\rm cos} > 0.97  rcos​>0.97, is a valuable robustness check. The paper correctly notes that the scatter is basis-dependent and that the independent Fisher (which works directly with the physical bispectrum) bypasses it. However, the headline noise-weighted central value r=0.84  r = 0.84  r=0.84 is taken from a specific set of weightings rather than the median of the null-space distribution; while this is conservative and justified, the text could briefly note that the Fisher validation already demonstrates robustness to the polynomial ambiguity.
MINOR — Overall clarity and length
The manuscript is exceptionally thorough (38 pages, multiple appendices, committed artifacts). This is a strength for reproducibility but produces some repetition (e.g., repeated emphasis on the Cai–Li resolution across abstract, introduction, Sec. II, and Appendix A) and dense cross-referencing. No scientific content needs removal, but minor streamlining of repeated caveats would improve readability without loss of rigor.
No MAJOR concerns were identified that would block publication. The discrepancy resolution is algebraically supported, the forecast is transparently a recast with explicit conditionals, the independent Fisher validation strengthens the statistical claims, and all limitations (especially assumption (d)) are prominently flagged rather than minimized.
Recommendation
MINOR REVISIONS
The manuscript makes a genuine contribution by cleanly resolving the Cai–Li discrepancy with verifiable algebra and by providing a carefully caveated sensitivity forecast. The requested revisions are limited to improved auditability of the central derivation, a modest expansion of the cubic-transmission argument, and minor clarifications on the heuristic nature of the systematic budget.
