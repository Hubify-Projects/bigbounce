This manuscript presents a very large DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys galaxy-chirality catalog and uses a flip-equivariant Vision Transformer pipeline to test for a large-scale projected chirality dipole. The catalog scale, public release, explicit estimator hierarchy, confidence-threshold sweep, injection tests, and unusually extensive systematics auditing are significant strengths. The central empirical result—that the selected high-confidence catalog does not show a statistically significant real-space dipole—is supported by the reported calculations.
However, several stronger claims concerning physical sensitivity, exclusion of dipoles, and attribution of the harmonic residual are not yet statistically justified. In particular, the analysis currently conflates an observed-label sensitivity calibration with sensitivity to an underlying physical chirality field, and the block-bootstrap “z≃−18z\simeq-18z≃−18” statistic is not a valid calibrated exclusion test. These are publication-blocking issues because both quantities are promoted as primary cosmological results.

MAJOR — The unresolved spatially varying classifier response prevents the claimed conversion of the high-confidence null into a robust cosmological null at sub-percent amplitudes.
The full-sample real-space analysis gives a z≃4.0z\simeq4.0z≃4.0–4.34.34.3 excess, whereas imposing peq>0.6p_{\rm eq}>0.6peq​>0.6 reduces it to 0.41σ0.41\sigma0.41σ while discarding roughly 70% of classified spirals. The confidence sweep in Sec. Confidence depends on image quality, morphology, inclination, redshift, depth, and PSF, all of which vary spatially and can modulate both signal response and selection.
The manuscript concedes that the peq>0.6p_{\rm eq}>0.6peq​>0.6 selection function is not propagated into the WLS covariance, that classifier confidence-versus-depth response remains unmodelled, and that approximately 47% of the harmonic residual amplitude remains unexplained. A null in a selected high-confidence subset is a valid result for that subset, but it cannot automatically be interpreted as a bound on the parent population or a physical dipole without measuring the subset’s spatially dependent transfer function.
The claim that inherited or classifier-related effects can only bias the dipole away from zero is also too strong. Spatially varying sensitivity or sign-dependent confusion can attenuate, rotate, or partially cancel a real dipole. Equivariance under image reflection removes one important algorithmic handedness bias, but does not guarantee spatially invariant physical-label response.

Minor concerns
MINOR — The primary pixel-permutation null is less physically appropriate than the per-galaxy label-shuffle null.
Permuting ApA_pAp​ among pixels does not preserve the relation between pixel variance and Nspiral(p)N_{\rm spiral}(p)Nspiral​(p), and uniform pixel weighting makes this heteroskedasticity relevant. The per-galaxy label shuffle preserves the count geometry and returns a similar result, which is reassuring.
Required fix: make the per-galaxy label-shuffle or binomial conditional null the primary calibration and retain pixel permutation as a robustness test. Explain whether the fitted monopole is re-estimated in every realization.
MINOR — The paper inconsistently treats the WLS analysis as both primary and diagnostic.
Table I marks the WLS 1.7%1.7\%1.7% test as “primary,” while Sec. III B calls it a diagnostic cross-check on a different, unthresholded sample and notes that the primary confidence-selection function is not propagated into its covariance. This ambiguity is repeated in the Abstract and Conclusions.
Required fix: choose one status. Under the current methodology, it should be a secondary template-disfavor diagnostic until a calibrated hypothesis test is supplied.
MINOR — The claim that monotone recalibration cannot affect the selected sample is not generally correct.
Appendix B(g) states that monotone recalibration merely relabels the numerical threshold and isolates the same high-confidence sample. That is true for a single globally monotone scalar transformation. Class-dependent, domain-dependent, or conditional calibration can change rankings and therefore sample membership. Such conditional calibration is precisely what may be required given survey-depth and morphology dependence.
Required fix: qualify this statement to global strictly monotone recalibrations applied identically to every source and class.

MINOR — The discussion of edge-on contamination overstates what equivariance guarantees for hard labels.
Appendix E first argues that edge-on contamination cannot create a directional bias because soft probabilities are flip symmetric, but later correctly acknowledges that the hard argmax is nonlinear and can preserve tie-breaking effects. The edge-on-isolated empirical test is useful, but the earlier categorical language should be softened.

MINOR — The physical interpretation in Sec. VI C is speculative and should be substantially reduced.
The manuscript mentions cosmic birefringence and Chern–Simons gravity as mechanisms that would “generically” produce or align a morphology-channel dipole, but no transfer function is derived. In particular, a connection between parity-violating gravitational-wave propagation and a projected galaxy-winding dipole is not generic without a galaxy-formation model.
Required fix: remove model-specific constraint language and retain only the statement that mapping primordial parity-violating sectors to this observable requires a currently unknown transfer function.

MINOR — The manuscript is excessively repetitive.
The distinctions among real-space, harmonic, block-bootstrap, and injection-recovery statistics are repeated in the Abstract, hierarchy section, multiple tables, Results, Discussion, Conclusions, and appendices. The caution is appropriate, but the repetition obscures the scientific argument and makes the paper substantially harder to referee.
Required fix: retain one concise estimator hierarchy, one summary table, and one limitations subsection. Remove repeated near-verbatim qualifications elsewhere.
MINOR — The archival state is not yet adequate for the unusually artifact-dependent presentation.
The paper repeatedly relies on live-branch scripts and JSON artifacts, while Data Availability says that the immutable tag, Zenodo DOI, and exact hashes will be added later. Because many methodological claims cannot be checked from the PDF alone, immutable archiving is important.
Required fix: create the frozen release, DOI, checksums, environment specification, and exact commands before acceptance. The manuscript should cite the immutable archive rather than a moving main branch.
MINOR — Novelty should be stated more precisely.
The main novelty is not a fundamentally new equivariant method, since CE-ResNet already supplied architectural chirality equivariance. The contribution is survey scale, three-class deployment, public catalog construction, and the systematic demonstration of how small classifier monopoles couple to a patchy footprint.
Required fix: foreground these contributions and avoid implying that test-time flip equivariance itself is the principal methodological novelty.
Overall assessment
The high-confidence real-space result is a credible non-detection in the selected predicted-label catalog. The manuscript also provides a valuable demonstration that small classifier asymmetries and survey geometry can generate misleading low-multipole signals. Those are publishable and potentially important contributions.
The present version, however, assigns cosmological meaning to observed-label injection thresholds without an end-to-end transfer calibration, promotes an uncalibrated block-bootstrap distance as a primary exclusion statistic, and describes an only partially modelled harmonic residual as systematics-attributed more strongly than the evidence permits. These problems require substantive statistical and interpretive revision, not merely editorial clarification. f_P4
