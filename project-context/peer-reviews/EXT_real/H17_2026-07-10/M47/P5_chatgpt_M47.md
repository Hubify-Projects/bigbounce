(1) VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

(2) ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Sections VI A and VIII C, characterization of the focal estimator: the manuscript repeatedly calls the analysis “catalog-native,” but only the GALZONE TARGET parent and OUT flag are catalog-native; the focal VoidFinder arm is assigned through the author’s point-in-sphere union of released hole spheres, which the manuscript itself acknowledges is not an official per-galaxy VoidFinder membership definition. The terminology and claimed methodological advantage must be corrected, and the choice of hole-union membership over the genuinely catalog-native V2 GALZONE definitions must be justified without reference to the observed results.

[MAJOR] Sections V B, VI A, and XV, post-hoc selection of the focal analysis: the headline estimator was promoted only after review and inspection of the results. Disclosure is appropriate, but the paper still presents its interval and p-value with a prominence resembling a prespecified primary test. The abstract, title, conclusions, and discussion should consistently frame this as one selected descriptive analysis among correlated exploratory variants, and any language implying confirmatory robustness or privileged inferential status should be removed.

[MAJOR] Sections VI A and VIII C, spatial covariance: the principal uncertainty estimate uses only 50 occupied NSIDE=4 sky clusters for a 78-parameter nonlinear standardization model. The alternative nearest-MAXIMALS clustering is not an adequate substitute because neither clustering scheme is demonstrated to capture correlations induced jointly by survey geometry, overlapping void holes, large-scale structure, and classifier systematics. The authors should provide inference robust to few clusters, such as CR2 or wild-cluster bootstrap procedures, and demonstrate stability across defensible angular and void-region clustering scales.

[MAJOR] Sections III D, VIII C, and XIII, selection into the analyzed parent: only 145,789 of 694,642 released GALZONE TARGETIDs enter the chirality join, yet the manuscript does not establish that inclusion in the classifier-labelled sample is conditionally comparable between void and non-void environments. Standardizing outcomes among matched rows does not correct environment-dependent catalog inclusion or confident-spiral classification. Inclusion probabilities and covariate distributions should be analyzed by environment, with weighting or quantitative bounds if missingness is differential.

[MAJOR] Appendix A and Section XIII, label-error sensitivity: the void-specific human-label validation has only 933 objects and an error-asymmetry confidence interval much wider than the reported environmental contrast. The manuscript correctly concedes that classifier error differing by environment cannot be excluded at the headline scale, but several passages nevertheless interpret the result as strong evidence that observed differences are classifier-monopole projections rather than environment-conditioned classification bias. Those interpretations must be weakened or supported by a substantially more precise environment-stratified validation.

[MAJOR] Sections IV, VI B, VII, and IX, T-Web analysis validity and scope: the canonical T-Web field is shown to be severely selection-function contaminated—the randoms-weighted reconstruction changes most galaxy labels and reduces the void volume fraction by roughly a factor of 23. A diagnostic whose environment assignments are this unstable should not occupy a large fraction of the manuscript or support mechanistic claims about cluster/filament behavior. The canonical T-Web results should be substantially condensed, with the completeness-weighted construction made primary within this secondary analysis.

[MAJOR] Appendix C and Sections XIII–XV, reproducibility and external dependencies: the manuscript states that the final Paper IV release, immutable repository tag, archival snapshot, and several artifact links do not yet exist, and it identifies discrepancies in historical source-file hashes. These are submission-critical dependencies, not future editorial tasks. The final classifier catalog and provenance, executable code, frozen focal rows, manifests, and DOI-tagged artifacts must be publicly available and independently verified before the analysis can be accepted.

[MINOR] Abstract, Sections VI A and XV, numerical reporting: estimates, standard errors, confidence limits, and p-values are quoted to five to eight significant digits despite modeling, clustering, and catalog-systematic uncertainties that do not support that precision. Report effect sizes in percentage points with appropriately rounded uncertainties and avoid implying numerical exactness.

[MINOR] Sections VI–XII, manuscript organization: extensive repetition of the same non-detection, sample ledgers, monopole explanation, caveats, and sensitivity conclusions obscures the focal question. The paper should be shortened substantially, moving most T-Web variants, artifact bookkeeping, and repeated interpretive qualifications to appendices or supplementary material.

(3) The narrowly stated central claim—a post-hoc, catalog-specific non-detection of a difference in classifier-labelled CW fraction between the constructed DESIVAST void and non-void samples—is supported, but its uncertainty calibration, selection sensitivity, reproducibility, and “catalog-native” characterization require major revision.
