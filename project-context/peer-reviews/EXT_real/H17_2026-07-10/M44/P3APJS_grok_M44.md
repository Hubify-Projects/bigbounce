VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, §3 (Three-tier catalog structure) and §3.5: The headline “validated catalog-grade subset of 268,519” applies a single label to a heterogeneous collection (DESI/SDSS/Planck pass detector-sensitivity injection-recovery for the broad/continuum class; NEOWISE passes only a geometry-QA mask by construction; eROSITA is excised entirely because its production score axis is irreproducible from any committed artifact). This makes the aggregate “validated” claim imprecise for users who will treat the merged catalog as uniformly vetted.
[MAJOR] Abstract, §3.1, Table 3 and reader’s guide: The paper correctly states that 98.7 % of DESI anomalies lie on sky/filler fibers and that the like-for-like science-target yield is only 2,468 clusters (≈0.92× the Liang et al. 2023 benchmark), yet it foregrounds process-volume multipliers (∼73×–141×) and the 195,829-object DESI tier. This framing risks overstating immediate scientific utility for targeted follow-up.
[MAJOR] §3.5 and §2.2 (tabular preprocessing): The eROSITA production threshold (0.259) cannot be recovered from the committed raw reconstruction scores on any of 16 monotone rescalings or three IsolationForest retrains; the production values are also non-monotone in the raw artifact. While the tier is excised and a reproducible top-298 membership list is supplied, the existence of an undocumented post-hoc rescaling step in the production run constitutes a serious provenance failure that affects confidence in the overall workflow auditability.
[MINOR] §2.4, §6.4(i) and §3.1: The DESI robustness claim rests on a single production-ensemble injection-recovery test (broad/extended class recovers 99–100 % at 5σ); the two k-fold checks are explicitly described as correlated stability probes computed on short-trained proxy models that fail the paper’s own val-loss ≤0.30 retain gate. This scope limitation should be stated more prominently in the abstract and conclusions.
[MINOR] §4.1 and cross-match paragraph: The 17.8 % “genuine novelty” fraction for the DESI top-1,000 is presented as a single-sample point estimate from an 18-catalog CDS X-Match without a matched control sample or breakdown of why the remaining sources are considered non-novel. This weakens the claim that the catalog contains a substantial reservoir of previously unknown objects.

The central claim that a validated 268,519-object multi-survey anomaly-candidate catalog has been produced from a native-trained scan of 37.3 million sources is supported by the machine-verified 5″ deduplication script and the per-survey injection-recovery results for the broad/continuum class, subject to the manuscript’s own explicit qualifications that the catalog is a process-volume candidate list (not spectroscopically confirmed new astrophysical objects) and that validation standards are heterogeneous across surveys.
