VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:
[MAJOR] Abstract + Sec. I + Sec. IV (Scope paragraph): The claim that the four routes are “basis-complete at the level of MPl-power-counting classes” and that two previously omitted operators are now “closed explicitly” is stated prominently, yet the text repeatedly qualifies the result as only a “channel-level, assumption-conditional amplitude statement, not an operator-level theorem.” This creates an internal tension that must be resolved by rewriting the abstract and introduction to match the precise scoping language used later in Sec. IV; the completeness argument (structural facts F1/F2 + NDA monotonicity + Fierz lemma) is not shown to exhaust all admissible dimension-6 parity-odd operators under minimal ECH without additional non-minimal couplings.

[MAJOR] Sec. IV A + Appendix D: The Route-1 vacuum-condensate exclusion rests entirely on the regulated NJL gap-equation analysis and the Fierz-by-Fierz projection lemma of Appendix C mapping the generated operator onto a repulsive scalar channel (Gscalar = −3/64 κ < 0). The lemma is load-bearing for the “condensate-excluded” claim, yet only a high-level statement appears in the main text; the explicit matrix and all algebraic steps must be reproduced or rigorously summarized in Sec. IV A so that a referee can verify the sign and sub-criticality without reading the full appendix.

[MAJOR] Sec. IV D (Route 2) and Sec. IV E (Route 3): Both amplitude budgets are constructed from “phenomenological ansatz” operators or one-loop forms “motivated by” but not derived from the cited works (Shapiro & Teixeira, Benedetti & Speziale, Mercuri). The resulting suppression factors (∼60 orders for R2, ∼41–67 orders for R3) are therefore illustrative upper bounds rather than derived results; the paper must either perform the explicit matching/renormalization-group integration inside the manuscript or clearly label every numerical suppression factor as an order-of-magnitude estimate whose precise coefficient does not affect the qualitative closure.

[MAJOR] Sec. X (Perturbation-Transparency Result): This is advertised as a Tier-I structural theorem. The excerpt supplies only subsection headings and a one-sentence statement; the full proof (scalar-sector decoupling, tensor-sector extension, term-by-term perturbed-tetrad expansion, and the explicit verification that the Holst term vanishes from all equations of motion) is not visible in the provided main-text excerpt. The complete, self-contained derivation must appear in Sec. X (or be shown to fit within the page limit) without hidden steps or reliance on unshown companion calculations.

[MINOR] Sec. II C 1 + Sec. XII A + Appendix B: The Ntot ≈ 92 value that drives both the dark-energy dilution bookkeeping and the structural tension with the matter-bounce fNL signature (Sec. XIV D) is obtained by fitting to ρΛ; the two alternative dimensional reconstructions (on-shell curvature dressing vs. local-operator promotion) are stated to agree only at order-of-magnitude level. The paper must display the explicit arithmetic that converts the +1 vs. +4 mismatch into Ntot ≈ 92 (including the precise Dinf factor) and must state the sensitivity of the fNL-erasure conclusion to the precise value of Ncoh.

[MINOR] Sec. IX + Table I + Sec. XIV: The 14-constraint catalog is repeatedly invoked as the central result, yet the excerpt provides only a high-level summary and refers barrier details to “Appendix A.” Every load-bearing numerical entry and every assumption underlying the catalog must be collected in a single, clearly labeled table or subsection of the main text so that the referee can audit the claim that “14 constraints map minimal-ECH route space” without hunting through appendices.

The central claim—that the four enumerated minimal-ECH dark-energy routes close at the channel/amplitude level under explicitly stated assumptions, that the Holst sector is perturbation-transparent for canonical scalar matter, and that a structural tension exists between the dark-energy dilution requirement and an observable matter-bounce fNL—is supported by the dimensional-analysis, Fierz-projection, and decoupling arguments given, once the scope qualifiers, explicit derivations, and appendix-to-main-text summaries are strengthened.
