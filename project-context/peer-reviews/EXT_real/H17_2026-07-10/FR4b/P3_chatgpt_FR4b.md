(2) ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract; §III “Three-tier catalog structure”; Tables I–II — The headline 268,519 “validated catalog-grade” objects do not arise from a uniform, calibrated selection. The count combines a DESI S>5 cut, an SDSS fixed-size 77,905-object “continuity slice” chosen to reproduce an earlier cross-transfer count rather than a statistical threshold, a fixed top-200 Planck list, and 419 NEOWISE objects whose only gate is a masking test guaranteed to pass by construction. For SDSS alone, the manuscript gives 77,905 objects for the headline slice, 19,253 for the native top 1%, and only 12 for S>5; choosing among these changes the supposedly validated catalog by tens of thousands of entries. “Validated” therefore has no consistent meaning across the four components.

[MAJOR] Abstract; §III A; Table III — The claimed 268,319 “point-source” anomalies are not demonstrably point sources. The manuscript’s own recount finds that approximately 98.7% of DESI anomaly clusters lack a primary science-class target bit and that 86% have DESI_TARGET=0, with the population described as predominantly sky-fiber, filler, secondary, or calibration spectra. A sky-fiber spectrum is an anomalous exposure or reduction product, not an astrophysical point source. Because DESI supplies most of the headline count, the manuscript must associate every retained detection with a verified astrophysical source or relabel the product as a spectrum-level reconstruction-outlier list.

[MAJOR] §II B; §III A; §VI D(i) — The DESI held-out validation does not validate the released memberships. The pairwise Jaccard calculation uses five models that score the full 47,000-spectrum pool, so each score vector includes spectra used to train that model; it is not a single concatenated out-of-fold score vector in which each object is scored only by a model that never saw it. Moreover, all five proxy models fail the paper’s own validation-loss retention criterion, they are not the production ensemble, and the production per-object held-out re-inference cannot be performed because the native score parquets were lost. The reported Jaccard values can at most show approximate ranking stability among undertrained proxy models.

[MAJOR] §III A; Table III; §III C — The DESI target-class bookkeeping is internally inconsistent. A 0.75% anomaly rate among approximately 4.9 million GALAXY spectra plus 0.037% among approximately 1.5 million QSO spectra implies roughly 37,000 class-restricted anomalies, whereas the “science-bit” recount reports only 2,468 matches while claiming closely related denominators. The later statement that 195,790 anomalies join the Redrock catalog and are 98.8% classified as GALAXY does not resolve this, because Redrock SPECTYPE is not evidence that a fiber was assigned to a primary astrophysical target. A row-level reconciliation using common identifiers and one explicit selection stack is required.

[MAJOR] §III A; §VI E — The claimed “like-for-like” comparison with the 2,685-object Liang et al. catalog is not like-for-like. Comparing 2,468 retained clusters from a 20.3-million-row DR1 science-bit denominator with 2,685 anomalies from an approximately 250,000-spectrum EDR sample by absolute count ignores the nearly two-order-of-magnitude difference in sample size, quality cuts, target classes, anomaly definition, and completeness. The relevant comparison must use matched input selections and report rates, precision, recall, and overlap, not the ratio of final catalog sizes.

[MAJOR] §II D Step 5; §VI D; Fig. 10 — Injection recovery measures sensitivity to the particular injected morphologies, not catalog purity or “reality.” The DESI test uses broad artificial structures on a specially selected clean substrate, SDSS uses continuum dips, and Planck uses standardized Gaussian bumps; none estimates the false-discovery rate, contamination by reduction artifacts, or completeness for the heterogeneous objects actually selected. The recovery threshold is also not shown to be identical to each released catalog threshold. The statement that “validation establishes that the 268,519 subset is real” is therefore unsupported; end-to-end negative controls, realistic simulations, independent labels, and representative follow-up are required.

[MAJOR] §III F; Table VII — The Planck validation is not statistically valid. Ten-degree patches drawn from one sky map are spatially correlated and may overlap across the random train/validation split, while 152 of the released top 200 patches are explicitly in the training split. The binomial calculation for 48 validation-set patches assumes independent trials, an assumption the patch construction violates; spatial correlation reduces the effective sample size, contrary to the manuscript’s statement that it “inflates” it. Per-patch standardization removes absolute-amplitude information, and injecting a 5σ bump after standardization without re-standardizing makes the recovery exercise especially easy and unlike the foreground, beam, and noise structures that dominate real CMB outliers. A credible analysis requires spatially disjoint training and testing, realistic CMB-plus-noise simulations, half-mission/null-map tests, foreground controls, and a look-elsewhere-calibrated statistic.

[MAJOR] §IV A; Fig. 6 — Absence from 18 catalogs is incorrectly called “genuine novelty.” A non-match may arise from footprint gaps, masking, survey depth, astrometric error, failed ingestion, sky-fiber positions, or an invalid source association; it does not establish that an astrophysical source is previously unknown. This is particularly problematic because the sample is drawn from DESI spectra while most DESI outliers are said to be non-primary fibers. The 178 objects require individual source validation, catalog-by-catalog coverage accounting, local-density random-match controls, forced photometry, and visual inspection. The separate 58.8% statistic is also internally inconsistent: it is described as pooling three top-100 samples but retains a denominator of 400 from a historical four-survey calculation involving the removed Gaia tier.

[MAJOR] §III C; Table IV; Fig. 4; Conclusions item 3 — The manuscript conflates the failed SDSS cross-transfer population with the released native-retrained slice. The UMAP/HDBSCAN clusters, emission-line taxonomy, and approximately 84% cool-dwarf statement are explicitly computed from the DESI-trained cross-transfer set, yet the conclusions present them as properties of “SDSS anomalies” generally. The actual 77,905-object released slice was chosen by fixed cardinality and requires its own clustering, taxonomy, contamination assessment, and external validation.

[MAJOR] §III C — The SDSS score–redshift argument is overinterpreted. A Spearman coefficient of 0.036 is negligible in effect size despite its small p-value, and redshift, target selection, signal-to-noise, wavelength coverage, spectral features, and pipeline fit quality all couple to reconstruction error. The assertion that instrumental effects are “redshift-blind and cannot produce” the trend is unjustified. A matched analysis controlling for S/N, class, magnitude, observing conditions, and target-selection variables is needed before the trend can support astrophysical purity.

[MAJOR] §IV C — The 637 positional coincidences do not constitute “cross-survey validation.” The fixed 5-arcsec radius ignores survey-specific positional uncertainties, source density, and blending; the RA-only shifted control is not geometry preserving; and the quoted ≲10 random-match expectation is based on approximate uniform-footprint calculations rather than pair-specific selection functions. Only three systems receive any spectroscopic examination. A probabilistic cross-match with local background densities and survey-pair random catalogs is required, and the remaining coincidences should be described simply as positional associations.

[MAJOR] §V A and Appendix E — The NANOGrav section is disconnected from the anomaly catalog and is not validated to PRD standards. Multiplying per-frequency posterior KDEs is not automatically a likelihood: the manuscript must demonstrate the required prior correction, quantify the loss of inter-bin covariance, and reproduce a collaboration benchmark. The reported Bayes factor depends on a KDE density deep in the γ=4.33 tail, but no bandwidth, finite-chain, or tail-density uncertainty is propagated. More fundamentally, comparing two fixed spectral indices is not a physical comparison between a matter-bounce gravitational-wave model and the population of astrophysical SMBHB models; no bounce amplitude, transfer function, PTA-band turnover, or model-specific prior is derived. This analysis should be removed or presented as a separate, fully developed study.

[MAJOR] Title; Abstract; Tables I–II — The “37.3 million sources and map patches” figure is a process-volume accounting that mixes retained native inputs, historical cross-transfer passes, the quarantined ACT scan, the larger Planck rescore bank, and pre-excision photometric reads. The manuscript itself gives mutually different totals of 36.76, 36.93, and 37.29 million depending on which passes are counted. A title stating that 37.3 million sources were scanned implies a unique input population and is misleading; the exact unique number of scientifically retained inputs should replace it, and the 73× and 141× process-volume comparisons should be removed from the scientific claims.

[MAJOR] §II A–B — The methods are not sufficiently specified in the paper to reproduce the selections or diagnose systematics. Crucial missing or inadequate details include construction of each training sample, wavelength grids and interpolation, bad-pixel and inverse-variance masks, handling of negative or near-zero median normalization, survey-specific quality cuts, ensemble aggregation, score uncertainty, and the relation between validation and catalog thresholds. A common validation-loss threshold of 0.30 is not intrinsically comparable across surveys with different dimensionalities, scaling, losses, and architectures. Repository paths cannot substitute for a self-contained methodological description and controlled baseline/ablation comparisons.

[MAJOR] Overall scope — The primary result is an astronomical reconstruction-outlier catalog, whereas both cosmological applications are explicitly non-detections and are methodologically disconnected from the catalog. As written, the manuscript lacks a focused, validated physics result appropriate for Physical Review D. A substantially rebuilt catalog study would be more naturally evaluated by an astronomical methods or data journal unless one cosmological application is developed into a rigorous, integrated analysis.
