VERDICT: REJECT

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract; §3 three-tier catalog definition; Table 2; §4.3 — The claimed “validated catalog-grade” count of 268,519 is not a statistically coherent catalog. The SDSS contribution of 77,905 objects is explicitly a fixed-size continuity slice chosen to reproduce the historical cross-transfer count, whereas the native top-1% selection contains 19,253 objects and the stated S>5 criterion contains only 12. The injection-recovery test is evaluated against a 99th-percentile threshold and therefore does not validate membership in the much larger 4.05% continuity slice. NEOWISE is included despite its only “validation” being a mask test that the manuscript states passes by construction. The inclusive 377,482 count additionally contains 113,342 LAMOST objects from a detector that failed its sensitivity gate and is known to be dominated by a training artifact. Neither number has a defensible common interpretation as a catalog of validated anomalies.

[MAJOR] §3.1 and Table 3 — The DESI science-target accounting is internally inconsistent. A 0.75% anomaly rate among approximately 4.9 million GALAXY spectra implies roughly 36,750 anomalies, and 0.037% among approximately 1.5 million QSO spectra implies roughly 555, yet the supposedly reconciled science-bit match contains only 2,371 GALAXY and 95 QSO objects. The explanation that these are different “filter stacks” does not reconcile counts that the table simultaneously places on the same class denominators. The row-level set intersections, unique identifiers, duplicate handling, and exact quality cuts must be demonstrated before either the 2,468-object benchmark or the statement that 98.7% of DESI anomalies are non-primary targets can be trusted.

[MAJOR] §2.2 and §6.4(i) — The DESI cross-validation is incorrectly described as a fully out-of-sample re-score. Each fold model is trained on 80% of the 47,000-object pool and then scores the entire pool, so 80% of every fold’s score vector consists of in-training objects. Pairwise Jaccard overlap among these full-pool score vectors measures model-to-model ranking stability, not out-of-fold catalog membership. A genuine cross-validation catalog would assign each object only the score from the model for which that object was held out. This is especially consequential because all of the proxy fold models fail the paper’s own validation-loss retain criterion.

[MAJOR] §2.2, §2.4, and §6.4(i) — No production-level held-out validation of the released DESI membership is available. The full 22.5-million-spectrum catalog includes the training spectra; the manuscript states that the native score parquets required for full held-out re-inference are unavailable; and the production catalog cannot be directly compared object by object with a production-quality out-of-sample run. The OOD and fold tests use different or deliberately short-trained models. These checks cannot establish the stability or reliability of the actual 195,829-object production selection.

[MAJOR] §3.1 and §6.1 — The manuscript has not established that the dominant DESI tier consists of astrophysical sources rather than observing-stream failures. It reports that approximately 98.7% lack a primary science-target bit and that 86% have DESI_TARGET = 0, but later describes 98.8% of matched spectra as Redrock GALAXY objects and treats that template classification as proof that they are not sky, fiber, or calibration artifacts. A Redrock template assignment is not an observing-quality validation. The paper needs a complete breakdown by fiber status, target program, exposure type, ZWARN, bad-pixel masks, sky-fiber designation, spectrograph, observing night, and calibration state. Inspection of the top 200 ranked cases is neither random nor representative and cannot support a catalog-wide contamination bound.

[MAJOR] §2.4 and §6.4(ii) — The injection-recovery program measures sensitivity to selected artificial perturbations, not catalog reliability. The surveys use different injected morphologies, substrates, score thresholds, and recovery definitions; the DESI test uses the cleanest 5% of spectra and a tail-excluded clean threshold rather than the released selection threshold. Recovering a conspicuous broad perturbation does not show that real high-score objects are astrophysical, does not estimate the false-discovery rate, and does not validate the membership list. The failure to recover narrow lines below approximately 15σ also excludes an important class of spectroscopic anomalies from the claimed catalog-grade scope.

[MAJOR] §2.2 and §6.3 — The spectroscopic selection function is not characterized sufficiently for a catalog paper. DESI is trained on only 47,000 of 22.5 million spectra, but the manuscript does not provide the training distribution across target classes, sky and filler fibers, spectrographs, observing conditions, signal-to-noise, redshift, wavelength coverage, or data-quality flags. The score is an unweighted MSE on spectra downsampled to 496 bins and normalized by each spectrum’s median, with no inverse-variance weighting or documented bad-pixel and sky-line masking. The demonstrated LAMOST training-set failure shows that these omissions are not hypothetical.

[MAJOR] §3.3, Figures 3–4, and Table 4 — The SDSS cross-transfer population and released native-retrained population are conflated. Both contain 77,905 entries only because the native slice was deliberately sized to equal the cross-transfer count; they are not thereby the same objects. The UMAP/HDBSCAN clusters, 84% cool-dwarf fraction, extreme-score distribution, and Table 4 taxonomy are explicitly derived from the cross-transfer set, while nearby text assigns astrophysical composition and high-redshift properties to “the 77,905 native-re-scored anomalies.” The manuscript provides neither the membership overlap nor equivalent analyses on the released native set. Results from one set cannot be used to characterize the other.

[MAJOR] §3.3 — The SDSS score–redshift result is overinterpreted. A Spearman coefficient of 0.036 is negligible in effect size, and redshift-dependent target selection, signal-to-noise, wavelength coverage, template failures, and calibration residuals can all induce score–redshift associations. The assertion that instrumental effects are “redshift-blind and cannot produce” the trend is false. This test does not demonstrate that the detector preferentially ranks genuine rare quasars.

[MAJOR] §3.6 — The Planck injection test is not a sufficient physical validation. A 5σ Gaussian bump is added after per-patch standardization and the patch is deliberately not re-standardized, producing a large, artificial distribution shift that an MSE autoencoder should detect trivially. No end-to-end CMB simulations, instrument-noise realizations, foreground residuals, scanning-pattern nulls, rotated-map controls, or block-spatial validation are presented. A fixed top-200 ranking from this test cannot be called a validated CMB anomaly catalog.

[MAJOR] §4.1 — The 17.8% “genuine novelty fraction” is not established. Absence of a positional match in a selected list of catalogs establishes only catalog non-match, not astrophysical novelty. This is particularly problematic because the manuscript reports that nearly all DESI anomaly clusters are non-primary, sky, filler, or secondary-program spectra. The failure of 178 objects to match even imaging catalogs used in the DESI ecosystem is at least as plausibly evidence of sky-fiber coordinates, footprint gaps, astrometric or provenance problems, masking, moving sources, or matching failures. Object-level image inspection, coverage masks, proper-motion propagation, source-versus-sky classification, and independent follow-up are required before “genuinely novel” is defensible.

[MAJOR] §4.1 and Figure 6 — The reported 58.8% aggregate SIMBAD-unmatched fraction has an impossible denominator. It is described as pooling the top 100 objects from three surveys, which would give 300 objects, but is calculated as 235/400 because the denominator retains a historical Gaia sample; the same passage then says Gaia is not in the current denominator. No valid numerator and denominator for the current three-survey sample are supplied. The figure and Table 2 therefore retain a stale statistic from an excised synthetic tier.

[MAJOR] Title, Abstract, Table 1, and Table 2 — The “37.3 million” scan volume is misleading. The retained-native body rows sum to 36,758,058, and the stated read/scored provenance sum is approximately 36.93 million, which rounds to 36.9 million rather than 37.3 million. The 37.292-million figure is obtained only by counting historical cross-transfer passes, superseded Planck processing, ACT, and pre-excision inputs. It is a count of processing operations with repeated or discarded inputs, not a native-trained scan of 37.3 million distinct sources and patches as the title states.

[MAJOR] Data Availability statement — The release description directly contradicts the catalog definition. It says the LAMOST tier is excluded from the released per-object tables and “every headline count,” although §§3 and 7 explicitly add 113,342 LAMOST detections to obtain the 377,482 headline. It calls a 378,480-row file the Path-C catalog even though the claimed catalog is obtained only after filtering out ACT, synthetic Gaia, and eROSITA rows. The manuscript also acknowledges that key DESI production-score artifacts and the Planck checkpoint/tensor needed for full re-inference are unavailable. The assertion that every headline result is independently recomputable from the public release is therefore not demonstrated.

[MAJOR] §3.2 — The twelve z≃6 quasar candidates are not adequately validated. Their redshifts are low-continuum-S/N Redrock template fits, the Gunn–Peterson and Z-arm criteria are physically correlated rather than independent, and the manuscript provides no line signal-to-noise thresholds, ZWARN distribution, alternative-template fits, spectrum panels, independent visual classifications, or follow-up observations. These objects may be released as automated candidates, but the present evidence does not support the stated physical interpretation.

[MINOR] Figures 1, 2, 6, and 8 — Several figures are unsuitable as evidence for the released product: Figure 1 force-includes the highlighted exemplars; Figures 2 and 6 retain excised or historical tiers; and Figure 8 uses explicitly non-catalog “display scores.” The manuscript is also highly repetitive and alternates among “validated,” “inclusive,” “native,” “cross-transfer,” and “continuity” populations without stable notation. A complete rewrite around one frozen catalog definition and one machine-readable schema would be required even after the scientific problems were resolved.

The central claim that the paper delivers a validated catalog-grade set of 268,519 real anomalies is not supported by the evidence presented, because its membership is partly post hoc or predetermined, its validation does not establish catalog purity or production-level out-of-sample stability, and its dominant DESI and SDSS sample definitions remain internally inconsistent.
