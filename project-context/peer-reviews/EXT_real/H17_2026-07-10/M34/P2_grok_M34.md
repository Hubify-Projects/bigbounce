VERDICT: MINOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Sec. I (Scope), abstract, and Sec. VII (systematic budget): The conservative 1.3σ floor (and 0.8σ edge) is obtained by transferring a proxy correlation ρ = −0.868 from the power-spectrum SDB Fisher channel because the per-triangle bispectrum covariance CovB of Heinrich et al. is not public; although the authors compute a channel-native surrogate covariance yielding ρ ≈ −0.42 and ~2.3σ, they retain the proxy as the quoted floor and explicitly label both as “marginal-sensitivity estimates, not channel-native measurement precisions.” This heuristic step must be elevated to a numbered limitation with a clear statement that the lower edge of the headline range is not derived from the bispectrum channel’s native covariance.
[MINOR] Sec. II A (The Prediction) and null-space analysis: The six-coefficient monomial expansion of the degree-9 polynomial is underdetermined (3-dimensional null space from three benchmark constraints), producing r = 0.85 ± 0.13 (range 0.55–1.14) under uniform Euclidean sampling at radius 50; while the headline adopts the narrower noise-weighted central value r = 0.84 ± 0.02, the broader distribution’s propagated significance percentiles (2.2–3.1σ) are shown only in passing and not folded into the final error budget or abstract. The basis choice and sampling measure should be justified more explicitly as conventional rather than unique.
[MINOR] Sec. II C (Assumptions, point (d)): The claim that cubic-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce is closed to a bounded δf_NL ≲ 10^{-3} systematic rests on single-clock nonlinear superhorizon ζ-conservation plus a gradient-expansion estimate (k η_bounce)^2 ~ 10^{-4}, upgraded from a scaling argument; while the evasion of the Quintin et al. no-go is correctly noted and the dressed-metric c_s^2 = 1 choice is identified as the sole quantization input, the paper itself states that “a fully rigorous computation evaluating all Maldacena cubic integrals with numerically computed bounce-modified mode functions would provide a definitive verification.” A short quantitative bound or explicit citation to such a calculation (or a clear statement that none exists) is needed.
[MINOR] Abstract, Sec. IV, and Fig. 2: The reported sensitivity envelope 1.3–2.75σ mixes endpoints obtained from qualitatively different procedures (CMB-Fisher signal-only weighting, LSS noise weighting, additive-quadrature GR + b_ϕ budgets, and proxy-marginalized floors); although the text notes that individual σ endpoints “are not directly comparable,” the abstract and figure present the range as a single coherent interval without a parenthetical caveat or footnote distinguishing the heterogeneous null procedures.
[MINOR] Sec. VI C (Bayes factor) and Table III: The four-corner grid and continuous marginalization are computed from a closed-form integral cross-checked by three Monte Carlo ensembles; however, the headline BF ≈ 9–14 (and the abstract statement) is tied to the specific choice of broad multifield competitor prior [−15, +15] and the recommended σ_theory = 1.0 Gaussian bounce prior. The strong dependence on competitor prior width (BF drops to ~4 for the curvaton-natural [−5, +5] interval) should be stated more prominently so readers immediately see that the quoted range is prior-choice dependent rather than a unique model-selection result.

The central claim that the corrected matter-bounce prediction f_NL = −35/16 yields a realistic (if marginal) SPHEREx detection significance of ~1.3–2.75σ while preserving qualitative discriminatory power against single-field inflation is supported by the explicit template-overlap map (r = 0.84 ± 0.02), the independent tree-level multi-tracer Fisher validation (reff ≈ 0.99), and the conservative systematic budget, once the proxy nature of the lowest edge is properly caveated.
