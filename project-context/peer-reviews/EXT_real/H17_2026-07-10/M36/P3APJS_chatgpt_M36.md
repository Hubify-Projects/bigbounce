VERDICT: REJECT

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] §§2.2, 2.4, 6.4(i), and Data Availability — The central catalog is not reproducible end to end. The manuscript states that the DESI production score parquets are unavailable, 86.6% of released identifiers are internal hashes, and only approximately 1.3% of released rows can be re-pulled for exact re-inference. Reproducing 268,519 by deduplicating already-generated lists verifies bookkeeping, not the anomaly detections. ApJS requires a traceable path from public input through preprocessing, checkpoint, score, threshold, and membership; restoring that path would require a new full scan and release. The prior production of a synthetic Gaia table and an irreproducible eROSITA score axis further necessitates an independent row-level provenance audit of every retained tier.

[MAJOR] §§2.2 and 6.4(i), Table 6(b) — The DESI selection function is undefined. The 47,000-spectrum “representative” training sample is not specified by reproducible strata, and essential details of parent-sample curation, masks, inverse variances, missing-arm handling, and rejected spectra are insufficient. Most importantly, the same nominal threshold flags 52.8% of an uncurated SPARCL sample but only 0.87% of the production stream. Calling this a “catalog-curation effect” concedes that membership depends critically on an unavailable production filtering operation, so the fresh-SPARCL validation sample does not validate the released catalog population.

[MAJOR] §3.1 and Table 3 — The DESI target accounting is internally irreconcilable. The stated rates imply approximately 36,750 GALAXY and 555 QSO anomalies in the validated-TARGETTYPE subset, yet the science-bit positional recount finds only 2,468 total science-class matches. The manuscript’s assertion that this approximately 15-fold discrepancy is merely “definitional” does not reconcile the row populations. Likewise, assigning 98.8% of joined rows a Redrock SPECTYPE of GALAXY does not establish that they are genuine galaxies when approximately 98.7% lack primary science-target bits and only about 0.1% reportedly have ZWARN = 0. A single row-level reconciliation with mutually exclusive target classes, quality flags, and shared identifiers is required before either the 2,468 comparison or the full-stream composition can be accepted.

[MAJOR] §§3.1 and 6.1 — The 195,829 DESI entries are not demonstrated to be astrophysical “sources.” The manuscript reports that approximately 86% have DESI_TARGET = 0 and that approximately 98.7% are not primary science-class targets, leaving a population dominated by sky, filler, secondary, calibration, or otherwise nonstandard fibers. Unweighted reconstruction MSE is particularly susceptible to throughput, sky-subtraction, arm-edge, and continuum-calibration residuals. The 0/200 artifact statement is based on the highest-ranked, nonrandom subset and checks peak residuals against only 11 known features; its binomial upper limit cannot be generalized to the full catalog. The observation-quality outlier stream must be separated from a catalog of astrophysical objects.

[MAJOR] §§2.4 and 6.4(ii) — The injection-recovery exercises do not establish that the selected catalog entries are “real.” They test a small set of synthetic morphologies, often on the cleanest 5% of spectra and against a tail-excluded 99th-percentile threshold rather than the catalog’s actual production selection. They provide no false-positive or contamination estimate. SDSS recovers only 7.2% of the emission-line variant, DESI is insensitive to narrow lines below approximately 15σ, Planck is tested with a large standardized Gaussian bump, and NEOWISE’s 100% result is guaranteed by applying the same latitude mask used to define recovery. These tests establish limited sensitivity to chosen injections, not catalog purity or astrophysical validity.

[MAJOR] Table 2 and §3 — The headline count is largely a tunable selection-design quantity rather than a measured anomaly population. The 77,905-object SDSS tier is deliberately chosen to equal the obsolete cross-transfer count, despite the native top-1% set containing 19,253 objects and the nominal S > 5 set containing only 12. Planck contributes a predetermined 200 patches, and NEOWISE contributes a predetermined top-1% selection. Because scores and thresholds are survey-specific and heterogeneous, changing arbitrary cuts can change the “268,519” headline substantially. The paper should instead release ranked per-survey catalogs and define each catalog-grade threshold from predeclared validation criteria.

[MAJOR] §3.6 — The Planck top-200 entries are not 200 independent anomalous sky regions. The method draws 200,000 nominally random 10° × 10° patches over the |b| ≥ 20° sky, necessarily sampling each sky location hundreds of times on average. Training and scoring use the same overlapping patch bank, a random patch-level validation split is not spatially independent, and a 5″ deduplication radius is physically meaningless for 10° patches. The held-out-binomial calculation therefore does not test memorization, and the 200 centers can be repeated views of a much smaller number of structures. A nonoverlapping tiling, spatially blocked train/test split, region-level clustering, and foreground/null simulations are required.

[MAJOR] §3.8 — NEOWISE cannot be included in a “validated catalog-grade” tier on the basis presented. Its sole gate is a mask-implementation test that passes by construction, not a detector-sensitivity or false-positive test, and its full-sample-fitted scaler has not undergone the train-only robustness check acknowledged as outstanding. It should be labeled exploratory until the anomaly detector itself is validated on held-out light curves or realistic injected variability signals.

[MAJOR] §4.1 — The claimed 17.8% “genuine novelty fraction” is unsupported. Absence from 18 catalogs is not equivalent to astrophysical novelty, particularly when the DESI top-ranked population is dominated by non-primary or sky/filler fibers. Genuine DESI science targets are generally inherited from imaging catalogs, so 178 top-ranked positions unmatched to Legacy Survey, Pan-STARRS, Gaia, WISE, and related catalogs demand a detailed audit for blank-sky fibers, coordinate failures, proper motion, masks, and catalog-query incompleteness. The defensible term is “unmatched candidate fraction,” restricted to spectra first verified as real astrophysical sources.

[MAJOR] §4.3 — The 637 cross-survey coincidences do not constitute validation of the catalog. A uniform 5″ radius ignores survey-dependent astrometric uncertainties, source density, proper motion, blending, and the fundamental difference between point sources and CMB regions. The random-coincidence estimate relies on RA-only shifts that the manuscript acknowledges do not preserve footprint geometry, yet the paper still quotes less than 2% contamination and more than 60 times the random expectation. A probabilistic cross-match with per-survey uncertainties and footprint-preserving rotations is required; CMB patches must be excluded from point-source association accounting.

[MAJOR] §§3.3–3.4, Figures 3–4, and Table 4 — Results from obsolete cross-transfer samples are mixed with claims about the released native-retrained catalogs. The 84% cool-dwarf fraction, HDBSCAN clusters, emission-line taxonomy, extreme SDSS score distribution, and LAMOST SIMBAD fraction are explicitly derived from cross-transfer populations, while adjacent prose discusses the native 77,905 and 113,342 tiers. These quantities cannot characterize the released products and must either be recomputed on the native selections or removed.

[MAJOR] §5 — The fNL application is not a valid downstream analysis of the catalog. The 5,384 “QSO candidates” have no spectroscopic-redshift requirement, while the angular-clustering bias ratio is measured relative to a parent anomaly population with a different and poorly defined redshift and angular selection function. The random catalog, redshift distributions, integral constraint, scale dependence, and covariance are not demonstrated sufficiently to interpret the fitted bias. The nonlinear α² mapping and clipped “[3.92, 8.98] envelope” are explicitly not a probability interval, yet are presented as the principal forecast summary, and Figure 9 uses a different 40,192-object tracer sample. This section should be removed or rebuilt with confirmed tracers, matched selection functions, and a fully documented Fisher calculation.

[MAJOR] §5.1 and Appendix E — The NANOGrav analysis is disconnected from the catalog and its evidential claims are overstated. Multiplying 30 marginal KDE likelihoods discards inter-bin covariance, so the resulting posterior-density ratios are not robust substitutes for a full timing-data or joint free-spectrum likelihood. Fixing γ = 3.0 or 4.33 also does not compare complete physical matter-bounce and SMBHB models. The stated environmental-SMBHB caveat effectively demonstrates that the “decisive” Bayes-factor wording has no decisive physical interpretation. This material should be removed from an ApJS catalog paper or replaced by a proper physical-model analysis using an official likelihood.

[MINOR] §4.1, Figure 6, and Table 2 — The 58.8% aggregate SIMBAD-unmatched statistic has an inconsistent denominator. A total of 235/400 cannot be the top 100 from the three current surveys named, and the text simultaneously states that the historical denominator included the removed Gaia tier and that Gaia is not in the current pooled denominator. The aggregate must be recomputed or deleted.

[MINOR] Figure 10 and the principal validation figures — Figure 10’s caption says Planck and NEOWISE are brought onto the plotted recovery axis, but neither appears in the plotted legend or curves, and the central DESI recovery curve is also absent. Figures 2–4 largely display superseded cross-transfer products rather than the released catalog, while Figure 8 uses explicitly non-catalog “display scores.” The main figures should show the canonical released selections and their actual validation results.

[MINOR] §2.2 and terminology throughout — The manuscript conflates three different uses of “sigma”: standardized reconstruction score S, standard deviations of a highly non-Gaussian validation-MSE distribution, and injected signal amplitude in units of local noise. S > 5 is not a calibrated 5σ detection, and the repeated language of “5σ recovery” risks implying such an equivalence. Distinct notation and non-significance terminology are required.

The central claim is not supported: the manuscript demonstrates that heterogeneous reconstruction-ranked candidate lists were generated, but not that 268,519 unique entries constitute a reproducible, validated, catalog-grade anomaly sample.
