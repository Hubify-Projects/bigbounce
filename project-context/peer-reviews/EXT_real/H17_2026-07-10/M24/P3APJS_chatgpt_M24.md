ISSUES:
[MAJOR] Title, Abstract, §§3.1 and 7 — The headline “268,519 reconstruction-outlier sources” is not a source-level astrophysical catalog. The dominant DESI component contains 195,829 anomalous spectra, yet only 2,468 of 190,015 deduplicated DESI clusters match primary science-class targets; the manuscript states that approximately 98.7% fall on non-primary spectra, including sky, filler, calibration, secondary, and target-of-opportunity fibers. These entries are not demonstrated to be point sources and must be separated into astrophysical-source and instrument/blank-sky products before any “catalog-grade source” count is quoted. ext_P3APJS_M24
[MAJOR] §3.1 and Table 3 — The DESI science-target accounting is internally unresolved. The reported 0.75% anomaly rate among approximately 4.9 million galaxies implies about 36,750 anomalies, and the 0.037% rate among approximately 1.5 million QSOs implies about 555, yet the positional science-bit recount finds only 2,468 total. Describing the factor-of-6–16 discrepancy as merely “definitional” is insufficient, particularly because the 20.3-million-row bitmask selection is described as broader than the 6.5-million-object TARGETTYPE subset. The joins, denominators, duplicate handling, and target-bit logic must be independently reproduced and reconciled. ext_P3APJS_M24
[MAJOR] §§3.1 and 6.5 — The claimed “like-for-like” comparison with Liang et al. is invalid. Comparing 2,468 detections from a 20.3-million-row denominator with 2,685 detections from roughly 250,000 spectra by absolute count conceals an approximately 0.012% versus 1.07% rate difference. A defensible comparison requires matched target definitions, quality cuts, score thresholds, completeness, and purity—not the ratio 2,468/2,685.
[MAJOR] §§2.2, 6.4, and Data Availability — The DESI catalog is not reproducible end to end from public inputs. The manuscript states that 86.6% of released identifiers are internal hashes, only about 1.3% of rows are re-pullable by identifier, and the native score parquets and exact input linkage were lost. Recomputing a deduplication count from a frozen membership list does not reproduce the catalog selection. Every released spectrum requires a stable archival identifier, observation/coadd identifier, exact preprocessing provenance, and a regenerable production score. ext_P3APJS_M24
[MAJOR] §§2.2 and 6.4 — The validation does not establish catalog purity or per-entry validity. The k-fold models are short-trained proxies that fail the manuscript’s own validation-loss gate, and two reported fold statistics are correlated because they use the same score vectors. The remaining production test injects one selected broad morphology into especially clean spectra and measures sensitivity, not the false-positive rate or astrophysical reality of the 195,829 selected entries. A full held-out rescore plus blinded, stratified validation across score, target class, S/N, observing conditions, and anomaly morphology is required.
[MAJOR] §§2.2, 3.1, and 6.3 — The anomaly score is inadequately controlled for instrumental noise. It is an unweighted reconstruction MSE with no inverse-variance weighting, while bad pixels, masks, sky residuals, spectral resolution, arm boundaries, and low-S/N channels contribute on equal footing. The manuscript also reports that the nominal DESI threshold flags more than 50% of an uncurated SPARCL sweep and acknowledges that approximately 44,000 B-dominant entries may be calibration artifacts. These facts are incompatible with a “catalog-grade” claim without comprehensive metadata-conditioned null tests and a noise-aware scoring analysis. ext_P3APJS_M24
[MAJOR] §3.3 and Table 2 — The 77,905-object SDSS contribution is an arbitrary fixed-size “continuity slice” selected specifically to equal the failed cross-transfer count; it is not a statistically or physically motivated anomaly threshold. The same native score distribution gives 19,253 objects at the actual top-1% cut and only 12 at S>5S>5S>5. The headline count therefore depends on a post hoc catalog-size choice and cannot be called a validated anomaly population. ext_P3APJS_M24
[MAJOR] §3.3, Figure 4, Table 4, and Conclusion item 3 — Most of the SDSS astrophysical characterization—14 HDBSCAN clusters, three populations, and the 84% cool-dwarf fraction—is computed from the failed DESI-to-SDSS cross-transfer set, whereas the released catalog uses a different native-rescore membership with the same imposed size. Equal cardinality does not imply equal membership. All classifications, figures, cross-matches, redshift trends, and conclusions must be recomputed for the actual released native set, with the overlap between the two memberships explicitly reported.
[MAJOR] §3.6 — The Planck validation is statistically misinterpreted. Preferential appearance of top-score patches in the validation split is not evidence against memorization; an overfit model would naturally reconstruct training patches better and place more held-out patches in the high-error tail. Moreover, the heavily overlapping 10° patches are not independent, so spatial correlation reduces—not increases—the effective sample size and invalidates the quoted binomial significance. The production set also includes training patches, no independent held-out rescoring is supplied, and the relevant checkpoint/tensor is described as unavailable.
[MAJOR] §3.8 — NEOWISE does not pass an anomaly-detector validation. Planting objects outside the ecliptic-latitude mask and “recovering” them by applying that same mask is a tautological software QA test. It provides no sensitivity, completeness, or contamination information for the autoencoder ranking. In addition, the scaler was fit to the full sample and the train-only-scaler robustness check remains unperformed because the feature table was retained only on the exited compute environment. NEOWISE cannot be included in a validated tier on this basis.
[MAJOR] §§3 and 4.3 — The combined headline has no coherent selection function. It sums an absolute DESI threshold, an arbitrary SDSS fixed-size slice, fixed top-N Planck and NEOWISE selections, and—in the 377,482 total—a LAMOST tier that explicitly fails validation and is 98% dominated by a known training artifact. Furthermore, a 5″ “unique-object” merge between optical point sources and centers of 10° CMB patches is physically meaningless. The publication should provide separate per-survey ranked products rather than a single catalog size or aggregate anomaly rate.
[MAJOR] §4.1 — The 17.8% result is not a “genuine novelty fraction.” It is an unmatched fraction for one top-1,000 stratum, apparently drawn from the full DESI stream rather than the verified science-target subset; unmatched sky/filler positions are not novel astrophysical sources. The quoted Wilson interval includes only binomial sampling uncertainty and omits catalog incompleteness, coordinate-linkage failures, proper motion, source blending, heterogeneous positional errors, and matching-radius systematics. Every one of the 178 objects requires target-status verification and manual or probabilistic counterpart auditing before this can be described as novelty.
[MINOR] §§4.2–4.3 — The spatial and random-coincidence analyses are not selection-function controlled. A Poisson test over occupied HEALPix pixels is dominated by survey footprints, while the RA-shift control does not preserve footprint geometry or local source density. The stated comparison of 637 coincidences with fewer than 10 random matches should be replaced by survey-mask-preserving rotations or mocks and probabilistic matches using per-survey astrometric uncertainties.
