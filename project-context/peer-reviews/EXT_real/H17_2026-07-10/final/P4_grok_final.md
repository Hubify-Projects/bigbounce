VERDICT: MINOR REVISIONS

Referee Report for Phys. Rev. D

This is a careful, methodologically sophisticated observational cosmology paper that presents the largest chirality-labeled spiral galaxy catalog to date (8.47 million galaxies, 3.2 million spirals) and reports a clean null result for the large-scale chirality dipole using a flip-equivariant Vision Transformer. The work directly confronts prior claims (particularly Shamir’s 1.7–4% asymmetry range) with substantially larger statistics, modern bias-hardening techniques (equivariant test-time averaging, dedicated non-spiral class, multi-axis audit), and an explicit estimator hierarchy that privileges the real-space dipole on the high-confidence subsample as the primary cosmological probe.

The analysis is transparent about limitations, pre-specifies the key selection cut via git commit, provides injection–recovery calibration of sensitivity (A₅₀ ≈ 0.75%, A₉₅ ∈ [1.0%, 1.5%]), includes a fully model-free GZ1-human-label cross-check, and correctly notes that the ℓ = 1 dipole is a parity-even isotropy test rather than a direct parity-violation probe. The public release of catalog, weights, and reproducibility scripts is a strong positive. I recommend minor revisions primarily to tighten the discussion of the remaining ~47% of the post-MASTER harmonic residual and to foreground the model-independent GZ1 test.

Major Comments

Harmonic-channel residual and the ~47% open item (Secs. IV D, Appendix D):
The paper correctly demonstrates that (a) the bulk of the pre-MASTER ℓ = 1 power is monopole–mask leakage (99.32% reproduction under a generative binomial-monopole null), (b) MASTER reduces but does not eliminate it, and (c) the post-MASTER +3.64σ (canonical) residual is systematics-attributed via an eight-anchor battery. The forward-modeling result—that imaging + morphology templates capture ~53% of the residual amplitude in the correct direction—is useful. However, the remaining ~47% is left as an explicit open item (classifier confidence-vs-depth response at production scale).
Because even the full residual amplitude (|a₁| = 6.95 × 10⁻³ ≡ Aₚ ≈ 0.695%) lies below the real-space estimator’s A₅₀ = 0.75% recovery floor (and ≳3–4× below A₉₅), it does not affect the primary null conclusion or the falsification criterion. Still, for a high-profile null paper, referees will want either (i) a clearer statement that this remainder is sub-threshold for the primary (real-space) estimator and therefore does not undermine the cosmological claim, or (ii) a short roadmap paragraph on how the planned per-galaxy morphology-purity map will close it. A one-paragraph addition in Sec. IV D or the Discussion would suffice and would preempt questions about whether a “hidden” cosmological contribution could still be present.
Independence from CE-ResNet pseudo-labels (Sec. II B, VI A):
The 66.5% reliance on CE-ResNet predictions for training labels is acknowledged, and the shuffle/permutation nulls therefore cannot by themselves test inheritance of survey-correlated structure. The authors correctly pivot to template-regression anchors (Appendix D) and, most importantly, to the fully model-free GZ1-human-only dipole test (N ≈ 46 000 confident spirals, z = −0.54σ). This cross-check is excellent and should be elevated: it appears late in the text and is mentioned only briefly in the abstract/introduction. A short dedicated paragraph early in Sec. VI A (or even a sentence in the abstract) emphasizing “a fully model-independent GZ1-human-label analysis on the same footprint returns a consistent null” would strengthen the independence claim without requiring new calculations.
Estimator hierarchy and the role of the block-bootstrap WLS fit (Table I, Sec. IV C, Appendix D):
The paper is admirably explicit that only rows P1–P2 carry cosmological weight and that all harmonic σ values are diagnostics. The block-bootstrap WLS template exclusion of a clean 1.7% dipole (z ≈ −7.6) is presented as a primary cross-check on the full catalog. Because this is a model-dependent template-disfavor statistic (not a calibrated frequentist upper limit), a brief clarifying sentence reminding readers that the primary sensitivity floor remains the empirical injection–recovery bracket on the real-space estimator would help avoid any misreading.

Minor Comments / Suggestions

Pre-registration / blinding language: The git-commit record for the p_eq > 0.6 cut is good practice. A single sentence noting that the cut was frozen before the dipole was evaluated (and that the full confidence-cut sweep leaves the null verdict unchanged) is already present; it could be made slightly more prominent in Sec. IV C.
GZ1 accuracy and dilution: The 69.91% chirality accuracy (κ = 0.40) is treated conservatively via the dilution factor g = 2a − 1 folded into the injection–recovery floors. This is correct, but a one-line reminder in Sec. VI B that the empirical A₅₀/A₉₅ already incorporate this dilution would close the loop for readers.
Data Availability section: The manuscript states that the catalog, model weights, and reproducibility scripts are released publicly. Ensure the final version contains explicit DOIs/URLs or Hugging Face / Zenodo identifiers so readers can immediately access them. (The current excerpt mentions the release but the precise access statement is truncated.)
Figure captions and axis labels: Several figures (especially the asymmetry maps and pseudo-C_ℓ panels) are information-dense. Minor polishing of captions to restate the exact mask, weight, and null procedure used for each panel would improve readability (standard PRD expectation).
Typos / minor wording: In the extracted text a few OCR artifacts remain (e.g., occasional dropped superscripts, “peq” vs. “p_eq” inconsistency). A final proofreading pass will catch these; none affect scientific content.

Summary

This is a strong, publication-ready paper that raises the methodological bar for future chirality/isotropy analyses. The combination of scale, equivariant architecture, explicit estimator hierarchy, generative null tests, and model-independent cross-checks makes the null result credible and the tension with earlier claims well-motivated. The two major points above are addressable with modest additions that do not require new computations. Once those clarifications are incorporated, the manuscript will meet the standards of Physical Review D.

I recommend minor revisions.
