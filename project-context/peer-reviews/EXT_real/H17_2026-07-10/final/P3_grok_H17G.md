VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, title, and opening paragraphs foreground the 268,519 validated count and ~73–141× multipliers as the primary deliverable, yet the text itself repeatedly defines this as a process-volume figure (full-instrument-stream scan of sky/filler fibers + science targets) whose like-for-like science-target benchmark is only 2,468 DESI clusters—≈0.92× the Liang et al. (2023) single-survey result on a comparable EDR target set. This framing is inconsistent with Phys. Rev. D standards for clear, non-misleading presentation of results; the multipliers are explicitly labeled non-like-for-like in §I and Table III but are still used to claim scale.
[MAJOR] Validation status is mixed and incompletely meets the paper’s own gates for a “catalog-grade” claim. DESI, SDSS, and Planck pass detector-sensitivity injection-recovery for the broad/continuum class (99–100% at 5σ for DESI broad; 64% SDSS; 100% Planck), but: (a) narrow single-pixel lines recover only at ≥15σ (explicit sensitivity floor of the 496-bin MSE scorer); (b) the two DESI fold-stability checks (Jaccard, tail preservation) use deliberately short-trained proxy models that fail the paper’s own val-loss ≤0.30 retain gate and are correlated readings of the same score vectors, not independent confirmations; (c) NEOWISE passes only a masking-geometry QA gate “by construction,” not a sensitivity test; (d) eROSITA (1.2% recovery) and LAMOST (5.8% recovery, 98% blue-excess artifact) fail and are excised or reclassified. The “validated 268,519” subset therefore rests on heterogeneous and partial evidence; the paper’s own three-tier taxonomy and candid scope notes in §III and §VI D (i) document this, but the headline claim does not.
[MAJOR] Reproducibility and provenance failures are acknowledged but undermine the multi-survey claim. The eROSITA production score axis (0.259 threshold) is irreproducible on the committed raw artifact (non-monotone; fails all 16 monotone rescalings and 3 IsolationForest retrains; §III E); the tier is excised from every count and released only as a membership list. The original Gaia tier was synthetic (source_id = 5×10^18 + i pattern, duplicates, unphysical magnitudes) and removed. These are not minor; they demonstrate pipeline issues that affect trust in the cross-survey deduplication and the “Path-C rebuild” protocol advertised as core methodology.
[MAJOR] The cosmological applications (§V) are null or marginal and do not support the implied science impact. Multi-tracer fNL yields α_jk = 0.19 ± 0.65 (0.29σ from null); de-biased point estimate returns exactly the single-tracer baseline σ(fNL) = 8.98 with no improvement; the 9.4% central shift is noise-driven and lies inside the 1σ envelope. NANOGrav 15-yr free-spectrum MCMC gives γ = 2.567 ± 0.382 (matter-bounce +1.13σ; circular-orbit SMBHB +4.61σ but only against that specific reference; Savage-Dickey factor decisive solely vs. idealized circular case). The paper correctly labels both “secondary demonstrations, not detections” and “no cosmological detection,” yet their inclusion as §V still suggests model-audit utility that the quantitative results do not deliver at current S/N.
[MINOR] Threshold and rate disclosures are internally consistent but require tighter scoping for readers. SDSS 77,905 is a fixed-size continuity slice (not native top-1% or S > 5); native S > 5 yields only 12 sources (~6500× compression vs. cross-transfer). LAMOST native top-1% is exploratory. Planck/NEOWISE use predetermined top-1% counts. Table II footnotes are detailed, but the main-text rate claims and “largest multi-archive anomaly search” phrasing still need explicit caveats that most rates are not data-driven frequencies and that cross-survey S values are incomparable.
[MINOR] Novelty assessment (17.8% genuine for DESI top-1,000 vs. 18 curated catalogs; Wilson CI ±1.2%) is a single-sample point estimate on a hand-curated top slice and is not survey-wide; three interesting DESI×SDSS cross-matches (including one time-variable source and one uncatalogued BAL QSO) are noted but not followed up with spectra or multi-wavelength confirmation in the provided text. This is useful but secondary to the catalog claim.
[MINOR] Methodological transparency is high (committed scripts, pipeline paths, injection-recovery artifacts, held-out tests), which is a strength. However, the paper reads as a technical report (extensive self-referential pipeline citations, long caveats, process-volume bookkeeping) rather than a concise Phys. Rev. D article. Consider moving detailed pipeline provenance, full per-survey threshold tables, and the weaker cosmological sections to appendices or a companion data-release note to sharpen focus on the validated subset and the 17.8% novelty result.

The manuscript contains substantial, honestly disclosed work and a usable (if heterogeneous) public catalog release. The above issues are addressable by reframing the headline claims around the science-target yield and explicitly validated tiers, tightening validation language, and de-emphasizing null cosmological results. With those revisions it would meet Phys. Rev. D standards for a data-analysis/catalog paper.
