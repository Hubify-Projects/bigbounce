VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, title, and Sec. IV (Four-Route No-Go): The phrasing “four-route no-go”, “channel-level closure”, and “amplitude closure for R1–R3” overstates the result as a structural theorem. The text repeatedly and correctly qualifies the claim as conditional on explicit scaling ansätze (R2–R3), single-scale NDA power counting with no light scale or cancellation (App. B), minimal field content only, and the four enumerated routes not being proven a complete diffeomorphism-invariant operator basis. These qualifications must be elevated to the abstract and title (e.g., “Constraints on four specific minimal-ECH channels under stated assumptions”) to prevent misinterpretation; the current wording risks appearing to claim more than is proven.
[MAJOR] Sec. X (Perturbation-Transparency Result): The headline statement that “torsion vanishes at all classical metric/scalar perturbation orders” and that the Holst sector “decouples from all scalar/tensor perturbation equations of motion” is potentially ambiguous between (a) exact vanishing on the torsion-free branch when spin density S=0 (true by the algebraic Cartan equation for pure scalar matter) and (b) all-orders perturbative stability including possible higher-order back-reaction. The Bianchi-identity argument (Rµ[νρσ]=0 on T=0) is rigorous for the linearised and exact background cases, but the all-orders claim requires an explicit inductive or non-perturbative argument that is only sketched; this must be clarified or restricted to the orders actually demonstrated.
[MINOR] Sec. IX (Structural Constraints catalog): The 14-barrier list mixes foundational assumptions (e.g., single-scale NDA, algebraic torsion), direct consequences of the perturbation-transparency theorem (Barrier 14/B8), and weaker or partially overlapping mechanism-class statements. Several barriers (Planck suppression, scale separation, parameter immunity) are essentially restatements of the same NDA counting. A shorter, explicitly tiered classification (Tier I: exact operator identities; Tier II: naturalness; Tier III: amplitude bounds under ansätze) would improve logical clarity and reduce redundancy.
[MINOR] Sec. IV A and Appendix D (NJL Route-1 closure): The regulated gap-equation exclusion of a vacuum scalar condensate is a solid technical result (repulsive Fierz-projected scalar channel Gscalar < 0 and |Geff|/Gcrit ≪ 1). However, the text does not sufficiently caveat the limitations of the mean-field NJL approximation in a curved-space, Planck-suppressed gravitational setting (possible non-perturbative resummation, cutoff-scheme dependence, or higher-dimensional operator mixing). A brief paragraph acknowledging these standard caveats would prevent the vacuum-exclusion claim from appearing stronger than the mean-field calculation strictly supports.
[MINOR] General presentation and length: The 63-page manuscript is densely self-referential (“this paper’s Appendix”, “companion [2]”, “Paper IV”, reproducibility tree). While the logical content is claimed to be self-contained, the sheer volume of internal cross-references and the long barrier catalog impair readability. Condensing Sec. IX, moving some technical verifications (symbolic Bianchi checks, full Fierz matrix) to a compact supplemental file, and reducing repetitive restatement of scope would make the novel contributions (perturbation transparency, explicit closure of Jackiw–Pi and parity-odd four-fermion partner, Fierz-by-Fierz lemma) more prominent.

One sentence on the central claim: The central claim—that the four enumerated minimal-ECH dark-energy routes are constrained at channel-amplitude or naturalness level under the stated single-scale NDA and minimal-coupling assumptions, with the Holst sector decoupling from scalar/tensor perturbations via the algebraic Bianchi identity—is supported by the explicit torsion-elimination map, the Fierz projection lemma, the one-loop coefficient bounds, and the dimensional no-go argument, provided the reader accepts the scope limitations and the heuristic character of single-scale power counting.
