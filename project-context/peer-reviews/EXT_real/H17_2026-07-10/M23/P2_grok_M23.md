VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, Sec. I, and Sec. IV: The headline sensitivity range ∼2.6–2.75σ (pre-systematics) and ∼1.3–2.75σ (post-systematics) is derived entirely from rescaling the single external Heinrich et al. [1] forecast (σ(f_local_NL)≈0.7) via an explicit but approximate template-mismatch factor r=0.84±0.02 plus a heuristic additive-quadrature systematic budget that relies on a proxy correlation (ρ≈−0.868 transferred from the power-spectrum SDB channel because the per-triangle bispectrum covariance Cov_B is not public). This is presented as the primary result without sufficiently prominent upfront separation from the in-house tree-level multi-tracer Fisher validation (which reproduces the local baseline to 2–11% and yields reff≈0.99 but is explicitly not the source of the headline numbers), making the “SPHEREx sensitivity recast” framing potentially misleading as to the degree of independence and robustness.
[MAJOR] Sec. II.A, Eq. (2), Table I, and Appendix A: The central correction of the literature value from the printed f_NL=−35/8 to the vertex-consistent −35/16 (certified via from-scratch re-summation of Cai et al.’s four cubic-action vertices at ϵ=3/2 in the squeezed limit, their ϵ-order-grouped intermediates, three-configuration benchmark matching, and Li et al.’s general-c_s formula at c_s=1) is traced to a specific −(99/128)∑_i k_i^3 discrepancy in the transcribed polynomial. While the paper states the explicit algebra and the four cross-checks, the full step-by-step squeezed-limit reduction of the four vertices (L_redef, L_ζζ̇², L_ζ̇∂ζ∂χ, L_ζ(∂_i∂_jχ)²) against the matter-domination Hankel mode functions is not reproduced in sufficient detail in the provided appendix excerpt or main text to permit immediate independent verification by a referee; this correction underpins every subsequent significance and Bayes factor and therefore requires expanded, self-contained algebra.
[MAJOR] Sec. II.C (assumptions (d) and (f)) and Sec. VII: The forecast is conditional on “faithful cubic-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce” (assumption (d)), closed only to a bounded δf_NL≲10^{-3} via single-clock nonlinear superhorizon ζ-conservation under the dressed-metric LQC quantization (with the sole quantization-dependent input being the sign of the sub-leading gradient coefficient). This is acknowledged as “the weakest link” and “verified only at linear order,” yet the headline ranges and BF≈9–14 are presented without a dedicated sensitivity plot or table showing how violation of (d) at the O((kη_bounce)^2) level or reactivation of torsion via non-negligible fermion density (assumption (f)) would shift the predicted amplitude or erase the signal entirely.
[MINOR] Sec. III.B, Eq. (6), and null-space analysis: The noise-weighted template-overlap factor r=0.84±0.02 (range 0.829–0.876 across physically motivated weightings) is robustly computed, but the broader null-space distribution r=0.85±0.13 (from 10,000 samples in the 3-dimensional coefficient null space of the six-monomial degree-9 polynomial) is reported in detail yet explicitly not propagated into the headline σ_eff or significances; the text therefore contains an internal tension between the conservative flat-weight r=0.84 adopted for all quoted numbers and the survey-optimal reff≈0.99 from the independent Fisher, without a single consolidated table that maps both into the final significance bands under identical systematic assumptions.
[MINOR] Sec. VI and Tables III–IV: The closed-form Bayes factor (Eq. 9) with three Monte-Carlo cross-validations is a clear methodological strength, but the reported BF≈9–14 range (and the four-corner grid) is highly sensitive to the choice of competitor prior width ([−5,+5] vs. [−15,+15]) and to the bounce prior (delta vs. σ_theory=1.0 Gaussian); the manuscript correctly labels these as “illustrative” yet still headlines them in the abstract without a dedicated prior-sensitivity figure or explicit statement that BF drops to ∼4 under the more physically motivated curvaton-natural prior, weakening the claim of “preserves qualitative discriminatory power.”

The central claim—that the corrected matter-bounce prediction f_NL=−35/16 yields a realistic SPHEREx sensitivity of ∼1.3–2.75σ (with BF≈9–14 against tuned multifield competitors) that preserves qualitative discriminatory power against single-field inflation—is supported by the template-overlap calculation, independent Fisher validation, and analytic Bayes-factor derivation, but only conditionally on the six listed assumptions, the proxy systematics budget, and the external Heinrich et al. baseline.
