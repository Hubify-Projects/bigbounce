VERDICT: REJECT

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, §III, and §VII — claim of a “validated catalog-grade” subset. The validation procedures do not establish that the 268,519 entries are real astrophysical anomalies or estimate catalog purity. Injection-recovery measures sensitivity to a few planted morphologies, validation loss measures reconstruction performance, and Jaccard overlap measures ranking stability; none supplies a false-discovery rate or shows that the selected objects are not instrumental, reduction, targeting, or domain-shift outliers. NEOWISE is included after a masking test that passes by construction, while fixed-percentile selections necessarily return “anomalies” even for pure noise. The manuscript’s own statement that “validation establishes that the 268,519 subset is real” is therefore unsupported.

[MAJOR] §III A and Table III — irreconcilable DESI population accounting. The dominant DESI component is said to consist overwhelmingly of non-primary targets: 98.7% of deduplicated clusters lack a primary science-class bit and 86% have DESI_TARGET = 0, including sky fibers and secondary/filler observations. Later, however, the manuscript interprets a Redrock SPECTYPE assignment for essentially every anomaly as proof that 98.8% are real galaxies rather than sky/fiber/calibration artifacts, even though only about 0.1% have secure ZWARN = 0 redshifts. Redrock template labels on poorly fitted spectra cannot establish astrophysical origin. Table III also gives, on nominally the same class denominators, rates of 0.75% versus 0.048% for galaxies and 0.037% versus 0.0063% for QSOs; calling this a “definitional” difference does not reconcile the underlying memberships. A row-level audit of target bits, observation type, primary status, redshift quality, and anomaly membership is required before these detections can be called point sources or catalog-grade objects.

[MAJOR] §III A and §VI E — invalid “like-for-like” comparison with Liang et al. Comparing 2,468 anomalies selected from approximately 20.3 million science-bit rows with a 2,685-object catalog obtained from roughly 250,000 DESI EDR science spectra is not like-for-like merely because both involve science targets. The denominators, data releases, target populations, quality cuts, anomaly definitions, and thresholds differ by orders of magnitude. The absolute-count statement “0.92× the benchmark” obscures an approximately 90-fold difference in scanned sample size and cannot be used as a performance comparison.

[MAJOR] §III C and Table II — the SDSS contribution is chosen arbitrarily. The 77,905-object native SDSS tier is explicitly defined to have the same size as the failed cross-transfer tier, rather than by a physical or statistical anomaly criterion. The manuscript’s own alternative native selections contain 19,253 objects at the top-1% knee and only 12 at S>5. Thus a major fraction of the 268,519 headline count is determined by an arbitrary continuity convention and could be changed by tens of thousands without changing the data or model. An objective, prospectively specified threshold tied to a calibrated null distribution, purity target, or science selection is required, followed by recomputation of all headline counts and cross-matches.

[MAJOR] §III C, Table IV, and Figs. 3–4 — cross-transfer and native SDSS catalogs are conflated. The UMAP/HDBSCAN clusters, cool-dwarf fraction, spectral taxonomy, and extreme score distribution characterize the DESI-trained cross-transfer set, not the native-retrained 77,905-object catalog used in the headline. Reusing the same count for two different memberships makes the provenance easy to misread, and the conclusions report cross-transfer classifications as though they describe the released tier. The native catalog requires its own score distribution, taxonomy, inspection, and external validation.

[MAJOR] §II B–D and §VI D — the score and validation gates do not define a statistically controlled catalog. A standardized reconstruction MSE is not a calibrated significance statistic when its validation distribution is non-Gaussian and heavy-tailed. The paper mixes an absolute S>5 threshold, fixed-percentile cuts, a fixed-size continuity slice, and a fixed top-200 selection, then combines them into one catalog count. The retention rule “validation loss ≤0.30 or injection recovery ≥50%” is heuristic, survey-dependent, and does not test contamination. The absence of inverse-variance weighting, per-class completeness curves, false-positive simulations, or a selection function prevents quantitative use of the catalog.

[MAJOR] §II B(c) and §VI D(i) — DESI generalization is not established for the production catalog. The training set contains only 47,000 spectra out of 22.5 million, with insufficient evidence that it spans observing conditions, target classes, sky fibers, calibrations, and low-S/N regimes. The five fold models used for stability checks all fail the manuscript’s own retention gate, with mean best validation loss about 1.91, and are explicitly not production-equivalent. The same threshold flags 52.8% of an uncurated out-of-distribution sample versus 0.87% of the catalog, a 61-fold instability that is labeled a “curation effect” but not quantitatively explained. The broad-feature injection test on the cleanest 5% of spectra and the top-200 visual check against only 11 known wavelengths cannot validate the full production population.

[MAJOR] §III F and Table VII — the Planck tier is not a validated CMB-anomaly catalog. The model is trained and scored on the same bank of spatially correlated, overlapping patches from a single sky realization, and the published set is a predetermined top 200 rather than a significance-controlled selection. Over-representation of validation patches in the tail can indicate a train–validation reconstruction gap; it does not establish that the patches are physical anomalies. The quoted binomial probability assumes independent patches despite acknowledged spatial correlation. Recovery of an artificial Gaussian bump added at five times the standardized patch rms is not a realistic CMB, foreground, beam, or instrumental-noise validation. End-to-end CMB-plus-foreground-plus-noise simulations, disjoint sky splits, realistic masks and covariance, and a calibrated look-elsewhere analysis are needed.

[MAJOR] §IV A and Fig. 6 — “genuine novelty fraction” is not demonstrated. Absence from 18 catalog cone searches is an unmatched-catalog fraction, not proof of a previously unknown astrophysical source. The analysis does not adequately condition on catalog footprints and depths, proper motion, coordinate epochs, local crowding, source morphology, or the large non-science-target fraction of the DESI catalog. The Wilson interval accounts only for binomial sampling and not these dominant systematics. Moreover, the pooled 58.8% statistic is internally inconsistent: 235/400 is described as the top 100 from three surveys, while the fourth historical Gaia sample is simultaneously invoked to explain the denominator and said to be excluded. These quantities must be recomputed and renamed.

[MAJOR] §V A and Appendix E — the NANOGrav analysis does not support a matter-bounce inference. This analysis does not use the anomaly catalog and is scientifically disconnected from the principal result. The manuscript does not derive the mapping from the stated bounce model to the PTA convention γ=3, including its amplitude, transfer function, or relevant parameter dependence. Multiplication of per-frequency KDEs neglects covariance beyond the compressed product, while the Bayes factor against γ=4.33 depends on an extreme KDE tail whose bandwidth robustness is not shown. Expressing displacement in units of a sample standard deviation is also inappropriate for the acknowledged non-Gaussian posterior. A density ratio between two fixed spectral indices is not evidence for the full matter-bounce model over realistic environmentally modified SMBHB models.

[MAJOR] §II D, §§III E–G, and the Data Availability statement — reproducibility claims conflict with the documented provenance. The manuscript reports an unrecoverable eROSITA score axis, a synthetic Gaia product that survived into earlier catalog versions, missing DESI production score products, and a Planck checkpoint and patch tensor residing on an exited node and not in the public release. It nevertheless states that every quantitative result is independently recomputable and that no headline result depends on unavailable artifacts. The catalog and immutable DOI are promised only at submission or arXiv posting rather than supplied with the review package. Given that the central claims depend on numerous external JSON artifacts, a clean-room rerun from frozen inputs, code, weights, and manifests is necessary before scientific review can be completed.

[MAJOR] Title, Abstract, Tables I–II, and §VII — the 37.3-million scale claim is misleading. The manuscript gives three different totals—36.758 million retained-native inputs, 36.93 million read/scored entries, and 37.292 million cross-transfer-inclusive process volume. The largest number includes historical or repeated passes and quarantined or excised inputs, yet the title presents it as a count of sources and patches scanned across six archives. Unique records and repeated computational passes must be reported separately. The accompanying 73× and 141× multipliers compare heterogeneous process-volume selections with a science-target catalog and should be removed from the scientific summary.

[MINOR] Figures and presentation. Several prominent figures display superseded, failed, or removed analyses rather than the primary catalog: Figs. 3–4 show cross-transfer results, Fig. 6 uses an obsolete pooled denominator, Fig. 9 shows the superseded fixed-α forecast, and Fig. 10 includes the removed Gaia tier. The primary native-retrained catalog needs direct diagnostic figures, while historical failure-mode plots should be moved to an appendix and clearly segregated.

CENTRAL CLAIM: No—the manuscript demonstrates that a very large set of reconstruction outliers was generated, but not that 268,519 entries constitute a validated, catalog-grade set of real astrophysical anomalies.
