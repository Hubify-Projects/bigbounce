VERDICT: MINOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MINOR] Abstract & Sec. I: The headline sensitivity range ∼1.3–2.75σ conflates qualitatively distinct procedures (noise-weighted template recast, proxy GR correlation from SDB channel, channel-native Fisher with in-house covariance, and bϕ-prior widening); the abstract must explicitly flag the 1.3σ floor as a conservative proxy-based marginal estimate rather than a direct channel-native precision.
[MAJOR] Sec. II A & Appendix A: The central claim that Cai et al.’s printed f_NL = −35/8 is erroneous (due to a −(99/128)∑k_i³ term in the transcribed polynomial) is supported only by the author’s re-summation and benchmark matching; the appendix must display the explicit four-vertex contributions, the exact discrepant monomial, and side-by-side comparison with Cai et al.’s intermediates and Li et al. formula so that the factor-of-two correction can be independently verified without external lookup.
[MINOR] Sec. II A & throughout: Repeated references to internal filenames, committed JSON outputs, and script names (e.g., c15_channel_native_fisher.json, null_space_analysis.py, phase3_bispectrum_shape_overlap.json) clutter the text and must be removed from the narrative, appearing only in a single Data Availability statement.
[MINOR] Sec. IV & VII: The independent tree-level multi-tracer Fisher validation reproduces the Heinrich et al. baseline to 2–11% and yields r_eff ≈ 0.99, which is valuable, but the paper must state more quantitatively how much the headline 1.3–2.75σ range would shift if the full public Heinrich covariance (including non-Gaussian terms) were used instead of the Gaussian diagonal-triangle surrogate.
[MINOR] Sec. V: The MegaMapper projections are explicitly labeled “uncalibrated” and “illustrative,” yet the abstract and Fig. 2 present 1.5–3.5σ and 3.7–3.85σ ranges without sufficient emphasis that they rest on a transferred SPHEREx systematic budget at z = 2–5 where GR projection kernels grow rapidly; a short dedicated caveat paragraph is required.
[MINOR] Sec. VI C & abstract: The Bayes-factor range ≈9–14 is correctly qualified in the text as “illustrative” and prior-dependent, but the abstract and concluding sentence must repeat this qualification at the same prominence given to the numerical range so readers do not misinterpret it as robust model-selection evidence.

The central claim that the corrected matter-bounce f_NL = −35/16 produces a marginal but potentially detectable SPHEREx signal (∼1.3–2.75σ after template mismatch and systematics) with illustrative Bayesian preference over tuned multifield competitors is supported by the explicit overlap calculation, independent Fisher validation, and closed-form Bayes-factor cross-checks, though the lower edge remains proxy-limited and the overall detection is marginal.
