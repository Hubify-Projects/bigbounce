VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Sec. IV A and Appendix D (Route 1 NJL closure): The claim that the regulated gap equation excludes a vacuum condensate because the scalar channel is repulsive (G_scalar = −3/64 κ < 0) relies on the Fierz projection lemma of Appendix C; however, the explicit mapping from (J^5 · J^5) to the scalar channel and the sign of the effective four-fermion coupling in the gap equation are not cross-checked against standard NJL literature results for the Hehl–Datta term, and the statement “no condensate forms at any coupling strength” requires the full effective-potential minimum analysis (not just the sign) to be shown or cited.
[MAJOR] Sec. IV D–E (Routes 2 and 3 amplitude budgets): The one-loop parity-odd operator (Eq. 17) and the chiral-count running ansatz (Eq. 19) are presented as illustrative upper-bound EFT estimates, but the paper does not demonstrate that these exhaust the possible contributions at that order (e.g., additional diagrams, higher-loop effects, or non-minimal fermion–Holst vertices could alter the coefficient); the ≳58–60-order suppression margin is therefore conditional on the specific ansatz chosen rather than a general bound.
[MAJOR] Sec. IX and abstract (14-constraint catalog and “basis-complete at the M_Pl-power-counting level”): While the paper carefully qualifies that the four routes are channel-level and assumption-conditional (not a full operator-level theorem), the catalog of 14 barriers is never reproduced as a single summary table in the main text, making it impossible for a reader to audit at a glance which barriers are load-bearing for each route closure and which are historical; this weakens the central “channel-level closure” claim.
[MINOR] Sec. II C 1 and XII A (inflationary suppression and N_tot ≈ 92): The dilution factor D_inf contains a phenomenological (T_reh/M_GUT)^{3/2} parity-odd phase-space prefactor whose justification is acknowledged as dimensional-analysis aesthetic rather than derived from a partition function; a short explicit derivation or reference for this factor (or a demonstration that the result is insensitive to it) is needed for reproducibility.
[MINOR] Sec. X (perturbation-transparency result): The theorem is correctly restricted to canonical scalar matter, but the abstract and introduction do not prominently restate the excluded cases (fermion spin density, propagating torsion, dynamical Immirzi, non-minimal couplings) when summarizing implications for bounce cosmologies and the dark-energy vs. f_NL tension; this risks over-reading the scope by casual readers.
[MINOR] Sec. I and IV (R4 naturalness closure): Closing Route 4 by “naturalness / explanatory-deficit objection” rather than amplitude mismatch is explicitly labeled as such, but the paper should add one paragraph contrasting this with standard naturalness arguments in the CC problem literature (e.g., how it differs from Weinberg’s no-go or quintessence tuning) to clarify why it counts as a “closure” at the same logical tier as the amplitude suppressions of R1–R3.

The central claim—that the four enumerated minimal-ECH dark-energy routes close at channel level under the stated assumptions, with the perturbation-transparency result holding for canonical scalar matter and a structural tension excluding simultaneous realization of ECH-sourced dark energy and an observable matter-bounce f_NL—is supported by the explicit operator reductions, dimensional power-counting, Fierz lemma, and one-loop estimates presented.
