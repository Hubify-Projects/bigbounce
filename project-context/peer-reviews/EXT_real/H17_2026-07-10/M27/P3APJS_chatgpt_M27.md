[MAJOR] §2.4 and §6.4—validation tests do not validate the released selections. The DESI injection-recovery calculation uses a 99th-percentile threshold on a tail-excluded “clean” holdout and broad synthetic perturbations, whereas the released DESI catalog uses S>5; SDSS is released using the unrelated continuity threshold; and Planck and NEOWISE use fixed-count selections. Injection curves must be evaluated using the actual released decision rules, with realistic families of astrophysical signals and instrumental defects.

[MAJOR] §3.1, Table 3, and §3.3—contradictory interpretation of the DESI population. Section 3.1 finds that only 2,468 of 190,015 deduplicated DESI clusters match primary science-class targets, that approximately 98.7% lack a primary science bit, and that 86% have DESI_TARGET = 0; §3.3 later argues that 98.8% are Redrock-classified galaxies and therefore are “not sky/fiber/calibration artifacts.” A Redrock template classification at a sky, filler, secondary, or calibration fiber position does not establish a real astrophysical source. The manuscript must reconcile these populations and remove or separately classify sky fibers, calibration spectra, secondary programs, failed extractions, and repeated observations before claiming a catalog of sources.

[MAJOR] §2.2, §6.4(i), and Data Availability—row-level reproducibility is absent for the dominant tier. The manuscript states that 86.6% of released DESI identifiers are internal hashes, only about 1.3% of released rows can be re-pulled, and the production score parquets and exact input linkage were lost. Releasing code and model weights does not make the published catalog independently reproducible when the spectra corresponding to most rows cannot be identified or rescored. [MAJOR] §2.1–2.3—spectral preprocessing is insufficiently specified and likely drives the result. The paper does not adequately document the wavelength grid, arm joining, bad-pixel and mask handling, inverse variances, interpolation, missing-arm treatment, training-pool sampling, ensemble aggregation, or treatment of negative and near-zero spectra. Dividing each 496-bin spectrum by its own nonzero-bin median is particularly problematic for sky and low-flux fibers and could amplify noise catastrophically. Given that nearly all DESI candidates are non-primary targets, this preprocessing must be demonstrated not to manufacture the anomaly tail.

[MAJOR] §2.2—statistical meaning of the anomaly score. Thresholds require empirical tail calibration on independent data, with estimated contamination and completeness, rather than sigma terminology derived from a z-transform.
