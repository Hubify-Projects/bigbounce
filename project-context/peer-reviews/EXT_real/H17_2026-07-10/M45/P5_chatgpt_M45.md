(2) ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Central claim / dependence on companion Paper IV. The manuscript's primary observable depends on classifier labels generated in an unpublished companion paper. Although the author argues that the environmental contrast is algebraically independent of the classifier monopole, referees still cannot independently assess label quality, calibration, environment-dependent misclassification, or provenance until Paper IV is publicly available and citable.

[MAJOR] Statistical status of the focal analysis. The manuscript explicitly states that the focal released-parent analysis hierarchy was changed after inspection of the data and is exploratory rather than preregistered. The reported p-value therefore cannot be interpreted with the strength implied by a confirmatory hypothesis test, and stronger separation between exploratory and confirmatory claims is needed.

[MAJOR] Multiplicity and analytical flexibility. The manuscript evaluates numerous environment definitions, T-Web parameter grids, DESIVAST variants, membership definitions, density measures, redshift stratifications, weighting schemes, footprint restrictions, and cross-validations before selecting a focal estimand. Although the paper discusses multiplicity qualitatively, it does not provide a rigorous framework showing how this analytical flexibility affects evidential strength.

[MAJOR] Covariance treatment for the focal estimator. The cluster-sandwich covariance is computed using coarse NSIDE=4 sky blocks, with an alternative clustering based on nearest void maximals presented only as sensitivity. The manuscript does not adequately justify that these clustering scales capture the true spatial dependence expected in large-scale-structure analyses.

[MAJOR] Physical interpretation. The manuscript appropriately states that the result is a classifier-label null in redshift space rather than a physical handedness constraint, but portions of the Discussion still compare the result with cosmological parity-violation literature. These comparisons should be substantially softened because the observable is not a direct physical handedness measurement.

[MAJOR] T-Web methodology. Large sections document known deficiencies of the T-Web implementation (selection-function contamination, survey-shell effects, randoms-weighted reassignment, under-resolved Rs=10 cells, poor agreement with ASTRA, disagreement with DESIVAST voids). These limitations substantially reduce the scientific weight of the secondary analyses and should be condensed or relegated to appendices rather than occupying much of the Results section.

[MAJOR] Reproducibility. The manuscript references numerous internal artifacts, JSON outputs, scripts, frozen release candidates, and Git commits that are not yet publicly archived under immutable DOIs or release tags. Physical Review D requires that essential analyses be independently reproducible at publication.

[MINOR] Presentation. The manuscript is substantially longer than necessary because many robustness checks are described repeatedly. The main scientific message would be clearer if the principal released-parent analysis remained in the main text while most sensitivity studies moved to appendices or supplementary material.

[MINOR] Statistical notation. The paper introduces multiple related statistics (σfrom half, σpred, σvs monopole, zΔ, permutation p-values, cluster-sandwich intervals). Despite the glossary, the notation remains difficult to follow and could be simplified.

[MINOR] Organization. Several caveats and methodological qualifications are repeated in multiple sections (Introduction, Results, Discussion, Limitations, Conclusions). Consolidation would improve readability.

[MINOR] Discussion of uncertainty. Cosmic variance, redshift-space distortions, and classifier uncertainty are repeatedly acknowledged but not incorporated into a unified uncertainty framework. A concise discussion of what uncertainties are and are not represented in the reported confidence interval would improve clarity.

[MINOR] Figures. Most figures adequately support the narrative, but several (particularly those illustrating sensitivity analyses) could be moved to supplementary material without reducing scientific content.

(3) Is the central claim supported? Yes—within the manuscript's stated scope, the evidence supports the limited claim that this exploratory analysis finds no statistically significant dependence of classifier-labelled spiral chirality on the released DESIVAST void/non-void environment, but it does not support broader claims about physical galaxy handedness or cosmological parity.
