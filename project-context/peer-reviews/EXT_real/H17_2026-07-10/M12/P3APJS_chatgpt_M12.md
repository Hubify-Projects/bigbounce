VERDICT: REJECT

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, §3, and Table 2 — the “validated catalog-grade” designation is not justified. The 268,519 entries are assembled using fundamentally different and partly arbitrary selection rules: DESI uses S>5; SDSS contributes a fixed-size 77,905-object “continuity slice” chosen to reproduce an earlier count, despite the native top-1% set containing 19,253 objects and the native S>5 set only 12; Planck contributes a predetermined top 200; and NEOWISE contributes a predetermined top 1% followed by a mask test that passes by construction. These procedures neither define a common selection function nor establish object-level validity, purity, or comparable anomaly significance. Disclosure that the validation is “mixed” does not make the aggregate catalog-grade.

[MAJOR] §3.1 — the dominant DESI tier is primarily an instrumental-stream catalog, not a catalog of astrophysical sources. The manuscript reports that approximately 98.7% of the 190,015 deduplicated DESI clusters do not match primary science-class targets and that 86% have DESI_TARGET = 0, yet all 195,829 detections are counted as source anomalies and most are described as point sources. Sky fibers, filler observations, calibration exposures, and failed or low-information spectra require separate treatment from astrophysical targets. No stratified audit by fiber type, observing condition, exposure, reduction status, or sky-subtraction metric is presented. The 2,468 science-class matches, not the 195,829 full-stream detections, are the only defensible basis for an astrophysical catalog claim.

[MAJOR] §3.1, Table 3, and §3.3 — the DESI target accounting is internally unresolved. The reported validated-TARGETTYPE rates imply roughly 3.7×10
4
 GALAXY/QSO anomalies, whereas the positional science-bit recount yields only 2,468; Table 3 states that these rates use the same class denominators but attributes the discrepancy to different filter stacks without providing an object-level crosswalk. Section 3.3 then states that all 195,790 primary-coadd anomalies join to the Redrock catalog and are 98.8% classified as GALAXY, while also stating that only approximately 0.1% have secure redshifts. A template class from an unsuccessful or warned Redrock fit is not evidence that a spectrum contains a real astrophysical source. The manuscript must reconcile identifiers, target bits, TARGETTYPE, SPECTYPE, ZWARN, and positional matches row by row.

[MAJOR] §3.1 and §6.5 — the claimed “like-for-like” comparison with Liang et al. is not like-for-like. Comparing 2,468 anomalies from a 20.3-million-row, program-row science-bit denominator with 2,685 anomalies from an approximately 250,000-spectrum EDR science sample by absolute count conceals an approximately 80-fold denominator difference and very different quality and target definitions. The corresponding rates, approximately 0.012% and 1.07%, are not remotely equivalent. The “0.92×” framing is therefore misleading and should not be used as a benchmark statement.

[MAJOR] §2.2, §6.4(i), and Data Availability — the principal catalog is not reproducible end to end. The manuscript states that 86.6% of released DESI identifiers are internal hashes, that only approximately 1.3% of released rows can be re-pulled from SPARCL, and that the native score parquets and original input linkage were lost with the compute pod. The provided deduplication script can recompute a count from already-generated membership lists; it cannot regenerate the spectra, preprocessing, scores, threshold membership, or catalog from the public archive. This contradicts the repeated claim that every headline result is independently recomputable and is incompatible with an ApJS catalog whose dominant component cannot be reconstructed or audited.

[MAJOR] §2.1–2.2 — the survey selection functions and preprocessing are insufficiently specified and inadequately controlled. The DESI training set is described only as 47,000 “representative” spectra, without a reproducible account of its target-class balance, fiber types, observing conditions, signal-to-noise distribution, reduction flags, or sampling procedure. The manuscript does not provide a complete survey-by-survey specification of wavelength interpolation, bad-pixel and arm-edge masks, inverse-variance handling, missing-data treatment, or quality rejection. A universal validation-loss gate of 0.30 is not meaningful across models with different dimensions, scaling conventions, and data domains. The unweighted MSE gives high-noise channels and instrumental residuals the same weight as well-measured astrophysical features, yet no noise-weighted sensitivity or catalog comparison is performed.

[MAJOR] §2.2 and Table 2 — the score thresholds are not statistically calibrated. Standardizing reconstruction MSE by a mean and standard deviation does not make a heavy-tailed reconstruction-error distribution Gaussian or turn S=5 into a controlled five-sigma false-positive threshold. No null distribution, expected false-discovery rate, stability of the threshold under retraining, or precision–recall benchmark is supplied. For several tiers the count is explicitly fixed in advance, so the quoted anomaly “rates” are design choices rather than measured occurrence rates.

[MAJOR] §6.4(i–ii) — the injection-recovery experiments do not validate the released memberships. The DESI test uses synthetic broad features planted into the cleanest 5% of a newly retrieved 20,000-spectrum sample and evaluates recovery against a tail-excluded 99th-percentile threshold; the connection between this test threshold and the released S>5 membership is not demonstrated. The result establishes sensitivity to that particular broad injected morphology under unusually clean conditions, not the purity or completeness of the 195,829 catalog entries. Narrow features require at least 15σ, and no realistic injections of sky residuals, flux-calibration errors, arm discontinuities, cosmic rays, low-S/N failures, or reduction artifacts are tested. The short-trained k-fold models fail the paper’s own validation-loss gate, and the reported Jaccard and tail-preservation statistics are correlated functions of the same proxy-model scores.

[MAJOR] §3.3, Figure 4, Table 4, and §6.2 — the SDSS evidence is largely computed on a different catalog from the one released. Figure 4, the 14-cluster HDBSCAN result, and the emission-line taxonomy describe the 77,905-object DESI-to-SDSS cross-transfer set, whereas the released tier is a native-retrained 77,905-object slice selected merely to have the same cardinality. The overlap between those two memberships is not reported. Equal sample size does not permit the cross-transfer composition, cool-dwarf fraction, cluster structure, or taxonomy to be attributed to the native catalog. Section 6.2 also continues to describe SDSS as a transfer-learning result, contradicting the claimed Path-C replacement. All SDSS characterizations must be rerun on the actual released membership.

[MAJOR] §3.6 — the Planck tier is not independently validated and is improperly combined with point sources. The native 200,000-patch bank is both the training/scoring pool and the source of the released top 200; no held-out inference of the released ranking is performed. Counting how many selected patches happened to lie in the nominal validation split is not a held-out scoring test, and the quoted binomial probability assumes independent patches despite overlapping 10° fields and spatially correlated CMB structure. Recovery of an artificial 5σ Gaussian bump added after patch standardization does not demonstrate sensitivity to, or purity against, real foregrounds, scan-pattern effects, mapmaking residuals, or point-source contamination. Finally, applying a 5-arcsec point-source friends-of-friends deduplication to the centers of 10° CMB patches is a category error; map regions and compact sources require separate data models and association rules.

[MAJOR] §3.8 — NEOWISE cannot be included in a validated tier. Its only reported gate plants sources outside the adopted ecliptic mask and then applies that same mask, so 100% “recovery” is mathematically guaranteed and contains no information about detector sensitivity. The feature scaler is fitted on the full sample, including the anomaly tail, and the train-only-scaler robustness check is explicitly still pending. The construction and selection function of the 43,518-source parent sample are also not adequately described. These 419 entries may be released as an exploratory list, but not as catalog-grade detections.

[MAJOR] §4.1 — the 17.8% “genuine novelty fraction” is not a valid discovery-rate estimate. The top-1,000 DESI sample is drawn from the full instrument stream, which the manuscript itself shows is dominated by sky, filler, and non-primary fibers; absence of an object-catalog counterpart at a sky-fiber position is expected and is not evidence of a novel astrophysical source. The 18-catalog match uses a single positional radius without local source-density corrections, likelihood-ratio matching, proper-motion treatment, footprint/depth completeness, or manual confirmation of the 178 unmatched cases. Chance matches in the combined deep catalogs are also not estimated. These objects may be described as unmatched fiber positions pending verification, not as a “genuinely novel population.”

[MAJOR] §3.1 and §6.1 — there is no adequate catalog-purity assessment. The sole DESI visual check covers the top 200 objects and appears to have been performed by one examiner using a narrow list of 11 sky/telluric wavelengths. It is neither blinded nor independently replicated and does not sample lower scores, target classes, sky fibers, observing conditions, or the other surveys. The 0/200 binomial bound applies only to that selected top-ranked set and to the stated artifact definition; it cannot be extrapolated to contamination in 195,829 DESI detections or 268,519 aggregate entries. A stratified, independently labeled validation set with inter-rater agreement and per-tier positive predictive values is required.

[MAJOR] §4.3 — the cross-survey association significance is unsupported. A uniform 5-arcsec radius is used for sub-arcsecond optical positions, NEOWISE sources with a much broader PSF, and CMB patch centers, without pair-specific astrometric uncertainties or association probabilities. The claimed expectation of at most ten random coincidences relies mainly on a non-geometry-preserving RA-shift control and asserts that all non-DESI×SDSS survey pairs contribute less than one coincidence without presenting the necessary pairwise footprint integrals; this is not credible given the sizes and overlapping footprints of the LAMOST, DESI, and SDSS tiers. The radius-sensitivity sweep demonstrates numerical stability of the count, not correctness or purity of the associations.

[MAJOR] Tables 1–2 and Data Availability — the catalog accounting and released product are internally inconsistent. The manuscript alternately uses 36.76, 36.93, and 37.29 million as the scanned volume, with the title value counting historical or superseded processing passes rather than a single set of unique inputs. Table 2 lists 20,000 as the Planck denominator even though the released top 200 were selected from 200,000 native patches, and it mixes native counts with cross-transfer SIMBAD fractions and classifications. Most seriously, the Data Availability section states that LAMOST is excluded from the released per-object tables and “every headline count,” while the 377,482 headline is repeatedly defined as including approximately 113,000 LAMOST detections. No complete catalog-schema table, column definitions, units, null conventions, identifier semantics, or exact tier-by-tier release inventory is provided in the manuscript.

[MAJOR] §3.5, §3.7, and the AI-methodology statement — the provenance failures require an independent audit of all retained tiers. One nominal survey product was generated by a synthetic fallback and another has an unrecoverable production score axis. Removing Gaia and isolating eROSITA are necessary, but these failures show that the pipeline’s prior internal checks did not prevent fabricated or irreproducible data products from entering the analysis. The assertion that analogous audits validate all retained surveys is supported only by repository-path references, while the DESI linkage and native scores are acknowledged to be lost. Acceptance would require independent, archive-to-output provenance verification for every retained row, not an author assertion that such an audit occurred.

[MAJOR] §5–5.1 — the cosmological analyses are unsupported by the catalog and should be removed from this submission. The relation between the 5,384-object angular-clustering bias sample and the separate 40,192-object redshift-binned Fisher tracer population is not defined, and no complete Fisher matrix, redshift distributions, angular masks, window functions, shot-noise treatment on the primary normalization, or observational-systematics covariance is supplied. The GOLD and SILVER cuts overlap as written, making the stated total of 1,122 ambiguous unless the tiers are explicitly disjoint. The NANOGrav calculation uses no anomaly-catalog information at all and is therefore not an application of the submitted data product; its tail-density Bayes factor also lacks KDE-bandwidth and free-spectrum-likelihood robustness tests. Both topics belong in separate, fully specified analyses.

[MINOR] Title, abstract, figures, and conclusions — the manuscript is excessively repetitive and retains obsolete analysis states. The abstract is nearly two pages, the same qualifications and counts recur throughout, Figures 2, 4, and 10 foreground quarantined, cross-transfer, or removed products, and terms such as “real,” “genuine,” “validated,” “largest,” and “like-for-like” are used more strongly than the presented tests permit. A publishable catalog paper would need a substantially shorter structure centered on one final data product, one consistent set of thresholds, and one reproducible validation framework.

CENTRAL CLAIM: The central claim that the 268,519-entry release is a validated, reproducible catalog of astrophysical anomaly candidates is not supported by the evidence presented.
