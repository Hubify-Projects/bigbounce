VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, Sec. IV (Scope paragraph), and title: The paper repeatedly qualifies its result as a “channel-level” assessment of four “enumerated” routes that “are not proven to be a complete diffeomorphism-invariant operator basis,” yet frames the outcome as a decisive “no-go,” “closure,” and “14-constraint catalog” that tightly constrains the minimal-ECH dark-energy program. This creates a mismatch between the modest logical claim actually established and the stronger language used in the title, abstract, and conclusions; the qualification must be elevated to equal prominence with the positive claims.
[MAJOR] Sec. IV F (Route 4) and Sec. IX (Barrier catalog): R4 is closed by a naturalness/explanatory-deficit argument (the same coupling that fits β_obs forces an ultralight m_θ ∼ H_0 tuning, “relocating the CC problem”). This is qualitatively different from the explicit amplitude-suppression arguments used for R1–R3 and from the operator-level statements in Secs. IV A–C. Treating “naturalness closure” on equal footing with amplitude closure without a separate tiered classification risks conflating a model-building aesthetic with a mathematical no-go.
[MAJOR] Sec. X (Perturbation-Transparency Result) and its use in Sec. IX (Barrier 14 subsuming Barrier 8): The theorem is explicitly restricted to canonical scalar matter and excludes fermion spin density, propagating torsion, dynamical Immirzi, non-minimal couplings, and boundary sectors. Because the dark-energy and bounce mechanisms under discussion frequently invoke or are sensitive to precisely these excluded sectors, the result’s domain of applicability to the overall ECH program must be delimited more sharply; the present wording risks overstating its reach.
[MAJOR] Sec. IV A and App. D (NJL gap-equation closure of R1 vacuum condensate): The Fierz projection onto the scalar channel yields a repulsive coupling and the gap equation admits only the trivial solution. However, the mapping from the ECH torsion-elimination operator to the regulated NJL model (cutoff choice Λ = M_Pl, mean-field truncation, validity of the Fierz rearrangement at strong coupling) is an effective-theory approximation whose robustness at Planckian densities is not demonstrated; non-perturbative or non-mean-field effects could reopen the condensate channel.
[MINOR] Sec. IX: The 13 (or 14) barriers are presented as a systematic catalog, yet most entries consist of a one-line title with only a parenthetical reference to a “Foundation” or “Branch.” For a self-contained PRD article the reader must be able to reconstruct, at minimum, the logical skeleton of each barrier from material inside the manuscript; either short derivations or explicit equation-level pointers must be supplied.
[MINOR] Sec. II C, App. B, and the N_tot ≈ 92 bookkeeping: Two distinct dimensional completions (on-shell curvature dressing vs. local-operator promotion) are invoked to obtain the same numerical dilution factor. While both readings agree at order-of-magnitude level, the paper must state which completion is used for each quantitative claim and verify that the subsequent barriers and tension arguments remain insensitive to the choice.
[MINOR] Overall length, notation density, and cross-referencing: At 63 pages the manuscript is at the upper end of what PRD normally accommodates. Repetitive restatements of scope/assumptions, the proliferation of custom barrier labels (B1–B14), and the heavy use of footnotes that send the reader to appendices or “companion papers” impair readability; selective condensation and clearer sign-posting of load-bearing versus illustrative material would strengthen the submission.

The central claim—that the four enumerated minimal-ECH dark-energy routes are constrained at the channel-amplitude level (amplitude suppression for R1–R3, naturalness deficit for R4) and that canonical scalar perturbations are transparent to the Holst sector under the stated assumptions—is supported by the dimensional power-counting, explicit operator enumeration, Fierz-projection lemma, and Bianchi-identity arguments given in Secs. IV, IX, and X, subject to the scope limitations and assumptions the paper itself enumerates.
