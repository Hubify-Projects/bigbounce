VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, §3 (three-tier catalog structure), and Table 2: The headline “validated catalog-grade subset of 268,519 unique anomalies” applies a uniform label to a heterogeneous product in which only DESI, SDSS, and Planck pass detector-sensitivity injection-recovery at 5σ while NEOWISE passes only a masking-geometry QA gate by construction; eROSITA (298 objects) and the former synthetic Gaia tier (500 objects) are excised entirely for irreproducibility and synthetic content. This renders the single validated count misleading without explicit per-component validity flags in every summary statistic.
[MAJOR] §3.5 and pipelines/p3 anomaly engine/r24conf erosita axis sweep.json: The eROSITA production score axis (threshold 0.259) is irreproducible from the committed raw reconstruction-score artifact; it fails all 16 tested monotone rescalings and three IsolationForest retrains, and the top-5 production scores are non-monotone (Spearman ρ = −0.10). Even though the tier is correctly excised to a membership-list addendum, the documented provenance failure indicates insufficient end-to-end pipeline documentation and version control for an ApJS catalog submission.
[MAJOR] §3.1, Table 3, and reader’s guide: The DESI science-target yield after 1″ positional match to primary BGS/LRG/ELG/QSO/MWS bitmask spectra is only 2,468 clusters (≈0.92× the Liang et al. 2023 benchmark); ≈98.7% of the 195,829 raw DESI anomalies lie on sky-fiber or filler spectra. The ~73×–141× “process-volume multipliers” therefore compare a full-instrument-stream scan against a science-target benchmark and are not like-for-like catalog-size gains; foregrounding the large headline numbers while burying this scope limitation in footnotes risks reader misinterpretation.
[MAJOR] §2.2, §2.4, §6.4(i), and desi injection recovery/desi injrec CORRECTED.json: DESI robustness rests on a single production-ensemble injection-recovery test (broad/extended class recovers 99–100% at 5σ) supported only by correlated fold-stability checks performed on deliberately short-trained proxy models (val loss > 0.30). Full per-object re-inference of the released 22.5 M-spectrum catalog is structurally blocked by exited-pod raw score parquets and mixed TARGETID/hashed identifiers (only ~1.3% of rows are re-pullable via SPARCL), so independent verification of the exact released catalog is not possible from the committed artifacts alone.
[MINOR] §2.4 (Path-C rebuild) and §3.4: The LAMOST native-retrain tier (113,342 objects) is retained in the inclusive 377,482 total as a “methodological lesson” despite 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact and 5.8% injection-recovery at 5σ. Its inclusion—even when explicitly flagged—complicates the reader’s ability to extract the clean validated subset without cross-referencing multiple footnotes and tier definitions.
[MINOR] §5 and abstract: The two cosmological applications (multi-tracer fNL and NANOGrav spectral-index check) are presented as secondary demonstrations yet occupy substantial text; both return null or marginal results (fNL shift lies inside the 1σ single-tracer envelope; NANOGrav γ = 2.567 ± 0.382 is only decisive against an idealized circular-orbit SMBHB reference). Their inclusion lengthens the manuscript without strengthening the primary catalog deliverable.

The central claim of a validated 268,519-object multi-survey anomaly-candidate catalog is only partially supported: the injection-recovery evidence is solid for the broad/continuum-dominated class in three surveys and the native-retrain protocol is clearly documented, but heterogeneous validation, irreproducible production axes in excised tiers, pod-lost artifacts, and the overwhelming dominance of non-science-target spectra prevent the full headline count from meeting uniform catalog-grade standards.
