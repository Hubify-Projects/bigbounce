VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] §2.2 (and §6.4(i) DESI robustness claim): The assertion that “the full count is directly recomputable via the committed standalone script” (pipelines/p3 anomaly engine/scripts/reproduce headline dedup.py) and that the anomaly axis is fully reproducible is not supported for the dominant DESI tier. The released catalog mixes real TARGETIDs (only 13.4%) with internal hashed identifiers; only ~1.3% of rows are re-pullable from SPARCL, and “a full per-object held-out re-inference of the released 22.5M-spectrum catalog with the production ensemble remains blocked” because raw native score parquets reside on an exited pod. The provided fresh-pull re-inference test demonstrates pipeline-level consistency but does not enable exact reconstruction or verification of the published 195,829-object list. This is a critical shortfall for an ApJS catalog data product.
[MAJOR] Abstract, §1, and §3 (three-tier catalog structure and headline counts): The primary deliverable is framed as a “validated catalog-grade subset of 268,519 unique anomalies” passing “detector-sensitivity injection-recovery gate.” However, validation is explicitly non-uniform and mixed: DESI/SDSS/Planck pass for the broad/continuum class only; NEOWISE passes only a masking-geometry QA gate “by construction, not a detector-sensitivity test”; narrow single-pixel lines recover only at ≥15σ (stated sensitivity floor); and ~98.7% of DESI anomalies lie on sky/filler spectra (only 2,468 science-target clusters). The “catalog-grade” label and process-volume multipliers (~141×, ~73×) therefore overstate the certified scope and immediate scientific usability relative to the like-for-like benchmark (2,468 vs. Liang et al. 2,685).
[MINOR] §3.5 (eROSITA DR1): The production score axis (threshold 0.259) is irreproducible on any of 16 tested monotone rescalings or IsolationForest retrains; the published values are non-monotone in the committed raw artifact (Spearman ρ = −0.10). While the tier is correctly excised and replaced by a reproducible top-298 membership list, the existence of an undocumented post-hoc rescaling step in the production workflow indicates a provenance gap that should be explicitly diagnosed and prevented in future pipeline iterations.
[MINOR] §3.1 and Table 3 (DESI science-target recount): The positional cross-match against zall-pix shows only 2,468/190,015 deduplicated clusters (~1.3%) match primary science-target bitmasks at 1″. The paper correctly identifies this as a fiber-assignment and TARGETTYPE-scope effect, but provides no quantitative assessment of the resulting selection function (e.g., bias toward fiber-collision-dense regions or secondary programs) or its impact on the high-z QSO candidate subsample and downstream multi-tracer applications.
[MINOR] §2.2 (tabular feature preprocessing) and §6.4(ii): Full-sample (rather than train-split-only) scalers are used for eROSITA and NEOWISE; the bounded robustness check exists only for eROSITA (top-298 Jaccard 0.76). The NEOWISE train-split-only check is listed as “queued.” This introduces a small but quantifiable risk of validation-set leakage into normalization constants that affects absolute MSE scale (though within-survey ranking is expected to be robust).

One sentence: The central claim—that a reproducible, multi-survey autoencoder anomaly catalog of 268,519 sources (with validated broad-class injection-recovery for the dominant DESI/SDSS/Planck components) has been produced from a 37.3-million-source native-retrained scan—is supported by the detailed Path-C protocol, per-survey gates, committed reproducibility scripts, and transparent excision of irreproducible tiers, provided the acknowledged limitations on exact per-object DESI re-derivation and non-uniform validation scope are treated as scope conditions rather than minor caveats.
