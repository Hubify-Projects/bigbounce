VERDICT: MINOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, Sec. I, and Sec. II A (central f_NL prediction): The factor-of-two correction from the literature value f_NL = −35/8 to the claimed −35/16 is foundational to every numerical result in the paper (it halves all significances and enters the Bayes factor). While four verification methods are cited (vertex re-summation at ε = 3/2, matching of Cai et al. intermediates, three-benchmark consistency, and Li et al. general-cs formula), the explicit squeezed-limit contributions of each of the four cubic vertices (L_redef, L_ζζ̇², L_ζ̇∂ζ∂χ, L_ζ(∂i∂jχ)²) are not tabulated or derived in the main text; the discrepancy is only traced to a specific −(99/128)∑k_i³ term in the transcribed polynomial of Cai et al. Eq. (37) with reference to Appendix A. This must be shown explicitly (side-by-side with the correct vertex sum) so referees can verify the correction without external reconstruction.
[MAJOR] Sec. I, Sec. IV, and Sec. VII (implied systematics section): The headline conservative floor ∼1.3σ relies on transferring a proxy correlation coefficient ρ = −0.868 from the power-spectrum scale-dependent-bias channel into the bispectrum Fisher because the per-triangle covariance Cov_B of Heinrich et al. is not public. Although a channel-native surrogate calculation (ρ ≈ −0.42, σ_marg ≈ 0.94) is performed as a cross-check, the justification for transferring the correlation across channels (different weighting, different triangle structure) is heuristic; the paper must quantify the sensitivity of the 1.3σ floor to plausible variations in ρ or provide a first-principles estimate of the bispectrum-channel GR–f_NL correlation using the same multi-tracer setup.
[MINOR] Sec. II A and throughout (null-space and template overlap): The 6-coefficient monomial basis for the degree-9 polynomial P is underdetermined (3 benchmarks → 3D null space), producing r = 0.85 ± 0.13 under uniform sampling. While the noise-weighted central value r = 0.84 ± 0.02 is robustly quoted and the shape cosine r_cos > 0.95 is stable, the basis choice and sampling measure introduce a representation uncertainty that is not fully propagated into the headline significance bands; the paper should state explicitly whether the quoted ±0.02 already folds in this component or whether it is treated as a separate systematic.
[MINOR] Sec. I and abstract (presentation of ranges): Multiple significance endpoints (noise-weighted 2.6–2.75σ, GR-only 1.5σ, all-combined 1.3–1.4σ, channel-native ∼2.3σ) are given from qualitatively different procedures. While the distinctions are explained, the abstract and introduction would be clearer with a single recommended “fiducial recast significance” (with explicit assumptions) plus a compact table summarizing how each endpoint is obtained.
[MINOR] Overall length and structure (37 pages): The manuscript contains extensive technical detail on weighting-scheme scans, 10 000-sample null-space Monte Carlos, injection-recovery tests, and internal .json artifacts. Some of this material (full coefficient tables, per-configuration overlap grids, surrogate Fisher matrices) could be moved to Supplemental Material or a reproducibility repository without loss of the core argument, improving readability for the central sensitivity and Bayes-factor claims.

The central claim—that the corrected matter-bounce prediction f_NL = −35/16 yields a SPHEREx detection significance of ∼2.6–2.75σ (optimistic, template-corrected) that reduces to a marginal ∼1.3–2.75σ after a heuristic systematic budget, while preserving useful discriminatory power (BF ≈ 9–14) against tuned multifield inflation—is supported by the internal consistency checks, independent tree-level multi-tracer Fisher validation (reproducing the Heinrich baseline to 2–11% and recovering r_eff ≈ 0.99), closed-form Bayes-factor derivation with three Monte Carlo cross-checks, and transparent disclosure of all assumptions and proxy limitations.
