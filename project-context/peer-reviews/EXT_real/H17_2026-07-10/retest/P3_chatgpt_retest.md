(2) ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, §III, and Table II—The headline “validated” count of 268,519 is not generated by a defensible common selection rule. SDSS contributes 77,905 objects solely through a fixed-size “continuity slice” chosen to reproduce the failed cross-transfer count, whereas the native top-1% selection contains 19,253 objects and the stated S>5 criterion contains only 12. Planck and NEOWISE likewise contribute predetermined top-N samples. The headline count is therefore adjustable by construction and has no objective interpretation as a catalog size, anomaly frequency, or basis for comparison with prior catalogs.

[MAJOR] Abstract claim that “validation establishes that the 268,519 subset is real,” §III three-tier definition, and §VI D—Injection recovery measures sensitivity to the particular planted templates; it does not measure catalog purity or establish that selected entries are astrophysical. There is no labeled negative-control set, false-discovery estimate, independent blinded vetting, or representative follow-up sample. NEOWISE’s gate is guaranteed by the mask definition, Planck uses a high-amplitude Gaussian bump, and SDSS recovers only 64% of continuum-dip injections and 7.2% of emission-line injections at 5σ. These tests cannot support the terms “validated,” “catalog-grade,” or “real” for the full heterogeneous sample.

[MAJOR] §III A, Table III, and §VI A—The DESI component is not shown to be an astrophysical-source catalog. The manuscript finds that approximately 98.7% of DESI anomaly clusters lack a primary science-class target bit and that 86% have DESI_TARGET=0, yet later treats Redrock SPECTYPE labels as proof that the same spectra are real galaxies or quasars rather than sky/filler/calibration observations. A template-fitting label does not provide that proof. Moreover, the preprocessing divides every spectrum by its nonzero-bin median without a stated numerical floor or sky-fiber-specific treatment; for near-zero-continuum sky spectra this can arbitrarily amplify residuals. Dedicated sky-fiber, blank-field, calibration-exposure, and low-continuum controls are required before the 195,829 DESI entries can be retained.

[MAJOR] §II B(c) and §VI D(i)—The five-fold test is incorrectly described as a fully out-of-sample validation. Each fold model is trained on 80% of the 47,000-object pool and then scores the entire pool, so an individual object is scored in-sample in four of the five score vectors. Pairwise Jaccard overlap of those full-pool top-1% sets is therefore not an out-of-sample catalog-stability statistic. More seriously, all five proxy models fail the manuscript’s own retention gate, with best validation loss around 1.91 versus the required ≤0.30. Full held-out inference with the production ensemble is unavailable, and the production threshold flags more than 50% of the uncurated SPARCL sample. These facts invalidate the claimed production-catalog robustness.

[MAJOR] §VI D(i) and Fig. 10—The load-bearing DESI injection test is not matched to the released selection function. It uses the cleanest 5% of spectra as substrates, a tail-excluded 99th-percentile threshold not demonstrated to be identical to the production S>5 cut, and a limited family of broad synthetic perturbations. Narrow features are recovered only at ≥15σ, while the corresponding SDSS emission-line recovery is 7.2%. The result cannot be extrapolated to the catalog’s full mixture of continua, lines, calibration residuals, sky spectra, and low-S/N observations. The omission of the DESI recovery curve from Fig. 10 is also unacceptable given that DESI supplies most of the claimed validated catalog.

[MAJOR] §II B and Table II—The score definition and preprocessing provenance are internally inconsistent. Equation (2) states that native scores are standardized using each survey’s own validation distribution, while Table II says that native SDSS and LAMOST results share the DESI-trained score scale. The tabular-survey scalers were fitted to the full samples, including validation objects and extreme tails, and the corresponding NEOWISE train-only-scaler test was not performed. In addition, S is merely a mean-and-standard-deviation rescaling of a highly non-Gaussian MSE distribution; it is not a calibrated Gaussian detection significance and should not be presented as a “5σ” criterion. No independent model-family benchmark is provided for the spectroscopic tiers, so seed-to-seed stability within one autoencoder architecture does not establish model-independent robustness.

[MAJOR] §IV A—The reported 17.8% “genuine novelty fraction” is only a catalog-nonmatch fraction for the top 1,000 DESI scores. Absence from 18 source catalogs does not establish a new astrophysical object, particularly when most DESI anomalies arise from non-primary target fibers. The analysis does not account adequately for local catalog depth, footprint gaps, proper motion, astrometric uncertainty, blank-sky fibers, extraction failures, or visual confirmation of a source at the fiber position. These objects must be described as unmatched candidates. The 58.8% aggregate statistic is also not defined consistently: the text describes three current top-100 samples but retains a denominator of 400 from a historical pool containing the removed Gaia tier.

[MAJOR] §IV B–C—The spatial and cross-survey statistics omit the required selection functions. Testing uniformity over occupied HEALPix pixels against a common Poisson mean is meaningless for surveys with radically different footprints and targeting densities. Correlating anomaly positions with Galactic latitude or dust without dividing by the corresponding parent-sample exposure does not measure an anomaly rate and cannot exclude foreground or instrumental systematics. The random-coincidence estimates use global densities or RA-only shifts that do not preserve the survey geometry, and the treatment of LAMOST-sized overlapping catalogs is not demonstrated. No significance or astrophysical-origin conclusion should be drawn from these analyses.

[MAJOR] Data Availability statement, §II D, and §§III E–F—The reproducibility claims contradict the disclosed provenance. Native score parquets are missing, the Planck checkpoint and patch tensor are said to reside on an exited node and not be in the release, full DESI production held-out inference cannot be run from the committed artifacts, and the eROSITA production score axis is irrecoverable. The DOI and public release are future commitments rather than reviewable materials. Local repository paths are not evidence of reproducibility. An immutable public release permitting end-to-end regeneration of every retained catalog component is a prerequisite, not a post-acceptance task.

[MAJOR] Title, Tables I–II, and journal scope—The “37.3 million” figure mixes retained native inputs, historical cross-transfer passes, the quarantined ACT run, and reads associated with excised components; the manuscript separately reports 36.758, 36.93, 37.272, and 37.292 million. The title’s description of a “native-trained scan of 37.3 million” across six archives is therefore misleading. More fundamentally, the primary contribution is an astronomical machine-learning catalog, while both cosmological sections are explicitly null, methodologically disconnected add-ons. There is no coherent Physical Review D physics result in the present submission.

[MINOR] Figures and organization—Figures 2–4 emphasize obsolete cross-transfer products and removed or quarantined tiers rather than the released native catalogs; Fig. 8 displays values explicitly identified as non-catalog scores; and Table II places excised samples and predetermined-count “rates” alongside retained results. The manuscript is excessively repetitive and relies on long caveats and repository paths to repair contradictory headline statements. It requires complete restructuring around a single frozen catalog definition, its actual selection functions, and directly relevant validation evidence.
