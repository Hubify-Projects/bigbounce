VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract and Sec. I: The headline sensitivity range ∼1.3–2.75σ (and the 1.3σ conservative floor) is obtained via an explicit but heuristic additive-quadrature systematic budget that imports a proxy correlation ρ = −0.868 from the power-spectrum SDB channel because the per-triangle bispectrum covariance Cov_B of Heinrich et al. is not public. This proxy is not derived from the actual bispectrum Fisher matrix and is acknowledged as non-native; the paper’s own channel-native GR–f_NL correlation (ρ ≈ −0.42) yields a higher floor (∼2.3σ), yet the more conservative proxy is retained in the abstract without a quantitative justification for preferring it over the in-house covariance surrogate.
[MAJOR] Sec. II.A and Appendix A (referenced throughout): The central claim that Cai et al.’s printed f_NL = −35/8 is erroneous (and that the correct squeezed-limit value is −35/16) rests on identifying a −(99/128)∑k_i^3 discrepancy in one transcribed polynomial. While the paper states that the exact vertex sum, Cai’s own ϵ-ordered intermediates, three-configuration benchmarks, and Li et al.’s cs=1 formula all agree on −35/16, the main text does not reproduce the four individual cubic-action vertex contributions or the explicit polynomial comparison; relegating the entire derivation to an unreproduced Appendix makes independent verification impossible for a claim that halves the literature amplitude.
[MAJOR] Sec. IV and Sec. VII: The independent tree-level multi-tracer Fisher (c13_independent_bounce_fisher.py) is presented only as a validation that recovers the Heinrich baseline to 2–11% and gives reff ≈ 0.99; the headline numbers continue to use the external σ(f_local_NL) ≈ 0.7. Because the independent covariance is Gaussian, diagonal-triangle, tree-level, and does not marginalize b2/bs2 or include non-Gaussian six-point terms, the validation is useful but insufficient to replace the external forecast; the paper must either promote the in-house Fisher to the primary result or quantify how the missing higher-order terms affect the recovery factor reff.
[MINOR] Sec. II.C (assumptions d and f) and Sec. VI: Assumption (d) (faithful cubic-order bispectrum transmission) is closed only by a single-clock nonlinear superhorizon ζ-conservation argument giving δf_NL ≲ 10^{-3}; this is the paper’s own “weakest link.” The evasion of the Quintin et al. no-go is likewise conditional on the dressed-metric LQC quantization (c_s^2 = 1 Lorentzian choice). Both are clearly stated, but the abstract presents the sensitivity as applying to “the matter bounce” without repeating these model-specific caveats at the same prominence as the numerical ranges.
[MINOR] Sec. VI.C and abstract: The Bayes-factor range BF ≈ 9–14 is obtained from a four-corner grid of delta vs. Gaussian (σ_theory = 1.0) bounce priors crossed with narrow [−5,+5] vs. broad [−15,+15] competitor priors. The abstract quotes the envelope without specifying that it corresponds to the recommended σ_theory = 1.0 broad-multifield cell (lower edge) up to the delta-prior theoretical maximum (upper edge); readers cannot reconstruct the quoted numbers from the abstract alone.

The central claim—that the corrected f_NL = −35/16 yields a realistic (if marginal) SPHEREx sensitivity of ∼1.3–2.75σ while preserving qualitative discriminatory power against single-field inflation—is supported by the explicit template-overlap calculation, the independent Fisher cross-check, and the itemized systematic budget under the stated assumptions, but only after the above derivations and proxies are made fully reproducible.
