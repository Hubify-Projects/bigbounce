(2) ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract, §V B, §VIII B, and Tables IV/XIV — The abstract calls the footprint-restricted contrast “pre-declared,” whereas §V B explicitly states that no timestamped plan preceded inspection of the data and that the primary path was selected post hoc. More seriously, the Bonferroni-5 family in Table XIV does not contain the advertised primary estimator: it uses the unrestricted, approximate k=20 VoidFinder contrast rather than the exact footprint-restricted contrast. Bonferroni correction over this selected five-row family therefore supplies neither family-wise coverage for the headline estimator nor correction for the much larger analysis tree. The study must be presented as exploratory and confirmed on independent data before confirmatory constraints are quoted.

[MAJOR] §VIII B/E, Table X — The primary non-void control is not selection-function matched. The stated “DESIVAST footprint” is an author-constructed union of projected hole-sphere discs intersected with a radial span, not the published BGS angular/radial completeness mask or a random-catalog representation of the selection function. It does not ensure matching in redshift, fibre completeness, imaging depth, apparent magnitude, size, morphology, classifier confidence, or sky position. Given the manuscript’s own evidence that chirality labels depend on target program, imaging leg, and confidence, this omission can either generate or conceal a void/non-void difference at the claimed sub-percent scale. The primary analysis requires an official-mask/randoms-based common parent and a covariate-adjusted, matched, or inverse-probability-weighted contrast.

[MAJOR] §V, §VIII B, Tables X–XI — The quoted binomial standard errors assume independent galaxies, although galaxies are nested within the same voids and share spatially correlated imaging, target-selection, and classifier systematics. Global label shuffling also destroys rather than preserves such correlations. The p=0.43 result may remain nonsignificant, but the 0.44-percentage-point counting interval and all subsequent bounds can be materially underestimated. Uncertainty should be obtained with void-level and sky-block bootstrap/jackknife methods, cluster-robust regression, or a permutation scheme preserving the relevant spatial and survey strata.

[MAJOR] Appendix A and §XII B — The conversion of the classifier-label bound to a 2.26-percentage-point physical-chirality bound is unsupported. The factor 2a−1 applies only under symmetric, nondifferential, environment-independent binary misclassification with known sensitivity and specificity. The manuscript’s void-stratified validation has a roughly ±3.7-percentage-point uncertainty in error asymmetry, several times larger than the advertised classifier-label bound, and therefore cannot exclude differential label bias at the relevant scale. The uncertainty in the attenuation factor is also not propagated. The physical bound should be removed unless a calibrated environment-dependent measurement-error model is supplied.

[MAJOR] §VIII C–D and Table XIV — The five “void definitions” do not estimate a common parameter. The V2-REVOLVER and V2-VIDE point-in-effective-sphere classifications are author-created spherical approximations to intrinsically nonspherical watershed regions, whereas GALZONE is the catalog-native membership definition. The sphere and GALZONE rows also use substantially different parent populations and survey support. In addition, REVOLVER and VIDE are pruning variants of the same watershed construction, not independent void finders. These rows cannot be treated as five interchangeable replications or combined into one model-independent upper bound; all estimators should be recomputed on a common valid parent using official memberships.

[MAJOR] §VIII and §XIII — The claimed 0.024-percentage-point bound on the dominant coherent redshift-space-distortion contribution is not established by the described procedure. Displacing galaxies and already identified holes while retaining the published void topology is not equivalent to reconstructing the density field and rerunning the void finder. Likewise, random line-of-sight perturbations with fixed void geometry test membership sensitivity, not the systematic change of the void catalog under RSD. These calculations may be retained as limited stress tests, but they cannot be called a bound on the physical RSD uncertainty or entered as a calibrated term in Table XI.

[MAJOR] §§IV, VI, and IX A — The canonical T-Web classification is demonstrably dominated by the survey selection function. The randoms-weighted rebuild changes the void volume fraction by a factor of about 23 and leaves only 26.6% of matched spirals in the same class. Agreement of the chirality null under two radically different, largely incompatible class assignments is not evidence that either assignment correctly measures cosmic-web environment; a nearly spatially uniform response will return a null under almost any partition. The randoms-weighted, boundary-controlled estimator should be the baseline, or the T-Web material should be removed as physical cross-validation and retained only as a pipeline diagnostic.

[MAJOR] Introduction, §XII B, Conclusions, and Appendix B — The claimed implication for bounce or inflation models is not derived. The manuscript states that no published model predicts the tested signal, and Appendix B explicitly introduces a noncovariant, slicing-dependent toy operator without a transfer function from primordial physics to galaxy handedness. Moreover, the primary DESIVAST analysis has no unique 25Mpc/h smoothing scale, so the primary bound cannot be identified with the secondary T-Web smoothing scale. Statements that “any such model must satisfy” the quoted bound, and the toy coupling estimate, should be removed unless an actual model calculation is provided.

[MAJOR] §XIII and Appendices A/D — The central CW/CCW labels come from a concurrently submitted, unreviewed companion paper whose arXiv identifier is still a placeholder and whose archival DOI is pending. A methodological synopsis cannot substitute for review of the training data, pseudo-label construction, calibration, failure modes, and final immutable catalog version. Any consideration for publication must be conditional on coordinated review of Paper IV, a frozen public archive with hashes and DOI, and re-execution of the present analysis using the final reviewed labels.

[MINOR] §IX B and §X — The external checks are overstated as cosmic-web “cross-validation.” Tempel group multiplicity does not map uniquely to void, wall, filament, and cluster environments, while the ASTRA comparison exhibits severe per-object label disagreement and limited EDR coverage. These analyses can be described as group-richness and alternate-partition null tests, but not as validation of the environmental classifications.

[MINOR] Throughout — The manuscript is excessively repetitive and uses inconsistent labels such as “primary,” “headline,” “pre-declared,” “designated primary,” and “exploratory primary” for different estimators. The speculative EFT appendix, extensive internal artifact bookkeeping, and repeated defenses of secondary analyses obscure the actual result; a substantially shorter manuscript with one fixed estimand, one common parent, and one valid uncertainty calculation is needed.

(3) The central claim is supported only in the narrow sense that the tabulated hard classifier labels show no significant difference in the author’s selected redshift-space samples; the advertised 0.9-percentage-point classifier-label bound, 2.26-percentage-point physical bound, and bounce/inflation interpretation are not supported.
