VERDICT: MINOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Section II.C (Assumption (d) and the paragraph on single-clock nonlinear ζ-conservation): The claim that cubic-order bispectrum transmission through the bounce is “closed to a bounded δf_NL ≲ 10^{-3} systematic via single-clock nonlinear superhorizon ζ-conservation” is asserted on the basis of a scaling estimate (k η_bounce)^2 ∼ 10^{-4} plus an extension of the linear-order Wilson-Ewing result [2]. No explicit cubic-order computation with bounce-modified mode functions is presented, and the applicability of the classical separate-universe/Maldacena conservation theorem across a quantum (LQC) bounce—where the background is modified by holonomy/inverse-triad corrections—requires a stronger, quantitative derivation or citation of a dedicated calculation; the current argument remains an order-of-magnitude closure rather than a demonstrated bound.
[MINOR] Appendix A (and the re-summation claim in Sec. II.A): While the −(99/128) ∑_i k_i^3 discrepancy in Cai et al.’s transcribed final polynomial is identified and the four-way certification (vertex re-summation, ε-ordered intermediates, three-benchmark matching, Li et al. cs=1 formula) is stated, the explicit term-by-term contributions of the four cubic-action vertices (L_redef, L_ζζ̇², L_ζ̇∂ζ∂χ, L_ζ(∂i∂jχ)²) evaluated at ε = 3/2 in the squeezed limit are not displayed. A compact table or equation block showing the individual vertex squeezed limits and their exact sum to −35/16 would allow immediate referee/reader verification.
[MINOR] Section I (Scope paragraph) and Sec. IV (Independent bispectrum Fisher validation): The headline significances are explicitly a recast of Heinrich et al. σ(f_NL) ≈ 0.7 rescaled by the template factor r ≈ 0.84, yet an independent tree-level multi-tracer Fisher (real-space and redshift-space) is also presented that reproduces the local baseline to 2–11 % and yields reff ≈ 0.99. The distinction between recast numbers (used in the abstract, Table V, and headline ranges) and validation numbers should be stated even more explicitly in the abstract and Sec. I so that readers do not inadvertently treat the 2.6–2.75σ band as fully independent.
[MINOR] Section VI (Bayes-factor prior discussion and Table III): The four-corner grid (delta vs. σ_theory = 1.0 bounce priors × narrow [−5, +5] vs. broad [−15, +15] competitor priors) is useful, but the physical motivation for adopting σ_theory = 1.0 as the “recommended baseline” (covering the 0.6–8 % ϵ-correction) versus the delta-function theoretical maximum should be tied more directly to the existing matter-bounce literature on quasi-dust corrections and the range of κ_ϵ; a one-sentence statement of why [−5, +5] is the “curvaton-natural” interval (rather than a purely illustrative choice) would strengthen the interpretation.
[MINOR] Section VII (systematic budget and proxy covariance): The conservative 1.3σ floor is derived from a transferred power-spectrum SDB correlation (ρ = −0.868) because the per-triangle bispectrum covariance Cov_B of Heinrich et al. is not public. While the later channel-native surrogate Fisher (ρ ≈ −0.001, σ_marg ≈ 0.88) is presented as a cross-check, the text should state once, in a single paragraph, which covariance is used for every quoted endpoint in Table V and the abstract so that the 1.3σ versus ∼2.5σ distinction is unambiguous.

One sentence: The central claim—that the corrected matter-bounce value f_NL = −35/16 sets a realistic SPHEREx/MegaMapper target (∼1.3–2.75σ after template mismatch and systematics) that preserves qualitative discriminatory power against single-field slow-roll inflation—is supported by the explicit overlap calculation, the independent in-house Fisher validation, the itemized systematic budget, and the closed-form Bayes-factor cross-checks, all conditional on the six stated assumptions.
