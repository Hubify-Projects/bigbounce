VERDICT: MAJOR REVISIONS

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract & §3 (three-tier structure) + §3.1: The primary deliverable is framed as a “validated catalog-grade” set of 268,519 unique anomalies (268,319 point-source), yet the authors explicitly state that ~98.7% of the DESI component (the dominant contributor) lie on sky-fiber/filler spectra rather than primary science targets; the like-for-like science-target yield is only 2,468 clusters (≈0.92× the Liang et al. 2023 benchmark). This makes the headline number a process-volume statistic, not a science-ready catalog increase, and the title/abstract overstate its immediate utility.

[MAJOR] §3.5 (eROSITA) + §2.2 (tabular preprocessing) + §6.4(ii): The eROSITA production score axis (threshold 0.259) is irreproducible from any committed artifact (fails 16 monotone rescalings + 3 IsolationForest retrains; non-monotone in raw scores); the tier is excised from every count and released only as a membership list. This constitutes a critical provenance failure in the production pipeline for one of the six surveys and undermines confidence in the overall “Path-C” reproducibility claim.

[MAJOR] §2.4 (Path-C rebuild) + §3.4 (LAMOST) + §3.7 (Gaia): Two of the six surveys are either excluded entirely (synthetic Gaia placeholder) or relegated to an “exploratory/failure-mode” tier (LAMOST: 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact, 5.8% injection-recovery at 5σ). The “validated” 268,519 subset therefore rests on only four surveys, three of which pass detector-sensitivity injection-recovery and one (NEOWISE) only a geometry-QA gate by construction. The pipeline required extensive post-hoc excision and reclassification, which should be presented as a more prominent methodological limitation.

[MAJOR] §2.2, §6.4(i) (DESI validation) + §3.6 (Planck): DESI robustness rests on a single production-ensemble broad/extended-class injection-recovery test (99–100% at 5σ on real SPARCL spectra); narrow single-pixel lines recover only at ≥15σ (explicit sensitivity floor). The five k-fold stability checks (Jaccard, tail preservation) were performed on deliberately short-trained proxy models that fail the paper’s own val-loss ≤0.30 gate and are therefore correlated, not independent, confirmations. Planck top-200 includes training patches (152/200 in train split vs. ~170 expected); the held-out over-representation test is suggestive but not a full held-out selection.

[MINOR] §3.3 (SDSS) + Table 2 footnotes: The published SDSS headline (77,905) is a fixed-size continuity slice at S ≥ 0.1060 chosen to match the cross-transfer count, not the native top-1% (19,253) or strict S > 5 (only 12 sources). Readers comparing to prior single-survey catalogs will be misled unless the three-threshold disclosure is moved into the main text and abstract.

[MINOR] §5 (cosmological applications): The multi-tracer fNL and NANOGrav sections are presented as “secondary demonstrations” that return null/marginal results (no improvement on fNL; γ consistent with matter-bounce but decisive only against an idealized circular-orbit SMBHB reference). These are appropriately caveated but occupy disproportionate space relative to their scientific yield and should be shortened or moved to an appendix to keep focus on the catalog.

[MINOR] Data-release & reproducibility (§2.4, §6.4(i)): Full per-object re-inference of the released 22.5 M DESI catalog is blocked because raw native score parquets reside on an exited pod and only ~1.3% of rows have recoverable TARGETIDs in SPARCL. While the scoring pipeline itself is committed and reproducible on fresh pulls, the exact released catalog rows cannot be re-scored identically; this should be stated as a quantified limitation in the Data Availability section.

One sentence: The central claim—that a reproducible, injection-recovery-validated multi-survey anomaly-candidate catalog of 268,519 reconstruction outliers has been produced from a 37.3-million-source scan—is supported for the broad/continuum-dominated class on the four retained validated surveys (DESI, SDSS, Planck, NEOWISE), provided readers accept the heavy process-volume framing, the exclusion of two surveys for reproducibility failures, and the fact that the large majority of entries are not on primary science targets.
