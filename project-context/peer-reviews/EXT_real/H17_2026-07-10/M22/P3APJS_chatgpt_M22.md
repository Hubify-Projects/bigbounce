VERDICT: REJECT

ISSUES:

[MAJOR] Abstract; §2.4; opening of §3 — The statement that “validation establishes that the 268,519 subset is real” is not supported. Injection recovery measures sensitivity to a selected synthetic perturbation; it does not measure catalog purity, false-discovery rate, astrophysical reality, or the correctness of individual memberships. NEOWISE’s only gate is explicitly a masking operation that passes by construction, yet its objects are included in the “validated catalog-grade” headline.

[MAJOR] §2.2; Table 2 and footnotes; §3.3 — The 268,519 count has no coherent selection function. DESI uses an absolute standardized-score cut, SDSS uses a fixed-size slice chosen solely to reproduce the earlier cross-transfer count, Planck uses a fixed top-200, and NEOWISE uses a fixed top-1%. Most seriously, SDSS contributes 77,905 detections even though its native top-1% set contains 19,253 and its native S>5 set contains only 12; selecting 77,905 to preserve “continuity” makes a large fraction of the headline count arbitrary rather than data-determined.

[MAJOR] Title, abstract, and §3.1 — Most DESI entries have not been demonstrated to be astronomical sources. The manuscript states that approximately 98.7% of DESI anomaly clusters lack a primary science-target bit and that 86% have DESI_TARGET = 0, encompassing sky fibers, fillers, calibration observations, and secondary/ToO programs. Such spectra cannot automatically be counted as “point-source” anomalies or candidate astrophysical objects; blank-sky and instrumental-stream outliers require a separate quality-control product.

[MAJOR] §3.1 and Table 3 — The DESI target bookkeeping is internally inconsistent. The reported per-class rates imply roughly 37,000 S>5 anomalies among the approximately 6.5 million validated-TARGETTYPE spectra, yet the supposedly broader primary-science-bit positional recount finds only 2,468 matches. A broader parent selection should not yield an order-of-magnitude smaller intersection if the labels and joins are consistent. The later claim that 98.8% of matched DESI anomalies are Redrock “GALAXY” objects and therefore not sky/fiber artifacts also conflicts with the target-bit accounting; Redrock template classifications of low-information or sky spectra are not evidence that a real source exists.

[MAJOR] §3.1 and §6.5 — The advertised “like-for-like” comparison with Liang et al. is not like-for-like. The manuscript compares 2,468 anomalies from a 20.3-million-row DESI science-bit denominator with 2,685 anomalies from an approximately 250,000-spectrum EDR sample. The corresponding rates are approximately 0.012% and 1.07%, differing by nearly two orders of magnitude; comparing only the absolute counts as “0.92×” obscures the denominator and selection differences rather than benchmarking the methods.

[MAJOR] §2.2, §6.4(i), and Data Availability — The central DESI catalog is not independently reproducible at the object level. The manuscript reports that 86.6% of released identifiers are internal hashes, only about 1.3% of released rows can be re-pulled by identifier, and the native score parquets and input linkage were lost with the compute pod. Reproducing a score distribution and an injection curve on newly retrieved spectra is not reproduction of the released 195,829 memberships. The headline-deduplication script verifies arithmetic on already selected tables, not the source-to-score-to-selection pipeline expected for an ApJS catalog.

[MAJOR] §2.2 and §6.4(i) — The DESI cross-validation is mischaracterized as fully out-of-sample. Each fold model scores the full 47,000-spectrum pool, so most scores in every fold vector are from spectra used to train that model; pairwise top-set Jaccard overlap among those vectors is therefore not an out-of-fold catalog test. Moreover, all five proxy models fail the paper’s own validation-loss retain gate, with a reported mean best validation loss of 1.91 versus the 0.30 criterion. A production-quality, genuinely out-of-fold score vector or a spatially and temporally independent test sample is required.

[MAJOR] §6.4(i) — The DESI injection-recovery experiment is not an end-to-end validation of the released selection. It injects a restricted broad-signal family into the cleanest 5% of re-pulled spectra and evaluates recovery against a tail-excluded 99th-percentile threshold that is not shown to be equivalent to the production S>5 selection. It does not test the dominant full-stream failure modes—sky fibers, arm calibration, continuum normalization, extraction failures, or low-S/N filler spectra—and supplies no negative-control estimate of false positives. The chosen plant family is also not shown to span the morphologies of the actual 195,829 candidates.

[MAJOR] §3.6 and Table 7 — The Planck tier is insufficiently validated. The top-200 patches are selected from a bank containing the training and early-stopping validation patches; finding 48 of 200 in the validation split does not establish independence or rule out selection effects. The nominal binomial calculation assumes independent patches even though 10° patches can share substantial sky area. In addition, the 5σ Gaussian bumps are added after per-patch standardization without re-standardization, so the test is not tied to the Planck noise covariance, foreground residuals, beam, or the actual preprocessing distribution.

[MAJOR] §2.4, §3.6, and §4.3 — Ten-degree CMB map patches cannot be treated as point detections and deduplicated by comparing their centers at 5 arcsec. Distinct centers may correspond to heavily overlapping regions or the same sky structure, while a zero 5-arcsec match to an optical source says nothing about patch overlap. The 200 Planck entries should be a separate region catalog with footprint geometry and overlap-aware clustering, not added to a “unique object” or “point-source plus patches” headline.

[MAJOR] §4.3 — The point-source deduplication is not adequate for a catalog-grade unique-object count. A uniform 5-arcsec friends-of-friends radius is applied across surveys with very different astrometric uncertainties and source morphologies; a PSF width is not an astrometric-error model. The radius sweep demonstrates numerical stability of the count, not correctness of the associations. Per-survey covariance, crowded-field behavior, proper motion, and a likelihood-ratio or Bayesian association model are needed, together with pairwise overlap and false-association tables.

[MAJOR] §3.8 and §2.2 — NEOWISE should not be in the validated tier. Its 100% “injection recovery” is a direct consequence of planting objects outside a mask and then applying that same mask, so it contains no information about detector sensitivity or anomaly-ranking stability. Its feature scaler is also fitted on the full sample, including the tail, and the manuscript states that the corresponding train-only-scaler robustness test remains unperformed.

[MAJOR] §4.1 — The 17.8% “genuine novelty fraction” is not established. Absence from 18 selected catalogs is only a catalog-nonmatch statistic, especially when the DESI sample is dominated by non-primary fibers and may include blank-sky positions. The analysis lacks a control sample, catalog-by-catalog completeness and footprint accounting, proper-motion treatment, local-density false-match estimates, probabilistic counterpart matching, and source-level confirmation of the 178 unmatched cases. These entries should be described as archival-catalog-unmatched candidates, not genuinely novel astrophysical sources.

[MAJOR] §3.4, Table 2, and §7 — The inclusive 377,482 catalog includes approximately 113,000 LAMOST entries from a detector that fails injection recovery and whose cross-transfer population is 98% a single blue-excess systematic. Calling this inclusive total a primary result or using it in “largest catalog” comparisons mixes a documented method failure with science candidates. The LAMOST set belongs in a separate failure-mode or engineering appendix and should contribute to no catalog-size claim.

[MAJOR] §3.5, §3.7, Acknowledgments, and Data Availability — The data-release narrative contains serious contradictions and provenance concerns. The Data Availability section says LAMOST is excluded from the released per-object tables and “every headline count,” although the 377,482 headline explicitly includes it; it also mentions a Gaia block carrying scores despite repeated statements that Gaia was removed from the catalog. Statements about Planck checkpoint availability, pod-resident products, and complete reproducibility are likewise inconsistent. The discovery of a synthetic Gaia fallback and an unrecoverable eROSITA production score axis shows that production provenance controls failed; all retained tiers require an independent end-to-end audit from archive query through final catalog row.

[MAJOR] §5.1 and Appendix E — The NANOGrav analysis is not an application of the anomaly catalog and should not be part of this ApJS catalog submission. It uses a factorized KDE free-spectrum product rather than the timing-data likelihood, and its large density ratio compares two fixed spectral indices, not a matter-bounce model against the environmentally diverse SMBHB population. The environmental caveat correctly concedes that the result has essentially no power to distinguish those physical origins; this material belongs in a separate, fully developed cosmology paper.

[MAJOR] §3.3 — The SDSS score–redshift result is substantially overinterpreted. A Spearman coefficient of 0.036 is negligible in effect size, and its small p-value is driven by sample size. Redshift correlates with observed wavelength coverage, continuum S/N, target selection, template-fit quality, and line placement, any of which can induce score trends; the assertion that instrumental artifacts “cannot” produce the observed association is false without matched controls or multivariable modeling.

[MAJOR] Data-product presentation throughout — The manuscript does not provide the catalog documentation expected for ApJS: a complete column-by-column schema, units, null conventions, source-identifier provenance, coordinate epochs, per-survey selection bits, score-axis definitions, duplicate handling, validity flags, and representative rows from the main catalog. Numerous repository paths and JSON filenames are not a substitute for a stable data-model table and an independently executable end-to-end validation recipe.

[MINOR] Title, abstract, Tables 1–2, and §6.2 — The “37.3 million” scale figure conflates unique native inputs with historical cross-transfer passes, superseded Planck banks, and pre-excision processing. The manuscript itself gives 36.76, 36.93, and 37.29 million under different accounting conventions. The title and abstract should use one clearly defined unique-input denominator, and obsolete cross-transfer figures should be moved out of the principal results rather than repeatedly interleaved with the native catalog.

The central claim is not supported: the manuscript shows that large ranked reconstruction-outlier lists were generated, but it does not establish that 268,519 entries constitute a reproducible, astrophysically real, and catalog-grade validated sample.
