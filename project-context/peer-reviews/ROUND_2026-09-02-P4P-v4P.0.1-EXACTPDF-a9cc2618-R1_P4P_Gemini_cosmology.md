## Summary recommendation
REJECT

This manuscript fails the standalone-reader test catastrophically and cannot be accepted in its current form. Submitted as a catalog paper to ApJS, it explicitly outsources all catalog construction, neural network architecture, validation, and systematics to unpublished `.tex` files residing in a repository. A journal article must be self-contained; it cannot serve merely as an extended abstract or a pointer to a GitHub repository. Furthermore, the central claim of sample-size superiority rests on a severe mathematical inconsistency: the authors mix the total catalog size with the much smaller tested sample size to artificially ensure their sample appears strictly larger than all literature comparisons, when in fact their primary test sample is smaller than at least one cited study. 

***

## Detailed Findings

**P4P-E1: Failure of Standalone-Reader Test / Missing Catalog Description**
*   **Location:** Section 2 (Lines 87-90), Section 3 (Lines 139-142), Section 4 (Lines 172-175), Data Availability (Lines 344-351), and References [15, 16].
*   **Problem:** The manuscript claims to be a catalog release paper but refuses to describe the catalog. It states: *"Full architecture, training-data provenance, bias-hardening audit... and per-region calibration are described in the archived release paper (15) and are not reproduced here."* Reference [15] is not a published paper, but an internal repository path: `pipelines/p2_chirality/chirality_catalog_paper.tex`. The same outsourcing occurs for the systematics (Lines 139-142) and the void/non-void diagnostics (Lines 172-175, pointing to Ref [16], another `.tex` file). 
*   **Required fix:** ESSENTIAL. The manuscript is vastly under-scoped for a catalog paper. The contents of the "archived release papers" must be folded into this manuscript. A reader must be able to evaluate the data, validation, and systematics without accessing unpublished repository files.

**P4P-E2: Mathematical Contradiction in Sample Size Claims**
*   **Location:** Abstract (Lines 21-22), Section 5.2 (Lines 228-229), and Table 1.
*   **Problem:** The abstract claims the null confirms isotropy *"on a sample 6–3,400× larger than the comparison catalogs"*. Line 228 states the literature samples are *"6 to 3,400 times smaller than the present catalog's supported-pixel sample."* This is mathematically false. The supported-pixel sample is $N = 887,472$. Table 1 lists Shamir 2022 with $N = 1,300,000$. $887,472$ is *smaller* than $1,300,000$ (ratio 0.68), not larger. The authors have cherry-picked their numerators: they used the *total* catalog size ($8.47 \times 10^6$) to derive the lower bound ($8.47\text{M} / 1.3\text{M} \approx 6.5$), but switched to the *supported-pixel sample* ($887,472$) to derive the upper bound against Shamir 2025 ($887,472 / 263 \approx 3,374$). 
*   **Required fix:** ESSENTIAL. You cannot mix the total un-tested catalog size with the tested sample size to claim across-the-board superiority. Recalculate all ratios consistently using the sample actually subjected to the dipole test ($N=887,472$). Acknowledge that the primary test sample is smaller than Shamir 2022.

**P4P-M1: Figure 1 Caption and Axis Mismatch**
*   **Location:** Figure 1 (Page 2).
*   **Problem:** The caption describes the figure as a *"CW-fraction map"*. However, the colorbar label is explicitly $(N_{\text{CW}} - N_{\text{CCW}})/(N_{\text{CW}} + N_{\text{CCW}})$. This formula computes the *chirality asymmetry* (ranging from -1 to 1, shown here as -0.08 to 0.08), not the CW-fraction (which would be $N_{\text{CW}} / (N_{\text{CW}} + N_{\text{CCW}})$, centered at 0.5). 
*   **Required fix:** MAJOR. Correct the caption to accurately reflect the plotted mathematical quantity (chirality asymmetry), or change the plotted data to match the caption.

**P4P-M2: Unsupported / False Quantitative Claim**
*   **Location:** Section 6 (Lines 290-292).
*   **Problem:** The text claims: *"At 8,474,531 total catalog objects and 887,472 in the primary supported-pixel test, this is the largest single sample brought to the question"*. As demonstrated in P4P-E2, the sample actually "brought to the question" (the primary test sample of 887,472) is smaller than the 1,300,000 sample in Shamir 2022. 
*   **Required fix:** MAJOR. Remove the claim of being the "largest single sample" for the dipole test, or explicitly clarify that while the parent catalog is the largest, the tested real-space sample is not.

**P4P-N1: Confidence Interval Precision**
*   **Location:** Section 4 (Line 159).
*   **Problem:** The reported 95% CI is $[-0.00504, +0.00795]$ for an estimate of $+0.00145$ with $\text{SE} = 0.00332$. A standard normal 95% CI computation yields $0.00145 \pm 1.96(0.00332) = [-0.00506, +0.00796]$. The reported bounds are slightly off.
*   **Required fix:** NIT. Verify the exact multiplier used (e.g., if a t-distribution was used, state the degrees of freedom) or correct the rounding to match the stated SE.
