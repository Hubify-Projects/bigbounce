## Referee Report

**General Comments:**
The manuscript presents a rigorous, channel-level assessment of whether the minimal Einstein–Cartan–Holst (ECH) torsion mechanism—which yields a repulsive spin-spin contact interaction capable of replacing the classical singularity with a bounce—can simultaneously source the observed late-time dark energy density. The author systematically closes four distinct dark-energy channels using a combination of amplitude bounds, a perturbation-transparency theorem, and a catalog of fourteen mechanism-class constraints. 

The algebraic and symbolic derivations explicitly presented in the text (including the Fierz rearrangement coefficients, the Nieh-Yan identity expansion, and the on-shell torsion contractions) are exceptionally clean and mathematically correct. The dimensional scaling arguments are well-calibrated. However, the manuscript currently violates the standalone-reader requirement by outsourcing the bulk of its barrier-catalog derivations to an unpublished GitHub repository draft. Furthermore, there is a technical drift between the abstract's summary of the operator list and the body's precise classification. These issues must be addressed before the paper can be considered for publication.

Below is an itemized list of findings.

### ESSENTIAL (Paper cannot be accepted without these fixes)

**1. P1N-E1: Abstract-Body Drift on Operator Classification (Pattern-045)**
*   **Location:** Abstract (Page 1) vs. Section VI (Page 8)
*   **Problem:** The abstract claims the six-member generating list is *"either an exact total derivative on the torsion-free branch, a Fierz-closed $M_{\text{Pl}}^{-2}$-suppressed contact term on-shell, or identically vanishing"*. This conflates two distinct mathematical conditions. Section VI correctly states that O2 and O3 are exact total derivatives *everywhere* (on any connection), while O1 and O6 are identically vanishing *on the torsion-free branch only*. The abstract's phrasing ("exact total derivative on the torsion-free branch") is mathematically inaccurate and misrepresents the body's calibrated statement.
*   **Required Fix:** Correct the abstract to match the body's exact classification (e.g., "is either an exact total derivative, identically vanishing on the torsion-free branch, or a Fierz-closed...").

**2. P1N-E2: Violation of Standalone-Reader Test / Outsourced Derivations**
*   **Location:** Section IV (Page 4) and Reference [23] (Page 11)
*   **Problem:** The manuscript introduces fourteen mechanism-class constraints (the "Barrier Catalog") which form the core of the paper's negative result. However, the text states: *"a companion systematic no-go survey carrying the barrier-catalog and route derivations in full detail is paper1c_nogo_survey/main.tex, pinned to the same commit [23]"*. Reference [23] is a link to an un-peer-reviewed, unpublished `.tex` draft in a GitHub repository. A published theoretical physics paper cannot rely on an external, unarchived repository draft for its primary load-bearing derivations. 
*   **Required Fix:** The paper must be self-contained. The derivations for the fourteen barriers currently residing in the GitHub draft must be incorporated into this manuscript, either by expanding Section IV or by adding a comprehensive Appendix. The page count (currently 11 pages) can easily accommodate this addition without exceeding CQG's length limits.

## Summary recommendation
**MAJOR REVISIONS**

**Justification:**
The manuscript is theoretically rigorous, and the explicit computations (such as the on-shell torsion irrep evaluation and the Fierz-closed contact term derivations) are flawlessly executed and dimensionally sound. The author's careful separation of statistical significances from different null procedures (Section V.C) and the meticulous tracking of normalization factors are highly commendable. However, the paper currently fails the standalone-reader test by outsourcing the detailed derivations of its 14-barrier catalog to an unpublished GitHub `.tex` file. Because these derivations are the structural foundation of the paper's "no-go" conclusion, they must be integrated into the manuscript (e.g., as an appendix). Additionally, the abstract contains a technical misstatement regarding the operator classification that must be aligned with the body text. Once the external derivations are incorporated and the abstract is corrected, the manuscript will easily meet the high standards of *Classical and Quantum Gravity*.

### ESSENTIAL (Paper cannot be accepted without these fixes)

**1. P2N-E1: Catastrophic Unit Conversion Error in Density Benchmark (Pattern-041)**
*   **Location:** Section II.A (Page 2, Eq. 4), Section V.B (Page 6), Section V.D (Page 7), and Abstract.
*   **Problem:** The manuscript calculates the magnitude of the contact term at a benchmark fermion density of $n_\psi = 100 \text{ cm}^{-3}$, claiming $\kappa n_\psi^2 = 9.954 \times 10^{-80} \text{ eV}^4$. This value is incorrect by **~57 orders of magnitude** due to an inverted $\hbar c$ conversion. 
    *   To convert a number density from $\text{cm}^{-3}$ to natural units ($\text{eV}^3$), one must **divide** by $(\hbar c)^3$. Using $\hbar c \approx 1.973 \times 10^{-5} \text{ eV cm}$:
        $n_\psi = \frac{100 \text{ cm}^{-3}}{(1.973 \times 10^{-5} \text{ eV cm})^3} \approx 1.3 \times 10^{16} \text{ eV}^3$
    *   Squaring this gives $n_\psi^2 \approx 1.7 \times 10^{32} \text{ eV}^6$. Multiplying by $\kappa = 8\pi / M_{\text{Pl}}^2 \approx 1.68 \times 10^{-55} \text{ eV}^{-2}$ yields the correct value:
        **$\kappa n_\psi^2 \approx 2.88 \times 10^{-23} \text{ eV}^4$**
    *   The author's value of $9.954 \times 10^{-80} \text{ eV}^4$ is exactly what you get if you mistakenly **multiply** by $(\hbar c)^3$ instead of dividing: $100 \times (1.973 \times 10^{-5})^3 \approx 7.68 \times 10^{-13}$, squared is $\approx 5.9 \times 10^{-25}$, times $\kappa$ gives $\approx 9.95 \times 10^{-80}$.
*   **Impact:** This arithmetic error propagates throughout the paper's quantitative claims:
    1.  In **Eq. (4)**, the ratio $\kappa n_\psi^2 / \rho_\Lambda$ is claimed to be $3.88 \times 10^{-69}$ ("$\approx 68$ orders of magnitude below"). The correct ratio is $\approx 1.1 \times 10^{-12}$ (only 12 orders of magnitude below).
    2.  In **Section V.B (Eq. 11)**, this flawed ratio is used to calculate the Route 3 suppression as $\sim 5.5 \times 10^{-75}$ (74 orders of magnitude). With the correct density, the suppression is $\sim 1.5 \times 10^{-18}$ (18 orders of magnitude).
    3.  In **Section V.D**, the direct rebuttal of Poplawski's proposal relies heavily on the "sixty-eight orders of magnitude too small" figure. While the qualitative conclusion (it is too small) survives, the quantitative argument is completely wrong.
*   **Required Fix:** Recompute all values dependent on $n_\psi = 100 \text{ cm}^{-3}$ using the correct dimensional conversion. Update the text in Sections II.A, V.B, V.D, and the Abstract to reflect the correct $\sim 12$-order-of-magnitude gap rather than the erroneous 68-order gap.

*(Note: The Fierz rearrangement coefficients in Eq. 5, the LQC critical density bounds in B12, and the one-loop birefringence bounds in Eq. 9 were all independently recomputed during this pass and found to be perfectly accurate. The error is strictly isolated to the $\hbar c$ conversion of $n_\psi$.)*
