Paper: Spectrally Unusual Sources at Scale: A Multi-Survey Catalog of 269,317 Recommended-Tier (378,280 Total) Path-C Unique Anomalies and a Native-Trained Novelty Fraction from 37.3 Million Sources and Map Patches (Houston Golden, June 2026)
Recommendation: MINOR REVISIONS
The core validated catalog-grade deliverable (≥268,519 unique anomalies from the four components that pass the relevant gates: DESI DR1, SDSS DR18 native, Planck native, NEOWISE geometry-gated) rests on a transparent, large-scale implementation of per-survey native autoencoder retraining with a multi-stage Path-C validation protocol. The work is ambitious in scope (37.3 M sources across seven archives), unusually detailed in its disclosure of failure modes, exploratory tiers, and residual caveats, and appropriately cautious in its cosmological applications. No fundamental blockers prevent eventual acceptance at a top journal, but one significant usability/reproducibility issue in the recommended-tier data product and a small number of presentational/interpretive points require attention before the high bar is met.
BLOCKERS
None. The validated subset is clearly separated from exploratory components; injection-recovery, Jaccard stability, and visual-inspection gates are applied and reported per survey; cross-survey deduplication and novelty quantification are performed with explicit false-match expectations and controls. No evidence of uncontrolled systematics that invalidate the headline numbers for the validated tiers, no internal numerical inconsistencies that survive the paper’s own reconciliation checks, and no over-claiming of detections (the f_NL and NANOGrav applications are presented as forecasts/illustrative consistency tests with explicit “noise-driven” and “environmental caveat” language).
MAJORS
eROSITA DR1 tier reproducibility (recommended-tier component, exploratory addendum)
The production-run score axis and 0.259 threshold cannot be recovered from any of the 16 monotone rescalings of the committed raw reconstruction scores, nor from retrained IsolationForest axes on the BigAE latent space. The n=298 membership list itself is the canonical, reproducible selection (top-298 on the committed raw-score artifact), and the paper discloses this limitation extensively (§III E, pipelines/p3_anomaly_engine/r24conf_erosita_axis_sweep.json). However, for a catalog paper whose recommended tier includes this component, the absence of a reproducible per-object score (or an alternative, committed, rank-order-preserving selection function) materially limits downstream usability, meta-analysis, or extension. The 95.3% overlap with the IF top-9,303 is noted as descriptive/internal-consistency only (shared latent).
Required action: In revision, either (a) commit and document an alternative reproducible ranking/selection criterion for the 298 objects (e.g., a stable IsolationForest or re-derived monotonic proxy on the raw scores) that can be exactly reproduced from the released artifacts, or (b) move the eROSITA contribution fully into a supplementary/exploratory appendix with strengthened language that the membership list is provided for completeness but carries no reproducible score axis for re-ranking or stacking. The current disclosure is honest but insufficient for a top-journal data product.
DESI B-dominant population (∼22.7% of the 195,829 anomalies)
The paper correctly flags this as a potential calibration-artifact hypothesis (§VI C limitation 3) and notes that visual inspection of the top-200 found no sky/telluric artifacts. However, no photometric color-cut test (u−g, SDSS-style, or Legacy Survey color selection) or cross-survey consistency check is presented to distinguish genuine astrophysical blue-arm anomalies from residual instrumental systematics. Given that this is one of the two dominant morphological classes in the largest single-survey tier, the hypothesis remains untested at the population level.
Required action: Add a short robustness test or explicit statement that this remains an open limitation (or perform a simple color-cut audit on a statistically meaningful subsample if feasible within the existing artifact set).
MINORS
Tier nomenclature and headline framing (minor presentational). The paper is careful to lead with the validated subset and to explain the science-target vs. full-stream distinction for DESI (2,468 vs. 2,685 like-for-like; 98.7% of DESI anomalies on non-primary TARGETTYPE spectra). However, the abstract-style opening paragraph and Table I still require the reader to parse multiple footnotes and the Path-C vs. cross-transfer distinction to understand what fraction of the 269,317 recommended tier is catalog-grade vs. exploratory. A single consolidated “validated / recommended / exploratory” summary box or flowchart early in the paper (or in the data-release manifest) would reduce cognitive load without changing any numbers.
Novelty fraction scope. The 17.8% (178/1,000; Wilson ±1.2%) genuine novelty candidate fraction is correctly presented as a single-stratum point estimate on the DESI top-1,000 score slice against 18 catalogs; the paper explicitly states that full-catalog extrapolation is empirically untested. This is already handled well, but a one-sentence reminder in the conclusions that the 58.8% SIMBAD-unmatched figure is a database-coverage diagnostic, not a discovery rate, would prevent any residual mis-citation.
Unweighted MSE limitation. Listed in §VI C (7), but its practical impact on the validated tiers is only bounded indirectly via the injection-recovery gates. A short note that a noise-weighted re-score of the top few hundred DESI objects (or a stratified validation slice) is planned or queued would close this cleanly.
Dense footnotes and Table I. The three-threshold disclosure for SDSS (continuity slice vs. top-1% vs. S>5) and the LAMOST native vs. cross-transfer reconciliation are essential but require careful reading. Minor copy-editing for flow would help.
Data-availability statement. The HuggingFace staging plan, manifest with SHA-256 hashes, and GitHub weights location are excellent. The placeholder “Zenodo DOI will be minted at submission” is standard and acceptable; simply ensure the final version cites the actual DOI or states that it is reserved.
Strengths (≥3, in rough order of impact)
Unprecedented scale with native per-survey retraining and explicit tiering. Processing 37.3 M sources across seven archives in a single framework, with native BigAE retrains for each survey, a documented Path-C six-step rebuild protocol, and clear separation of validated (DESI+SDSS+Planck+NEOWISE) vs. exploratory (Gaia, eROSITA, LAMOST) components is a substantial technical achievement. The 21.5× LAMOST rate compression after native retrain and the ∼6,500× SDSS rate compression diagnostic are powerful internal consistency checks.
Rigorous, multi-gate validation and transparent limitation accounting. 5-fold Jaccard (DESI), OOD holdout Jaccard, injection-recovery at multiple amplitudes/morphologies, systematics masks, 7-way 5″ deduplication with FoF chain audit, and an explicit 10-item Table V of residual caveats with resolution status set a high standard for reproducibility and honesty. The LAMOST 98% blue-excess training-bias artifact is turned into a constructive methodological lesson rather than hidden.
