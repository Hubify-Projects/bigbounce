## Most concerning observational issue

**Finding 1 — The 378,280 headline still hides a 30%-of-volume LAMOST exploratory tier.** This is the single biggest survey-instrumentation discipline issue in the paper. The two-tier (378,080 point-source + 200 Planck CMB-patch) decomposition is *better* than the previous single-number 378,280 headline (Wave 14-BB was a real improvement). But it still bundles 113,342 LAMOST native-retrain objects whose 5σ continuum-dip recovery is 5.8% — i.e., below the formal Path-C gate by an order of magnitude — into the same tier as the 195,829 DESI catalog-grade objects whose 5-fold Jaccard stability is 0.862 (gate-PASS) and whose 100K-spectrum held-out Jaccard is 0.732.

The honest three-tier framing:

- **Catalog-grade (gate-PASS + held-out validation):** ~264,938 objects — DESI 195,829 + SDSS native 12 (or top-1% slice at 19,253 / 77,905) + Planck native 200 + NEOWISE-masked 419 + eROSITA 298 + Gaia 500.
- **Exploratory (gate-FAIL with informative cross-validation diagnostic):** ~113,342 objects — LAMOST native top-1% slice, retained as methodological lesson.
- **CMB-patch sky regions (not point-source detections):** 200 — Planck native top-200.

The 264,938 catalog-grade figure is the right "what you should cite as a discovery deliverable" number; 378,280 is the right "what was scanned and ranked in the full pipeline" number. The current abstract conflates these by reporting 378,280 as the headline with the LAMOST tier hidden behind a footnote.

This is BLOCKER because: (a) it's externally visible to any LAMOST- or DESI-team reader on first pass; (b) it's in the abstract and title; (c) the fix is a four-paragraph rewrite of the abstract and a Table 1 row rearrangement, not a recompute. The science content is correct — the *headline numerical framing* is what's misleading.

**Finding 2 — eROSITA 298 anomalies is an unanchored top-cut from 930,203 DR1 sources, with three downstream problems.** The published 298-source catalog is internally honest about its IF cross-validation stability (81.5% — highest of any Path-C survey), but the paper does not say: (a) eROSITA DR1 is *western Galactic hemisphere only* — the LMC concentration is a footprint artifact; (b) the 5σ subspace-injection recovery rate is 1.2% (below the formal gate) — this is the *detection sensitivity*, not the *training-sample robustness*, and the paper headlines the latter while burying the former; (c) eROSITA DR2 (Nov 2025) is now released with the eastern Galactic hemisphere added, but the paper never says whether the catalog should be considered superseded.

**Finding 3 — Three of seven retained surveys are operationally below 5σ injection-recovery gate.** LAMOST 5.8%, Gaia 5.2%, NEOWISE 1.5% post-mask rejection without recovery test on rejected. Only DESI, SDSS native (at continuum-dip), and Planck native carry both gate-PASS and held-out validation. The paper does not state this clearly in the abstract; it bundles the three operationally-unvalidated surveys into the 378,280 headline. A SPHEREx- or DESI-team reviewer would block on this until the abstract distinguishes catalog-grade from exploratory.

The three findings are linked: the 378,280 headline is the artifact of treating LAMOST exploratory, eROSITA top-cut, and NEOWISE post-mask on the same footing as DESI catalog-grade. Fix: separate the three tiers explicitly in the abstract and Table 1, with the catalog-grade tier (~264,938) leading.
