How We Rate Novelty

Every result in this research program is rated on a five-level novelty scale. The scale is designed to be conservative: we would rather underrate our own contributions than overclaim.

Level Label Definition
N4 Breakthrough New physical law, new symmetry, or first observation of a predicted effect. Requires experimental confirmation.
N3 Strongly Novel New theorem, new method, or first comprehensive mapping of an observable to a theory. Goes substantially beyond all prior work on the same question.
N2 Moderately Novel New quantitative result within a known framework, new combination of existing tools, or first systematic exploration of a known problem.
N1 Verification Independent re-derivation or numerical verification of a known result. Confirms prior work but does not extend it.
N0 Prior Art Established result from the literature that we use as a foundation. No novelty claimed.

We rate our own work conservatively. No result is claimed at N4 (breakthrough). The strongest results are N3 (new theorem or method). This is an honest accounting: the page that proves we know what is ours and what is not.

1. Perturbation-Transparency Theorem

Paper 1, §12 · N3 — New Theorem

What it is

A formal all-orders proof that the Barbero-Immirzi parameter \(\gamma\), which controls the bounce dynamics in Einstein-Cartan-Holst (ECH) cosmology, is completely invisible in all perturbative observables — scalar, vector, and tensor. The bounce mechanism leaves no fingerprint in the CMB or galaxy surveys. This is a 5-step proof chain: zero spin density for scalar matter → zero torsion → Levi-Civita connection at all perturbative orders → Holst term reduces to topological Nieh-Yan invariant → no perturbative dynamics from \(\gamma\).

What existed before

The general understanding that ECH with scalar matter has zero torsion is implicit in Hehl et al. (1976) and the Einstein-Cartan literature broadly. Freidel, Minic & Takeuchi (2005) established the parity-violating coupling structure of the Holst term. The connection between zero scalar spin density and vanishing torsion is a standard result in EC theory. Calcagni & Mercuri (2009) and Mercuri (2009) discussed the topological nature of the Holst term in various matter-coupling scenarios. de Berredo-Peixoto et al. (2012) noted that in GR the Holst term does not affect dynamics because the dual Riemann contraction vanishes when torsion is absent. Långvik et al. stated that for minimally coupled matter the connection reduces to Levi-Civita, noting that nonminimal scalar couplings could restore Holst dynamics.

What's new

While the individual ingredients are known (as cited above), no prior work assembled them into an explicit all-orders perturbation theorem for minimally coupled scalar matter in ECH. We formalized this as an explicit theorem with five numbered steps, proved it extends to tensor perturbations (not just scalar), and verified numerically that the topological identity holds to machine precision. The result has structural consequences: it means every surviving testable prediction from the bounce must be mechanism-independent (depending only on the contraction dynamics, not on what causes the bounce).

Computational verification

Numerical test: \(|\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}| < 10^{-15}\) across 1000 random Riemann tensors with the symmetries of FRW perturbations. This confirms the Holst term vanishes identically as a topological invariant.

How to check

Paper: §12 (Perturbation-Transparency Theorem), 5-step proof chain. Script: research/transparency_verification/verify_holst_vanishing.py

2. 14-Barrier Systematic Closure Map

Paper 1, §11 · N3 — New Systematic Method

What it is

A complete catalog of 14 independent structural barriers that close all standard mechanism classes tested from a nonsingular quantum bounce to late-time dark energy within the ECH framework. Each barrier is named, quantified with equations, and cross-referenced against the specific foundation or branch study that established it. The map covers 7 foundation studies (A–G) and 17 research branches (H–W).

What existed before

Individual barriers are known in various contexts. Mass-coupling issues in Poincaré gauge theory are discussed in Blagojević & Hehl (2013). Scale separation and fine-tuning problems are standard in the cosmological constant literature. 't Hooft (1979) established technical naturalness as a criterion. Weinberg (1989) cataloged the cosmological constant problem. Various groups have noted specific difficulties with torsion-based dark energy, including Shie, Nester & Yo (2008).

What's new

No prior work systematically tested and closed all standard mechanism classes we identified and tested for connecting a nonsingular bounce to late-time dark energy within a single theoretical framework. The 14 barriers are organized into a complete closure argument: mass-coupling lock (Barrier 1), Topological-Shift Duality (Barrier 2, itself an original theorem — see N3-4), scalar-tensor universality (Barrier 3), Planck suppression (Barrier 4), attractor-sensitivity dilemma (Barrier 5), parameter immunity (Barrier 6), Liouville conservation (Barrier 7), and seven more. The systematic exhaustion across 7 foundations and 17 branches — with each branch opened only after passing a 4-question filter — is unprecedented in scope for this class of problem.

Computational verification

Each barrier is backed by its own derivation in the corresponding research/foundation_*/ or research/branch_*/ directory. Fine-tuning numbers (e.g., \(10^{-122}\) for Planck suppression, \(10^{-57}\) for graviton-loop fine-tuning in PGT) are computed analytically with dimensional analysis cross-checks.

How to check

Paper: §11 (Systematic Barrier Catalog), Table of 14 barriers with equations. Research files: research/foundation_A_pgt/ through research/foundation_G_bounce_vacuum_selection/ (7 foundation studies) and research/branch_H_bounce_only/ through research/branch_W_alp_curvaton_tilt/ (17 branch analyses).

3. \(f_{\rm NL} = -35/8\) Forecast Package

Paper 2 · N3 — New Observable Mapping

What it is

The first comprehensive forecast package for testing the matter-bounce non-Gaussianity prediction with upcoming surveys. This combines a parameter-free theoretical prediction with instrument-specific sensitivity calculations, Bayesian model comparison, and a systematic robustness analysis across multiple potential failure modes. The prediction is \(f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local} = -35/8 = -4.375\), which is 300 times larger than standard inflation and opposite in sign.

What existed before

Cai, Chen, Easther & Brandenberger (2009) derived \(f_{\rm NL} = -35/8\) for exact matter-dominated contraction. Heinrich, Doré & Krause (2023) published SPHEREx bispectrum forecasts for generic \(f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local}\). Dalal et al. (2008) established scale-dependent bias as a complementary \(f_{\rm NL}\) probe. MegaMapper science case documents (Schlegel et al.) provide survey specifications. Li & Brandenberger (2014) re-derived a related result with a factor-of-2 difference in convention. Sullivan et al. forecast generic local-PNG performance for MegaMapper and SPHEREx. Heinrich et al. explicitly noted their SPHEREx bispectrum forecast did not yet include wide-angle or GR effects.

What's new

To our knowledge, no prior work combines all of the following into a single matter-bounce-specific forecast: (a) SPHEREx and MegaMapper sensitivity forecasts specific to the bounce prediction; (b) Bayesian model comparison with 600,000+ Monte Carlo realizations showing bounce favored at ~8-17:1 over tuned multifield (prior-dependent; up to >300 with sharp priors); (c) GR-projection robustness analysis showing the signal survives marginalization over relativistic corrections; (d) template-mismatch quantification (amplitude recovery \(r \approx 0.85\text{--}0.90\), CMB Fisher near 0.90, LSS/SDB nearer 0.85; physics-derived full-commutator polynomial (6,2,−18,10,−66,18) from 2×(Eqs. 34+35+36) at ε = 3/2); (e) \(\varepsilon\) correction bounded [1–8%] for \(w = -0.003\) departure from exact matter domination (Hankel singularity at \(\varepsilon = 3/2\)); (f) cubic bounce transmission estimate (0.024% correction); (g) Li-Brandenberger convention resolution establishing that even the worst-case value (\(-35/16\)) is detectable at \(4.4\sigma\) by MegaMapper. No independent verification of \(-35/8\) found in the 2020–2024 literature.

Computational verification

Eight independent computational programs back this forecast:

  • research/fisher_robustness_surface/ — Fisher matrix sensitivity surface
  • research/bayesian_discrimination_program/ — 600K+ MC Bayesian model comparison
  • research/fnl_epsilon_correction/ — \(\varepsilon\) correction bounded [1–8%]
  • research/matter_bounce_parameters/algebraic_commutator_proof.py — Exact-arithmetic proof: 2×(Eqs. 34–36) = Eq. 37
  • research/matter_bounce_parameters/phase3_fisher_overlap.py — \(\ell\)-space Fisher template overlap (CAMB + Planck noise)
  • research/current_data_extraction/F1_bispectrum/scripts/f1_injection_recovery.py — 200-realization MC injection recovery
  • research/current_data_extraction/F3_cmb_residuals_eb/scripts/ — NaMaster EB analysis + injection + frequency consistency
  • research/cubic_bounce_transmission/ — Cubic bounce bispectrum transmission

Key equation: \(f_{\rm NL}^{\rm local} = -\frac{35}{8}\) with physics-derived polynomial (6,2,−18,10,−66,18), verified via algebraic commutator proof and injection recovery.

5. Physics-Derived Full-Commutator Polynomial

Paper 2 · N3 — New Algebraic Result

What it is

Using Cai et al.’s own intermediate vertex contributions (Eqs. 34–36), we derive the full-commutator shape polynomial algebraically: (6, 2, −18, 10, −66, 18). This is proven by exact rational arithmetic (Python Fraction) at multiple configurations. The published Eq. 37 coefficients (3, 1, −9, 5, −66, 9) are the single-time-ordering values. The factor of 2 is the in-in commutator: \(i\langle[\zeta^3, L]\rangle = -2\,\mathrm{Im}\,\langle\zeta^3 L\rangle\).

What existed before

Cai et al. (2009) published Eq. 37 with the single-ordering coefficients. Li et al. (2017) obtained a factor-of-2 different result. No prior work traced the factor-of-2 to the commutator, identified the printed coefficients as single-ordering values, or derived the full-commutator polynomial from the paper’s own intermediate equations. A literature search (2009–2024) found no independent numerical verification of \(-35/8\).

What's new

  • Algebraic proof: \(2 \times (\text{Eqs. 34+35+36}) = \text{Eq. 37}\) at equilateral (exact) and folded (exact)
  • Physics-derived polynomial: (6,2,−18,10,−66,18) — the full-commutator result
  • Published (3,1,−9,5,−66,9) identified as single-ordering coefficients
  • Template overlap with true polynomial: \(r \approx 0.85\text{--}0.90\) (less mismatch than the fitted polynomial suggested)
  • No independent \(-35/8\) verification found in the 2009–2024 literature

How to check

research/matter_bounce_parameters/algebraic_commutator_proof.py — Run with Python 3. Uses exact Fraction arithmetic. Reproduces all benchmarks.

6. Independent EB Birefringence Analysis

Paper 2 · N2 — Independent Robustness Check

What it is

An independent measurement of cosmic birefringence \(\beta\) from Planck SMICA maps using NaMaster with B-mode purification. Includes a resolution ladder (NSIDE 256–2048), frequency consistency test (100/143/217 GHz), injection recovery, and miscalibration marginalization.

Key results

  • Lead result: \(\beta = 0.19 \pm 0.03°\) at NSIDE=1024
  • Frequency consistency: All 3 HFI channels positive, spread < 3σ
  • Injection recovery: All levels pass (0–0.5°), including \(\beta = 0.27°\) with zero bias
  • Resolution stress test: NSIDE=2048 gives \(\beta = 0.07 \pm 0.02°\) (high-\(\ell\) instability)
  • Miscalibration marginalization: \(\beta = 0.16 \pm 0.09°\); prediction (0.27°) at 1.2σ

What's new

The resolution ladder and frequency consistency test for the matter-bounce ALP prediction. The NSIDE=2048 stress test revealing high-\(\ell\) instability. Injection recovery validating the NaMaster estimator for the full science range. These are supporting rather than primary results, but they demonstrate active data engagement beyond theoretical forecasting.

4. Topological-Shift Duality

Paper 1, §11.2 (Barrier 2) · N2+ — Sharpened Formal Synthesis

What it is

An original theorem proving that mass protection and geometric content are mutually exclusive for pseudoscalar fields coupled to the Nieh-Yan 4-form. If the Nieh-Yan term is topological (as in standard EC theory), the pseudoscalar mass is shift-symmetry protected but the coupling is a total derivative with no dynamics. If the Nieh-Yan term is non-topological (as in metric-affine gravity), the coupling generates dynamics but the shift symmetry is broken and no mass protection exists. You cannot have both simultaneously.

What existed before

The Nieh-Yan term and its topological properties in EC theory are known from Nieh & Yan (1982). Metric-affine gravity with non-topological Nieh-Yan has been studied by Obukhov et al. and others. Karananas (2015) and Castillo-Felisola & Vera (2020) discussed pseudoscalar couplings to topological terms. The general tension between shift symmetry and non-trivial dynamics is recognized informally in the axion literature. Bombacigno et al. (2021) showed the Nieh-Yan term loses topological character with nonmetricity. Karananas et al. (2025) argued that the relevant operator fails to break shift symmetry and cannot generate a potential.

What's new

Our contribution is to formulate the known tension as a precise biconditional duality with explicit conditions, synthesizing the insights of Bombacigno et al. and Karananas et al. into a named structural result within the ECH barrier catalog. We proved that the two desirable properties (mass protection via shift symmetry, and non-trivial geometric content) are structurally incompatible for any pseudoscalar coupled to the Nieh-Yan 4-form. This is a general structural result applicable beyond ECH — it constrains any attempt to use topological terms in gravity as sources for light pseudoscalars.

Computational verification

Analytical proof. The duality follows from the definition of the Nieh-Yan form and the relationship between topological character and shift symmetry. Verified symbolically in the Foundation B study.

How to check

Paper: §11.2, Barrier 2 (Topological-Shift Duality). Research files: research/foundation_B_lock_breaking/ (Phase 1 and Phase 2 analyses).

N1 — Verification & Synthesis

Several results in this research program are independent verifications or re-derivations of established physics. We claim NO novelty for these foundations — only for the synthesis built on top of them and for the structural results (N3, N2) that emerged from systematically stress-testing them.

ECH Action & Field Equations

The Einstein-Cartan-Holst action with the Barbero-Immirzi parameter and the resulting modified Friedmann equation are standard results from the EC and LQC literature. We re-derived them as a foundation for our analysis but do not claim this derivation as novel. Our contribution begins with what we did with these equations.

Modified Friedmann Equation from LQC

The bounce-modified Friedmann equation \(H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho\left(1 - \frac{\rho}{\rho_c}\right)\) is due to Ashtekar & Singh (2011) and the LQC community. We use this as an input, not a result.

Four-Fermion Interaction from Torsion

The result that integrating out torsion in EC theory produces a four-fermion contact interaction is due to Hehl et al. (1976) and Kibble (1961). This is textbook material in EC gravity. We verified it as part of Foundation A but assign it N1 (verification only).

fNL = -35/8 Derivation

The core prediction \(f_{\rm NL} = -35/8\) for exact matter-dominated contraction is due to Cai, Chen, Easther & Brandenberger (2009). We verified it algebraically (Cai action audit) and numerically, but the derivation itself is prior art. Our N3 contribution is the forecast package built around it, not the prediction itself.
